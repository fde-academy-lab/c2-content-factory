Update this project's memory with the teaching approach the requester set on 12 October 2026. It
applies to every week, day, Saturday and project built from now on, and it replaces what it
supersedes. Keep people's names out of memory: roles only.

Remember:

- The case leads. Every teaching day opens on an engineering case at a company the learners use,
  such as Uber, Ola, Swiggy, Zomato, Blinkit, Zepto, Flipkart, Amazon India, PhonePe, Google or an AI
  startup, in a named city, state or country, with the metric, its value before and after with dates,
  the data the analyst holds, the ask and the known caveats. Example of the register: "Uber,
  Hyderabad: after a peak-hour earnings guarantee, peak online hours rose 24 percent while completed
  peak trips rose 3 percent; is the scheme working, which metric should it pay for, and is anyone
  gaming it?" Each case is labelled illustrative (the company is real, the situation and the data
  are synthetic), claims nothing the company actually did, and uses public facts only from a dated
  source. No stakeholder drama opens a day.
- Kalpa stays the programme's client: the TA lab and one unguided exercise run the same method on
  Kalpa's version of the problem, which keeps the weeks connected and the Saturday bank in reach.
- Each case climbs a ladder of sub-problems from a weak learner to a hard interview: L1 a hand count
  on about twelve rows, L2 the full data in the smallest code, L3 the trap and the denominator, L4 the
  caveats an engineer must name, L5 the decision with its cost and the objection, L6 the interview at
  Uber, Swiggy, Google and AI-startup depth with weak and hire-grade answers side by side and an
  AI-company variant of the same idea.
- Every deck, notebook, exercise, case study, project, Saturday paper and note teaches in a blend of
  styles: Veritasium's misconception and prediction first; 3Blue1Brown's and StatQuest's visual
  derivation of the maths from tiny numbers before the formula; Krish Naik's line-by-line code-along
  to a working result; ByteByteGo's connected maps of how ideas link; takeUforward's ladder of
  problems from easy to interview grade; Brilliant's problem-first interactivity and explorable
  explanations; the Feynman technique; plus retrieval of earlier days, interleaving, fading worked
  examples, wrong answers to debug, and peer instruction (predict, vote, discuss, vote again).
- Each week ships one interactive HTML explorer that grows day by day: a connected map of the week
  on its home screen and one section per day that animates the mechanism, asks for a prediction and
  names the formula last.
- Pictures are drawn by our own code: mermaid for mechanisms and maps, matplotlib for data pictures,
  short GIFs from our own data with a still beside each.
- A day runs in three or four chapters sized to its minutes, with no item, slide or word quota; review
  is one fresh-graduate read, one hiring-interviewer read, one fix pass and the verify command.
- Week 4 (26 to 31 October 2026) is the first week built this way: Uber Hyderabad for metric design
  and its stakeholder defence, Swiggy Instamart Pune for basket analysis, a food-delivery membership
  in Chennai for cohorts and funnels, a Lucknow fulfilment centre for forecasting across the Diwali
  shift (20 October 2025 to 8 November 2026), and a Saturday of a 120-minute paper, a transfer drill
  and paired mock interviews.
