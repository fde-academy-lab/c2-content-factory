## Monday: framing a prediction problem, the split and the baseline, linear and logistic regression

| Company | Year | Lesson | Concrete fact | URL | Verified |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Uber (DeepETA) | 2022 | Frame the target before choosing the model: Uber predicts the residual between the routing engine's ETA and what happened, with the routing engine as the baseline that stands until a learner beats it. | "We then use machine learning to predict the residual between the routing engine ETA and real-world observed outcomes." "For several years, Uber used gradient-boosted decision tree ensembles to refine ETA predictions." The new model "must improve significantly over the incumbent XGBoost model" on MAE and "must return an ETA within a few milliseconds at most"; it is "the highest QPS (queries per second) model at Uber". No accuracy numbers are published. Post dated 10 February 2022, authors Binaykiya, Hu, Cirit, Hora. | https://www.uber.com/blog/deepeta-how-uber-predicts-arrival-times/ | 28 Oct 2026 |
| Google (Rules of ML, Martin Zinkevich) | page last updated 25 Aug 2025 | A heuristic is the first baseline and the first model should be simple and interpretable; linear and logistic regression are named as the debuggable starting point. | Rule 1: "Don't be afraid to launch a product without machine learning." "If you think that machine learning will give you a 100% boost, then a heuristic will get you 50% of the way there." Rule 4: "Keep the first model simple and get the infrastructure right." Rule 14: "Starting with an interpretable model makes debugging easier" (linear, logistic and Poisson regression). | https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/guides/rules-of-ml | 28 Oct 2026 |
| Booking.com (KDD 2019 paper, Bernardi, Mavridis, Estevez) | 2019 | The problem set-up is a design choice: the same business question can be a regression, a classification or a ranking problem, and a set-up is judged by whether a simple model beats a trivial baseline on it. | Section 4, "Before solving a problem, design it": "a regression predicting the length of a stay, turned in to a multiclass classification problem"; set-ups are compared by "the performance of simple models against trivial baselines like random and popularity. Setups where simple models can do significantly better than trivial models are preferred"; "often the best problem is not the one that comes to mind immediately". Figure 3: six algorithm iterations and four problem set-ups on one recommendations product, the problem re-cuts among the largest gains. Paper pages 1743 to 1751 of the proceedings. | Canonical DOI https://doi.org/10.1145/3292500.3330744 (ACM page returned 403 in this session). How a trainer tells it, under two minutes each:

- Uber DeepETA. The map engine already gives an ETA, so Uber did not ask the model to predict minutes from scratch; it asked it to predict how wrong the map engine is, then added the correction back. The baseline sat in production for years as gradient boosted trees, and the deep model had to beat it on MAE while answering in a few milliseconds, or it did not ship.
- Google Rules of ML. Zinkevich's first rule is to launch without ML if a rule gets you half the gain, and his fourth is to make the first model simple so you find out whether the pipeline, the split and the labels are right. Rule 14 is why Monday opens on linear and logistic regression: when the number is wrong you can read the weights and see why.
- Booking.com problem design. The same question, how long will this guest stay, was tried as a regression and as a multiclass classification, and the classification set-up unlocked more value. Booking picks between set-ups by one test the class can run on Monday: does a simple model beat random or popularity on that set-up by a clear margin.

## Tuesday: the confusion matrix, precision and recall, class imbalance, thresholds set from error costs

| Company | Year | Lesson | Concrete fact | URL | Verified |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stripe (Radar technical guide) | page last updated 15 Dec 2021 | The threshold is a business policy set from the two error costs, and the break-even precision falls out of margin arithmetic; the model shapes the curve, the policy picks the point on it. | "The precision of our policy is the fraction of transactions we block that are actually fraudulent"; "recall is the fraction of all fraud that is caught by our policy". "The model controls the shape of the precision-recall curve." "The policy controls where on the curve we're operating." Worked example at a 26 dollar sale, 8 percent margin, 15 dollar chargeback fee: "one fraudulent sale costs you the profit of $38.92 / $2.08 = 18.71 legitimate sales" and "your break-even precision is 1 / (1 + 18.71) = 5.07%". "33% of consumers said they wouldn't shop again at a business after a false decline." Block rule written as "P(fraud)>0.7". | https://stripe.com/guides/primer-on-machine-learning-for-fraud-protection | 28 Oct 2026 |
| PhonePe (P2P fraud model) | 2022 | In payments the false positive blocks a genuine transfer, so the system is tuned for very high precision, versions are compared on recorded precision and recall, and labels are checked by humans. | Post of 28 July 2022 by Nipun VK: PhonePe must "design fraud prevention systems at very high precision"; "Various classification algorithms were tried"; "The precision and the recall of the various versions were recorded"; operations staff reviewed a sample and called senders to validate. Outcome: "Fraud rate was reduced by 40% on consumer to consumer transactions on PhonePe" and "Number of daily fraud complaints reduced by 38% overall". Scale: "over 2.5 billion transactions in a month", "100 million transactions on a single day" in April 2022. The fraud-to-genuine ratio and the final precision and recall are not published. | https://tech.phonepe.com/building-phonepes-real-time-transaction-model-to-prevent-frauds-a-journey/ | 28 Oct 2026 |
| Razorpay (Vulcan) | 2026 | Labels arrive late and compare at a fixed alert volume: raising recall by reviewing more payments is a threshold move, so a model claim is only fair at the same number of alerts. | Post dated 10 September 2026: "Fraud may be confirmed only when a dispute appears weeks later, and some fraud is never reported." Fraud results are "read at the same alert volume"; "catching more fraud by reviewing more payments is a threshold change, not proof that the model improved"; "the 5× result was reported without an increase in alerts"; "This is based on early results, published at launch." The 8X figure quoted in press coverage is not in the post's text and is not verified. | https://razorpay.com/blog/vulcan-how-razorpay-built-a-foundation-model-for-payment-decisions | 28 Oct 2026 |
| Flipkart with CMU (BIRDNEST ratings-fraud paper, Hooi and others) | 2015, published 2016 | Precision at k is what a review team can act on: hand labelling the top 250 flagged accounts gave the precision figure, and the review budget is the threshold. | Flipkart data: 1.1M users, 550K products, 3.3M ratings. "We applied BIRDNEST to detect the 250 most suspicious users and provided them to Flipkart; these accounts were investigated and hand-labelled by Flipkart, finding that 211 users of the top 250 flagged by BIRDNEST were involved in fraud." Precision "of 1.0 for the first 50 users", "over 84% for the top 250". Pattern found: accounts "spamming 4/5 star ratings to multiple products from a single seller" or "spamming 1/2 star ratings to multiple products from another seller". | https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.06030 (PDF read) | 28 Oct 2026 |

How a trainer tells it, under two minutes each:

- Stripe Radar. Put the arithmetic on the board: a 26 dollar sale at 8 percent margin earns 2.08, a chargeback costs 38.92, so one missed fraud wipes out 18.71 good sales and the break-even precision is 5 percent. Then ask the room why a lender, whose false approve costs the whole loan, sits at a very different point on the same curve than a marketer whose false nudge costs one SMS.
- PhonePe. A blocked genuine transfer is a customer who cannot pay a friend, so PhonePe tuned for precision first and still cut the fraud rate by 40 percent and complaints by 38 percent. Ask what they did about labels: they sampled predictions and phoned the senders, because a confusion matrix is only as honest as its ground truth.
- Razorpay Vulcan. Fraud labels show up weeks later as disputes, and some never show up, so the training set is always missing recent positives. Razorpay's own rule is the one to copy: compare two models at the same alert volume, because "more fraud caught" with more alerts is just a lower threshold.
- Flipkart ratings fraud. The model ranked 1.1 million accounts and Flipkart investigated the top 250 by hand: 211 were fraud, the first 50 all were. That is precision at k drawn as a curve, and the point to pick on it is the number of accounts the trust team can investigate this week.

## Wednesday: feature engineering and data leakage

| Company | Year | Lesson | Concrete fact | URL | Verified |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zech and others, chest X-ray pneumonia models across NIH, Mount Sinai and Indiana (PLOS Medicine) | 2018 | A model will learn any feature that predicts the label in training, including where the image came from; the leak shows up as a drop on data from a new site. | 158,323 radiographs from three systems (NIH 112,120; Mount Sinai 42,396; Indiana 3,807). Joint NIH plus Mount Sinai model: internal AUC 0.931, external AUC 0.815 at Indiana (P = 0.001). CNNs detected the hospital system of a radiograph for 99.95 percent of NIH images (22,050 of 22,062) and 99.98 percent of Mount Sinai images, and separated portable ward films from emergency films "with 100% accuracy"; the Figure 2 caption says the CNN "learned to detect a metal token that radiology technicians place on the patient". Pneumonia prevalence 41.1 percent on portable inpatient films against 32.8 percent in the emergency department. A model ranking cases only by each hospital's average pneumonia prevalence reached AUC 0.861 on the joint test set. | https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371/journal.pmed.1002683 | 28 Oct 2026 |
| Kaggle IJCNN 2011 Social Network Challenge, winning entry by Narayanan, Shi and Rubinstein | 2011 | Leakage through outside data: the anonymised test set could be re-identified from a public crawl, so the "features" were the answers; the lesson is to ask where every input would come from at prediction time. | Challenge graph 1,133,547 nodes and 7,237,983 edges; test set 8,960 edges, half true and half fake; 119 teams. The authors crawled Flickr between mid-December 2010 and mid-January 2011 (2,038,803 nodes, 163,579,517 edges). "By de-anonymizing much of the competition test set using our own Flickr crawl, we were able to effectively game the competition." Winning entry: "a combined test Area Under Curve (AUC) of 0.981". They told the organisers once in the lead; the organisers "found the method acceptable". | https://arxiv.org/abs/1102.4374 (PDF read) | 28 Oct 2026 |
| Airbnb (Applying Deep Learning to Airbnb Search, Haldar and others, KDD 2019 paper, arXiv 2018) | 2018 | Feature engineering is about the shape of the inputs: unnormalised features stalled training, distribution plots exposed logging bugs, and a derived feature (offset from map centre) beat the raw one. | "In our very first attempt at training a NN, we simply took all the features used to train the GBDT model and fed it to the NN. This went down very badly. The loss would saturate in the middle of training ... We traced the issue to the fact that the features were not normalized." Fix: (value minus mean) over standard deviation for bell-shaped features, log((1 + value) / (1 + median)) for power-law ones. Bug-spotting: "we had bugs related to currencies in the prices logged for certain geographies. And for periods greater than 28 days, the logged price was the monthly price instead of the daily price. These errors showed up as spikes on the initial distribution plots." Location: raw lat/lng replaced by the log of the offset from the map centre. | https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.09591 (PDF read) | 28 Oct 2026 |
| Uber (Michelangelo platform, UberEATS delivery time) | 2017 | A feature is a definition that must be computed the same way at training and at serving; Uber's three feature kinds (request inputs, seven-day aggregates, last-hour aggregates) are the ones the class will build. | Post of 5 September 2017 by Hermann and Del Balso. UberEATS predicts total meal preparation and delivery time "before an order is placed and again at each delivery stage", from request inputs such as "time of day" and "delivery location", historical aggregates such as a restaurant's average prep time over the previous seven days, and near-real-time aggregates such as average prep time over the last hour. Feature Store of about 10,000 features refreshed daily; "the same expressions are applied at training time and at prediction time". | https://www.uber.com/blog/michelangelo-machine-learning-platform/ | 28 Oct 2026 |

How a trainer tells it, under two minutes each:

- Zech chest X-rays. A pneumonia model scored 0.93 at home and 0.82 at a hospital it had never seen, and the same network could tell which hospital an image came from 99.95 percent of the time, partly from a metal token taped to the patient. Portable ward films carried more pneumonia than emergency films, so "which machine took this" was a feature the model learned instead of lungs.
- Kaggle social network challenge. The organisers scrubbed every user ID, and the winners rebuilt the identities by crawling Flickr themselves, reaching AUC 0.981 largely by looking the answers up. The question to carry into every feature review is the one that would have caught it: at prediction time, where does this column come from, and could it contain the label?
- Airbnb features. The first neural net was fed the same raw features as the tree model and the loss stopped moving mid-training because nothing was normalised; one scaling rule fixed it. The same team then found a currency bug and a monthly-versus-daily price bug by plotting histograms and looking for spikes, which is the whole feature-engineering habit in one slide.
- Uber Michelangelo. Delivery time is predicted from three kinds of features, and the trap is that "average prep time over the last hour" means one thing in a notebook and another in a live service. Uber's answer was a feature store where a single expression is applied at training and at serving, so the class names its features as definitions, never as columns.

## Thursday: overfitting, regularisation, cross-validation, when a simpler model should win

| Company | Year | Lesson | Concrete fact | URL | Verified |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Airbnb (same KDD 2019 search paper) | 2018 | Start with the simple model, and a per-item ID feature overfits when each item has only a few hundred labels; the learning curve shows it. | "we started off with some intricate custom architectures, only to get overwhelmed by their complexity and ended up consuming cycles." "The first architecture that we finally managed to get online was a simple single hidden layer NN with 32 fully connected ReLU activations that proved booking neutral against the GBDT model." Listing-ID embeddings: "in the different variations we tried, listing ids mostly led to overfitting"; Figure 5 shows training NDCG climbing toward 1 while test NDCG stays flat; "Even the most popular listing can be booked at most 365 times in an entire year." The production DNN took 195 input features, hidden layers of 127 and 83 ReLUs, and 1.7 billion training pairs to close the train-test gap. | https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.09591 (PDF read) | 28 Oct 2026 |
| Caruana and others, pneumonia risk and 30-day readmission (KDD 2015, Microsoft Research and NewYork-Presbyterian) | 2015 | The more accurate model was set aside for the one that could be read and repaired; the AUC gap between the simple and the complex model was under 0.02; the interaction count was chosen by cross-validation and bagging was used against overfitting. | In the 1990s CEHC study "the neural net had AUC=0.86 compared to 0.77 for logistic regression", yet the rule learner found "HasAsthma(x) => LowerRisk(x)" because asthmatics were admitted "directly to the ICU" and treated aggressively; the neural nets were set aside because their lack of intelligibility "made it difficult to know what other problems might also need fixing", and the team went forward "only with models that were intelligible such as logistic regression, even if they had lower AUC". 2015 pneumonia data (14,199 patients, 9,847 train and 4,352 test, 10.86 percent died): logistic regression AUC 0.8432, GAM 0.8542, GA2M 0.8576, random forests 0.8460, LogitBoost 0.8493, "less than 0.02" apart. GA2M keeps 10 of 1,035 possible pairwise terms, "k chosen by cross-validation", with 100 rounds of bagging "to reduce overfitting". | https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/KDD2015FinalDraftIntelligibleModels4HealthCare_igt143e-caruanaA.pdf (canonical DOI 10.1145/2783258.2788613) | 28 Oct 2026 |
| Google Flu Trends, as analysed by Lazer, Kennedy, King and Vespignani (Science, 14 March 2014) | 2014 | Fifty million candidate features fitted to 1,152 points is overfitting at scale; the tell was terms about high school basketball that tracked flu season. | "the methodology was to find the best matches among 50 million search terms to fit 1152 data points"; developers reported "weeding out seasonal search terms unrelated to the flu but strongly correlated to the CDC data, such as those regarding high school basketball. This should have been a warning that the big data were overfitting the small number of cases"; "the initial version of GFT was part flu detector, part winter detector", and it "completely missed the nonseasonal 2009 influenza A-H1N1 pandemic". | https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/7312037d-0e0d-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b/content (Harvard DASH open-access PDF; DOI 10.1126/science.1248506) | 28 Oct 2026 |

How a trainer tells it, under two minutes each:

- Airbnb listing IDs. Giving every listing its own learned vector sent training NDCG toward 1 while test NDCG never moved, because a listing can be booked at most 365 times a year, so each vector had too few labels to be anything except memory. Show the two curves from Figure 5 side by side, then show the 32-node network that shipped first because it proved the pipeline before anyone added capacity.
- Caruana pneumonia. The neural net scored 0.86 and the logistic regression 0.77, and the hospital shipped the regression, because the rule learner had just shown that the data taught "asthma lowers risk" and nobody could check what else the net had learned. Twenty years later the intelligible GA2M model sat within 0.02 AUC of random forests, and the asthma term could be set to zero by hand.
- Google Flu overfitting. Fifty million search terms were ranked against 1,152 weekly flu counts, and terms about high school basketball made the cut because basketball season is flu season. Ask the room what 50 million candidates and 1,152 rows does to the chance of a spurious winner, then show how a held-out season or a regularised fit would have flagged it.

## Friday: justifying a model to a committee, the cost of errors in money, how to know a model has gone stale

| Company | Year | Lesson | Concrete fact | URL | Verified |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zillow (Zillow Offers wind-down, 8-K and Q3 shareholder letter of 2 November 2021) | 2021 | A forecast error has a price in money and in jobs; the committee question is what one point of error on the model's output costs, and whether errors in both directions were in the plan. | 8-K: Zillow "recorded a $304.4 million write-down of inventory associated with Zillow Offers", "expects to record additional charges of approximately $240 million to $265 million" in Q4, and the wind-down will "result in approximately a 25% reduction of the Company's workforce", citing "home pricing unpredictability, capacity constraints and other operational challenges". Shareholder letter (Exhibit 99.3, signed by Rich Barton and Allen Parker): "We have been unable to accurately forecast future home prices at different times in both directions"; the business was "underpinned by the need to forecast the price of homes accurately three to six months into the future"; unit economics swung "approximately 1,200 basis points from Q2" to a projected minus 500 to minus 700 basis points in Q4; "further scaling up Zillow Offers is too risky, too volatile to our earnings and operations". | https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1617640/000161764021000085/z-20211102.htm and https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1617640/000161764021000085/exhibit993.htm | 28 Oct 2026 |
| Booking.com (same KDD 2019 paper, Section 3) | 2019 | Offline metric gains did not predict business gains across 23 model pairs, so the committee should be shown the randomised trial, never the AUC alone. | Section title: "Modeling: offline model performance is just a health check". "increasing the performance of a model, does not necessarily translates to a gain in value." Figure 4: "a total of 23 comparisons (46 models)" of offline gain (ROC AUC or Mean Reciprocal Rank) against business gain in a randomised controlled trial: "Pearson correlation is -0.1 with 90% confidence interval (-0.45, 0.27), and Spearman correlation is -0.18 with 90% confidence interval (-0.5, 0.19)". Four explanations named: value performance saturation, segment saturation, uncanny valley effect, proxy over-optimization (a click model that ends up "just driving clicks"). Section 5: "an increase of about 30% in latency costs more than 0.5% in conversion rate". | As on Monday: DOI https://doi.org/10.1145/3292500.3330744, text read from the mirror PDF named above | 28 Oct 2026 |
| Instacart (item availability model, March 2020) | 2020 | A model goes stale when the world changes under it; the signal was the found-rate metric collapsing, and the first fix was a threshold change while retraining caught up. | Fortune, 9 June 2020, Jonathan Vanian: "The accuracy of a metric used to evaluate how many items are found at a store dropped to 61% from 93%", "tipping off the Instacart engineers that they needed to re-train their machine learning model"; Sharath Rao, director of machine learning: "A shock to the system"; by June the metric was "correct about 85% of the time". Instacart's own post of 10 July 2023: out-of-stock cancellations "spiked during the onset of COVID"; "ML models exhibit a sluggish response as they require ample observations to adapt"; thresholds on top of the model were adjusted "without having to train new ML models"; customers with a perfect found rate are "twice as likely to retain and re-order". | https://fortune.com/2020/06/09/instacart-coronavirus-artificial-intelligence and https://company.instacart.com/tech-innovation/how-instacarts-item-availability-evolved-over-the-pandemic | 28 Oct 2026 |
| Google Flu Trends (same Lazer and others paper) | 2014 | Staleness shows in the residuals: a model that misses high 100 weeks out of 108, with this week's error predictable from last week's, has drifted, and the cause was the search engine changing underneath it. | "GFT was predicting more than double the proportion of doctor visits for influenza-like illness (ILI) than the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention"; the revised model "has missed high for 100 out of 108 weeks starting with August 2011"; "last week's errors predict this week's errors (temporal autocorrelation)"; the two causes named are "big data hubris and algorithm dynamics", the second being Google's own changes to search. | Same DASH PDF as Thursday | 28 Oct 2026 |

How a trainer tells it, under two minutes each:

- Zillow. The house-flipping arm needed to know a home's price three to six months out, missed in both directions, and the quarter closed with a 304 million dollar write-down, up to 265 million more expected, and a quarter of the company's jobs gone. Hand the room the committee question in Zillow's own words: unit economics swung 1,200 basis points in two quarters, so what error band on the model would have made that visible in advance?
- Booking.com offline versus business. Across 23 model pairs the offline gain and the trial gain had a correlation around zero, with confidence intervals that straddle it, and the paper lists why: saturation, segment saturation, the uncanny valley and over-optimised proxies. The habit it leaves is that a model is justified by its randomised trial, and the offline metric is only the health check that earns it one.
- Instacart. In March 2020 the found-rate metric fell from 93 to 61 percent inside weeks, because shoppers started hoarding and the model had learned a world that no longer existed. The monitoring lesson is the metric itself, the operating lesson is that the first fix was a threshold, and the modelling lesson is Instacart's own line that models respond sluggishly because they need enough new observations to adapt.
- Google Flu drift. The second Google Flu model ran high for 100 of 108 weeks and its errors were predictable from the week before, which is the textbook residual plot of a stale model; the search engine had changed and the model had not. Pair it with Google's own Rule 37, "Measure Training/Serving Skew", which compares training against holdout, holdout against next-day data, and next-day against live scores to separate drift from engineering bugs (https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/guides/rules-of-ml, verified 28 Oct 2026).
