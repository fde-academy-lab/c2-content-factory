# COHORT 1 LEARNINGS
## What happened, and what changed because of it

Cohort 1 ran February to August 2026 with 30 students. Every entry below pairs an observed failure with the binding fix that is now in force for Cohort 2. The fix column overrides any Cohort 1 document.

---

## STRUCTURE

**Observed.** Students reported through the cohort that there was no structure and no clarity about what was coming. Build weeks improved the situation without resolving it. In a six-month programme, a first month that does not land cannot be repaired in months two through six.

**Fix.** The full day-wise plan exists before the cohort starts and is published to the student portal. Every day names its topics, its prerequisites, and what the learner will be able to do at the end of it. Nothing is decided week by week.

---

## TRAINERS

**Observed.** Trainer quality did not track with experience, pedigree, or cost. A trainer with a Google background rated 3.75 when given no delivery instructions. A hire who interviewed strongly failed a basic technical check once on the ground. Paying flight costs and premium day rates bought no guarantee.

**Observed.** Trainers who were given only a topic taught whatever examples they were personally comfortable with. Concepts got covered. The industrial framing and the scenario continuity did not survive.

**Fix.** Content carries the session. Approximately 60 to 70 percent of each session is fixed by the material, 30 to 40 percent is trainer discretion. Trainers receive a deck, trainer notes, demo code, exercises, and a quiz. Coverage of the fixed portion is tracked against the day sheet, and session transcripts confirm what was actually delivered.

---

## EXAMPLES AND DOMAINS

**Observed.** Examples changed domain constantly. Amazon in one session, Uber in the next, Swiggy after that. Learners at this intake level were re-orienting to a new business context each time instead of building depth on a concept.

**Fix.** One connected scenario, client zero, carried across the programme. The same data re-expressed as the toolset grows. Domain changes at most twice in 20 weeks. Full rules in `02_Content_Doctrine.md`.

---

## WORKLOAD

**Observed.** Too many weekly projects. Cognitive overload was the recurring complaint, and project quality dropped because attention was split.

**Fix.** Mini projects run in build weeks only, five across the programme. Weekly take-home work varies in type: implementation tasks, module-to-module pre-reads, exercise continuations. It is not a project every week.

---

## ASSESSMENT LOAD

**Observed.** The weekly written test plus the weekly assessment cycle consumed disproportionate time relative to what it revealed.

**Fix.** A daily Kahoot, ungraded; a Saturday recap paper on regular weeks, objective, AI-free and ungraded, built from interview questions; three major exams in Weeks 5, 10 and 16; and mini projects, mock interviews and business GDs in the five build weeks. Daily coding practice carries no marks, and whether it runs on CodeChef is still open.

---

## CURRICULUM GAPS

**Observed.** The curriculum listed topics without listing prerequisites. Sessions depended on concepts and tools that had not been established, and trainers spent teaching time on setup and back-fill.

**Fix.** Every session declares its prerequisites, both the concepts assumed clear and any tool being used for the first time. Setup instructions for tomorrow ship tonight.

---

## PLATFORMS

**Observed.** Platform sprawl across Slack, LMS, GitHub, CodeChef and Neo, with overlapping purposes and no single place a learner could look.

**Fix.** Neo for assessment. CodeChef for daily practice. GitHub for exercises, discussions and portfolios. LMS for content and submissions. No Slack in Cohort 2.

---

## CAPSTONE TIMING

**Observed.** The capstone began before the cohort had the specialisation depth to attempt it seriously.

**Fix.** The capstone runs Weeks 17 to 20, announced at the end of Week 16 and built through three gates. Build weeks at Weeks 3, 6, 9, 12 and 15 carry the project practice that used to sit inside an over-long capstone.

---

## OUTCOMES

Confirmed Cohort 1 outcomes, from a cohort of 30 with 11 placement-waiver students:

- One learner secured a forward deployed engineer role independently
- One learner secured an external internship carrying a pre-placement offer

## WHAT THE PROGRAMME HEAD'S HANDOVER ADDS, 27 SEPTEMBER 2026

The handover to the on-ground manager pairs seven Cohort 1 failures with their Cohort 2 answer.

| Cohort 1 | Cohort 2 |
|---|---|
| There was no structure, and the programme felt like a series of trainers. | Every day has a fixed shape, a written row and a day pack. |
| Trainer pedigree did not predict teaching quality. | A fixed core that the row measures, prepared in ninety minutes. |
| A slow first month never recovered. | A Week 0 baseline, and a Week 1 that opens on a question the room argues about. |
| Mock scores never crossed 50 percent. | Five vivas on the learners' own projects, challenges logs, and coaching from mid-programme. |
| Live demos failed, and on-the-spot questions went badly. | A live demo and a defence every build, and AI-free segments that are observed. |
| Overselling caused avoidable rejections. | Learners are positioned at strong entry level with applied depth, and never as experienced. |
| Too many evaluations did not align, and the weekly checks drifted to code. | Five graded components with the same number of events per week, and the Saturday paper built from interview questions. |

What the ground team protects: the Saturday paper every regular week, AI-free segments that are
visibly AI-free, challenges logs kept live, and any trainer deviation from the row reported the
same day.

## CAPSTONE SCORING CALIBRATION

From the Cohort 1 capstone panel. Carry this into Cohort 2 rubric design and assessor briefing.

Rubric dimensions and maxima: Problem and solution fit /15, Architecture /20, Agentic depth /20, Demo /15, Rigour /10, Communication /10, Q&A /10.

The practical ceiling is 80, not 100. Highest marks actually awarded per dimension in Cohort 1: problem and solution fit 12, architecture 16, agentic depth 16, demo 12, rigour 8, communication 8, Q&A 8. Cohort mean was around 54.

Agentic depth is scored on whether real AI engineering happened, independent of whether the thing shipped. Q&A is the widest-spread column and the real separator between teams. Deployment claims get checked against reality. Silent teammates get interrogated separately.

Assessor notes run three to six sentences, name the specific moment, state why the mark landed where it did, and end on the fix or the unanswered question.

## INTAKE PROFILE

The intake skews below average, with a small number of strong entrants. Content that assumes competence loses most of the room. Content pitched at the weakest learner bores the strongest.

The resolution is tiering inside every session rather than pitching to an average: the fixed content lands for the middle, a prepared stretch task holds the strong, and a prepared prerequisite recovery task catches the stuck. Both extras are built in advance.

The outcome the programme is judged on is placement into GenAI, agentic AI, ML engineering and forward deployed engineering roles. Interview-grade fluency in a small number of things beats shallow coverage of many.
