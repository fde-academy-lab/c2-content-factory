## THE TEACHING DAY

Standard shape for a teaching-week day:

1. Scenario setting
2. Trainer demo
3. Guided exercise, trainer solves step by step, students mirror
4. Unguided exercise, students attempt in session, solution released at close of session, the same exercise carries forward as the assignment
5. Kahoot traps round
6. Kahoot: six to eight items plus the return question from the previous day, ungraded
7. Close-out

Content covers approximately four hours of the teaching day. The remaining time is trainer discretion. Coverage of the four hours is not discretionary.

Daily competitive coding runs on CodeChef in an evening slot as practice. It carries no assessment weight, and no artifact states its clock time.

The daily written test used in Cohort 1 is gone. The daily check is Kahoot, scored on correctness and
speed together, at low weight, kept as an attention and retention signal rather than as a marks
component. The weekly pen-and-paper test moved to Saturday, where it runs AI-free for about two hours
and is followed by the interview-answer discussion; it is ungraded.

**Corrected 13 September 2026.** This section previously named a daily platform MCQ on Neo and a Friday
pen-and-paper test. The Neo daily pool was removed in favour of Kahoot alone, and the Structure tab
puts the recap test on Saturday. No test runs during a build week.

## THE BUILD WEEK

| Day | What happens |
|---|---|
| Monday | The Programme Head introduces the build week online: the five sub-problems, the group-of-four structure, the week's shape, and the rule that no new material is taught. Groups scope their sub-problem in their own words and open the challenges log. |
| Tuesday to Thursday | Groups build. The trainer solves a deliberately smaller slice of one sub-problem in the open and shares progress daily, and each day opens with a checkpoint every group answers in two minutes. Wednesday carries the catch-up reserve, released to build time when it is not needed. |
| Thursday | Mock interviews open: individual, asynchronous, roughly 20 minutes each, half technical and half a viva on the group's own work. |
| Friday | The industry expert arrives for two days. Group discussions run at about 30 minutes per group on the week's problem space, and a first tranche of presentations runs where the roster allows. Builds freeze that night. |
| Saturday | The expert plus a senior industry leader who flies in for the day. Presentations with live demos, roughly 25 to 30 minutes per group, then open questioning. Every grade closes in-week. |

Five sub-problems per build week, three groups each, so the panel hears alternate viewpoints on the same business problem back to back.

The group discussion is a separate running thread from the mini project. It runs at progressive complexity across the programme and is not tied to the module's technical content. The mini project is tied to the module.

**Corrected 13 September 2026.** This table previously put the industry expert and a one-hour business use case discussion on Monday, with the build running Thursday to Saturday. The Structure tab and the Week 3, 6 and 9 curriculum tabs both describe the shape above, and both outrank this file, so the table now matches them.

No tests run during build weeks.

Mock interviews during build weeks are individual, asynchronous, roughly 20 minutes each. Sixty students is roughly 20 hours of mock capacity per build week.

## METRICS

**WAS**, the Weekly Academic Score, is the metric visible to IIT and CDF. Objective components only. This is the weekly metric for institutional reporting.

**WPS**, the Weekly Performance Score, is internal. WAS plus TA weekly score plus faculty weekly score plus subjective elements. Never exposed upward, never named in student-facing material.

CGPA = Total divided by 1000, divided by 100. Computed at programme end only.

Twenty scored WAS weeks, Weeks 2 to 21. Week 1 is baseline only and scores zero WAS.
