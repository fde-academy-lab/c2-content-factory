Six steps: skills, interview questions and what to practise on your own
Step | Weeks | Capability | Role you can interview for | Focus on | By the end you can answer | Practise on your own
Analyst first | 1 to 4 | Read, question and explain data | Data or Business Analyst | Python for data, SQL to the depth an interviewer probes (joins, window functions, validation), statistics you can defend, business metrics, and the one-page note to a stakeholder. | Sales dropped 15 percent last month; how would you investigate? Your join grew the row count; name the cause and the check. | Work through SQLBolt and PostgreSQL Exercises alongside Week 2, and redo each week's exercises from a fresh Codespace with the notebook closed.
Machine learning | 5 to 6 | Predict and evaluate honestly | Entry-level Data Scientist | The modelling loop with a baseline first, the metric chosen from the business cost of an error, features without leakage, and a model you can justify in a short memo. | Accuracy is 96 percent on a 96 to 4 split; is that a good model? What is data leakage, and how do you catch it before it ships? | Rebuild the week's model from a blank notebook, rescore it when the cost of a miss changes, and watch the StatQuest lessons the pre-reads name.
Inside a language model | 7 to 9 | Know what the model is doing | Foundation for the AI Engineer track | Training runs you can debug, loss curves you can read, tokens, attention and embeddings explained in plain words, and the cost of a feature worked out from tokens to a monthly number. | Your loss printed nan; what are your first two moves? What is a token, and why do token counts decide cost? | Watch the 3Blue1Brown neural network lessons and Karpathy's backprop lecture in Week 7, read The Illustrated Transformer in Week 8, and rerun every training run with a second seed.
Generative AI and retrieval | 10 to 12 | Build a grounded assistant | GenAI or AI Engineer | Prompts that survive a model change, structured outputs with validation, function calling, retrieval with reranking, and an evaluation harness that reports real numbers. | How do you keep an LLM feature inside budget? The answer cites the right document and is still wrong; how do you debug that? | Keep a prompt library with the failures that shaped it, build a small golden set for your own retrieval task, and measure retrieval separately from generation.
Agents | 13 to 15 | Make it act, safely, in production | Agentic AI Engineer | Tool schemas a model can act on, agent patterns and when a plain workflow beats an agent, guardrails against injection and overspend, checkpoints and retries, and tracing in production. | When does a workflow beat an agent? How do you stop an agent from looping or overspending? | Trace one agent run end to end and write down every step, record each failure you hit and the structural limit that fixed it, and cost every task.
Forward deployed engineer | 16 to 20 | Frame, propose, build, defend | Forward Deployed Engineer | Separating the stated problem from the real one, a shortlist with disqualification criteria, trade-offs quantified before choosing, a POC or MVP pitch with options A, B and C, and a capstone you can defend line by line. | Walk me through a project: what broke, and what did you do? How would you increase sales for an online retailer? | Keep the challenges log current, rehearse the two-minute version of your capstone story, and prepare against each job description before the interview.
Placement preparation runs through the whole programme: every mock interview is practice against a fixed rubric, behavioural questions join from the third, the challenges log on every project becomes your interview material, and company interviews come in the final weeks with preparation against each job description.
