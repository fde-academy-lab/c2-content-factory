Component | Status | Detail
Week types | Locked | Four kinds of week. Week 0 is the in-person baseline week before teaching starts: a diagnostic by topic, one-to-one baselines, Python and SQL brush-up, and the first classes on problem framing and solution thinking; it carries no marks. Regular teaching weeks (1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 10, 11, 13 and 14, plus the forward deployed block in Week 16) run the daily teaching shape, and all but Week 16 close on the Saturday recap block. Build weeks (3, 6, 9, 12 and 15) run the mini project and carry no tests. Capstone weeks are 17 to 20: three sprints with a gate each, then the demo and defence week.
Regular-week Saturday | Locked (format revised 19 Sep) | A pen-and-paper weekly recap paper, AI-free and objective: fill in the blank, true or false, one correct option, more than one correct option, scenario sets, applied maths and ordering, graded easy, medium and hard, and tagged to the roles it serves. It runs 60 minutes at the least (the ME1 and ME2 weeks), 90 to 110 in a typical week and 120 in the weeks that carry the most interview weight (SQL in Week 2, transformers in Week 8), then a break, then marking and the solution discussion. Papers are swapped and marked against a key, so scores compare across the room and from week to week; the Academic TA leads the discussion as an interview-answer discussion with random call-outs. Ungraded: a performance indicator, never a marks component. The papers for Weeks 1 to 8 are built; the papers for Weeks 10, 11, 13 and 14 are still to be built to the same blueprint.
Interview question sets | Locked (format) | Each regular week's Saturday row carries the paper's blueprint and the interview anchors the items descend from, questions only. The items and their key sit in the 'Saturday papers' tab, which is INTERNAL: the student paper prints the Item column only. Tags: [S] staple asked everywhere · [F] frequent in GCC and product screens · [SV] service-major screen opener · [D] differentiator. The tagging is this programme's own calibration for 0-3 year Indian-market candidates.
Major exams | Revised 21 Sep | ME1 in Week 5 (70 marks), ME2 in Week 10 (100 marks), ME3 early in Week 16 (130 marks), 300 in all. The gradient was 50, 100 and 150, and it was flattened on 21 September after an AOC faculty member's suggestion. ME3 left Week 15 on an AOC operations member's suggestion, so that Build 5's mini project, mock interview and GD no longer share a week with a major exam. It sits in the first half of Week 16 Monday, covers RAG, agents and production, and posts wholly to Module 9. ME1 and ME2 run during their weeks as a continuous activity; their exact day and slot are not fixed yet, so no artifact states one.
Assessment pool | Proposed to AOC | Total 1000 marks in five graded components: major exams 300, mini projects 150, mock interviews 200, business GDs 200 and the capstone 150. The 'Evaluation schema' table at the foot of this tab carries the counts, the weeks, the modes and the arithmetic, and the 'AOC inputs' table under it records what the committee said and what was done with it. The schema stays a proposal until the AOC locks it, so no learner-facing artifact states a weight or a percentage yet. The senate proposal's percentage buckets and passing rules still need reconciling with this pool.
Ungraded indicators | Locked | Daily Kahoot quizzes, the Saturday recap paper and the Week 0 diagnostic carry no marks; all three are read as performance indicators. Attendance, practice exercises and engagement are tracked the same way and carry none of the 1000 marks.
Build-week anatomy | Locked (Build 1) | Monday: the Programme Head introduces the mini projects online. Groups of four for the mini project, the GDs and the presentations. Five client-zero sub-problems per build week, three groups per sub-problem, so the panel hears alternate viewpoints on the same business problem. Build Monday to Thursday with the trainer's parallel build and daily checkpoints. The industry expert attends Friday and Saturday only, running GDs at about 30 minutes per group plus some project presentations; a senior industry leader flies in on Saturday, same-day return, for presentations. All grades close in-week. Build 1 mocks: a share with the Principal Advisor, the rest split between the Programme Head and the Academic TA. Staffing for Builds 2 to 5 is not yet detailed. Builds 4 and 5 follow the same anatomy in this workbook, with two differences: Build 4 moves expert day one to Thursday because Christmas Day falls on its Friday, and Mock R5 in Build 5 runs as a two-round simulation.

Cohort 2 | Role tracks and their mapping
Two primary tracks, two supported, one fitment layer, one deliberate exclusion.
Role track | Priority | What the role asks for | Where it is covered
GenAI / AI Engineer | Primary | LLM internals, prompting, RAG design and evaluation, cost and latency judgment, production reliability | Wk 7-12, 14
Agentic AI Engineer | Primary | Agent development, tool design, trajectory evaluation, guardrails, deployment | Wk 13-15
Data Analyst / Business Analyst | Supported | SQL at depth, business metric design, basket and cohort analysis, Excel fluency, stakeholder communication | Wk 1-4, Build 1
Data Scientist (entry) | Supported | ML core, honest evaluation, feature work, experiment design | Wk 4-6
Forward Deployed Engineer | Fitment layer | Problem framing, solution options with trade-offs, quantified recommendation, client-facing communication | Wk 0, every build week, Wk 16
Computer Vision | Excluded | A different teaching approach and skill set. Cannot be added inside five months alongside the tracks above | Not covered, not claimed
HOW TO READ THIS
Primary | The programme is designed for these. Every student should be interview-ready here.
Supported | Genuinely covered and interviewable, sharing the same data and ML spine rather than running as a separate track.
Fitment layer | A way of working layered on top of the technical content. It improves performance in AI engineer, analyst and consulting-flavoured interviews, and opens forward deployed roles where companies hire at entry level. Most forward deployed openings still ask for client-facing experience, so it is positioned as fitment and not as a guarantee.
Excluded | Deliberately out of scope, recorded so nobody positions candidates into it.
