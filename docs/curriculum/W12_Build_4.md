## Mon 21 Dec 2026 · Build 4 · Online project introduction; groups scope their Kalpa Logistics desk and price their options

### Business scenario of the day

KALPA LOGISTICS, THE BUILD 4 UNIT. Kalpa Logistics moves parcels and freight across India and South-East Asia. Its head of operations has watched Retail's assistant answer from policy with the clause shown, and has the same problem on five desks: the answer sits in a document, nobody finds it fast enough, and a wrong answer damages a shipment or loses a claim. The documents are real, messy and unfamiliar. Groups of four take one of five sub-problems, three groups per sub-problem.
THE FIVE SUB-PROBLEMS, each with citations mandatory and its numbers reported: (1) The hub floor: handling procedures for fragile, cold-chain and restricted goods; the assistant shows its clause and declines when the procedure is silent. (2) The claims desk: damaged and lost shipments, answered from a claims policy and carrier contracts that disagree on liability. (3) The cross-border desk: which papers a shipment needs, from guides that change by effective date, so a stale answer is the risk. (4) The sales desk: rate cards and service commitments held in tables, where the cut decides whether a price can be found at all. (5) The control tower: for a delayed shipment, the most similar past incidents and the playbook step that applies, from logs full of codes that meaning search misses.
Every sub-problem is the Week 11 method over documents the room has never seen. Your role all week: you are the engineer the head of operations will question, and the panels will ask for your cut, your retrieval and faithfulness numbers, and what broke.

MONDAY. The Programme Head introduces the build week online: the sub-problems, the groups of four, the week's shape around the Christmas holiday, and the rule that no new material is taught. Groups read their documents, write the twenty questions their desk would ask, price their options, and open the challenges log.

### Thinking we train, before any tool

The first move is Week 11 Monday's: a thin pipeline running end to end before anything is tuned. The second is the week's forward deployed move, quantified option evaluation. Before building, each group tables three ways to answer its desk's questions, for example keyword search alone, retrieval with a model, and the whole document set in a long context, and puts a cost per question, a latency and an expected quality on each, with the assumption behind every number. A group that cannot say why it chose retrieval, in numbers, has skipped the move the week exists to train.

### Trainer agenda

1. The Programme Head introduces the build week online: sub-problems, groups of four, the week's shape (60 min).
2. Sub-problem allocation: fifteen groups at target intake, three per sub-problem (30 min).
3. Groups read their documents, write twenty questions the desk would ask, table three options with numbers, and open the challenges log with entry one (rest of day).
4. Close-out: scopes and option tables pinned; tomorrow's checkpoint stated (15 min).

## Wed 23 Dec 2026 · Build 4 · Build day three: measured improvement, the catch-up reserve, and Mock R4 opens

### Thinking we train, before any tool

An improvement is a change with a before and an after on the same golden set, and the challenges log records the changes that did not help as faithfully as the ones that did. The desk's particular difficulty decides where to look first: the cut for tables, metadata for effective dates, keyword search for codes, conflict handling for contracts, the decline rule for silent procedures.

4. Mock R4 opens per roster: individual, roughly 20 minutes each, half on retrieval design and half a viva on the group's system (afternoon).
5. Close-out: each group states its headline in three rates: right, wrong, declined (15 min).
