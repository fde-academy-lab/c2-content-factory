# Meta-content: what never appears on a learner slide

The test: would this sentence make sense to someone reading the deck alone,
six months later, who was never in the room? If it only makes sense to the
trainer standing in front of the slide, it is meta-content.

## Facilitation instructions
Move to speaker notes, always.

| On the slide | Rewrite |
|---|---|
| Take answers by show of hands | Move to notes. Replace with a substantive line about the content |
| Ask the room which one they have seen | Move to notes |
| Give this slide time, it lands hard | Move to notes |
| Do not reveal the answer yet | Move to notes |
| The room will split on row two | Move to notes. On the slide say what makes the row hard, as a fact |
| Circulate and check line one | Move to notes |
| Two minutes, no discussion | Keep only the duration if the learner needs it |

## Deck self-reference
| On the slide | Rewrite |
|---|---|
| The five words this deck uses | Value, field, record, dataset, system |
| Deck 1 in five lines | The whole method in five rules |
| As we saw on the previous slide | Name the thing instead of the slide |
| Deck 2 covers the rest | Name the four questions that are still open |
| Everything in this deck is about X | Everything that follows is about X |
| Answered later in the deck | Delete, or close the loop with a callback band at the slide that answers it |

## Programme framing
Delete entirely. No agenda slides, no audience slides, no "how this session
runs", no learning-objective lists, no recap-of-yesterday slides, no cohort
survey results.

The one exception: a single promise line on the title slide, phrased as what
the learner will be able to do.

## Pedagogical scaffolding
Delete entirely. Beat labels, rhythm markers, section headers named after
teaching moves ("the problem", "the intuition", "the picture"), and any
visible marker of the instructional design.

## Design rationale and confidence marking
Belongs in the chat reply or the speaker notes, never on a slide. That
includes sizing arithmetic, why the structure is what it is, what was
assumed, and whether a device is established practice or a course
construction.

The exception: where the reader needs it to be correct, phrase it as a plain
fact about the content, such as "figures are illustrative", never as a note
about how the material was built.

## Allowed on slides
- A one-line promise on the title slide
- Learner-facing activity instructions: "in pairs, ten minutes, pick one
  case, short lines only"
- A duration on an activity
- A deck identifier in the title slide subtitle
