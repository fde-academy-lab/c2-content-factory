# Before Wednesday: four minutes about you

Ships tonight. Nothing is assigned: the diagnostic is done, and tomorrow's brush-up teaches from what
it found.

## What tomorrow is

The first half is the Python brush-up, about three hours, covering the Python that Week 1 uses. It
runs in two tracks on the same material: one teaches it step by step and the other practises it.
A TA tells you your track as you arrive.

The second half is the introductions: every learner speaks to the cohort for four minutes.

During lab time on Wednesday or Thursday, a TA sits with you for ten minutes with your ratings and
your report from today's diagnostic side by side. Together you agree where you start and the two things
you will fix first, and that goes on your baseline card, which only you and the TA see.

If you missed today's diagnostic, you sit it first thing on Wednesday, before the brush-up starts.

## Your four minutes

| Minutes | What to cover |
|---|---|
| Two on you | Who you are, what you have done so far, and the role you are here to become |
| Two on one thing you built | What it did, your own part in it, one thing that broke, and what you would change |

Two parts of that story are where interview answers about projects fall apart, so give them the
most thought:

- **Your own part.** Say what you did, in the first person. A project told entirely as "we" leaves
  the listener unable to tell what you can do.
- **What broke.** Pick a real failure and say what you did about it. A project where nothing went
  wrong sounds rehearsed, and the fix is usually the most interesting minute of the story.

Any project counts: a college assignment, an internship task, a script that saved someone an hour.

The interview question behind the four minutes is a staple:

- [S] Tell me about yourself in one minute, with one thing you have built.

Bring your laptop with the codespace working, since the brush-up runs in it.
