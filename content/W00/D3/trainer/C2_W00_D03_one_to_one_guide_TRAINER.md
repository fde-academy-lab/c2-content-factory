# The one-to-one guide: the baseline card in ten minutes

TRAINER ONLY. For the TA running a one-to-one on Wednesday or Thursday, beside the practice track or
during lab time.

## Bring

A blank baseline card, the learner's Profile line from the diagnostic's key workbook, and the
learner, who brings the report email the form sent them on Tuesday. The Profile line carries the
section scores, the rating against the scored band for each area, the calibration and a one-line
note for this conversation.

## The ten minutes

| Duration | Move | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 2 min | Their read first | The learner says which section felt hardest and why, before any score is shown |
| 3 min | The two columns | The card's table is filled from the report email: the learner's 1-to-4 ratings beside the section scores. The two of you find the area where they disagree most, with the questions behind it open on the report |
| 3 min | What they can do alone | One line per area in the learner's words, naming tasks they can do today |
| 2 min | Two actions, each with a date | One action on the widest gap and one on the Python Week 1 Monday opens with, each with how the learner will know it is done and by when |

Both of you sign the card by Saturday. It goes home with the learner, and the TA keeps a copy off the
repository.

## Three rules

1. Only the learner's own scores appear: never another learner's score, the workbook's bands, a
   level label or a comparison with the room. When a learner asks where they stand against the
   others, the honest answer is that no card shows anyone else's score.
2. The rating is the learner's own claim and the section score is what one sitting found. The card
   puts them side by side, and the conversation is about the gap between them. The Profile's
   calibration says whether the learner over-claims or under-claims on average, which is worth one
   sentence and no more.
3. The Python track is where Wednesday's brush-up started for this learner, and it moves when the
   work does. Say so if the learner reads it as a verdict.

## Actions that work, and actions that do not

| Works | Does not work |
|---|---|
| Finish Wednesday's hands-on notebook with every check passing, by Friday | Practise more Python |
| Read the discussion post for every question missed in the weakest section, then redo those questions from a blank notebook, by Sunday | Revise the basics |
| Write the librarian's three questions as three functions, run them on the next week's log, and check the answers against the notebook's, by Monday | Get better at functions |

An action works when someone else could check it: a named task, a finished state and a date.

## What to record

Note which learners have had their one-to-one and the two actions each agreed, on the TAs' own sheet
and never in the repository. Anyone left over carries to Thursday's lab time, and every card is
signed by Saturday.
