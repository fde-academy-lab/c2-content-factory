# Brief for Saturday's guest: a working forward deployed engineer

TRAINER ONLY. The host sends it to the guest well before Saturday.

## The cohort

Thirty-five learners on IIT Gandhinagar's PG Diploma in AI-ML and Agentic AI Engineering, Cohort 2:
twenty weeks, full-time and residential. The programme builds forward deployed AI engineers, and every
build week in it is run the way a forward deployed engineer would run it. Saturday is the first time
the cohort meets someone who does the job.

## Their week so far

| Day | What they did |
|---|---|
| Monday | The programme's orientation and their workspace setup |
| Tuesday | A baseline diagnostic in Python, SQL, numbers, a short business case and judgment calls |
| Wednesday | A Python brush-up, and four minutes each on themselves and on one thing they built |
| Thursday | Python carried into SQL, an SQL brush-up, and a class on stating a problem before solving it, on a hostel mess that wanted "an app that predicts how many students will eat" |

Each learner arrives with three written questions for you, and a one-page problem card from a field
they know.

## What we ask of the session

The job itself, and their questions. About four hours, and up to six, as one block with breaks. A
shape that has worked for sessions like this, to adapt as you like:

| Part | Duration | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| A day in the job | About 45 min | What the work is, who you work with, how much is code and how much is conversation |
| One engagement, told end to end | About 60 min | The problem as the client first stated it, what it turned out to be, the options on the table, what shipped, and what broke |
| Their questions | About 60 min | The host puts the learners' written questions first, then opens the floor |
| A problem, worked with the room | About 45 min | One real ask, anonymised: the room asks, you answer only what is asked, and together you reach the problem underneath |

## Two requests

1. Anything a client has not made public stays out of the session. Learners take notes, and some will
   write about the day.
2. The failures matter more than the successes. The cohort has spent the week learning that a project
   told without what broke sounds rehearsed, and your version of that story will stay with them.

## What the host arranges

The room, the screen and the breaks, confirmed with you in advance, and the learners' questions sorted
into a stack before you start.
