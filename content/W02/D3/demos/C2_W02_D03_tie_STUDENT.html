07 · Experiment cards

What happens to the number when you change one assumption?

Read the situation and the hypothesis, decide what you expect, then run. Each card says whether its numbers are Kalpa's or invented.

Experiment A · invented members

What if Marketing widens the list by two places?

Situation. Marketing likes the list and asks for two more places, taking the twelve invented members' list from 8 to 10.

Hypothesis. At 10 the line no longer runs through a tie, so ROW_NUMBER and RANK agree, and DENSE_RANK still ships more than asked.

Watch for. The gap between the rule bars at each list size.

What happened.

Why it matters. A tie only changes the count where it straddles the line, so every change to N needs the count read again. On Kalpa, Business and Student have 35 and 20 Q2 buyers, so a list of sixty still holds every buyer there, and the list should keep saying so.

Read the line after every change to N, because a tie matters only where the line cuts it.

Experiment B · invented members, simulated runs

What if the tiebreaker is dropped?

Situation. Someone removes member from ROW_NUMBER's ORDER BY, leaving spend alone, and the list of eight is run on Monday and again on Tuesday with no change to the data.

Hypothesis. The two runs can disagree on who is eighth, because H and I both spent Rs 5,900 and the database may return tied rows in either order.

Watch for. The name in the eighth row of each run.

What happened.

Why it matters. A member who is on Monday's list and off Tuesday's with the same spend gets a retention call one week and silence the next, and nobody can explain why.

ROW_NUMBER is repeatable only with a unique tiebreaker, and even then it drops a tied member by a rule nobody in the business chose.

Experiment C · invented member

What if the holiday month is filled with zero?

Situation. To stop LAG skipping the gap, someone fills Member Q's missing August with a zero before the window runs.

Hypothesis. September no longer reads as a fall, and August now reads as a fall to zero, so the flag that ends in August accuses him instead.

Watch for. The August dot and the month the flag lands on.

What happened.

Why it matters. Filling the gap with zero turns every holiday into a collapse, and a retention team would ring members to ask why they stopped buying in the month they were away.

A month with no order is no reading, so it breaks the run and is never filled with zero.

Experiment D · real Q2 data

What if the running total starts on 6 July?

Situation. The plan line starts on Monday 6 July, so someone starts the running actual there too, leaving out the 25 orders of 1 to 5 July.

Hypothesis. The actual closes short of Monday's Rs 9,84,00,000 by exactly those five days, and the quarter reads as a miss.

Watch for. The sign of the gap against plan at the close.

What happened.

Why it matters. Meera would be told the quarter missed plan when it landed on it, and the fix is one check anyone can run: the last booked-to-date must equal the quarter's total.

A running total starts at the start of the period, and it closes on the period's total.

What happened appears here after the run.

Experiment E · real Q2 data

What if the running total is ordered by date alone?

Situation. Twelve orders were booked on 22 July, and the running total is ordered by order_date with no order_id after it.

Hypothesis. Rows of the same date are peers, so all twelve show the day's closing figure.

Watch for. The first and last rows of 22 July.

What happened.

Why it matters. A running total that jumps a whole day at once hides the order that moved it, and two runs of an ambiguous order can disagree row by row.

A running total needs an order that no two rows share, so the date comes with the order id.

09 · Glossary

Which words did the day use, and where did each first matter?

Window function
A calculation across related rows that keeps every row and adds a column, where GROUP BY collapses the rows to one per group.Chapter 1, notebook 01, the day's picture.
PARTITION BY
The part of a window that sets the group the calculation restarts in, such as the segment for a ranking or the member for LAG.Chapter 2, notebook 02; chapter 4, the hurried flag.
ROW_NUMBER
Numbers the rows 1, 2, 3 in the window's order and breaks a tie arbitrarily unless the order carries a unique tiebreaker.Chapter 1, notebook 01; chapter 3, notebook 03.
RANK
Gives tied rows the same position and leaves a gap after them, so a top N can ship more than N rows when a tie sits at the line.Chapter 3, notebook 03.
DENSE_RANK
Gives tied rows the same position with no gap after them, so ties higher up let extra rows under the line.Chapter 3, notebook 03, the Retail-Core count.
LAG
Reads a value from an earlier row of the partition in the window's order; LAG(spend, 1) is the row before and LAG(spend, 2) the row two back, and LEAD reads forward.Chapter 4, notebook 04.
Running total
SUM over a window ordered through the period, so each row carries everything up to and including itself.Chapter 5, notebook 05; the escalated case.
Peer rows
Rows that share the window's ORDER BY value; a running total gives all of them the same figure, the total at the last of them.Chapter 5, notebook 05, the orders of 22 July.
CTE
A named step written with WITH, used today to compute a position and then filter it outside, because a window cannot sit in WHERE.Chapter 2, the two-minute error.
