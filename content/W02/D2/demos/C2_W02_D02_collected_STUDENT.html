Booked against collected, without lying

“Booked revenue is not collected revenue. Some orders are paid in two instalments, some are refunded, some were never paid at all. Show me, order by order, what we actually collected against what we booked in Q2. If there is a gap, I want to know which orders and which channel.”

Anand Iyer, finance controller, Kalpa Retail, to the data and AI team at Kalpa's Global Capability Centre

You sign off the collected number, and Anand will ask how you know it is not double-counted. This page runs the day's key decision, how payments are attached to orders, on invented records only: the five orders and seven payments of the morning's tiny tables, and a slightly larger invented sample of twelve orders. Change one assumption at a time and watch the rows, the gap, the bridge and the sentence to Anand move with it.

    06 · Experiment cards
    
      
    Five experiments, one change each
    
      
    Each card runs on the invented tiny tables and holds every setting still except one. Read the
        situation and the hypothesis, decide what you expect, then run.
    
      
    
        
    
          
    Experiment A · invented records · join type
    
          
    INNER against LEFT, on a clean payments table
    
          
    Situation. Payments are already rolled up to one row per order with the retry counted once, and the
            quarter condition sits in the ON clause. The only thing left to choose is the join.
    
          
    Hypothesis. INNER reports the quarter as fully collected, and LEFT brings the unpaid order back and opens
            a gap of Rs 800.
    
          
    Watch for. The orders counted in the report, and the gap cell.
    
          
    
          
    
            
            
          
    
          
    
            
    What happened. 
    
            
    Why it matters. The INNER report is the one that tells Anand there is nothing to chase, and the order
              it hides is exactly the one he asked about.
    
            
    Every join answers a question about the rows that do not match; choose the join by that question.
    
          
    
          
    What happened appears here after the run.
    
        
    

        
    
          
    Experiment B · invented records · payments grain
    
          
    Raw payment rows against one row per order
    
          
    Situation. A LEFT JOIN report puts booked and collected side by side, with no quarter condition. T-2 was
            paid in two instalments and T-3 has two payment rows.
    
          
    Hypothesis. At raw grain booked reads Rs 9,300, because T-2 and T-3 each count twice; rolled up to one row
            per order, booked drops back to Rs 5,800 and collected does not move.
    
          
    Watch for. Rows out and the booked cell, and whether collected changes at all.
    
          
    
          
    
            
            
          
    
          
    
            
    What happened. 
    
            
    Why it matters. A both-columns report at the wrong grain inflates booked and makes the collected share
              look like half, which is how a collections panic starts on a quarter that is mostly paid.
    
            
    A key that repeats on one side multiplies the other side's rows before any number is summed.
    
          
    
          
    What happened appears here after the run.
    
        
    

        
    
          
    Experiment C · invented records · condition position
    
          
    The quarter condition in WHERE, then in ON
    
          
    Situation. The report is a LEFT JOIN on clean payments, and a colleague adds “paid in Q2” as a
            filter on paid_date, because Anand asked what was collected in the quarter.
    
          
    Hypothesis. In the WHERE the filter throws away T-4, whose paid_date is NULL, and the LEFT JOIN behaves as
            an INNER one; in the ON clause T-4 stays.
    
          
    Watch for. The orders counted and the gap, which should fall to zero under WHERE.
    
          
    
          
    
            
            
          
    
          
    
            
    What happened. 
    
            
    Why it matters. The query still says LEFT JOIN, so a reviewer reading it trusts that every order is
              present, and the only sign of the loss is the count.
    
            
    A condition on the right-hand table belongs in the ON clause, or the LEFT JOIN becomes an INNER one.
    
          
    
          
    What happened appears here after the run.
    
        
    

        
    
          
    Experiment D · invented records · retries
    
          
    The retry counted once
    
          
    Situation. The LEFT JOIN report closes its count, five orders in and five rows out, and still says more
            was collected than booked. The platform lead said the feed sometimes double-posts when the gateway retries.
    
          
    Hypothesis. Counting each order and instalment once removes the retry, collected falls by Rs 1,500 and the
            gap turns from minus Rs 700 to Rs 800.
    
          
    Watch for. The collected cell and the sign of the gap.
    
          
    
          
    
            
            
          
    
          
    
            
    What happened. 
    
            
    Why it matters. A negative gap reads as customers overpaying, and it hides a real unpaid order behind a
              retry of almost twice its size.
    
            
    A join is done when its row count is explained: rows in, rows out, and the difference named.
    
          
    
          
    What happened appears here after the run.
    
        
    

        
    
          
    Experiment E · invented records · HAVING grain
    
          
    The double-paid list, at two grains
    
          
    Situation. Before anyone calls a customer about a refund, the double-paid list is built with HAVING
            COUNT(*) > 1 on the payments table.
    
          
    Hypothesis. Grouped by order, the list names T-2 and T-3; grouped by order and instalment, it names only
            T-3, because T-2's two rows are its two instalments.
    
          
    Watch for. Which orders stay on the list, and the amount a refund call would chase.
    
          
    
          
    
            
            
          
    
          
    
            
    What happened. 
    
            
    Why it matters. A refund call to a customer who paid their second instalment on time is a complaint the
              team created, and it lands on the finance controller's desk.
    
            
    Two payment rows are not a double payment until the grain says they are the same payment.
    
          
    
          
    What happened appears here after the run.
    
        
    
      
    
    
    

    
    
      
    07 · Glossary
    
      
    The day's words, and where each first mattered
    
      
    
        
    
          
    Grain
    What one row of a table stands for: one order, one payment, or one order and instalment. Say it
            for each table before you join them.Round 1, the tiny tables traced row by row.
    
          
    Fan-out
    The rows a join adds when a key repeats on one side, so every matching row on the other side is
            copied once per repeat and any sum over it grows.Round 1, the naive join, Rs 8,500 against
            Rs 5,800 booked.
    
          
    INNER, LEFT, RIGHT, FULL
    INNER keeps only the rows that match; LEFT keeps every row of the left table;
            RIGHT keeps every row of the right table; FULL keeps the unmatched rows of both.Round 1,
            rung 1, six, seven, seven and eight rows.
    
          
    Reconciliation
    Proving a number against its source: rows in, rows out, and the total after the join
            tied back to the total in the table it came from.Round 2, the row-count check.
    
          
    Bridge
    A chart that starts from one total and walks, move by named move, to another, so every rupee of
            the difference has a name.Round 2, booked to collected to what the feed posted.
    
          
    Anti-join
    A LEFT JOIN kept only where the right side found nothing, written as WHERE the right key IS
            NULL; it lists the orders with no payment.Round 3, the unpaid list.
    
          
    Orphan
    A row whose key points at nothing on the other side, such as a payment for an order id the
            orders table does not hold.Round 1, P-7 in the RIGHT JOIN; round 3, the reverse
            anti-join.
    
          
    Instalment
    One of the planned part-payments of a larger order, numbered by instalment_no, so two rows
            for one order can both be honest money.Round 1, why T-2 appears twice.
    
          
    Retry
    The same instalment posted twice when the payment gateway tries again: same order, same
            instalment, same amount, and money counted twice.Round 3, the double-paid list at the right
            grain.
