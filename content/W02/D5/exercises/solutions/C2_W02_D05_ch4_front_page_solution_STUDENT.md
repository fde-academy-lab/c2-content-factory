# Which answers hold in the chapter 4 set on the front-page number, and why?

Answers: 1c 2b 3b 4c 5d 6a

Meera's chief of staff asked for one number on the front page of Monday's growth review deck, with its
trend. The number is revenue in Q2 (July to September 2026) against Q1 (April to June 2026): counted
once per order and tied to the warehouse, Rs 9,84,00,000 against Rs 10,00,00,000, down 1.6 percent. A
card carries the number's period, its comparison and its base, and a sentence on what moved it; a
change is measured on the earlier period, and a change in a rate is quoted in percentage points.
Chapter 4 wrote the card as "Q2, July to September 2026: Rs 9.84 crore, down 1.6 percent on Q1, April
to June 2026 (Rs 10.00 crore)". Two of the six items are design items: 4 and 6.

## Which skill does the chapter 4 set test?

The skill is writing a card a director cannot misread. A director reads a bare number against
whatever they remember, and a bare percentage against whatever they fear. The card closes those gaps
by printing the period, the comparison and the base beside the number, and measuring every change on
the figure it is compared with. The design items ask what the card prints when a director changes its
scope, and which comparison it carries when the board reviews each month against a plan.

## Why does each of the six keys hold, from the bare total to the monthly review?

### Q1. What does a card that reads "Revenue Rs 19.84 crore" lack?

The draft is the export's grand total, Kalpa's revenue for April to September, and a director
remembers Rs 10.00 crore for Q1.

The key is c, "Its period, the months the number covers". Rs 19.84 crore is right for two quarters,
and with no months printed the director reads it as one quarter against Q1's Rs 10.00 crore, up 98.4
percent, when Q2 fell 1.6 percent. The card needs its months and its comparison.

- a, "Its base, the company's revenue in the same months": this is the company's revenue, so its
  share of the whole is 100 percent, and printing it would not stop the misreading.
- b, "Its trend, the monthly line drawn beside the number": a trend helps once the number is read
  right, and does not say the number covers six months.
- d, "Its segment split, the tree on the next page": the split explains the number and leaves its
  period unknown.

### Q2. What change does the Retail-Plus line print, and what sits beside it?

Retail-Plus booked Rs 5,85,770 in Q1 and Rs 4,13,380 in Q2; the company booked Rs 9,84,00,000 in Q2.

The key is b, "Down 29.4 percent, beside Rs 4.13 lakh, Rs 5.86 lakh and 0.4 percent". The change is
(Rs 4,13,380 less Rs 5,85,770) over Rs 5,85,770, down 29.4 percent, a fall of Rs 1.72 lakh. Beside
it go the two quarters and the segment's share of company revenue in Q2, Rs 4,13,380 over
Rs 9,84,00,000, 0.4 percent, so the meeting argues about members who order less often instead of a
collapse.

- a, "Down 41.7 percent, beside Rs 4.13 lakh, Rs 5.86 lakh and 0.4 percent": the base is right, and
  the change is divided by Q2 instead of Q1, Rs 1,72,390 over Rs 4,13,380.
- c, "Down 29.4 percent alone, since a percentage carries its own base with it": a percentage hides
  its base, so 29.4 percent of Rs 5.86 lakh reads like 29.4 percent of the business.
- d, "Down Rs 1.72 lakh alone, since a rupee figure needs no base beside it": without the base and the
  share, nobody can tell whether Rs 1.72 lakh is large or small.

### Q3. Which line reports a share that moved from 20 to 25 percent?

An invented app channel carried 20 percent of revenue in Q1 and 25 percent in Q2.

The key is b, "App share up 5 points on Q1, to 25 percent of revenue". A share is a rate, so its
change is quoted in points: 25 less 20 is 5 points, and the line names where it ended.

- a, "App share up 5 percent on Q1, to 25 percent of revenue": 5 percent of 20 percent is one point,
  so this line says the share rose to 21 percent.
- c, "App share up 25 points on Q1, to 25 percent of revenue": a rise of 25 points would mean the
  share started at zero.
- d, "App share up 0.25 on Q1, to 25 percent of revenue": 0.25 is the relative change written as a
  bare ratio, with no unit, which a director cannot read in two minutes.

### Q4. Which card answers a director who asks for the number without Business?

A design item. Without Business, revenue was Rs 9.86 lakh in Q1 and Rs 8.15 lakh in Q2, and the
company booked Rs 9.84 crore in Q2.

The key is c, "A second card, headed All except Business: Rs 8.15 lakh, down 17.3 percent, 0.8 percent of company revenue".
The director's question is fair, so it gets its own card beside the company card, with its own scope,
period, comparison and base. Rs 8.15 lakh against Q1's Rs 9.86 lakh is down 17.3 percent, and Rs 8.15
lakh over the company's Q2 of Rs 9.84 crore is 0.8 percent. Down 1.6 percent for all segments and
down 17.3 percent without Business are then never read as one figure.

- a, "The company card, replaced by a new line: Q2 revenue down 17.3 percent on Q1": the line
  drops its scope, so the room reads a 17.3 percent fall in the whole company.
- b, "The company card left as it is, since Business is part of the revenue Finance signs": true, and
  it refuses a fair question, which sends the director back to typing over cells.
- d, "A second card, headed All except Business: Rs 8.15 lakh, down 17.3 percent, 0.4 percent of company revenue":
  the share is taken of Rs 19.84 crore, the two quarters added together, so the card halves the
  consumer segments' weight in Q2.

### Q5. What is the honest reading of July's jump on the company line?

The company line runs Rs 3.12 crore in June, Rs 4.51 crore in July, Rs 2.69 crore in August and
Rs 2.64 crore in September; without Business it slides from Rs 3.32 lakh in June to Rs 2.48 lakh by
September.

The key is d, "Business invoices landed in July, and the consumer line fell all quarter". Business
carries about 99 percent of revenue, so a few large corporate invoices landing in one month move the
company line, while the consumer line, which the invoices do not touch, fell from June to September.

- a, "A monsoon sale lifted every segment in July, and the lift faded by August": the consumer line
  fell in July too, so no segment-wide lift shows.
- b, "July was the half-year's best month for growth across the whole business": the jump sits in one
  segment's invoices, and the rest of the business fell.
- c, "The rise is seasonal, and it will come back next July without any action": one half-year holds
  one July, which cannot show a season.

### Q6. Which comparison does September's card carry when the board reviews each month against its plan?

A design item. The board now reviews each month against its plan line. September's revenue was
Rs 2,64,40,430, August's Rs 2,69,02,240, and September's plan, an invented figure, is Rs 3,00,00,000.

The key is a, "September against its plan: Rs 2.64 crore, 11.9 percent below the plan's Rs 3.00 crore".
A card's comparison is whatever its readers judge the number against, and this board judges each
month against its plan: September fell short by Rs 35,59,570, which is 11.9 percent of the plan's
Rs 3,00,00,000. A board that reviews against plan is the fact that would move chapter 4's card off
the quarter against the last.

- b, "September against August: Rs 2.64 crore, down 1.7 percent on August's Rs 2.69 crore": the
  arithmetic holds, Rs 4,61,810 on August's Rs 2,69,02,240, and the comparison is one this board no
  longer judges by; it belongs on the trend line beside the card.
- c, "Q2 against Q1: Rs 9.84 crore, down 1.6 percent, since the deck already prints it": it answers
  the old quarterly review, and the board has asked a monthly question against its plan.
- d, "September against its plan: Rs 2.64 crore, 13.5 percent below the plan's Rs 3.00 crore": the
  shortfall is divided by the actual Rs 2,64,40,430 instead of the plan it is measured against.

## Why is down 41.7 percent with the right base beside it (item 2, a) the most tempting wrong answer?

In option a the base is printed and the arithmetic still fails. The change belongs to the earlier period:
the fall of Rs 1,72,390 is 29.4 percent of Q1's Rs 5,85,770. Divided by Q2's smaller Rs 4,13,380, the
same fall reads 41.7 percent, a number that is larger, more alarming and still looks precise. The
check is to divide the change by the period the card says it is measured on.

## Where does Avenue Supermarts, DMart's owner, put all four parts in its first two lines?

Avenue Supermarts headlined its results for the quarter to 30 June 2025 as "Standalone Total Revenue
up by 16.2% at Rs.15,932 Crore", and its release adds that revenue "stood at Rs.15,932 crore, as
compared to Rs.13,712 crore in the same period last year" (Avenue Supermarts press release, 11 July
2025, checked 30 September 2026). The number, its period, its comparison and its base are all in the
first two lines, which is the card chapter 4 built for Kalpa.
