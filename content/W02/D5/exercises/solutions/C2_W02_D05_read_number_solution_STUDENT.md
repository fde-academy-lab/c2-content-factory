# Solutions: read the number

Answers: 1b 2d 3c 4b

## Q1. All three

"Revenue: Rs 9.84 crore" answers none of the three questions. Of what: all channels, one segment,
one country? Over when: a quarter, a year, to date? Against what: up or down, from where?

A director reading this will fill in all three from whatever was in the previous slide, and
whatever they fill in becomes what the number means when they repeat it.

## Q2. The denominator

The period is there, and the comparison is there in the word "down". What is missing is what the
figure is across: 462 orders, or the whole book, or one segment.

This is the one people miss, because a number with a period and a percentage already feels
complete. Most disagreements about a number turn out to be disagreements about its denominator,
discovered late.

## Q3. The denominator belongs to a different number

"Down 34.9 percent" is a change in Retail-Plus order **count**. "462 orders in the quarter" is the
total order count across every segment.

Two true figures, side by side, that do not describe each other. A reader will assume the second
is the base of the first, and it is not.

This is worse than a missing denominator, because it looks like a complete number and the error is
invisible without knowing where each figure came from.

## Q4. They will supply their own reason

A trend line shows direction and says nothing about cause. A director looking at a falling line
will attach a reason to it, usually the most recent thing they heard, and that reason travels with
the chart into the next meeting.

Option a is what we hope happens and it is not what happens under time pressure. One sentence
beside the chart, naming the branch, is what costs you nothing and saves the quarter's narrative.

## Rewriting Q1

One workable version:

> Kalpa Retail collected Rs 9.84 crore in Q2 across 462 orders, against Rs 10.00 crore in Q1, a
> fall of 1.6 percent driven by Retail-Plus order frequency rather than by customer count.

Denominator, period, comparison, and the branch. It survives being repeated by somebody who was
not in the room.
