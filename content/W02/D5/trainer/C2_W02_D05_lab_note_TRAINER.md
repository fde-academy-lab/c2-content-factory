# The practice lab: TA note, Week 2 Friday

**TRAINER ONLY.** For the TA who runs `exercises/practice/C2_W02_D05_lab_STUDENT.md` after the
second block. About an hour; the solution file opens at the end.

| Problem | Minutes | Where learners stall | The one hint to give |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Who owns it, eight asks | 15 | Q3 and Q8. Removing rows feels like an Excel job because the button is there; a what-if feels like it needs the warehouse because it is about the future. | "Who has to trust this number, and how often is it rebuilt?" |
| 2. The misread, three cards | 12 | Q10: they reach for the cause. Q11: they read past the word "up". | "Read the card aloud to someone who has not seen the tree. What do they ask first?" |
| 3. Predict the pivot | 15 | Counting the rows: they forget the gateway order has two rows, or add the instalments once. | "Write the rows out: seven, then two and two, then two. Now add what each row carries." |
| 4. A sheet you did not build | 20 | Q15: they blame the date filter. Q18: they accept 25 percent because Rs 80,000 is a quarter of Rs 3.20 lakh. | "Which of this morning's four checks would you run first, and what does it compare?" |

**What a finished lab looks like.** Eighteen letters, and for problem 4 one sentence per item naming
the check that caught it. The key is `acabcabcbdcdacbadb` in item order (the solution file's Answers
line). Learners who finish early open the decision tool in `demos/` and fix its five planted defects.

**Common wrong answers worth a minute with the room.** Q13 at option b (10 rows): the learner has
confused Remove Duplicates with counting each order once, which is round 1's second trap; walk the
five-row example on the companion page's experiment A. Q16 at option b: sorting the ids is what makes
an approximate match look plausible, so it makes the defect worse.
