# Three timed analyst cases

Each case is a question an analytics screen asks, set in a situation. Read it once, think for three
minutes on paper, then answer aloud to your partner in two minutes. Two answers go to the room
before the model answer is read.

Listen for the order of your own moves: a screen is scored on what you do first, what you skip and
the reason for each, long before the final number.

## Case 1: two hours and a raw export

*[F] You have two hours and a raw export; what do you do first, and what do you skip?*

A food-delivery client's sales fell 15 percent last month. The head of growth sends you the raw
order export at the start of the afternoon and wants an answer by the end of it. Nobody has checked
the file. Walk through the first thirty minutes step by step, say what you would skip to finish in
time, and say what you would never skip.

Your paper: the first three moves, in order; the one thing you skip and why; the one thing you
never skip and why.

## Case 2: zero rejects and a Rs 20 lakh gap

*[S] Walk me through how you clean and check a dataset you have never seen.*

Your cleaning pass on a 50,000-row export from a new system reports zero rejects. The dashboard
built on it shows a quarter Rs 20 lakh higher than Finance's books. Your manager asks you to walk
through how you cleaned the file and what you check now.

Your paper: the checks in the order you run them; what "zero rejects" makes you suspect; what you
say to Finance before you have found the gap.

## Case 3: 42 percent on twelve visits

*[D] A stakeholder attacks your caveat in front of the room; how do you hold it without
overclaiming?*

A new checkout flow converted 42 percent of 12 visits in its first week. The current flow converts
31 percent of 1,200. Your note says "promising, not proven". In the product review, the product
head says: "Forty-two beats thirty-one. Your caveat is costing us a week. Ship it."

Your paper: your first sentence back; what the data can and cannot say; the test you would offer
and how long it would take.
