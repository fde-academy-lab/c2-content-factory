    03 · Experiment cards

    Four experiments, one change each

    Read the situation and the hypothesis, decide what you expect, then run. Every record in these cards is invented to isolate one mechanism.

    Experiment A · invented records

    A failure turned into zero

    Situation. Five invented order amounts reach a profile, and one of them reads "n/a". The profile either logs the failure or turns it into 0.

    Hypothesis. The coerced profile will report every amount convertible and a smaller total, and nothing will say an order was lost.

    Watch for. The failures count and the lowest dot on the strip.

    What happened.

    Why it matters. A zero is a claim that the order was worth nothing. On the day's file the same move reports every amount convertible and a Q1 that reads as the dashboard's figure.

    A failure is counted and logged, never turned into a number.

    What happened appears here after the run.

    Experiment B · invented records

    The line number in the key

    Situation. Five invented rows, two of them exported twice, each carrying the file line it came from for the rejects log.

    Hypothesis. A whole-record dedupe finds no duplicates while the line is in the record, and finds both once the line is left out.

    Watch for. The duplicates count on each run.

    rows 5 · distinct order ids 3 · duplicates found: not run

    What happened.

    Why it matters. A dedupe that reports zero sends Finance hunting for revenue that was never earned. The count of distinct keys against rows is what catches it.

    Say what makes two rows one order before counting duplicates.

    What happened appears here after the run.

    Experiment C · invented records

    A fence around the tail

    Situation. Eight invented Business orders, one of them twice the next. A fence at a multiple of the median decides what counts as an outlier.

    Hypothesis. A tight fence removes real revenue and turns a small dip into a collapse.

    Watch for. The fence line and how many dots fall to its right.

    What happened.

    Why it matters. On the day's file, removing the largest Q2 order turns a 1.6 percent dip into a 17.1 percent fall, and Finance's books still hold that order.

    Large is not wrong: check the record, keep it, and show it both ways.

    What happened appears here after the run.

    Experiment D · invented records

    Counts that reconcile

    Situation. Four invented rows, one pair sharing an id: the first copy's amount is unreadable and the second's is Rs 2,600. The books say Rs 9,600.

    Hypothesis. Keeping the first copy reconciles the rows and leaves the rupees Rs 2,600 short.

    Watch for. Both reconciliation lines, rows and rupees.

    books Rs 9,600 · not run

    What happened.

    Why it matters. On the day's file the same pass reconciles 201 rows and lands Rs 1,790 short of the books, which rounds to 1.9 crore and fails an analyst who ties out to the rupee.

    Reconcile twice, in rows and in rupees, to the books.

    05 · Glossary

    The day's words, and where each first mattered

    Profile
    Three counts per field before any total: present, convertible to the type it needs, and distinct.Half one, S10 to S12; notebook 01, section 2.
    Convertible
    A value the field's type accepts, such as an amount int() can read.Half one, S10; notebook 01, section 2.
    Rejects log
    Every row whose value failed with no valid copy to stand for it, with its file line, the field, the value and the reason; after the identity rule it is empty on this export.Half one, S15 and S45; notebooks 01 and 04.
    Decisions log
    Every cleaning act with its reason, so an analyst can follow the pass.Half two, S4 to S6; notebook 06, section 1.
    Identity rule
    What makes two rows the same thing; for an order, the ERP's order_id.Half one, S20 and S30; notebooks 02 and 03.
    Duplicate
    A second row for the same thing under the identity rule, whether or not every field matches.Half one, S18 to S25; notebook 02.
    Drop, default, keep and flag
    The three decisions for a missing or malformed value, each moving a different number.Half one, S38 and S40; notebook 04, section 1.
    Outlier
    A value far from the rest, which is a question about a record and never a reason to delete it.Half one, S54 and S55; notebook 05, section 4.
    Reconciliation
    Proof that the clean data is the same data: input equals kept plus set aside plus rejected, in rows and in rupees.Half one, S51; half two, S7 to S10; notebooks 05 and 06.
    Revenue bridge
    A total walked to another total, one move per cause, each backed by the rows that carry it.Half one, S51; notebook 05, section 1.
    JSONDecodeError
    The error json raises when a file stops being valid JSON, naming the line and column.Half one, S16; notebook 01, section 5.
