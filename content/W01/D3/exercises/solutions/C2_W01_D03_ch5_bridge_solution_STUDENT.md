# Which answers hold in the chapter 5 set on the proof of Anand's Rs 1.9 crore, and why?

Answers: 1d 2b 3c 4a 5c

Anand Iyer, Kalpa Retail's finance controller, wants proof, one cause at a time, that the books, Finance's own record of Q1, are right at Rs 1,90,00,000, and Marketing wants to know whether Tuesday's finding, a 49 percent fall in orders per customer for Retail-Plus, Kalpa's paid membership tier, survives the clean file.

Two of the five items are design items: 3 and 4.

### Q1. What does a bridge from Rs 3,40,00,000 to the books' Rs 3,10,00,000 support?

Q1 as exported is Rs 3,40,00,000, the copies set aside by the identity rule, which keeps one row per order, carry Rs 30,00,000, and the books say Rs 3,10,00,000; the bridge walks the first total to the second one cause at a time.

The key is d, "The books are right, and the copies account for every rupee". Rs 3,40,00,000 less Rs 30,00,000 is Rs 3,10,00,000, the books exactly, so the copies explain every rupee of the gap.

- a, "The export is right, and the books missed Rs 30 lakh of orders": reverses the finding.
- b, "Neither is right until the rows are re-entered from source": the bridge already closes, and re-entry would only add error.
- c, "The books are right, and the copies explain most of the gap": the bridge closes, so nothing is left over.

### Q2. What leads the note once a 38 percent fall turns out to be 23?

Tuesday's report said a segment's orders per customer fell 38 percent, and on clean data the fall is 23 percent.

The key is b, "The 23 percent, with why it moved from the 38 first reported". The corrected number leads, with what changed and why, so the reader can trust the next number too.

- a, "The 38 percent, since leadership has already seen that figure": repeats a number known to be wrong.
- c, "Both figures side by side, with no view on which one stands": leaves the reader to pick, which is the analyst's job.
- d, "The 23 percent alone, since the 38 was measured on bad data": drops the change, and whoever remembers the 38 will ask.

### Q3 (Design). Which proof goes to Anand for the third quarter?

For Q3 the export and the books differ by Rs 8,40,000, the logged moves explain Rs 7,90,000, and a warehouse record of every Q3 order, loaded by a separate system, is complete for the quarter.

The key is c, "Q3 rebuilt from the warehouse record, and the bridge as its check". The bridge leaves Rs 50,000 unexplained, and a source independent of the export and complete for the quarter can prove the figure on its own. The bridge then checks it, and the two together can say what the Rs 50,000 is.

- a, "The bridge as it stands, with Rs 50,000 in an unexplained line": a bridge with an unexplained line has not closed.
- b, "The warehouse record's total alone, since that source is complete": a total with no bridge says which number is right and gives no reason.
- d, "The bridge's Rs 7,90,000 now, and the Rs 50,000 at month end": sends a proof known to be incomplete.

### Q4 (Design). How has the gap Marketing's campaign aims to close changed?

Marketing sized a frequency campaign on Tuesday's 1.65 against 1.25 orders per customer, and the clean file reads 1.45 against 1.25.

The key is a, "It has halved, from 0.40 to 0.20 orders per customer". The gap is Q1 less Q2: 1.65 less 1.25 is 0.40 and 1.45 less 1.25 is 0.20, so the campaign aims at half what Tuesday showed, and its sizing has to be redone on 0.20.

- b, "It is unchanged, since Q2's 1.25 did not move at all": Q2 held while Q1 fell, so the gap moved.
- c, "It has gone, since clean revenue fell only 1.6 percent": revenue held because revenue per order rose while orders per customer still fell.
- d, "It shrank by an eighth, as 1.45 sits 12 percent under 1.65": measures the change in Q1 alone, and the gap is Q1 less Q2.

### Q5. What goes in the note when Marketing asks for Q2 without its largest order?

The largest Q2 order is 1.8 times the next, from a Business account, one of Kalpa's sales to companies, that ordered in both quarters with every field valid, and Marketing asks for Q2 without it.

The key is c, "Q2 with it, flagged, and the figure without it shown beside". Nothing about the record is wrong, so it is revenue, and the note shows both readings and says which one Finance's books hold.

- a, "Q2 without it, since one order that size distorts the quarter": an order's size says nothing against it, and Finance's books hold the order.
- b, "Q2 with the order capped at the next largest, to keep the shape": invents a smaller order nobody booked.
- d, "Q2 without it, and a footnote that names the account": a footnote leaves the order's booked value, the price it was charged, out of the quarter.
