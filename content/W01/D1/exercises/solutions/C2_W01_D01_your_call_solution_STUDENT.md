## The part worth arguing about

Which branch goes in the sentence. A sentence that names customers first can be as strong as one
that names orders per customer, if its evidence and its caveat are as specific: 19 customers is a
count with no comparison yet, and 1.11 is a rate with no comparison yet. The model sentence in the
notebook's solution names orders per customer because 17 of the 19 delivered customers bought only
once, which makes frequency the branch where a small change is worth the most; that is a reason,
and the reader can disagree with it, which is what a reason is for.

## Where the pattern lives in production

A metrics review in any product or retail team runs on exactly this shape. The analyst who states
the definition beside every number, and the limit of the window beside every claim, is the one
whose next request for data gets approved, because the reader can see what the number is and is not.
