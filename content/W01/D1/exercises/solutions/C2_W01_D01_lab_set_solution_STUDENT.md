## The idea being tested

The lab runs the day's four habits again on files nobody has shown you. A growth idea is placed by
the branch it moves and priced by what moving that branch costs. Lifts multiply along the tree, so a
discount needs more quantity than its cut. Every leaf carries a numerator, a denominator and a
definition, and customers are counted by their id. The typical order is the median when one order
can move the mean. Problem 4 puts every one of those traps into one file of twelve invented orders.

## The part worth arguing about

Item 5. Pairs will split between customers and orders per customer, and both are right for one
version of the redesign. That is the lesson: "an app redesign" names no behaviour, so the answer is
a question back, "which behaviour is it meant to change?"

Problem 4's text amount. The sum stops on it, and that is a two-minute fix, never a finding. The
findings in that file are the cancelled orders, the repeat customers, the one large order and the
multiplying lifts.

**Kavya's review.** "Four habits, one file: definition, distinct ids, median, multiply. If you ran
all four on the pop-up without being told which trap was where, you are ready for Tuesday."

## Where the pattern lives in production

A first export from a new store, a new market or a new product is where these traps meet at once,
because nobody has cleaned it and everybody wants a number from it. Analysts who run the four checks
before they report are the ones whose first number survives the review.
