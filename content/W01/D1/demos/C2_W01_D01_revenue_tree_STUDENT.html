03 · Experiment cards

Four experiments, one change each

Read the situation and the hypothesis, decide what you expect, then run. The numbers in these four cards are invented to isolate one mechanism, and each card says so.

Experiment A · invented orders

One bulk order joins five ordinary orders

Situation. Five ordinary orders sit between Rs 1,900 and Rs 2,600. A business customer then places one order of Rs 90,000 in the same window, replacing the Rs 2,600 order in the list.

Hypothesis. The mean will rise by about the bulk order's extra size divided by five, and the median will not move.

Watch for. The rose mean line leaving the pile of dots, and whether the green median line moves at all.

What happened.

Why it matters. A note that calls Rs 19,720 the typical order describes nobody on the list, and a budget sized on it is sized for customers who do not exist.

Report the median as the typical value, and name the extreme record on its own line.

Experiment B · arithmetic

Two 10 percent lifts

Situation. Retention lifts orders per customer by 10 percent and bundles lift items per order by 10 percent, in the same quarter.

Hypothesis. Revenue rises by 21 percent, where adding the two lifts would say 20.

Watch for. The last line of the working, and the one point of difference.

100 × 1.10 × 1.10 = ?

What happened.

Why it matters. Small lifts on two branches beat one large lift on a single branch when the single lift is expensive, and a planner who adds percentages underestimates both the upside and the downside.

Branches of a tree multiply, so lifts compound and falls compound.

What happened appears here after the run.

Experiment C · arithmetic, with a calculator

The discount that buys too little

Situation. Marketing proposes 15 percent off everything for a month and expects 10 percent more items sold.

Hypothesis. Revenue falls, because 10 percent more volume at 85 percent of the price is less than today.

Watch for. The revenue multiple, and the volume lift the discount would need just to stand still.

break-even volume lift = 1 / (1 − 0.15) − 1 = 17.6%

What happened.

Why it matters. A discount trades margin for quantity, and it pays only when quantity rises by more than the price falls, which is a number to agree before the campaign rather than discover after it.

A discount of d needs a volume lift of 1 / (1 − d) − 1 just to stand still.

Experiment D · invented records

An amount typed as text

Situation. Four invented orders reach the accumulator, and one amount was typed by a person as “1,800”, in quotes, with an Indian comma.

Hypothesis. The sum stops on that record with a TypeError, and wrapping it in int() still fails, with a ValueError, because of the comma.

Watch for. The pass the loop stops on, and the exact last line of each error.

What happened.

Why it matters. A fix that works on one value is a patch; cleaning decides, per field, what counts as a valid value and what happens to the rows that are not, which is Wednesday's work.

Read the record that failed before you fix anything, and say what you patched.

Each run makes one attempt; three attempts end in a clean total.

    06 · Glossary

    The day's words, and where each first mattered

    Revenue tree
    Revenue drawn as the product of the counts and prices that make it, less discounts, so each leaf is something the business can move.Half one, S7; notebook 1, section 2.
    Branch and leaf
    A branch is any box on the tree; a leaf has nothing multiplying into it and is where an initiative lands.Half one, S9 and S12; notebook 1, the placement drill.
    Numerator and denominator
    The top and the bottom of a rate, said out loud before it is computed.Half one, S8; notebook 1, section 2.
    Rate and window
    A rate leaves the team with its numerator, its denominator and the dates it covers.Half two, S6; notebook 3, section 2.
    Booked, delivered
    Two honest readings of sales: every order placed, or only the orders that reached a customer and stayed there.Half one, S3 and S4; notebook 1, section 1.
    Kernel
    The Python process behind a notebook, which remembers the cells it ran, in the order it ran them, since it last restarted.Half one, S16; notebook 2, section 1.
    Restart and Run All
    The one test of a notebook: every cell once, top to bottom, from an empty kernel.Half one, S19; notebook 2, section 1.
    Dictionary and key
    One record as named values, reached by exact key name.Half one, S20; notebook 2, section 2.
    List and index
    Records in order, reached by position counted from zero.Half one, S21; notebook 2, section 3.
    Accumulator
    A total started before a loop, updated once per record inside it, and read after it.Half one, S22; notebook 2, section 4.
    TypeError
    An operation met a type it cannot use, such as adding text to a number.Half one, S24 and S25; notebook 2, section 4.
    NameError, KeyError, ValueError
    A name no cell made; a key typed differently from the record; text int() cannot read as a number.Half one, S18 and D27; half two, S9.
    Mean
    The total spread evenly over the count; it multiplies back to the total.Half two, S12; notebook 4, section 1.
    Median
    The middle of the sorted list, or the average of the two middles when the count is even.Half two, S14 and S16; notebook 4, section 3.
    Skew
    A few values far above the rest, which pull a mean away from the median.Half two, S19; notebook 4, section 5.
