# Self-check: know you are right before anybody reads it

Every checkpoint is something you can verify alone. If one fails, the fix is named beside it.

---

## Part 1, the numbers

| # | Checkpoint | What you should see | If it does not match |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The file loaded | 24 orders | The loader still names today's file; change the file name in the setup cell |
| 2 | Revenue, all booked orders | Rs 5,69,540 | If the sum stops part way, print the record the loop stopped on and read its type before you change a line |
| 3 | Customers | 19 distinct customer ids | 24 means you counted rows; count each id once |
| 4 | Orders per customer | 1.26, which is 24 over 19 | 0.79 is the rate upside down |
| 5 | The typical order | The median, Rs 2,765 | Rs 2,730 or Rs 2,800 means you took one middle of an even count; average the two |
| 6 | The mean against the median | The mean is about eight and a half times the median | A mean close to the median means some amounts never made it into your list |

When all six match, read the top of your sorted list and put what you find into your sentence on
what surprised you, with its number.

## Part 2, the tree you built

Read your page back and answer each question yes or no. Two noes means rewrite it.

1. Does every leaf carry its denominator, in words?
2. Could somebody who has never seen that business tell what it sells from your five rows?
3. Is the cost of moving each leaf written in the owner's terms?
4. Is the leaf you picked the one your own reasoning points at, rather than the easiest to write
   about?
5. Is the number you would ask for one the owner would actually know?

## Part 3, the line

Your line names one variant for today's tree and one for your business, and gives a reason for the
second in the business's own terms. If the reason could apply to any business, it is not yet a
reason.
