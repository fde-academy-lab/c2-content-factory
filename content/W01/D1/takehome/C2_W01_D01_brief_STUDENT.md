# Take-home: the tree for a business you know

Two pieces of work. The first is about Kalpa and takes about forty minutes. The second is about a
business you can see with your own eyes and is the one that will come up in an interview.

Bring both to tomorrow's session. The first thing that happens tomorrow is that one of these gets
walked through in front of the room.

---

## Part 1. Two tree nodes on a file you have not seen

Open `notebooks/C2_W01_D01_hands_on_STUDENT.ipynb`. It reads
`data/C2_W01_D01_takehome_STUDENT.py`, which is a second sample of Kalpa Retail orders. None of
today's numbers appear in it, so nothing from the demo can be pasted across.

Fill every `__TODO__`, run each check, and post the four letters at the end.

**What you are proving:** that you can compute a leaf on a file nobody has explained, and that you
noticed what the file could not tell you.

---

## Part 2. The revenue tree for a business you can watch

Pick a business you can actually observe: the canteen, a kirana store near where you live, an app
you use most days, an auto stand, a gym. Not a company you have read about. One you can stand in
front of.

**Build the tree.**

1. Write the five branches for that business, in its own words. A canteen does not have "orders per
   customer", it has "how many times a week the same person eats here".
2. For each branch, write the metric with its numerator and its denominator.
3. For each branch, write what it would cost the owner to move it, in that owner's terms. Not
   "marketing spend" but "a board outside the gate" or "staying open an hour later".
4. Name the branch you believe moves most for that business, and say why in one sentence.
5. Name the one number you would have to ask the owner for, that you cannot see from outside.

**Write it as one page.** A drawing plus five rows plus two sentences is a page. Prose is not.

---

## Part 3. One paragraph, and this is the one that matters

> If you were handed Rs 5 lakh to grow that business by fifteen percent, which branch would you
> spend it on, and what would you check first to find out whether you were wrong?

Under 120 words. Name the check before you name the spend.

---

## What makes this hard to shortcut

Part 2 is about a business an assistant has never seen and cannot look up: your canteen, your
street, your gym. If your five branches read like a generic retail example rather than the place
you walked past this morning, it will show, and it will show fastest in Part 3, where the check has
to be something a person could actually do on Tuesday morning.

The strongest submissions last cohort all had the same tell: a number the writer had counted
themselves.
