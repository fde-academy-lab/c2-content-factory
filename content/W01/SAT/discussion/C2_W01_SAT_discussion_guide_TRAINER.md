# Saturday discussion guide

**TRAINER ONLY.** Led by the Academic TA. Seventy-five minutes, after the paper and the break.

The discussion is worth more than the marking. Every answer is treated as an interview answer, and
the format is deliberately uncomfortable: papers are swapped, and call-outs are random.

---

## The shape of the block

| Segment | Duration | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Swap and read | 10 min | Papers swap. Each learner reads the paper they are holding once, end to end, without marking. |
| The three that carry the week | 35 min | D1, C1 and E1, walked one at a time with random call-outs |
| The rest, at pace | 20 min | The remaining answers, walked quickly, peers marking as you go |
| The transfer | 10 min | F1, which is next week's opening move |

---

## The three that carry the week

### D1: what a p-value means

**Why this one first.** It is the most-failed item on the paper and the most-asked in interviews, and
it is failed in a specific way: the definition is right and the negation is missing.

**How to run it.** Ask three learners in a row for the definition, taking each without comment.
Write all three on the board as said. Then ask the room which of the three would survive somebody
saying "so there is a three percent chance we are wrong".

**The call-out that works:** pick somebody whose paper you are holding and ask them to read the
negation they wrote. If they did not write one, ask them to write one now, aloud, in one sentence.

**What to leave up:** the correct sentence and the crossed-out wrong one, side by side, for the rest
of the block.

### C1: Finance disagrees with your dashboard

**Why.** It is the trust question the market asks most, and the half everybody drops is the refusal.

**How to run it.** Ask for the first move. You will get "reconcile", which is correct and easy. Then
ask: **what would you refuse to do?** Wait. The silence here is the teaching.

**The answer to draw out:** adjusting your own figure so the two systems agree. Reconciling and
fabricating differ only in whether the steps are written down.

### E1: the four-part note

**Why.** It is the week's deliverable and the thing a hiring manager actually reads.

**How to run it.** Ask one learner to read their four sentences aloud. Then ask the room which part
is missing or weakest, and re-read the repaired version. Do this twice with two different papers.

**What to listen for:** a caveat that is a hedge rather than a condition. "This may not be accurate"
is a hedge. "Nothing here measures the reorder feature" is a condition, because it names what would
change the claim.

## Running random call-outs without losing the room

| Do | Do not |
|---|---|
| Name the person, then the question, then wait | Ask the question to the room and take the first hand |
| Give sixty seconds, and let the silence run | Rescue somebody at fifteen seconds |
| Take a wrong answer, write it on the board, and ask the room to repair it | Correct it yourself |
| Come back to the same person later with an easier one | Leave somebody who struggled sitting with it |

The last row matters more than the rest. A learner who is called, struggles and is never called
again learns that being called is a punishment.

---

## The bridge into Week 2

Close on this, and it takes five minutes.

> "Anand has his reconciliation. On Monday he asks for the same numbers **every** Monday, computed
> from the warehouse itself, with nothing a person can mistype. The tree does not change. The ladder
> does not change. The tool does."

Then one question to the room, and take three answers: **which parts of this week's work should
never be done in a notebook again, and why?**

The answers you want to hear: anything Finance depends on, anything that has to run unattended,
anything an auditor will read. That is Monday's first slide.

---

## What to record

Nothing is graded. Two things are worth noting for the week's read:

1. Which items more than a third of the room lost. Those get one level up in Week 2's return
   questions.
2. Who answered well when called cold. That is the mock-interview signal, and it is the only signal
   Week 1 produces that is worth carrying forward.
