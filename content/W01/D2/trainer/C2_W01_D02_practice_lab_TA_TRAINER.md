# Where does the practice lab stall, and which one hint moves each learner on?

**TA note, Week 1 Tuesday. TRAINER ONLY.** The learner file is
`exercises/practice/C2_W01_D02_practice_lab_STUDENT.md`, and the solution,
`exercises/solutions/C2_W01_D02_practice_lab_solution_STUDENT.md`, opens when the lab closes.

Answers: 1d 2b 3b 4a 5c 6c 7a 8d 9b 10d 11a 12c

The set is about an hour of work: four problems that climb from ordering the rungs of the day's
investigation to the first four lines of tonight's memo to Meera Raghavan, Kalpa Retail's CEO.
Revenue in the class file is booked revenue, every order placed at the price charged before any
cancellation or return, and the day found it down 11.0 percent between two closed quarters.

## How do you run the lab?

Open with two minutes on the ask: tonight's memo starts from Problem 4, so the lab is the memo's
first draft. Let the room work alone through the first three problems, walk the room during Problem
4, and close with ten minutes in which two learners read their four memo lines aloud and the room
names the number each line rests on. Give each stalled learner the one hint below for their problem
and nothing more, since a second hint turns the problem into a demonstration.

## Where do learners stall on each problem, and what is the one hint?

### Problem 1. Why must the windows match before the tree runs?

Learners who ran the chapters in order still put "decompose" before "like with like", because the
tree felt like the real work. Item 2 then goes wrong for the same reason, or the learner picks the
rate per week, which evens out the length of the windows and leaves their position alone.

The one hint: "What would have stopped the 25.9 percent from reaching the slide at all?"

### Problem 2. Is a rate per day across 92 and 91 days a mismatched window?

Item 4 is the stall. Learners see 92 days against 91 and call it a window mismatch, when a rate per
day evens out the unequal length and both windows are whole quarters, so their position already
matches. Item 5 is missed by learners who read only the dates and never the definitions.

The one hint: "Both sides are whole quarters, and the rate divides each by its own days. What is
left to be unequal?"

### Problem 3. Which two branches moved to keep the Kochi store's revenue flat?

Learners compute revenue's change, see 0.6 percent, and stop. Others pick the revenue per customer
figure in item 7 because it moves the most.

The one hint: "Revenue is flat. Which two branches moved in opposite directions to keep it flat?"

### Problem 4. Where is the number each memo line rests on?

Learners pick the right lines in items 9 to 12 and then write their own versions without the window,
the definition or the numbers. The fourth line is the hardest, since many write the reorder feature
as the cause.

The one hint: "Read your line to me as Anand would, looking for the number it rests on. Where is it?"

## What do you watch for in Problem 4?

- A learner whose `tree_for` does not reproduce 114 and 86 orders and 69 customers on the whole
  quarters has a bug to fix before any line is written, so send them back to chapter 3's check.
- A memo line that averages the four segments' orders per customer is the chapter 3 trap coming
  back, so ask for the weighted figure.
- A learner who asks why Retail-Plus shows 24 orders in May gets the day sheet's answer: write
  "May, 24?" on the parking board and say that Wednesday's session reads the file line by line. Do
  not explain it tonight, since Wednesday's lesson rests on the room finding the cause itself.
- Each learner counted the orders without a discount field in their own notebook in chapter 2, so
  point a learner who lost the count back to that cell and do not read it out.

## What does a finished lab look like?

Twelve letters are posted, with four memo lines under them in the learner's own words. Each line
carries its window or definition and its number, and the fourth names two hypotheses with the data
that would settle each.
