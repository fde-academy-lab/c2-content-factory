03 · Experiment cards

Five experiments, one change each

Read the situation and the hypothesis, decide what you expect, then run. Each card changes exactly one assumption and names the number that moves.

Experiment A · the window

The dashboard tile against the closed quarter

Situation. Marketing's deck puts the Q2 dashboard tile, exported on 15 September, beside the whole of Q1. Meera reads the percentage aloud in the leadership review.

Hypothesis. Once both windows cover 13 weeks, the fall shrinks from about a quarter of revenue to about a tenth.

Watch for. The Q2 line, which stops at week 11 on the tile, and the percentage in the title.

What happened.

Why it matters. A 25.9 percent fall reads as a crisis and rushes the Rs 12 crore acquisition budget; an 11.0 percent fall between closed quarters is a question to answer first.

Confirm the drop on matched windows before you explain it.

What happened appears here after the run.

Experiment B · the discount default

An absent discount read as zero

Situation. The tree's last branch is discounts. Some orders carry no discount field at all, and the hurried fix reads every absent field as Rs 0.

Hypothesis. Counted only over the orders that record the field, the share of orders with a discount is far above the zero-filled share.

Watch for. The gap between the two bars in Q2, and the percentage in the title.

What happened.

Why it matters. The zero-filled share says half the orders went without a discount, which argues for extending it to the other half; the largest recorded discount is Rs 150, so the branch is at most Rs 12,900 a quarter against a Rs 23,00,000 fall.

Missing means unknown until someone chooses a default and writes down why.

Experiment C · the roll-up

The average of four segment averages

Situation. A colleague has orders per customer for each of the four segments and averages the four to get the company figure for the note.

Hypothesis. The weighted figure falls four times as far as the averaged one, because Student's two customers count as much as Retail-Core's thirty-four in an average of averages.

Watch for. The company column at the right of the chart, and whether it matches round 2's 1.65 and 1.25.

What happened.

Why it matters. A 6.0 percent fall says frequency is not the branch and Marketing may be right; a 24.6 percent fall says frequency carries the drop.

Roll a rate up with its weights; never average the averages.

Experiment D · the helper

A helper that prints instead of returning

Situation. Last quarter's pct_change prints “check by hand” for any change beyond 30 percent and returns nothing in that case. The summary keeps only the falls it gets back.

Hypothesis. The two largest moves vanish from the summary, and nobody notices because the printed lines carry no segment name.

Watch for. The number of segments going in against the number of rows coming out.

What happened.

Why it matters. The short summary sends Business accounts to the front and tells the head of Retail-Plus his tier is not in the table, when his tier has the largest fall of all.

A function returns its answer; count the groups in and the groups out.

What happened appears here after the run.

Experiment E · the measure

Rupees against orders

Situation. The same segment split can be drawn in rupees or in orders, and Meera will hear whichever one reaches her first.

Hypothesis. In rupees one segment carries almost the whole fall; in orders a different segment does.

Watch for. Which bar is tallest in the bridge, and its share in the title.

What happened.

Why it matters. The rupee story rests on three orders, too few to call a trend today; the order story is 22 members whose buying halved, which is a change in behaviour.

Say which measure a share is of, and show both when they point at different segments.

07 · Glossary

The day's words, and where each first mattered

The investigation ladder
Five rungs climbed in order: is the drop real, which branch of the tree, which segment, mix against rate, and the hypothesis with its evidence.The ask, before round 1; every notebook opens on its rung.
Matched window
Two periods of the same length and the same kind, compared as closed quarters or as a rate per week.Round 1; notebook 01.
Rate
A numerator over a denominator over a window, and a rate that arrives without its denominator is a rumour.Round 1; notebook 01.
Dictionary accumulator
A dictionary started empty before a loop, with one total per key updated inside it, which groups records by segment or month.Round 2; notebook 02.
Decomposition
Revenue split into customers, orders per customer and revenue per order, whose ratios multiply back to the revenue ratio.Round 2; notebook 02.
Default for a missing field
The value a team decides an absent field takes, written down with its reason, since an absent value is unknown until then.Round 2; notebook 02.
Typical value and spread
The median of a group with its minimum, maximum and range, which says both where a group sits and how far it reaches.Round 3; notebook 03.
Function and return
A named block that applies one decision to any group and hands its answer back with return; a function that only prints hands back None.Round 3; notebook 03.
Groups in, groups out
The count of groups a comparison started with against the count it reports, which catches a group that dropped out silently.The escalated case; notebook 04.
Weighted roll-up
A company rate computed from the totals, so each segment counts in proportion to its size.Round 3 and the escalated case; notebooks 03 and 04.
Mix against rate
A change in an overall rate split into the part caused by the groups' shares moving and the part caused by the groups' own rates moving.The escalated case; notebook 04.
Hypothesis and evidence
A cause stated as a claim that could be wrong, with the data that would settle it, and timing is the first test.The second case; notebook 05.
