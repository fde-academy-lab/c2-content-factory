## The idea being tested

The ladder runs in a fixed order because each rung protects the next one: a comparison on
mismatched windows makes every later number wrong, and a hypothesis named before the decomposition
is a guess dressed as a finding. The new case in Problem 3 shows the same tree hiding a fall in
frequency behind flat revenue, and Problem 4 turns the day's numbers into the memo's first lines.

## The part worth arguing about

Item 8. Flat revenue is the most comfortable number a manager can see, and the invented Kochi store
shows why the tree runs even when the total has not moved: 40 more customers bought and frequency fell,
which existing customers buying less often or new customers ordering once could each produce. Whether the Q1 buyers came back is the same set-overlap check
the second case ran on the class file.

## Where the pattern lives in production

A written investigation memo with a first line that states the window and the definition is what
a senior analyst reads before anything else, and what an interviewer listens for in the first
twenty seconds of "sales dropped, how would you investigate?".
