# Solution: Can you run the day's investigation alone, from the order of its rungs to the memo's first four lines?

Answers: 1d 2b 3b 4a 5c 6c 7a 8d 9b 10d 11a 12c

The practice lab runs the day's investigation into Kalpa Retail's fall from Q1 to Q2 once more
without the trainer: the order of its rungs, three draft comparisons, an invented pilot store in
Kochi whose revenue stayed flat, and the first four lines of tonight's memo to Meera Raghavan, the
CEO. Revenue is booked revenue, every order placed at the price charged before any cancellation or
return, and Monday's revenue tree splits it into customers x orders per customer x revenue per order.
One of the twelve items is a design item: 2.

## What does the lab test?

The ladder runs in a fixed order because each rung protects the next one. A comparison on
mismatched windows makes every later number wrong, and a hypothesis named before the decomposition
is a guess dressed as a finding. The Kochi store in Problem 3 shows the same tree finding a fall in
frequency behind flat revenue, and Problem 4 turns the day's numbers into the memo's first lines.

## Problem 1. In which order does a sales-drop investigation run?

### Q1. In which order do Kavya's five cards run?

Five shuffled cards: isolate the branch and the segment (p), name a hypothesis with its evidence (q),
confirm the drop is real (r), decompose the change along the tree (s), compare like with like (t).

The key is d, "r, t, s, p, q". Confirm the drop, compare like with like, decompose, isolate, then
hypothesise.

- a, "s, p, r, t, q": it decomposes before anyone knows the drop is real.
- b, "r, s, t, p, q": it decomposes before the windows match.
- c, "t, r, s, q, p": it starts on like with like before a drop is confirmed, and hypothesises before
  isolating.

### Q2. What should the skipped rung have sent Meera on 15 September? (Design)

Marketing's 25.9 percent set a dashboard tile read on 15 September, with Q2 open, against all of Q1,
because one rung was skipped.

The key is b, "The same 11 weeks of each quarter, minus 17.0 percent, switching to closed quarters at
the close". The skipped rung is like with like. With Q2 open, the same 11 weeks of each quarter
control for both length and position, minus 17.0 percent, and the day Q2 closes the closed quarters
answer directly.

- a, "The tile against all of Q1, minus 25.9 percent, switching once Marketing agrees the method is
  fair": it is the unmatched comparison that made the slide.
- c, "A rate per week, minus 12.4 percent, switching to last year's Q2 once the export arrives from
  Finance": a rate evens out the length of the windows and leaves their position alone, and the
  season is a different question from an open quarter.
- d, "The tree's leaves, frequency and order value, switching to the segments once they are split":
  it decomposes a drop nobody has confirmed.

## Problem 2. Which of three draft comparisons set like against like?

### Q3. Which reading fits the draft line on Retail-Core's revenue?

"Retail-Core booked Rs 60,950 from 1 July to 15 September, against Rs 80,460 across Q1."

The key is b, "Unfair: the two sides cover windows of different length or position". Rs 60,950 is
Retail-Core's booked revenue from 1 July to 15 September, about 11 weeks, set against all 13 weeks
of Q1; its closed Q2 is Rs 72,510.

- a, "Fair: the two sides share a window, a definition and a denominator": the windows differ.
- c, "Unfair: the two sides count revenue under different definitions": both sides are booked
  revenue.
- d, "Unfair: the rate on one side divides by a different population of customers than the other":
  no rate is involved.

### Q4. Which reading fits the draft line on revenue per day?

"Q2 booked revenue per day was Rs 2,03,261, against Rs 2,30,769 a day in Q1, across 92 and 91 days."

The key is a, "Fair: the two sides share a window, a definition and a denominator". A rate per day
divides each quarter by its own days, so the windows compare fairly.

- b, "Unfair: the two sides cover windows of different length or position": the rate per day is the
  fix for unequal length.
- c, "Unfair: the two sides count revenue under different definitions": both sides are booked.
- d, "Unfair: the rate on one side divides by a different population of customers than the other":
  the denominator is days, and each side uses its own.

### Q5. Which reading fits the draft line on delivered Q2 revenue?

"Delivered revenue in Q2 was Rs 1,28,64,680, against booked revenue of Rs 2,10,00,000 in Q1."

The key is c, "Unfair: the two sides count revenue under different definitions". Delivered revenue
sits on one side and booked on the other, so the gap mixes the fall with cancellations and returns.

- a, "Fair: the two sides share a window, a definition and a denominator": the definitions differ.
- b, "Unfair: the two sides cover windows of different length or position": both are closed
  quarters.
- d, "Unfair: the rate on one side divides by a different population of customers than the other":
  no rate is involved.

## Problem 3. What does the revenue tree say about a store whose revenue stayed flat?

### Q6. How many orders per customer did the Kochi store book in each quarter?

The invented Kochi store had 400 customers and 520 orders in Q1, and 440 customers and 506 orders in
Q2.

The key is c, "1.30 to 1.15, a fall of 11.5 percent measured against the Q1 figure". 520 over 400 is
1.30 and 506 over 440 is 1.15, and the fall measured against Q1 is 11.5 percent.

- a, "1.30 to 1.15, a fall of 13.0 percent measured against the Q2 figure": it measures the change
  against Q2.
- b, "0.77 to 0.87, a rise of 13.0 percent in how often each customer buys": it divides customers by
  orders, the rate upside down.
- d, "520 to 506, a fall of 2.7 percent in the orders the store booked": those are orders, with no
  denominator.

### Q7. What is the Kochi store's revenue per order in each quarter?

The invented store booked Rs 7,80,000 on 520 orders in Q1 and Rs 7,84,300 on 506 orders in Q2.

The key is a, "Rs 1,500 to Rs 1,550, a rise of 3.3 percent against Q1's order value". Rs 7,80,000
over 520 is Rs 1,500 and Rs 7,84,300 over 506 is Rs 1,550, a rise of 3.3 percent.

- b, "Rs 1,950 to Rs 1,782, a fall of 8.6 percent in spend per customer": that is revenue per
  customer, a different branch.
- c, "Rs 1,500 to Rs 1,550, a rise of 0.6 percent, the same as revenue's": 0.6 percent is revenue's
  own change.
- d, "Rs 1,500 to Rs 1,550, a rise of 3.2 percent against Q2's order value": it measures the change
  against Q2.

### Q8. Which reply to the Kochi store manager holds?

The manager reads the store's flat revenue as proof that nothing moved and wants to skip the review.

The key is d, "New customers may have masked existing ones buying less often, so check Q1's buyers".
Customers rose 10 percent, frequency fell 11.5 percent and order value rose 3.3 percent, and 1.10
times 0.885 times 1.033 is 1.006, which is the flat revenue. Flat revenue hid a fall in how often
people buy, and the next check is whether Q1's customers came back.

- a, "Revenue is flat, so the manager is right and the review can be skipped this quarter": it trusts
  the total.
- b, "Order value rose 3.3 percent, so the store's pricing is working and needs no change": it reads
  one branch alone.
- c, "Customers rose 10 percent and so did revenue, so the new customers carried the quarter": it
  credits the new customers and misses the fall in how often customers buy.

## Problem 4. Which four lines open the memo to Meera, and which numbers do they rest on?

### Q9. Which first line states the drop?

The memo's first line states the drop so that nobody can argue with its window.

The key is b, "Revenue fell 11.0 percent between two closed 13-week quarters, Rs 2.10 to Rs 1.87
crore, booked." It carries two closed quarters of 13 weeks, the definition and the numbers.

- a, "Revenue fell 25.9 percent quarter on quarter, from Rs 2.10 crore to Rs 1.56 crore on booked
  orders.": it uses the tile's window.
- c, "Revenue fell about 9.5 percent this quarter, from roughly Rs 2.1 crore to roughly Rs 1.9
  crore.": the rounding loses Rs 3,00,000 of the gap.
- d, "Revenue fell sharply between the two quarters, and the fall is large enough to need action
  now.": it has no number.

### Q10. Which second line names the branch of the tree that moved?

The memo's second line names the branch that moved, with its numbers.

The key is d, "Customers held at 69, every one buying in both quarters, and orders per customer fell
1.65 to 1.25." Customers held and nobody was lost or gained, so the branch is frequency, 1.65 to
1.25.

- a, "Customers fell, so acquisition is the branch to fund, as the marketing lead has said from the
  start.": customers did not fall.
- b, "Averaged across the four segments, frequency fell only 6.0 percent, so no single branch moved
  much.": it averages the segments' averages.
- c, "Order value rose 18.0 percent, so the fall must sit in the number of customers who bought from
  us.": it infers one branch from another.

### Q11. Which third line names the segment in behaviour and in rupees?

The memo's third line names the segment behind the fall, in how its customers behaved and in rupees.

The key is a, "In behaviour, 22 Retail-Plus members placed 26 orders against 51, and in rupees it is 3
Business orders." It gives both halves of the finding, each with its numbers.

- b, "In behaviour and in rupees alike, the fall sits in Business, which lost Rs 22,29,720 across
  three orders.": Business is the rupee story, and the behaviour story sits in Retail-Plus.
- c, "In rupees, the fall sits in Retail-Plus, whose revenue halved from Rs 1,43,550 to Rs 78,300 in
  the quarter.": Retail-Plus is a small part of the rupees.
- d, "Every segment fell by a similar share, so no segment needs to be named ahead of the others in
  the memo.": the fall is concentrated.

### Q12. Which fourth line states a cause Kavya would sign off?

The memo's fourth line states the cause the way Kavya, the team's senior analyst, would sign it off.

The key is c, "Two causes stay open: the reorder feature, settled by its logs, and a July change, by
the tier's log." It names two hypotheses, each with the data that would settle it.

- a, "The cause is the broken reorder feature, as the head of Retail-Plus has reported from his
  members.": it states a hypothesis as a fact.
- b, "The cause cannot be known from an order file, so the memo leaves the question of cause to
  others.": it gives up when the file can already test timing.
- d, "The cause is a market-wide slowdown in July, which explains why every channel fell in the same
  months.": it claims a cause that no data in the file supports.

## Which item is worth arguing about?

Item 8. Flat revenue is the most comfortable number a manager can see, and the invented Kochi store
shows why the tree runs even when the total has not moved: 40 more customers bought and frequency
fell, which existing customers buying less often or new customers ordering once could each produce.
Whether the Q1 buyers came back is the same set-overlap check chapter 5 ran on the class file.

## Where does this pattern live in production?

A written investigation memo whose first line states the window and the definition is what a senior
analyst reads before anything else, and what an interviewer listens for in the first twenty seconds
of "sales dropped, how would you investigate?".
