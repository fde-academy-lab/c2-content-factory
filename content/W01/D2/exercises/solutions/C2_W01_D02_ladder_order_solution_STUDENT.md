# Solution: the ladder, and the comparisons that are not fair

Answers: 1c 2a 3b 4d 5b 6c

---

| Item | Key | Why, and why the rest fail |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | `c` | A named cause is rung five. Arriving with it is not a crime; treating it as established without the four rungs below is. The complaint came from the person whose tier it would excuse, which is worth noticing. |
| **2** | `a` | Rung one asks only whether the fall happened, and that needs both totals on one definition of revenue. A chart is a picture of the same thing. Finance agreeing is useful and is not your own check. The customer count is rung three. |
| **3** | `b` | A per-week figure divides out the window difference. Option c is the trap: orders per customer over eleven weeks and over thirteen weeks are still two different measures, because there is more time for a second order in the longer one. |
| **4** | `d` | A rename moves the same people between buckets, so one segment appears to collapse and another appears from nowhere. The total holds, which is exactly why the total does not protect you. |
| **5** | `b` | The factors have to multiply to the revenue change. 0.94 against 0.89 is not rounding; it is a factor unaccounted for. Everything built on an unclosed decomposition inherits the gap. |
| **6** | `c` | Placed and delivered are different denominators, so the two quarters are measuring different populations. The other three each keep one definition on both sides. |

## The one to carry forward

Item 3 is the rung the market's candidates skip most often, and it is the one that makes an
interviewer decide whether you have done this before. "Compare rates rather than totals" sounds
careful and is not enough on its own.
