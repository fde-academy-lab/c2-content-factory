# Solution: How much of the Retail-Plus fall can the broken reorder button explain, and what evidence goes in Meera's memo?

Answers: 1c 2d 3b 4a 5d 6a

The head of Retail-Plus, Kalpa Retail's paid membership tier, blames the fall in his members' orders on the app's reorder button, which one member says has been broken for six weeks, a break the team reads as 25 August. The tier's members placed 51 orders in Q1, then 18 between 1 July and 24 August and 8 from 25 August to 30 September, and Meera Raghavan, the CEO, wants a memo on what the data can and cannot say about the cause.

Four of the six items are design items: 1, 3, 5 and 6.

### Q1. Which plan of tests fits the memo due tonight? (Design)

The memo is due tonight, and four tests are on the table: before and after the break (A), Retail-Core as a comparison (B) and the tier by channel (C), each minutes on the export, and the app's reorder logs (D), a request days away.

The key is c, "A, B and C tonight in that order, and the request for D sent today". Each cheap test can rule a cause in or out tonight from the export already open, timing first since a cause cannot come after its effect. Only the logs settle it, so the request for them goes today and the memo does not wait for them.

- a, "D first, since it settles the question, then A to C if the logs run late": the memo waits days while three tests sit unrun.
- b, "A alone, since timing already shows the fall began before the break": timing caps the button and says nothing about the season or the channel.
- d, "B, C and D, since the complaint already dates the break for us": the complaint gives a date, and testing it against the fall's timing is the first test.

### Q2. What is wrong with the hurried memo's 25 orders?

The hurried memo charges the button with 25 orders and Rs 65,250, the tier's whole fall from Q1's 51 orders to Q2's 26.

The key is d, "It charges the button with about eight weeks of losses from before it broke". Q1 against all of Q2 takes in 1 July to 24 August, when the tier's pace had already fallen to 2.29 orders a week.

- a, "It uses booked orders instead of the delivered orders the board pack counts": the definition is the same in both quarters.
- b, "It should be measured in members, 22 in both quarters, and not in orders": orders are the right unit for a reorder button, and the members did not change.
- c, "It counts only the 13 and 8 app orders, leaving out the web and the store": it counts every channel.

### Q3. At most how many orders can the button explain? (Design)

The tier placed 18 orders in the 55 days before the break and 8 in the 37 days after it.

The key is b, "About 4, since the pre-break pace gives 12.1 in 37 days and 8 came". 18 orders in 55 days is 0.327 a day, about 12.1 in the 37 days after the break. The tier placed 8, so about 4.1 orders is the most the button can explain, and it is a ceiling, since anything else still worsening would claim part of it.

- a, "About 10, since 18 orders came before the break and 8 came after it": it compares windows of 55 and 37 days, chapter 1's trap.
- c, "About 25, the tier's whole fall from Q1's 51 orders to Q2's 26": it charges the button with the fall that came before it broke.
- d, "About 12, the 12.1 orders the pre-break pace predicts after the break": 12.1 is what was expected, and 8 of those came.

### Q4. Which cause does Retail-Core's 95 percent rule out?

Retail-Core kept 95 percent of its Q1 orders and Retail-Plus 51 percent.

The key is a, "A season that hit every customer alike". A season that hit everyone would have moved Retail-Core too. A season that hit members harder still fits, and last year's Q2 by segment tests it.

- b, "Any change in the tier's benefits in July": a change to the tier touches members only and fits the pattern.
- c, "The reorder button": the comparison segment says nothing about the button.
- d, "A monsoon dip that hit members harder": that version of the season survives the test.

### Q5. Which evidence goes with each of the memo's two hypotheses? (Design)

The memo names two hypotheses, the button after 25 August and a change for members in July, and each needs its own evidence.

The key is d, "The button with the reorder logs, and a July change with the tier's change log". Each hypothesis is settled by data the export does not carry, and no two share their evidence: the reorder logs test the button, and the tier's change log tests a change for members in July.

- a, "The button with the tier's change log, and a July change with the app's reorder logs": the pairs are swapped.
- b, "The button with Retail-Core's orders by week, and a July change with the tier's channels": both are cuts of this export, which can cap a cause and cannot settle it.
- c, "The button with the tier's renewals, and a July change with the app's release date": renewals and a release date each belong with the other hypothesis.

### Q6. What does a pace corrected by Retail-Core's change show? (Design)

The second route scales the tier's pre-break pace by Retail-Core's own change in pace across the break, 0.946, and compares it with the 8 orders the tier placed after the break.

The key is a, "About 3.5 orders, inside the ceiling, as part of the dip hit Retail-Core too". 11.45 orders expected against 8 placed is about 3.5, an independent correction that lands under the ceiling of about 4, which is what a ceiling predicts.

- b, "About 11.5 orders, the button's full cost once the season is taken out": 11.5 is the corrected expectation, of which 8 came.
- c, "The ceiling was wrong, since the two routes differ by about 0.6 orders": a lower figure from a stricter route is what a ceiling predicts, so it is no error.
- d, "The button explains nothing at all, since Retail-Core also slowed after 25 August": Retail-Core slowed about 5 percent, far less than the tier.
