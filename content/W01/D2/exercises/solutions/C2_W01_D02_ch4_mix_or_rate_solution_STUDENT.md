# Solution: Revenue per order rose 18 percent while revenue fell: did customers pay more, or did the mix of orders change?

Answers: 1b 2c 3d 4a 5b 6a

The marketing lead read the 18.0 percent rise in Kalpa Retail's revenue per order, Rs 1,84,211 in Q1 to Rs 2,17,442 in Q2, as customers happy to pay more, and asked for a price rise in the plan. Revenue per order is blended over every segment; the mix is each segment's share of orders, a segment's rate is its own revenue per order, and pricing Q2's mix at Q1's rates separates the mix part of the Rs 33,231 rise from the rate part.

Four of the six items are design items: 2, 3, 4 and 6.

### Q1. Which reading of the table answers Marketing's price claim?

Marketing reads the 18.0 percent rise in blended revenue per order as customers paying more, and the table gives each segment's share of orders and its revenue per order in both quarters.

The key is b, "No segment rose 18 percent, so small member orders leaving the blend explain it". The blend rose 18.0 percent while no segment rose that far, because Retail-Plus's small orders fell from 44.7 to 30.2 percent of orders and Business's lakh-sized ones rose to 19.8 percent.

- a, "Business rose 5.0 percent, so prices across the range can safely rise 5 percent": Business's own rise is 5 percent on its own orders and says nothing about consumer prices.
- c, "Student rose 14.8 percent, so the students' higher prices explain the blended rise": Student is 8 percent of orders at about a thousand rupees each, too small to move a blend of lakhs.
- d, "Retail-Core fell 4.9 percent, so its prices should be cut to win the orders back": a fall inside Retail-Core says nothing about the blend's rise.

### Q2. Which reading answers each of four questions about the rise? (Design)

Four questions about the rise (its size, whether any segment paid more, how much of it is mix, and the typical order) are matched to four readings: the per-segment rates (P), the medians (Q), the blended change (R) and the mix-and-rate split (S).

The key is c, "1R 2P 3S 4Q". The blend gives the size of the rise, the per-segment rates say whether anyone paid more, the split puts rupees on the mix, and the median is the typical order.

- a, "1R 2S 3P 4Q": the split cannot say whether a given segment paid more, and the rates cannot size the mix.
- b, "1S 2P 3R 4Q": the size of the rise is the blend itself, which the split only shares out.
- d, "1R 2P 3Q 4S": a median cannot size the mix, and the split says nothing about a typical order.

### Q3. When would the per-segment table be enough without a mix split? (Design)

Which fact would make the table of each segment's own revenue per order enough, with no mix split needed?

The key is d, "Segments with similar order sizes, or shares that held still". Mix moves the blend only when segments differ in order size and their shares move. Without either, the per-segment rates tell the whole story.

- a, "A rise in revenue per order larger than the 18 percent seen this quarter": a bigger rise still needs splitting.
- b, "Segments whose own rates all moved by less than the blend did": that is this quarter, where the split was needed to put rupees on the gap.
- c, "Four segments instead of two": the number of segments does not decide whether mix matters.

### Q4. How much of the Rs 33,231 rise is mix? (Design)

Q2's order mix is priced at each segment's Q1 revenue per order, to find how much of the Rs 33,231 rise, from Rs 1,84,211 to Rs 2,17,442, is mix.

The key is a, "Rs 2,07,112, so the mix is Rs 22,902 of the rise, about 69 percent". Business's share rose from 17.5 to 19.8 percent at about Rs 10.4 lakh an order, which lifts the blend to Rs 2,07,112 with no segment's rate moving: Rs 22,902 of the Rs 33,231 rise.

- b, "Rs 2,07,112, so the mix is Rs 10,330 of the rise, about 31 percent": Rs 10,330 is the rest, the rate part.
- c, "Rs 1,93,414, so the mix is Rs 9,203 of the rise, about 28 percent": that prices Q1's mix at Q2's rates, which isolates the rate part.
- d, "Rs 2,17,442, so the mix is all Rs 33,231 of the rise, every rupee": Rs 2,17,442 is Q2's actual figure, mix and rate together.

### Q5. Which segment carries most of the rate part?

The rate part of the rise, what is left once the mix is priced, is split by segment to see which carries most of it.

The key is b, "Business, since its lakh-sized orders dominate the rate part". Business's 17 orders each grew about Rs 52,000 on average, so almost all of the rate part is Business, while the consumer segments moved by a few hundred rupees at most.

- a, "Retail-Plus, so members did pay noticeably more for each order they placed": Retail-Plus's rise is about Rs 200 an order on 30 percent of orders, about Rs 60 of the rate part.
- c, "Retail-Core, so everyday shoppers carry the rise": Retail-Core's rate fell.
- d, "Student, whose rate rose most in percentage terms": the largest percentage sits on the smallest orders, about Rs 12 of the rate part.

### Q6. What does a split into Business and everyone else put on the mix? (Design)

The envelope route treats Kalpa as Business and everyone else: Business's share of orders rose from 17.5 to 19.8 percent, and a Q1 Business order was worth Rs 10,36,125 more than a consumer order.

The key is a, "About Rs 23,000, within 1 percent of the split, so the call holds however it is cut". 0.022 times Rs 10,36,125 is about Rs 23,000, against Rs 22,902 from four segments, so an independent route that could have disagreed lands within one percent.

- b, "About Rs 23,000, so the four-segment split was out by Rs 137 and needs correcting": the two routes differ by what the consumer segments' own mix does, so a small gap is expected and is no error.
- c, "About Rs 2,300, since a 2.2-point move in share moves the blend 2.2 percent at most": the change in share multiplies a gap of lakhs between a Business order and a consumer one.
- d, "All Rs 33,231, since only Business's share moved while the consumer prices held": the rate part, Rs 10,330, is Business's own orders growing, which the envelope leaves out.
