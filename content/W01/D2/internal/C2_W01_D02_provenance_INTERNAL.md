## Decisions that depart from a source

1. **Where the segment finding appears.** The row protects the room's route to Retail-Plus. It stays
   out of the morning deck, notebooks 01 to 03 and their saved outputs, the guided exercise, the three
   round sets, the Kahoot and the pre-read. It appears from the afternoon on, after the room has found
   it: the afternoon deck, the case briefs, notebooks 04 and 05 with their solutions, the companion,
   the notes, the cheat sheet and the later board drawings. Customers flat and the overall frequency
   fall are the trainer's round 2 demonstration, because rung two cannot be taught without them.
2. **The traps are assigned to rounds differently from the spine's list order.** The spine lists
   windows, the average of averages, the missing discount and the silent helper. The pack runs
   windows in round 1, the missing discount in round 2 (the tree's last branch), the average of
   averages in round 3 (where segments arrive, and where it prints only the two averages so the
   segment stays hidden), and the silent helper in the escalated case, where the four-segment table
   is the task.
3. **The rupee fall is attributed honestly.** On the file as exported, Business carries Rs 22,29,720
   of the Rs 23,00,000 fall, from three fewer orders. The row's "almost entirely in Retail-Plus"
   holds for orders (25 of 28) and for the consumer segments (93 percent of the Rs 70,280 consumer
   fall). The pack states both and makes the difference rung four, mix against rate, and the
   afternoon's debrief of wrong answers. Most of the Business difference is Wednesday's two duplicated
   corporate rows; learners are told only that tomorrow's reconciliation checks it.
4. **The window trap is staged on the file.** The data carries two closed quarters, so the wrong
   comparison is Marketing's Q2 tile cut on 15 September (Rs 1,55,59,950 over 11 weeks), computed from
   the same rows. The cut date is invented.
5. **Interview questions 6 to 12 and their tags are this pack's construction**, added as the spine
   asks (case-style follow-ups to the row's five anchors) and tagged with the row's legend.
6. **The reorder complaint's date.** The row says the feature has been broken for six weeks; the pack
   reads that back from the week of delivery, which puts the break in late August, and says it is an
   assumption wherever the timing test uses it.

7. **The morning deck runs to 52 slides against the standard's "about 40"**, because each round carries
   its full sequence and every Predict slide has its Answer slide; the afternoon deck runs to 29. The
   D slides and the round variants are the cut lines the day sheet names.
8. **The study notes run to about 5,400 words against "about 4,000"**, carrying three rounds, two cases
   and twelve full interview answers; the interview answers are where to cut.
9. **Notebook 05 adds Retail-Plus orders per week around the assumed break** (3.9 in Q1, then 2.3 and
   1.5), computed from the file, because 18 against 8 orders compares windows of 55 and 37 days.
