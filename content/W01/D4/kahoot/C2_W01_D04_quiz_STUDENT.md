## Q1. Ten shuffles gave gaps of 2 to 6. The real gap is 5. Surprising?
*Tests: the p-value is a comparison against the chance-only worlds you made.*

a) Yes, because a gap of 5 is toward the top of the range observed
b) No, chance produced gaps this large most of the time  <- correct
c) Yes, because ten shuffles is far too few to conclude anything at all
d) It cannot be said without knowing the average of the ten shuffles

## Q2. "p equals 0.03 means a 3 percent chance we are wrong." Is that right?
*Tests: the misreading that gets said aloud in meetings.*

a) Yes, that is the standard interpretation used across the industry
b) Yes, if the sample is large enough for the approximation to hold
c) No, it is the share of chance-only worlds this extreme or more  <- correct
d) No, it is the probability that chance alone produced the finding

---

## Q3. 40 percent on 12 orders against 31 percent on 400. Which do you trust?
*Tests: the rule of thumb, and that a bigger percentage is not better evidence.*

a) The 40 percent, because it is the larger of the two movements
b) Neither, because the two are measured on different populations entirely
c) Both equally, since a percentage already accounts for the base size
d) The 31 percent  <- correct

---

## Q4. Revenue rose 6 percent after the discount. Ask what first?
*Tests: a fair comparison needs a control group that resembles the treated one.*

a) Who received the discount, and how they differ from who did not  <- correct
b) How much margin the discount cost the business over those two weeks
c) Whether the same campaign was run in the equivalent period last year
d) Whether six percent is large enough to matter to the business plan

---

## Q5. The aggregate favours the campaign and every segment says the opposite.
*Tests: when a comparison reverses, the aggregate was driven by the mix.*

a) The aggregate, because it uses all the customers rather than a subset
b) Neither, because a contradiction means one of them was computed wrongly
c) The segments, because the aggregate is being driven by the mix  <- correct
d) The aggregate, if the segments have too few customers to be reliable

---

## Q6. What are the four parts of the note, in order?
*Tests: the shape that stops a CEO being misled in two minutes.*

a) Summary, detail, appendix, next steps in the usual business order
b) Claim, evidence, caveat, action  <- correct
c) Question, method, result, conclusion as in a scientific paper
d) Context, analysis, recommendation, risk as in a consulting deck
