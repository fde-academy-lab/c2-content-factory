# Solution: round 3 set, the count behind the rate, and who got it

Answers: 1b 2c 3d 4a 5b 6c 7a

## The idea being tested

A rate is a numerator over a count, and a comparison is only as fair as the groups in it. Items 1 to
4 ask how far a rate can be trusted from its count. Items 5 to 7 ask who ended up in each group, which
is the question the afternoon asks of the monsoon sale.

## The part worth arguing about

Item 5, option a. The app may really make buying easier, and the comparison still cannot show it,
because the people who installed it were different before they installed it. The fair test is to
offer the install prompt to a random half of similar customers and compare after.

## Where the pattern lives in production

"Users who do X retain better" is the most common slide in product reviews, and X is usually
something engaged users do anyway. The question that exposes it is item 5's: who chose to be in the
group?
