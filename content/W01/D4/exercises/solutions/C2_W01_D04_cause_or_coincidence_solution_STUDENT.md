# Solution: cause, or coincidence?

Answers: 1c 2b 3c 4b 5d 6a

---

| Item | Key | Why, and why the rest fail |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | `c` | The reminder almost certainly went to patients with a phone number on file, who differ from those without one in ways that also predict turning up. Ask who got it before believing what it did. `d` is a reasonable instinct and answers a different question. |
| **2** | `b` | This is the app-install version of the monsoon sale. The heavy spenders installed the app because they shop often, so the comparison measures who installs rather than what the app does. `d` is a real possibility and is not the main one. |
| **3** | `c` | One store, one month, no comparison. A refit happens alongside a new season, a local event and whatever the rest of the estate did. Comparable stores that were not refitted are the missing half of the sentence. |
| **4** | `b` | Both figures are correct. Half the exposed group is Retail-Plus against forty percent of the control, and Retail-Plus spends far more, so the blend is pulled upward by who is in it. `a` is the instinct to look for an arithmetic error when none exists. |
| **5** | `d` | Two things changed in the same window and the data holds no way to separate them. `a` and `b` each pick one and assert it. `c` sounds careful and is not: last year's same month had neither change, so it removes nothing. |
| **6** | `a` | This is the only vignette with a fair comparison in it, because somebody assigned the discount at random. The answer is allowed to be "no effect", and treating that as a failed experiment is how teams keep running tests until one agrees with them. |

## The writing task

Marketing's claim was:

> "The monsoon sale lifted revenue six percent. We should repeat it for Diwali."

A defensible rewrite:

> "Exposed customers spent six percent more than unexposed ones. That gap comes from the mix rather
> than the discount: half the exposed group is Retail-Plus against forty percent of the control, and
> within both segments exposed customers spent three percent less. I cannot say the campaign failed
> either, because nobody randomised it."

And what would make the intended claim answerable:

> "Assign the next discount at random within a segment, so the two groups differ only in whether they
> received it."

**What loses credit:** a rewrite that says the campaign failed, which over-corrects and is as
unsupported as the original, or one that drops the six percent rather than explaining it. The number
is real; what it measures is the thing to fix.
