## The idea being tested

Every campaign readout needs three questions answered: who got it, who did not, and what else
changed. The design items ask which set-up answers the marketing lead's Diwali plan, what the
hold-back costs, which fact would switch the call, and what two imperfect routes can say together.
The other items ask what to check first on a before-and-after jump and how to hold the line when the
split is called cherry-picking.

## The part worth arguing about

Item 5, option d. The split does use more customers than the month counts, and some pairs will call
it the winner. Size is a different question from fairness: both routes compare groups a rule chose,
so the honest line is that neither finds a lift the sale can claim, and the Diwali hold-back is what
turns "cannot claim" into an answer.

## Where the pattern lives in production

eBay's paid search experiment switched brand-keyword ads off in some markets and found almost all
the lost clicks and sales came back through natural search, because the customers who clicked the
ads were going to buy anyway. Any campaign aimed at customers who already buy has that shape, and a
random hold-back is how a team finds out before it spends.
