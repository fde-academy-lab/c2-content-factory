## The idea being tested

A shuffle test builds worlds in which the quarter made no difference and counts how often those
worlds make a gap as large as the real one. The design items ask why the shuffle fits this file and
this reader, what would switch the call, and what a second route adds. The other items ask the
question every share raises: in which world was it counted, and what does that let the note say?

## The part worth arguing about

Item 2, option c. Pairs who choose it are reasoning about speed, and on this file speed never
decides: the shuffle runs 5,000 deals in under a second. The call turns on the shape of the data and
on who reads the answer, which is why a large, well-behaved file and a team standard are the facts
that move it.

## Where the pattern lives in production

Booking.com runs thousands of experiments a year, and its teams expect most ideas to fail, so an
analyst there reads a share against the usual wobble many times a week. The habit that keeps those
readings honest is item 4's rewrite: the share, the world it was counted in, and nothing more.
