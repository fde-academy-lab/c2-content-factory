# Solution: round 1 set, real or the usual wobble

Answers: 1c 2a 3d 4b 5a 6c 7d

## The idea being tested

A shuffle test builds worlds in which the quarter made no difference and counts how often those
worlds make a gap as large as the real one. Every item asks the same thing in a new place: in which
world was the share counted, and what does that let the note say?

## The part worth arguing about

Item 5. Some pairs defend b, because a large share "shows" nothing happened. It shows only that the
data cannot tell a small real change from the wobble. A note says "no sign of a change beyond the
usual wobble", which is true, and never "nothing changed", which the data cannot know.

## Where the pattern lives in production

Every experimentation dashboard in an analytics team prints a p-value beside each metric, and the
first sentence written about it is usually item 2's. The habit that prevents it is the rewrite in
item 3, said the same way every time.
