# Solution: chapter 5 set, the note that may say not yet

Answers: 1b 2a 3c 4d 5c 6d 7b

## The idea being tested

A CEO's note carries each number with its partner (a share with its world and its direction, a rupee
figure with its whole, a rate with its count, a lift with its groups) and a decision in the same
place. The design items give new situations and ask which form a weekly request needs, what to cut
when a note runs long, and which second route catches a note whose decision does not follow from its
numbers. The other items ask whether a line passes the audit, how "not yet" is said so that it still
answers, and the order of the audit.

## The part worth arguing about

Item 5, option a. Tracing every figure is the check most teams run, and some pairs will pick it by
reflex. It passes this note, because the note's numbers are right and only its decision is wrong. A
note can quote a correct split and still recommend the opposite of what the split says, and the check
that catches it applies the rule the analysis ended on and compares the decision.

## Where the pattern lives in production

Amazon's meetings run on six-page narrative memos read in silence at the start, and Jeff Bezos's
2017 letter to shareholders says the company does not use slide presentations. The discipline is the
same at any length: every claim carries its evidence and its caveat, and the reader leaves with a
decision and the reasoning behind it.
