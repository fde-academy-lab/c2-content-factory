# Student is up 40 percent, the fastest rise on the page: how many customers stand behind it, and should budget move there?

Chapter 3 set, six items, after chapter 3: items 1 and 2 run live in the chapter's last three
minutes; the rest are the practice lab's stretch or tonight's work.

> "Student is up 40 percent; should I move budget there?"
>
> Meera Raghavan, CEO, Kalpa Retail

Kalpa Retail's Student segment placed 40 percent more orders in Q2 than in Q1, the fastest rise of
its four segments, and Meera asks whether acquisition budget, the money spent winning new customers,
should follow it. A rate is one count over another, so chapter 3 counted what the rise stands on, in
orders and in customers, and built a chance reference for a count: a coin flip per order deals each
order to Q1 or Q2 at random, 5,000 times, and the share is how often those deals make a rise as
large as the real one; the movement chance makes every quarter is the usual wobble. The same share
can also be found exactly, by listing every possible deal of the orders and counting. The chapter's rule of thumb: distrust a rate with fewer than thirty
customers behind it, since more orders from the same few customers add no new evidence. Kavya Nair,
the senior analyst on the team, reviews every line before it reaches Meera.

**Who needs the answer.** Meera decides whether acquisition budget follows the fastest riser. A rise
that chance made sends a quarter's spend to a segment that may be flat next quarter.

**The questions on the way.**

- What goes back to the app team that wants Rs 2 lakh for a city whose orders rose 60 percent?
- How far does one order moved between quarters shift a 40 percent rise on 24 orders?
- Which plan goes to Meera for a rise that stands on 9 customers?
- Which do you trust in an interview, 55 percent on 9 users or 38 percent on 2,000?
- Which chance route fits a segment on 18 orders, and which fits one on 60?
- Which line goes in the note about a 50 percent rise on 6 orders and a 30 percent rise on 300?

Every segment, count and rate in these items is invented, so each tests the habit on numbers the

### Q1. What goes back to the app team that wants Rs 2 lakh for a city whose orders rose 60 percent?

The app team reports that a new city's orders rose 60 percent, from 10 orders to 16, all from four
customers, and asks for a Rs 2 lakh launch budget. Coin flips on 26 orders make a rise that large in
about 16 of every 100 worlds. What goes back to the app team?

a) Fund it: 60 percent is the fastest rise on the page
b) Not yet: 16 in 100 is common, and four customers are too few
c) No: 16 in 100 shows the rise is chance, so the city is flat
d) Fund half of it, because chance makes the rise only 16 times in 100

### Q2. How far does one order moved between quarters shift a 40 percent rise on 24 orders?

An invented segment's orders rose from 10 in Q1 to 14 in Q2, a rise of 40 percent. If one Q2 order
had landed in Q1 instead, what would the rise have been?

a) About 18 percent
b) About 30 percent
c) About 36 percent
d) About 40 percent, unchanged

### Q3. Which plan goes to Meera for a rise that stands on 9 customers?

A segment's rise stands on 9 customers, and new customers arrive at about 2 a quarter. A paid test
aimed at new customers would bring about 25 in two weeks for Rs 3,750. Which plan goes to Meera?

a) Wait about eleven quarters for thirty customers, since paid customers are not real
b) A paid test on the 9 customers already counted, to see if they buy again
c) Wait one quarter, since the next quarter's rise will settle it either way
d) The paid test: Rs 3,750 reaches thirty customers in two weeks; waiting takes years

### Q4. Which do you trust in an interview, 55 percent on 9 users or 38 percent on 2,000?

In an interview: 55 percent on 9 users against 38 percent on 2,000. Which do you trust, and how do
you say it?

a) 55 percent, since it is the higher of the two rates
b) Neither, since two rates can only be read after a shuffle
c) 38 percent as the estimate, and 55 percent as a lead to measure
d) 38 percent, since 2,000 users make it the true rate for everyone

### Q5. Which chance route fits a segment on 18 orders, and which fits one on 60?

Kavya wants the exact chance share for two invented segments, one standing on 18 orders and one on
60. Which route fits each?

a) List every deal for both, since listing is exact
b) Flip coins for both, since flips are always enough
c) List every deal for the 18 orders, and flip coins for the 60
d) List every deal for the 60 orders, and flip coins for the 18

### Q6. Which line goes in the note about a 50 percent rise on 6 orders and a 30 percent rise on 300?

Two invented segments reach Meera's page. X rose from 6 orders to 9, which is 50 percent; Y rose from
300 orders to 390, which is 30 percent. Which line goes in the note?

a) X is the growth story, because its rise is the larger
b) Y's rise as worth testing on its count, and X's as a lead
c) Neither, since both rises could be the usual wobble
d) Both, weighted equally, as each is computed on its own orders
