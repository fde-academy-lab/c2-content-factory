# Round 1 set: real, or the usual wobble?

Fifteen minutes, mid-round, after the room has run the Retail-Plus shuffle. Seven items, each a
question a Kalpa stakeholder or Kavya would put to you. Items 2 to 5 are four p-value sentences to
judge as defensible or wrong.

Post one line, seven letters in item order, no spaces:

```
Post exactly this shape: xxxxxxx
```

---

### Q1

The head of Retail-Core says her members spent Rs 110 less each in Q2 and asks whether to worry.
Of 5,000 shuffles, 1,724 made a fall of Rs 110 or more. What do you tell her?

a) Worry, since any fall in a paying segment belongs in Monday's review
b) The fall is real, since 1,724 shuffles is a large number of worlds
c) It sits inside the usual wobble: chance makes a fall that size about a third of the time
d) Nothing yet, since a shuffle test needs ten thousand shuffles before anyone can read it at all

### Q2

A draft note reads: "p = 0.03, so there is a 3 percent chance our Retail-Plus finding is wrong."
Which verdict does Kavya give it?

a) Wrong: the share was counted in worlds where nothing changed, so it is not the chance of being wrong
b) Defensible, because 0.03 sits below the 0.05 line that most analysts use
c) Defensible if the shuffle used a fixed seed, since the share is then exact
d) Wrong only in its wording: it should say a 97 percent chance of being right, which states the same idea positively

### Q3

A second draft reads: "If the quarter made no difference, a fall this large would turn up in about
3 of every 100 shuffles." Is it defensible?

a) Wrong: it should say the drop is 97 percent certain
b) Wrong: a p-value is never stated in shuffles
c) Defensible only once a second run with another seed agrees to the third decimal place
d) Defensible: it names the share and the world it was counted in

### Q4

A third draft reads: "p = 0.03, so the Retail-Plus drop is large." Is it defensible?

a) Defensible, since a small share only ever comes from a large gap
b) Wrong: the share says how surely a gap beats chance and says nothing about its size
c) Defensible for Retail-Plus, though not for segments with fewer members than twenty-two
d) Wrong, since a share must fall below 0.01 before an analyst may call any drop large in a note to a CEO

### Q5

A fourth draft reads: "Retail-Core came back at 0.34, which proves nothing changed for Retail-Core."
Is it defensible?

a) Wrong: chance makes a gap that size often, which is different from proving nothing changed
b) Defensible, because a large share proves that the two quarters were the same
c) Defensible, as long as the shuffle ran five thousand times with one seed
d) Wrong: 0.34 means a 34 percent chance that Retail-Core really did fall, which is too much risk to leave out

### Q6

A pair shuffled the ten cards ten times, and one of the ten gaps reached the real Rs 880. What
should they do before writing any sentence about the cards?

a) Write that chance makes a gap of Rs 880 one time in ten
b) Stop, since ten cards are too few for any test at all
c) Run many more shuffles, since ten deals cannot measure a share
d) Call the gap real, since Rs 880 was bigger than almost every gap they dealt by hand on the table

### Q7

An analyst reports that 0.973 of the Retail-Plus shuffles were "at least as extreme", having counted
the shuffles whose gap was at or below the real one. What went wrong, and what should the note say?

a) Nothing went wrong: 0.973 means the drop is almost certainly real
b) The seed was wrong, so the whole run has to be repeated with a different seed before anyone reads it
c) The share should be divided by the real gap before it is read
d) The wrong tail was counted: falls at least as large are about 0.027 of the shuffles
