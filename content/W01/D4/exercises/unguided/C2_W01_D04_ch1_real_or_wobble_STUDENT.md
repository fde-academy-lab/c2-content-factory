# Is the Retail-Plus fall real, or the wobble Kalpa sees every quarter?

Chapter 1 set, six items, after chapter 1: items 1 and 2 run live in the chapter's last three
minutes; the rest are the practice lab's stretch or tonight's work.

> "One: Retail-Plus is down, smaller than first reported. Real, or the wobble we see every quarter?"
>
> Meera Raghavan, CEO, Kalpa Retail

Retail-Plus is Kalpa Retail's paid membership tier. Its 22 members bought in both Q1 and Q2, and
their delivered revenue per member, the money Kalpa kept once cancelled and returned orders are left
out, fell between the two quarters. Every quarter's numbers wobble a little by chance, so chapter 1
built a chance reference: many invented worlds in which nothing changed, and a count of how often
those worlds make a gap as large as the real one. The flip test builds the worlds from each member's
own two quarters, with a coin deciding which of the pair counts as Q1. The label shuffle, also called
the pooled shuffle, puts every value in one pool and deals it into two piles at random. The textbook
paired test is a formula for pairs, run in one library call. The share, which textbooks call the
p-value, is the fraction of chance-only worlds with a gap at least as large as the real one, counted
for falls only or for a move that large in either direction. Kavya Nair, the senior analyst on the
team, reviews every line before it reaches Meera.

**Who needs the answer.** Meera Raghavan, Kalpa Retail's CEO, decides at Monday's growth review
whether the Retail-Plus fall gets a budget of its own. A wobble read as a real fall funds a fix for
nothing, and a real fall read as a wobble lets the tier drain.

### Q1. Which chance reference fits the store team's request, and which fits the app team's?

b) Shuffle the labels for the store team, and flip each member's pair for the app team
c) Shuffle the labels for both, since each request compares two groups of numbers

### Q6. Which share does the note report when pooling and flipping disagree on a heavy-buyer tier?

An invented tier of 30 members, where heavy buyers stay heavy, fell by Rs 314 a member. Pooling the
