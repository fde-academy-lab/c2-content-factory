# The second case: marketing pushes back

Forty-five minutes, in pairs, after the break. One partner builds the answer, the other plays the
marketing lead for the last ten minutes, and then you swap roles for the reply. The notebook is
`notebooks/C2_W01_D04_ex2_second_case_STUDENT.ipynb`: eight lettered choices in four moves.

> **The client asks.** "Your segment split is slicing the data until it says what you want. Exposed
> Retail-Plus members spent Rs 4,850 in August. That is far above the Rs 3,200 our unexposed customers
> averaged. The sale works. We repeat it for Diwali."
>
> The marketing lead, Kalpa Retail, in the growth review with Meera listening

## The four moves

| Move | The question you answer | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Reproduce | Can you make marketing's Rs 4,850 and Rs 3,200 appear from the exposure table? | 8 |
| 2. Name the mismatch | Who sits in each of marketing's two groups, and what else differs between them? | 10 |
| 3. Rebuild | What do exposed and unexposed customers look like like for like, and in the same mix? | 12 |
| 4. Hold the line | What did 15 percent off cost, and what do you say to marketing in two sentences? | 15 |

## Rules for the pair

- Marketing's numbers are correct arithmetic. Your job is the comparison, never the arithmetic.
- Every average you quote carries the number of customers behind it.
- The reply offers a way to settle the question at Diwali; a reply that only says "you are wrong" has
  lost the room.
- The partner playing marketing may push once more after the reply, with any argument the table allows.
