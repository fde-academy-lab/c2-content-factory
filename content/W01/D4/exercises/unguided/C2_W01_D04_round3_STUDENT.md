the count behind a rate. Items 5 to 7 are the confounder in three Kalpa vignettes, invented for the

### Q1

Two invented segments reach Meera's page. Segment X rose from 6 orders to 9, which is 50 percent;
segment Y rose from 300 to 330, which is 10 percent. Which line goes in the note?

a) X is the growth story, since its rise is five times Y's
b) Y's rise as a finding, and X's as a lead with its count beside it
c) Neither, since both rises could be the wobble and nothing can be said about either segment
d) Both, weighted equally, since each rise was computed on its own segment's orders

### Q2

a) About 1.5 points
b) About 15 points
c) About 6.7 points
d) About 0.67 points

### Q3

In the interview: 42 percent on 12 users against 31 percent on 1,200. Which do you trust?

a) 42 percent, since it is the higher of the two rates
b) 31 percent, since 1,200 users make it the true rate for everyone
c) Neither, since the two groups were never compared with a shuffle and so cannot be read at all
d) 31 percent as the estimate, and 42 percent as a lead to measure on more users

### Q4

Below how many observations does the rule of thumb treat a rate as a lead to watch?

a) Thirty
b) Ten
c) One hundred
d) One thousand, since anything smaller moves with every new order that comes in

### Q5

Customers who installed Kalpa's app spend 40 percent more than customers who did not, and the product
team wants to push installs to lift revenue. What is the most likely confounder?

a) The app's design, which makes buying easier than the website does for most customers
b) Customers who already buy often are the ones who bother to install the app
c) The season in which the comparison happened to be made
d) The payment method the customers used

### Q6

Stores that ran a loyalty kiosk grew faster than stores that did not, and the kiosks went to the
busiest stores first. What would a fair comparison need?

a) Kiosk stores against every other store, as the first report did
b) Kiosk stores this year against the same stores last year
c) Kiosk stores against similarly busy stores without one, or kiosks placed at random
d) A shuffle test with more shuffles, which would remove the difference between busy and quiet stores

### Q7

members who had ordered in August. What does the September gap mostly measure?

a) The difference between recent buyers and everyone else
b) The coupon's effect on orders in September
c) Nothing at all, since coupons never change behaviour
d) The effect of September itself, which is a busier month than August for every customer segment
