Meera Raghavan, Kalpa Retail's CEO, asked three questions for Monday's growth review, and the
morning's chapters answered them. Retail-Plus, Kalpa's paid membership tier, fell Rs 1,110 a member
from Q1 to Q2; flips of each member's two quarters make a fall that large in about 3 of every 100
chance-only worlds and a move that large either way in about 6, and the fall is worth Rs 24,420 a
quarter, 0.19 percent of the company's delivered revenue. Student's 40 percent rise in orders stands
on fewer than thirty customers. Marketing's 6 percent lift from the monsoon sale is a blend, one
average over segments mixed together: inside Retail-Plus and inside Retail-Core, the customers who
got the sale spent 3 percent less. Chapter 5 wrote the answers as a four-part note, claim, evidence,
caveat and action, under 200 words, and audited it. The audit reads each line of a note on its own
and asks whether it could send Meera's money the wrong way; a headline note that repeated each number
the way it first appeared failed the audit in all nine of its cells. Kavya Nair, the senior analyst
on the team, runs the audit before any note reaches Meera.
