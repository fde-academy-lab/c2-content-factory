01 · The guided walk

What would chance alone produce?

Ten invented cards hold what five members spent, a Q1 card and a Q2 card each. The same members sit in both quarters, so each member's two cards stay together. If the quarter made no difference, each member's two cards could have come in either order, so the walk tosses a coin per member and asks how often a gap as large as the real one turns up. The cards are invented; every share on this walk is computed here, live, from them.

02 · The simulator

The flip machine: real, and worth acting on?

Set a true fall per member from last quarter to this one, how many members there are, how much members differ from each other, how much each member's own spend wobbles between quarters, and what acting would cost. Choose the direction the test counts before you run. The machine invents the members, measures each one's two quarters, flips each member's pair 2,000 times and gives two separate verdicts. Every member here is invented by a seeded generator. Try the big, quiet crowd to watch a fall beat the wobble while staying too small to fund; then drag how much members differ and run again. The flips hardly move, because each member's fall is measured against their own last quarter, while the pooled shuffle that ignores the pairs spreads wider.

The direction the test counts, chosen before you run

The sliders and the direction set up the next run; nothing is computed until you press Run.

This page reads a share under 1 in 100 as rare, 1 to 10 in 100 as borderline and over 10 in 100 as the usual wobble. The bands are the page's convention, set before any run; a team agrees its own before a test, and the note gives both directions whichever it chose.

03 · Experiment cards

Four experiments, one change each

Read the situation and the hypothesis, decide what you expect, then run. Every number in these four cards is invented to isolate one mechanism, and each card says so; the shares are computed on the page.

Experiment A · invented sentences

Read the share aloud

Situation. An invented drop between two quarters gives a share of 0.03 from 2,000 shuffles counting drops only, and 0.06 counting a move that large either way. Four analysts each write one sentence about it.

Hypothesis. Two of the sentences describe the share as a share of chance-only worlds, and two turn it into the chance that the finding is wrong.

Watch for. Which sentences start from “if nothing had changed”, and which quietly swap the question.

  1. There is a 3 percent chance the drop happened by chance.
  2. If nothing had changed between the quarters, a drop this large turns up in about 3 of every 100 shuffles.
  3. We are 97 percent sure the drop is real.
  4. The drop is borderline against the usual wobble, about 3 in 100 one way and 6 either way, and its size in rupees is a separate question.

What happened.

Why it matters. Meera acts on the sentence she hears, so a share read backwards turns a 3 in 100 coincidence into a 97 percent certainty that nobody measured.

The share answers “if nothing had changed, how often?”; it never gives the chance the finding is wrong.

Experiment B · invented orders

The same Rs 20 gap, more orders

Situation. Orders fall anywhere between Rs 1,000 and Rs 3,400, and last quarter's average sits exactly Rs 20 above this quarter's. The page runs 300 shuffles at 100, 1,000, 5,000 and 20,000 orders per quarter.

Hypothesis. The share falls as the orders pile up, while the Rs 20 stays Rs 20.

Watch for. The share sliding toward zero as the orders pile up, and the size of the gap at every point.

What happened.

Why it matters. With enough orders almost any gap beats the wobble, so “real” alone never tells Meera whether to spend; the rupees against the cost do.

A small share says the gap is unlikely to be the wobble; only its size in rupees says whether it matters.

Experiment C · invented coin flips

Forty percent on a handful

Situation. A rate stands on a number of orders per quarter, each from a different customer, and each order succeeds on a fair coin in both quarters, so nothing changed at all. The page runs the two quarters 2,000 times.

Hypothesis. On a handful of orders, chance alone often makes the rate rise by 40 percent or more; on hundreds, almost never.

Watch for. The right-hand column as you move the slider down toward six.

What happened.

Why it matters. A 40 percent rise sounds like a budget case, and on a few dozen orders it is the kind of jump a coin produces by itself, so the count decides whether the rise is evidence or a lead.

Count what a rate stands on, in customers as well as orders, before you read its rise: under thirty customers, call it a lead.

Experiment D · invented campaign

Every group fell, the blend rose

Situation. Big spenders who were not exposed to an invented campaign average Rs 5,000 and those exposed Rs 4,800; small spenders average Rs 1,000 not exposed and Rs 960 exposed. One in five of the not-exposed group are big spenders.

Hypothesis. If the campaign mostly reached big spenders, the exposed group's blended average rises far above the rest even though each group fell by 4 percent.

Watch for. The blend columns pulling apart while the big and small columns stay nearly level.

What happened.

Why it matters. The blended rise is a fact about who received the campaign, so crediting it to the campaign funds more of something that lowered spend in every group it touched.

Split an aggregate by segment before you credit a campaign, and name who got it.

    06 · Glossary

    The day's words, and where each first mattered

    Chance reference
    The spread of gaps chance alone produces when nothing changed, by flipping each member's pair or by shuffling labels, which the real gap is read against.Morning, chapter 1; notebook C2_W01_D04_01.
    Flip test
    For the same members measured twice: a coin per member decides which of its two quarters counts as Q1, and the share of many tosses with a gap at least as large as the real one is read.Morning, chapter 1; notebook C2_W01_D04_01.
    Label shuffle
    For two groups of different customers: shuffle the group labels across everyone, recompute the gap, repeat many times, and read the share at least as large as the real one.Morning, chapter 1, and the afternoon's chapter 6; notebooks C2_W01_D04_01 and C2_W01_D04_06.
    p-value
    That share: how often a gap this large turns up in worlds where nothing changed. It is never the chance the finding is wrong.Morning, chapter 1; notebook C2_W01_D04_01.
    One direction or both
    Whether the test counts only gaps as large in the same direction, or large gaps either way, decided before the test is run. When the question came after the gap was seen, the note gives both.Morning, chapter 1; notebook C2_W01_D04_01.
    Significant against important
    Significant means the gap beats the usual wobble; important means its size in rupees beats the cost of acting. A gap can be one without the other.Morning, chapter 2; notebook C2_W01_D04_02.
    Confidence interval
    A range of plausible sizes for the gap, named today and taught later.Morning, chapter 2; notebook C2_W01_D04_02.
    The count behind a rate
    How many orders, and how many customers, a rate stands on, said beside the rate.Morning, chapter 3; notebook C2_W01_D04_03.
    Rule of thumb of thirty
    A rate standing on fewer than about thirty customers is a lead to watch, and more orders from the same few customers add no new evidence.Morning, chapter 3; notebook C2_W01_D04_03.
    Blend
    One average taken over groups mixed together, such as everyone who got the sale, whatever their segment.Morning, chapter 4; notebook C2_W01_D04_04.
    Mix
    The share of each kind of customer inside a group. Two groups with different mixes can differ in a blend with no difference inside any segment.Morning, chapter 4; notebook C2_W01_D04_04.
    Lift
    How much more the group that got a campaign spent than the group that did not, as a share of the second. A lift is credited to the campaign only when the comparison is fair.Morning, chapter 4, and the afternoon's chapter 6; notebooks C2_W01_D04_04 and C2_W01_D04_06.
    Confounder
    Something that differs between the groups being compared and also moves the outcome, such as who received a campaign.Morning, chapter 4; notebook C2_W01_D04_04.
    Fair comparison
    A comparison of groups alike in everything except the thing being credited.Afternoon, chapter 6; notebook C2_W01_D04_06.
    Held-back group
    Customers kept out of a campaign on purpose, chosen at random, so the rest can be compared with them fairly.Afternoon, chapter 6; notebook C2_W01_D04_06.
    Placebo test
    The same test run on months with no campaign, to see what an ordinary month does; if quiet months show gaps as large, the campaign month carries no sign of the campaign.Afternoon, chapter 6; notebook C2_W01_D04_06.
    Simpson's reversal
    Every group moves one way while the blended total moves the other, because the mix of groups changed.Morning, chapter 4; notebook C2_W01_D04_04.

    Claim, evidence, caveat and action, where “not yet” is an answer and the action says what data would settle it.Morning, chapter 5; notebook C2_W01_D04_05.
