## What does the [D] transcript say?

Used in the fallback (section 4, 25 minutes): read in pairs, then scored.

**The question as the interviewer says it.** "A category manager is sure the click-through fell
because shoppers buy differently after the monsoon, and wants a two-row redesign that sits higher on
the page, tested next week. You think the ranking caused the fall. What do you say?"

**The clarifying questions a strong candidate asks.** "Which window does the 6.1 percent cover, and
which the 3.8?" "Did anything else ship on 7 September besides the ranking?" "Is the redesign meant
to replace the ranking fix or to run beside it?"

**The structure.** The four moves: restate, answer from the evidence, concede what is true, say what
would change your mind; then one chart and one next step.

**The trap the interviewer fishes for.** Winning the argument: dismissing the manager, or agreeing
to the redesign to keep the peace.

| | Weak answer | Hire-grade answer |
|---|---|---|
| Restate | "That is just a guess." | "You think habits changed after the monsoon, and that a new layout would win the clicks back." |
| Answer | "The data says the ranking is the problem." | "The fall sits in milk's slots: milk clicked at 1.2 percent, and every other suggestion at 6.4 percent, up from 6.2." |
| Concede | Nothing conceded. | "Position matters: slot 1 draws 8.2 percent against 5.4 for slot 3, so a layout is worth its own test." |
| What would change it | Not said. | "If the other slots had fallen with milk's, the habits story would lead." |
| Next step | "Let us not redesign anything." | "Re-rank first; test the layout separately in two zones, so each change is measured on its own." |

**Three follow-ups and the line a hire-grade answer gives each.**

| Follow-up | The line |
|---|---|
| "How do you know the monsoon did nothing?" | "Non-milk suggestions clicked better after the change, 6.4 against 6.2; changed habits would have hit every slot." |
| "Why not ship both changes together and save a week?" | "Then neither can be credited; a two-zone test of the layout alone tells us what it adds." |
| "The manager outranks you. Now what?" | "I restate their goal, show the one chart of click-through by slot, and ask for the separate test." |
