# Did your slice give the numbers it should, and did your paragraph say what they mean?

The self-check for tonight's take-home: the strip's ranking rerun for one zone or one time of day on
Swiggy Instamart's Pune carts. Illustrative case: the company is real, the situation and the data are
synthetic, built for this class. Open this after your notebook runs.

**Who needs the answer.** You, to know whether your numbers and your call would survive the growth
lead's first question.

**The questions on the way.**

- Can you say what a slice does, in plain words, before any number?
- Did your slice give these counts and lifts?
- Which pairs should have moved, and why?
- Does your paragraph carry the four things a strong one carries?

## Can you say it in plain words first?

Say each of these aloud, in one sentence, before reading on:

1. A slice holds fewer carts, so every pair rests on fewer of them, and a lift on a few carts can move
   by luck.
2. A floor written as 1 percent of the slice is a smaller count than 1 percent of the city, so it
   admits pairs on thinner ground.
3. A pair that leads the city and leads the slice is a pair the strip can trust in that slice.

If any sentence would not come out, re-read chapter 3's watch-list slide and notebook 03's floor
cell before checking numbers.

## Which pairs should have moved, and why?

- In Kothrud in the evening and Baner in the morning the city's pairs lead the slice, in a slightly
  different order. The strip holds.
- In Hinjewadi at night, the smallest slice, coriander with ghee rests on 8 carts and pav with
  potatoes on 10, and both slip in above bread with butter; a third pair, on 7 carts, sits fifth, just
  below them. A floor of 1 percent of 649 carts lets in any pair on 7 carts or more, which is the thin
  ground the city's floor of 400 carts keeps out.
- In Viman Nagar in week three, green chillies with spinach rests on 13 carts and slips in second.
  That week is also the curd stock-out: curd sits in 1 percent of the slice's carts against 26 to 30
  percent in the zone's other weeks, so any ranking by count in this slice demotes curd for a reason
  that has nothing to do with shoppers.

## Does your paragraph carry the four things?

A strong paragraph names numbers from your slice, names the pair it distrusts with the carts under
it, says what would change its mind, and ends on one call. A weak one quotes the city's numbers, calls
every pair above the floor a winner, or says "the data is inconclusive" without saying which count
would settle it.
