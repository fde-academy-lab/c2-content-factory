# Which answers hold in the drill on one pair's three numbers, and why?

Answers: 1b 2c

The drill read coriander and green chillies from four counts: 6,000 carts with coriander, 3,200 with
green chillies, 1,440 with both, 40,000 in all. Support is 3.6 percent. Confidence is 0.24 from
coriander and 0.45 from green chillies. Chance alone puts the pair in 480 carts, so lift is 3.00.

**Who needs the answer.** You, to check your two letters. The growth lead will put this pair in front
of the category manager, and a misread count either costs a slot or loses one that pays.

**The questions on the way.**

- What does the drill test?
- Why is each key right, and each other letter wrong?
- Which item is worth arguing about?
- Where does the pattern live at work?

## What does the drill test?

It tests the three numbers from four counts, read in both directions, and one design habit: a number
that matters is checked by a second route that reaches it from different counts.

## Why is each key right, and each other letter wrong?

| Item | Key | Why it holds | Why the others fail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1, the three numbers | b | Support is 1,440 of 40,000, 3.6 percent; 1,440 of the 3,200 chilli carts is 0.45; expected is 40,000 x 0.15 x 0.08 = 480, and 1,440 / 480 = 3.00, so the pair turns up three times as often as chance would put it | a reads the confidence from the other side, 1,440 of 6,000; c takes coriander's own share, 6,000 of 40,000, for the support; d divides one confidence by the other, 0.45 / 0.24 = 1.88, which is no lift |
| Q2, the second route | c | Lift is also confidence over the partner's share: 0.45 / 0.15 = 3.00 | a is a ratio of confidences; b is support over coriander's share, which gives the confidence 0.24; d is the confidence from chillies, 0.45 |

## Which item is worth arguing about?

Q1's option d, 0.45 / 0.24 = 1.88. It looks like a measure of pull because it compares the two
directions. It compares the two items' sizes instead: green chillies are rarer, so more of their
carts hold coriander than the reverse. Lift reads the same from both sides, 3.00, which is why it is
the number to rank by.

## Where does the pattern live at work?

A category analyst reads a pairs table this way before any bundle is priced, and an interviewer asks
for the second route because a number with one route has never been checked.
