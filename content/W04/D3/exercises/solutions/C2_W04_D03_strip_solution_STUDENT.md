## What does the set test?

It tests the slot's rupee value built from four numbers, the month figure on the right share of
slots, a caveat that changes the quoted number and keeps the decision, an answer to a stakeholder who
is partly right, and the same base-rate idea inside an AI system's evaluation.

## Which item is worth arguing about?

Q1's option a, Rs 12.6 lakh. It is a correct product of three numbers, Rs 14,040 x 3 slots x 30 days,
and it answers a different question: what the strip would gain if milk had filled every slot. The log
shows milk on half the slot impressions, so the recoverable gain is half of it. An interviewer who
hears Rs 12.6 lakh asks one question, "how many slots did milk hold?", and the answer decides
whether the figure survives finance.

## Where does the pattern live at work?

Every placement a growth team sells internally, from a strip slot to a homepage banner, is priced this
way: views, click-through, conversion, margin, then the cost line. Swiggy's own shareholder letter
describes its Maxxsaver discount as margin returned to the user, which is the cost line a rupee case
has to carry (checked 12 October 2026). AI companies score recommendation models against a
popularity baseline for the same reason the strip should have been: a model that suggests popular
items looks accurate and changes nothing.
