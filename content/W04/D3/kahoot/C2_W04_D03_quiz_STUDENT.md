## Q1. Which count answers "do these two turn up together more than chance would put them?"
*Tests: Support, confidence and lift, derived by counting: which count answers which question.*

- Support, the share of carts holding both items
- Confidence, the share of one item's carts that also hold the other item
- Lift, the carts seen holding both over the carts chance gives  <- correct
- The pair count, the number of carts that hold both of the items

## Q3. An item sits in 75 percent of carts, and a partner reads confidence 0.76 toward it and lift 1.01: promote the pair?
*Tests: The confidence trap; lift near one, on numbers that are not the day's.*

- Promote it, since a confidence of 0.76 is a strong bundle
- Promote it to heavy buyers only, who buy both more often
- Wait a month, and promote it if the confidence keeps rising
- Drop it as a bundle, since a lift of 1.01 says chance  <- correct

---

## Q4. Ice cream and chocolate sauce read lift 4.60 on 100 carts, support 0.25 percent: what stops a slot?
*Tests: The support floor and the watch list.*

- The lift is too high to be anything but an error in the data
- It shows beside ice cream only, too rarely to earn a slot  <- correct
- Ice cream sells only in the summer months, so it is seasonal
- Chocolate sauce carries the higher margin of the two items

---

## Q5. A promotion's value in rupees is what times what, less what?
*Tests: Cross-sell and upsell reasoning; expected value in rupees: the cost line.*

- Total sales times the margin, less the slot's own cost
- Clicks times the selling price, less the returns that follow the sale
- Incremental adds times the margin, less the discount handed back  <- correct
- Impressions times the confidence, less the item's own base rate in carts

## Q6. An objection to your number is partly right: what are the four moves of the answer?
*Tests: Tuesday's four moves, the return question, on today's objection.*

- Restate it, answer from the evidence, concede what is true, say what would change your mind  <- correct
- Thank the objector, explain the method again, show more data, then ask the room for approval
- Concede the point, rework the slide, present it again, then close the meeting
- Restate it, rebut every point it makes, escalate to the city head, then decide on your own

## Q8. Cart C033861 holds eggs, ice cream and chocolate sauce: how many pairs does it add to the count?
*Tests: Basket analysis on the order items: one cart's items become pairs.*

- 3, one for each two of its items  <- correct
- 6, each pair counted in both orders of its names
- 1, the one pair that beats chance
- 9, every item paired with every item, itself included
