# Can you make the day's eight calls about cohorts and funnels in twenty seconds each?

The quiz has eight items, ungraded and scored on correctness and speed together. Seven are today's,
at least one on each of the day's five topics, and one is the return question from Wednesday. Each
item names the topic and what it tests, so an item dropped for time says what was lost. The cohorts
in item 3 are invented for the quiz.

Today's case: Zomato Gold in Chennai, where the membership dashboard read month-two retention flat at
38 percent while every signup cohort fell, and members' add-to-cart to order on Android fell from 58
to 44 percent after a release on 14 September. A member with no order in a month is not retained that
month, and she is still a member. Illustrative case: the company is real, the situation
and the data are synthetic, built for this class.

**Who needs the answer.** The trainer needs it to close the day, and every learner needs it before
tonight's take-home. An item most of the room misses is a call likely to reach the membership lead
wrong on Friday, so the trainer says it again before the room leaves.

**The questions on the way.**

- What does a cohort fix that a blended average cannot?

- What does a curve's floor say about the business?
- How can you tell a step's number is an artefact?
- What was Wednesday's verdict on bread and milk?

## Q1. What does a cohort fix that a blended retention number cannot?
*Tests: Cohort construction, what a cohort is for.*

- The month each member joined, so members of different ages stop mixing  <- correct
- The number of members, so that every month counts the same people
- The calendar month, so that every member is read in one and the same month
- The order value, so that big and small orders count the same

---

## Q2. Android members: add-to-cart to checkout start held at 80; checkout start to order fell 72.5 to 55. Which step owns the loss?
*Tests: Funnel stages, drop-off, denominators, reading the step that changed, inside the checkout.*

- Add-to-cart to checkout start, since it is where checkout begins
- Search to menu view, since it reads 118 percent
- Checkout start to order placed  <- correct
- Menu view to add-to-cart, since at 34 percent it is the lowest

---

## Q3. Two old cohorts each fell five points from May to June, yet blended retention read 46 percent both months. How?
*Tests: Blended numbers against cohort truth, the blend held up by a new cohort, on numbers that are not the case's.*

- The data must be wrong, since a blend moves whenever its cohorts move
- Members who had left came back in June and filled the gap
- The blend gives the older cohorts more weight than the new one
- A large new cohort joined at a high early rate and held the average  <- correct

---

## Q4. Each Gold cohort drops to about 38.5 percent in month two, then slides 0.3 points a month, a floor that erodes slowly. What does that floor mean for the business?
*Tests: Retention curves: shape and floor, what a curve's floor means for the business.*

- About 38 in 100 members make ordering a habit and carry repeat orders  <- correct
- The dashboard's target is met, so the membership needs no further work
- About 62 in 100 members cancel Gold in their second month
- The curve has stopped falling, so no cohort can drop any further

---

## Q5. Search to menu view reads 118 percent per session. How can you tell it is an artefact, a number made by the way the count is taken?
*Tests: Funnel stages, drop-off, denominators, the skippable step.*

- Members search more than once in a session, so searches are counted twice
- Menus also open from the home carousel, with no search before them  <- correct
- It counts members on top and sessions underneath, two units in one rate
- A rate above 100 is rounding, since each count was rounded first

---

## Q6. Wednesday: bread to toned milk read confidence 0.74 and lift 1.04 on Instamart's Pune carts. The verdict?
*Tests: Wednesday's lift near one, the return question, outside today's five topics.*

- A strong pair, since three in four bread carts hold milk
- Promote it to heavy buyers, who buy both items most often
- Chance: milk is in 71 percent of all carts anyway  <- correct
- Too few carts to say anything about the pair yet

## Q7. Menu view to add-to-cart reads 34 percent per session and 61 percent per member. Which number is wrong?
*Tests: Funnel stages, drop-off, denominators, two units, two questions.*

- The 34, since a member is what the business keeps
- Neither: each answers its own question, with its unit named  <- correct
- The 61, since a funnel is always counted in sessions
- Both, since one step can only ever have a single correct rate

---

## Q8. "Retention dropped; walk me through it." What comes first?
*Tests: The fix-first decision and its evidence, the order of the investigation, the interview staple.*

- Name the likeliest cause, then look for the data that supports it
- Split by platform, since a release is the most common cause
- Build the cohort table, since a cohort shows every cause at once
- Check the count is right, then what the number counts  <- correct
