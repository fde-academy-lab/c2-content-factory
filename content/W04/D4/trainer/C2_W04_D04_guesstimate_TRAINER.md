# Guesstimate of the day: how many people ride the Delhi Metro on a weekday?

**TRAINER ONLY.** Five minutes, in the interview room slot or as the lab's warm-up. The room
estimates first; the anchor is revealed last. The catch is the day's lesson in another city: a number
is only as good as its unit. People, trips from gate to gate, and DMRC's passenger journeys, which
count each line a rider uses, are three different counts of one crowd.

## How does a strong candidate run it?

| Step | What they say | The numbers |
|---|---|---|
| Clarify | "People, trips or line uses? I'll count people who ride at least once on an ordinary weekday, then turn that into trips and then into DMRC's unit to check." | |
| Map | "People in the area the metro serves, times the share who ride on a weekday." | |
| Assume, with ranges | "About 2 crore people within reach, from 1.5 to 2.5 crore; 15 percent ride on a weekday, from 10 to 20. The share is the input I'm least sure of, so it carries the widest range." | Every input is an assumption, said as one |
| Calculate | "2 crore x 0.15 is about 30 lakh riders. Most ride there and back, so about 60 lakh trips." | About 15 lakh to 50 lakh riders across the ranges, so 30 lakh to 1 crore trips |
| Convert | "DMRC counts a passenger journey for each line used. If a quarter of trips change lines once (assumed), 60 lakh trips is about 75 lakh line uses." | Trips x 1.25, an assumption to state |
| Validate | "DMRC's current daily average is 64.06 lakh journeys; if a quarter of trips change lines once, my own assumption, that is about 51 lakh trips. A daily average usually takes in weekends, which DMRC's figure does not say, and a weekday sits above it, so my 60 lakh trips sits inside my range of 30 lakh to 1 crore. The check leans on my 1.25, so it tests the order of size, not the digits." | The Tribune, 9 August 2026, citing DMRC: "an average of 64.06 lakh journeys currently" (checked 12 October 2026) |
| Cross-check | "From the supply side: trains a day, times trips each train makes, times the people who board along a run, which is more than a train holds at one moment, since riders get on and off between stations; I'd size each with a stated assumption." | Named, never computed with unverified numbers |
| The catch | "DMRC counts a passenger journey for each line a rider uses, so a trip with one change counts twice. The record 82,44,903 boardings on 17 September 2026, a bus-strike day, beat the earlier record of more than 81 lakh passenger journeys, which suggests one count under two names; that is my reading, and no source sets the two side by side. Validating trips against line uses, or a weekday against a strike day, compares two different things." | Republic World, 18 September 2026, quoting DMRC's post; The Tribune; Deccan Herald (PTI), 30 August 2023, and PTI via LatestLY, 2018, for the unit (each checked 12 October 2026) |

## What does each wrong turn look like, and what do you say?

| The wrong turn | What to say |
|---|---|
| Starts from trains and stations with no load factor | "How full is a train at noon against at 9? Which number would you check first?" |
| Validates people against journeys | "How many trips does a commuter make in a day, and how many lines does each trip use?" |
| Validates trips against DMRC's count with no conversion | "What does DMRC count when a rider changes lines?" |
| Claims precision, "6 percent low" | "Your inputs ranged from 15 to 50 lakh riders: what precision can the answer carry?" |
| Gives one number with no range | "Which input are you least sure of, and what happens to the answer at its ends?" |
| Treats the record day as an ordinary weekday | "What happened on 17 September? A bus strike sent riders to the metro." |
