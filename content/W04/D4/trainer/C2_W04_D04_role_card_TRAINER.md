# Role card: the app team's lead who says the checkout is fine

**TRAINER ONLY.** The TA plays this role in the afternoon's unguided block (half two, S39), and the
trainer plays it in the fallback block's last part (S9 of the fallback deck). Read it once before
the session; say the lines in your own voice.

Zomato Gold, Chennai. Illustrative case: the company is real, the situation and the data are
synthetic, built for this class.

## Who is the app team's lead, and what do they want?

The app team's lead shipped the new Android checkout on Monday 14 September. Their team's dashboard
watches the step they own most closely, carts that start checkout, and it reads 80 percent before
the release and 80 percent after it, on every platform. They want the room to stop blaming the
release and look at the membership: "the sale members never meant to stay".

**Partly right, on purpose.** Starting checkout did not break, and the sale-week members are weak.
What they miss is the next step: once checkout has started, members on Android complete the order
55 percent of the time against 72.5 before the release, while on that same step members on iOS went
from 71 to 70 and non-members on Android from 65 to 64.

**The chart they bring.** Carts that start checkout, by platform, from 1 August to 30 September with
14 September marked: a flat line at 80 percent on every platform, for members and non-members alike.

## What do you say, and when?

| Moment | Line to say |
|---|---|
| Opening, to the whole room | "Carts that start checkout held at 80 percent on every platform, before and after. Our flow works." |
| To a group that quotes 58 to 44 | "Add-to-cart to order is two steps. Ours, starting checkout, did not move. Pick the right step." |
| To a group that blames the sale cohort | "Exactly. Those members came for a discount. Fix the membership, not my checkout." |
| To a group that offers one overall number | "Overall for whom? My chart has every platform on it." |
| To a group that concedes everything | "So the release is fine? Then why did you blame it this morning?" |

## What answer should move you, and what should not?

Concede only to an answer that makes Tuesday's four moves in order:

1. **Restate** until you agree: "You're saying starting checkout held on every platform, so the new
   flow works."
2. **Answer from the evidence:** completing the order once checkout started fell from 72.5 to 55
   percent for members on Android, from the release week, while starts held at 80.
3. **Concede what is true:** the chart is right about starting checkout, and the sale-week members
   are weak, which is a separate problem with its own fix.
4. **Say what would change their mind:** if the order records showed orders holding while the event
   log fell, the fall would be in the counting; and if members who failed came back and ordered later,
   the release would cost a delay rather than orders. Neither holds: the records match the log, and
   retries within a day did not rise.

Do not move for an answer that calls the chart wrong, that quotes the overall fall from 58 to 44
without the step inside it, or that blames the release with no comparator.

## How do you close the exchange?

Say "Fine. Show me the member checkout on Android, by week, on Friday." to the group whose answer made
all four moves, and "I am not convinced yet." to every other group, naming the move it skipped.
