# Which answers hold in the drill on cohort construction, and why?

Answers: 1b 2c 3a

The drill counted the invented gym's register by age. January's ten joiners kept 8, 6, 5, 5 and 5 at
ages one to five, and February's kept 7, 5, 3 and 3. The table that answers "which month's joiners are
weakest" puts joining months down the side and ages across, every cell over the whole batch, and
compares the batches at the same age.

**Who needs the answer.** You, to check your three letters. The gym's owner sends one retention offer
to whichever batch the table says is weakest.

**The questions on the way.**

- What does the drill test?
- Why is each key right, and each other letter wrong?
- Which item is worth arguing about?
- Where does the pattern live at work?

## What does the drill test?

It tests the three moves of cohort construction: fix each member's joining month, count each later
month as an age with the joining month left out, and keep the whole batch under every cell, so that
batches can be compared at the same age.

## Why is each key right, and each other letter wrong?

| Item | Key | Why it holds | Why the others fail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1, January's row | b | 8, 6, 5, 5 and 5 of 10, read from age one to age five | a reads the row backwards, from June to February; c divides each month by those who visited the month before (6 of 8, 5 of 6, 5 of 5), so the row climbs back to 100; d counts the joining month as age one, though everyone visits the month they join |
| Q2, February's row | c | 5, 3 and 3 of 10 at ages two to four | a starts the row again at age one; b divides each month by the month before (5 of 7, 3 of 5, 3 of 3); d is March's row, which starts a month later |
| Q3, the first table | a | Joining months as rows and ages as columns keep each batch's members fixed, and the age-one column compares all three at the same age: 80, 70 and 60 percent | b mixes three ages in one calendar column, which is why April crowned March; c ranks members, and a member who joined earlier has had more months to visit; d is one blend over every batch and names none of them |

## Which item is worth arguing about?

Q3's option b. April's column is the table the owner already has, and it shows March ahead, 6 of 10
against 5 and 5. It is a true count of April, and it answers the wrong question: January's members
were at age three in April, February's at age two and March's at age one. A batch at its first month
always looks strong, so a calendar column rewards the newest batch. Read at the same age, March is the
weakest.

## Where does the pattern live at work?

Every subscription business, from a gym chain to a food-delivery membership, keeps a cohort table with
joining month on the rows and age on the columns. The first question a reviewer asks of any retention
claim is which members sit under the number, and from which month.
