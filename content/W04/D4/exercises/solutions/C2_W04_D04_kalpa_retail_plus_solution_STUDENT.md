# Which answers hold in the Kalpa Retail-Plus set, and what does the second page say?

Answers: 1d 2a 3c 4b

The set moved the afternoon's call to Kalpa Retail's Retail-Plus tier. The first table that answers
"everyone, or the monsoon-sale members" is the cohort table with each cohort's size. After the lab,
the counts say every cohort fell with age, the August cohort started lowest in its month one, and
blended retention sat near 41 percent all quarter because of the mix. Add-to-cart to checkout fell
from 10 of 12 to 7 of 11 inside the auto-reorder window, about 2 checkouts in three weeks: a fall worth
checking, on counts too thin to settle it alone.

**Who needs the answer.** You, to check your four letters and your page. The head of Retail-Plus
decides on Friday whether to chase the monsoon-sale members or send the app team back to the
checkout.

**The questions on the way.**

- What does the set test?
- What does a strong second page say?
- Why is each key right, and each other letter wrong?
- Which item is worth arguing about?
- Where does the pattern live at work?

## What does the set test?

It tests the day's method on the client's own data, where the counts are small: the cohort table as
the first answer to "who", a headline that names both problems the blend hides, a fall sized in
checkouts with its doubt stated, and a call that orders the work.

## What does a strong second page say?

1. **The call.** Ask the app team to check the checkout step inside the auto-reorder window first,
   and confirm the fall on the next three weeks; judge the monsoon-sale cohort on its own curve.
2. **The evidence.** Add-to-cart to checkout per session: 28 of 34 before 20 August, 10 of 12 from
   20 August to 9 September, 7 of 11 from 10 to 30 September. Every cohort's active share falls with
   age, and the August cohort, 32 members, was active at 26 of 32 in its month one against 13 of 14
   and 12 of 13 for the July and June cohorts.
3. **The cost, with its doubt.** About 2 checkouts in three weeks at the earlier rate; 11 carts is too
   few to call the size, so the page says "about 2" and "to confirm".
4. **The objection.** The app team says the reorder feature has been fixed and the blended number has
   not moved. The blend did not move because every cohort fell while the large August cohort joined:
   30 of 74, 36 of 88 and 49 of 120. The step to look at is checkout inside the window.
5. **What would change it.** If the next three weeks' carts check out at the earlier rate, the window's
   fall was noise, and the monsoon cohort's weak month one becomes the first job.

Every rate names its unit: September's checkout is 7.8 percent of sessions and 24.5 percent of
active members, and the page says which one it uses.

## Why is each key right, and each other letter wrong?

| Item | Key | Why it holds | Why the others fail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1, the first table | d | Fixing each member's joining month separates "every cohort fell" from "one cohort is weak", and the sizes show how much each weighs in the blend | a is the number that hid the fall; b answers "where in the app", the second question; c averages over every cohort and cannot tell the two readings apart |
| Q2, the headline | a | Every cohort's share falls with age, the August cohort's month one is the lowest, and 40.5 to 40.9 percent is the mix holding the blend up | b reports the blend that hides both; c is false, since the cohorts fall month by month with age, never all at once in September; d is false, since every cohort falls |
| Q3, the size and its doubt | c | At 10 in 12, the 11 window carts would give about 9 checkouts; 7 came, so about 2 are missing, and 11 carts is too few to settle a fall on its own | a counts the window carts that never checked out, which include the ones the earlier rate also loses; b is false, since the gap is about 2 sessions; d reads a fall in points as a count |
| Q4, the call | b | The step fell inside the window, and the app team can check it; the counts are thin, so it is confirmed before anything is rebuilt; the cohort gets its own curve | a chases a cohort first while a step may still be losing checkouts; c reports the blend that hid both problems; d rebuilds a step members are allowed to skip |

## Which item is worth arguing about?

Q4's option a. The monsoon-sale cohort is the largest and the weakest in month one, and a group can
argue that it is the bigger problem in members. The counts do not yet say the checkout step's fall
is real, so a group that puts the cohort first on the grounds that 11 carts settle nothing has a
defensible call. What it cannot do is skip the confirmation: the step goes on the page either way,
with the three weeks that would settle it.

## Where does the pattern live at work?

A client's own data is usually smaller than a case study's, and a step can fall by two events. The
habit that travels is to state the size in events with its count beside it, and to name the check
that would settle it, before anyone rebuilds anything.
