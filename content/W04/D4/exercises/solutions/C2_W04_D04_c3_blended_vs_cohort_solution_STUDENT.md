# Which answers hold in the drill on blended numbers against cohort truth, and why?

Answers: 1d 2b 3c

The drill read an invented gym's dashboard over three cohorts. The first month counts X and Y, 240 of
500, 48.0 percent; the second counts all three, 478 of 1,000, 47.8 percent. X fell from 60 to 55 and
Y from 40 to 36, and the number barely moved because Z joined 500 strong at 52. The plain mean misses
the dashboard because it gives every cohort one vote whatever its size, and the proof is the rebuild:
each cohort's rate weighted by its size has to land on the direct count.

**Who needs the answer.** You, to check your three letters. The owner reads one number a month, and
a flat one hides two falling cohorts.

**The questions on the way.**

- What does the drill test?
- Why is each key right, and each other letter wrong?
- Which item is worth arguing about?
- Where does the pattern live at work?

## What does the drill test?

It tests the one idea under every blended number: a rate over several cohorts is their average
weighted by size, so it moves when the mix moves as well as when behaviour does, and it is believed
only once the rebuild from its cohorts equals the direct count.

## Why is each key right, and each other letter wrong?

| Item | Key | Why it holds | Why the others fail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1, the two readings | d | 240 of 500, then 478 of 1,000 | a averages the rates with one vote each, 50.0 and 47.7; b leaves Z out of the second month, though the dashboard counts it from then on, 218 of 500; c reads one cohort, Y, and calls it the dashboard |
| Q2, the plain mean | b | (60 + 40) ÷ 2 gives 200 members and 300 members one vote each; weighted by size, (120 + 120) ÷ 500 is 48.0 | a is wrong, since both rates already divide by every member of the cohort; c is wrong, since no rounding moves 48.0 to 50; d is wrong, since Z joined in the first month and is counted only from the second |
| Q3, the second route | c | 200 × 0.55 + 300 × 0.36 + 500 × 0.52 = 478, of 1,000, the same as the direct count | a is the plain mean, which agrees here only by luck of the sizes, 47.7, and missed by two points in the first month; b checks one blend against another and proves neither; d reads one cohort |

## Which item is worth arguing about?

Q3's option a. In the second month the plain mean lands at 47.7, a tenth from the dashboard, so it
looks like a check that passed. It passed by accident: in the first month the same check missed by
two points, 50.0 against 48.0. A check that agrees only when the cohorts happen to be similar in size
proves nothing. The weighted rebuild equals the direct count by arithmetic, so when it misses, a
cohort has been left out or counted twice.

## Where does the pattern live at work?

Every monthly active-user number, retention headline and conversion rate on a dashboard is a blend
over cohorts of different sizes and ages. When a company's user base grows fast, the new users' high
early rates can hold the blend up while each older cohort declines, which is why reviewers ask for
the number by cohort before they accept "flat".
