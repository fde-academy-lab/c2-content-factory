## Where does a curve stop falling?

This comes up at work whenever a team plans for its regular customers: the floor of the curve is the
share that stays.

### Q1. Where is January's floor, and from which age is it read?

*Level L2 · asked in every analytics screen.* Worked: January reads 80, 60, 50, 50 and 50. Ages three
and four read the same, so the floor is called: 50 percent, the five of ten who made visiting a
habit, first read at age three. Which line matches the working?

a) 80 percent at age one, its highest point
b) 30 percent, February's floor, since floors match across batches
c) 50 percent, first read at age three
d) 60 percent at age two, just after the big drop

### Q2. Read fairly, in which order do the three batches fall?

*Level L2 · asked in product-company screens.* Half worked: a fair comparison reads every batch at an
age all three have lived. At age two, January reads 60 and February 50. Read March at the same age.
Which order holds?

a) January, February, March: 60, 50 and 40 at age two
b) March, then January and February, as April's row ranks them
c) All three tie, since each lost 20 points between ages one and two
d) March, February, January, since the newest has had least time to leave
