# What does each step of a college's admissions funnel read, and which step owns the loss?

The same college as SECTION 4: one programme's admissions, last cycle and this cycle, counted in
applications, and a sister programme whose entrance test is optional. SECTION 4 read the funnel on
the slides, and notebook 04 read it in code. The college and every number on this page are invented
for practice.

This is a drill inside SECTION 4, on paper, with no code, in about five minutes.

**How to read an item.** Its level runs from L1, a count by hand, to L6, the call an interviewer asks for; "a design call" asks you to choose a way and say why. "Screens" are interviews, and a GCC is a global capability centre, a multinational company's own technology and analytics centre in India.

**Who needs the answer.** The dean, who has one admissions team for one fix this year. A step blamed
because it is low sends the team to a step that works.

## What does each step read, and in which unit?

This comes up at work when any team brings a funnel: each rate divides by the step just before it,
and the chart names whether it counts applications or people.

### Q1. What do this cycle's four step rates and its overall conversion read?

*Level L1 · asked in every analytics screen.* Worked: 800 of 1,000 sat the test, 80 percent; 400 of
800 were shortlisted, 50; 300 of 400 accepted, 75; 240 of 300 joined, 80. The overall divides the last
step by the first: 240 of 1,000, 24 percent, and the step rates multiply to it. Which line matches the
working?

a) 80, 40, 30 and 24 percent, each over the 1,000 applications
b) 20, 50, 25 and 20 percent, with 76 percent overall
c) 80, 50, 75 and 80 percent, with 24 percent overall
d) 80, 50, 75 and 80 percent, with 80 percent overall

### Q2. Counted per applicant, what does joined over the top of the funnel read, and what must the chart say?

*Level L3 · asked in product-company screens.* Half worked: per application it is 240 of 1,000, 24
percent. The same 240 joiners came from 900 applicants. Which line holds?

a) 24 percent, since the joiners are the same 240
b) Under 24 percent, since an applicant can fail twice
c) 26.7 percent, so the per-application 24 was wrong
d) 26.7 percent, with the unit named on the chart

## Which step owns the loss?

This comes up at work when an owning team is asked to fix its step: the step that moved against its
own past owns the loss, and the lowest step may be low by design.

### Q3. The dean's committee proposes to redesign shortlisting, since it loses half the applications. Which reply holds?

*Level L3, a design call · asked in GCC analytics screens.*

a) Agree: at 50 percent, shortlisting is the lowest step and loses the most
b) Decline: shortlisting read 50 in both cycles, and acceptance fell 90 to 75
c) Agree: lifting the lowest step lifts every step after it, joining included
d) Decline: the test comes first, so its 200 no-shows are where the loss starts

## What does a step above 100 percent say?

This comes up at work when a step on a dashboard reads above 100 percent: the step is one people can
skip, and the number is made by the way it is counted.
