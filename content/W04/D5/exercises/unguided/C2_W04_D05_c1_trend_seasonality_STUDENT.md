## Which comparison reads growth?

A forecaster reads the beat before the climb, because a beat read as growth moves every later number.

### Q1. The mess (invented) used 25 kg of rice on Monday and 40 kg on Sunday: which comparison tells you whether it is growing?

*Level L1 · the chapter's live check.*

a) Sunday's 40 kg against Monday's 25 kg, the latest two days
b) Sunday against the average weekday of the same week
c) Monday against the Saturday just before, its neighbour
d) The same day a week back, or the same week a year back

## How long is the season, and where is the climb?

Sixteen weeks of rice at the mess (invented), in kg, week by week:

| Week | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rice, kg | 190 | 186 | 194 | 238 | 192 | 188 | 196 | 240 |

| Week | 9 | 10 | 11 | 12 | 13 | 14 | 15 | 16 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rice, kg | 194 | 190 | 198 | 242 | 196 | 192 | 200 | 244 |

### Q2. How long is the season in the sixteen weeks, and what repeats?

*Level L2 · asked in analytics screens.*

a) Four weeks: every fourth week, a guest dinner, runs about 48 kg higher
b) Seven days: Sunday's biryani lifts every week by the same amount
c) Sixteen weeks: the series rises once, to 244 kg in week 16
d) There is no season: the weeks wobble around a level near 200 kg, nothing more

### Q3. The mess manager (invented) plans next term's rice and asks whether the mess is growing: which reading of the sixteen weeks answers it?

*Level L3, a design call · asked in product-company screens.*

a) Week 4 against week 3, the two latest neighbours of that cycle: 238 against 194 kg
b) Each guest week against the guest week before it: 238, 240, 242, 244, a climb of 2 kg a cycle
c) The mean of all sixteen weeks against week 1 alone, since the mean smooths the guest dinners away
d) The largest week against the smallest week, since together they span the whole range
