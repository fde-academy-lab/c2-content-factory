## What does the set test?

It tests chapter 3's four habits: count the size and the lean separately, hide the latest periods as
far ahead as the order reaches, score in the unit the cost is in, and keep MAPE for comparing sizes.

## Why is each key right, and each other letter wrong?

| Item | Key | Why it holds | Why the others fail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1, the five misses | a | The sizes 12, 8, 5, 15 and 6 add to 46, and 46 over 5 is 9.2 kg; the signed misses add to 0, so no lean | b reads two negatives as a lean, though the signed misses cancel; c averages the signed misses, which hides the size; d is the sum before dividing |
| Q2, rules P and Q | d | Both miss by 10 kg on each day, so both MAEs are 10; P always over, Q once over and once short, and a kilogram short costs differently from a kilogram thrown away | a and b judge by direction alone; c is untrue, since MAE drops the signs |
| Q3, which days to hide | c | The order reaches seven days ahead, so the last seven are hidden and forecast from the day before them | a lets a rule borrow days that come after the one it forecasts; b picks days by their result; d hides fewer days than the order reaches |
| Q4, which score for whom | b | The manager orders in kg and pays in rupees; a percent cannot be priced, but it compares messes of different sizes | a reverses the two uses; c leaves the comparison across sizes with no fair score; d hands the manager a number that cannot be priced and that explodes on a nearly closed day |

## Which item is worth arguing about?

Q4's option c. MAPE does explode when the rice used falls near zero, a closure day for example, and
that is a real reason to distrust it. The fix is to keep it for comparing sizes on days that are
never near zero, rather than to drop the only fair score across messes.

## Where does the pattern live at work?

Every forecast review at a company with branches of different sizes, dark stores at a quick-commerce
company or kitchens at a cloud-kitchen chain, scores each branch in its own unit and compares
branches in percent.
