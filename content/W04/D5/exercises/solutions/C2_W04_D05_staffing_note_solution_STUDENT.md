## What does the set test?

It tests the afternoon's decision end to end: choosing the forecast on the hidden weeks, the 2025
festival and the priced misses together, sizing a week's shifts from it, and naming the fact that
would move the plan.

## What does a hire-grade note say?

> **Book 20,275 packer shifts for 12 October to 30 November 2026, peaking at 3,719 in the week of 19
> October.** The peak falls two weeks before Diwali's week of 2 November, as it did in 2024 and 2025,
> so the forecast copies each week by its distance from Diwali and adds the 4 percent a year the
> centre grew. On thirteen hidden weeks it missed by 6,615 a week (about Rs 35,800 of idle shifts if
> high, Rs 1.65 lakh of late shipments if low), and on 2025's festival by 4,034 a week. Three facts would
> move it: a sale that opens at a different distance from Diwali (re-align by the sale), a 2025 peak
> capped by capacity (overtime reached 1,638 shifts that week), and growth other than 4 percent. To
> finance: your 4 percent is right and your weeks are not; copied by calendar the peak lands on 5
> October. To the vendor's sponsor: the fit is within 1.4 percent, and the forecast missed thirteen
> hidden weeks by 16,400 a week.

## Where does the pattern live at work?

Every peak a company staffs for, from a fulfilment centre's festival to a payments company's sale
day, ends in this note: a number, the week it lands, the error in money each way, and what would
change it.
