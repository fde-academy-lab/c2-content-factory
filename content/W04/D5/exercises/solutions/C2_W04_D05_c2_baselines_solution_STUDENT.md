# Which answers hold in the chapter 2 set on the three baselines, and why?

Answers: 1c 2d 3b

The mess's (invented) Sunday beat makes the seasonal naive the rule for next Sunday: 42 kg. A closure
breaks every copy that lands on it, so the week after Diwali copies the week after last year's
closure. A series that repeats and climbs takes the seasonal naive plus the climb: 196 plus 2, 198 kg.

**Who needs the answer.** You, to check your letters. The mess manager orders on the rule you pick.

## What does the set test?

It tests chapter 2's three copies and the two ways a copy fails: a beat it does not respect, and a
festival or a climb it does not carry.

## Why is each key right, and each other letter wrong?

| Item | Key | Why it holds | Why the others fail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1, next Sunday | c | Sunday is the beat's dip and rise: last Sunday's 42 kg is the copy that respects it | a and b copy weekdays into a Sunday, so both land near 27 kg; d averages weekdays, 25.75, and also misses the biryani |
| Q2, the week after the closure | d | Copying by distance from the festival lands on last year's week after the closure, when the hostel was filling again | a and b copy a closed week into an open one; c lands on whichever week sat 364 days back, which is last year's Diwali week whenever the festival moved, as it did by eleven days at the stall |
| Q3, week 17 | b | Week 17 opens a cycle: week 13 opened the last one at 196 kg, and each cycle climbs 2 kg, so 198 | a copies a guest week into an ordinary one; c drops the climb, the yearly-copy trap in small; d draws one line through guest and ordinary weeks alike and lands above the last guest week |

## Which item is worth arguing about?

Q2's option c. A year back usually is the right season, which is why the seasonal naive wins most
weeks. It fails exactly where a festival moves, and the week after a closure is next to the festival,
so the calendar copy is the one most likely to land on the closure itself.

## Where does the pattern live at work?

Every retailer that plans around a moving holiday, Diwali, Eid or the Chinese New Year, lines its
weeks up by distance from the holiday before copying last year, and adds the year's growth on top.
