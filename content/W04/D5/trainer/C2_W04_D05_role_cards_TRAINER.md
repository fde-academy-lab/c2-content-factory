# Role cards: finance, and the vendor's sponsor

**TRAINER ONLY.** In round three of the tournament (chapter 7, half two S10) the trainer plays
finance and the TA plays the vendor's sponsor; in the staffing note (half two, S14) both object again,
and the fallback's second transcript replays them. Read both cards once before the session and say the
lines in your own voice.

Flipkart, the Lucknow fulfilment centre. Illustrative case: the company is real, the situation and
the data are synthetic, built for this class.

## Card 1: who is finance, and what do they want?

Finance owns the centre's budget for the festive season. Their forecast is last year's same eight
weeks plus 4 percent, because "last year plus 4 is what the budget holds", and they want the shift
plan to match it.

**Partly right, on purpose.** The 4 percent is the growth the two years show, and the training
weeks alone give the same: the second year shipped 4.0 percent more than the first. On the thirteen hidden weeks their rule ties festival-aligned
at 6,615 shipments a week. What they miss is the shape: the rule copies calendar weeks, so it puts
2026's busiest week on 5 October, two weeks before the rush, and against last year's eight weeks
counted from Diwali it sits 9.5 percent below, 308,048 shipments short of festival-aligned. On 2025's
festival a calendar copy like theirs missed by 60,488 a week.

### What do you say, and when?

| Moment | Line to say |
|---|---|
| Opening, to the whole room | "Last year plus 4 is what the budget holds. Anything above it, I need to see in rupees." |
| To a group that quotes the 6,615 tie | "So my rule ties yours on your own scoreboard. Why would I change it?" |
| To a group that quotes 19.5 percent above last year | "Nineteen and a half percent? The budget holds 4." |
| To a group that offers only a number | "What does that do to my budget, and in which weeks?" |
| To a group that concedes everything | "So 4 percent was wrong all along?" |

### What answer should move you, and what should not?

Concede only to an answer that makes Tuesday's four moves in order:

1. **Restate** until you agree: "You want a plan the budget can hold, grown by the 4 percent the
   two years show."
2. **Answer from the evidence:** the tie is real on weeks far from Diwali, where the two rules are the
   same arithmetic; around the festival the calendar copy books the peak on 5 October, and on 2025's
   festival it missed by 60,488 a week against 4,034 for the copy counted from Diwali.
3. **Concede what is true:** the 4 percent stays in the plan; festival-aligned is your rule with the
   weeks moved to match Diwali.
4. **Say what would change it:** a third year, or a sale that opens at a different distance from
   Diwali.

Do not move for an answer that averages your number with the vendor's, that calls budgets always
wrong, or that drops the 4 percent.

### How do you close the exchange?

Say "Fine. Keep my 4 percent and move the weeks; show me the window in rupees." to the group whose
answer made all four moves, and "I am not convinced yet." to every other group, naming the move it
skipped.

## Card 2: who is the vendor's sponsor, and what do they want?

The vendor's sponsor is the manager who brought the vendor's demand model in. The model was fitted
to the 104 weeks and promises the festive window 22 percent above last year, and the sponsor wants
it to set the plan.

**Partly right, on purpose.** The model does fit history closely: its worst fitted week is 1.4
percent off. Priced, its misses on the hidden weeks cost least, Rs 11.9 lakh against Rs 13.5 lakh for
festival-aligned, because it errs high and a spare shipment costs Rs 5.42 against Rs 25 for a late
one. What the sponsor misses: on the weeks it never saw it missed by 16,400 a week, 148 percent worse
than festival-aligned, too high in 12 of 13 weeks and climbing from 3.6 percent to 11.5 percent too
high; nobody chose how high it runs. Its 22 percent is measured against last year's calendar weeks;
against the weeks counted from Diwali it sits 6.1 percent above, with its peak on 5 October.

### What do you say, and when?

| Moment | Line to say |
|---|---|
| Opening, to the whole room | "The model fits every week of the last two years within 2 percent. Your copy of last year cannot say that." |
| To a group that quotes 16,400 | "Thirteen quiet weeks in the monsoon. The model is built for the festival." |
| To a group that quotes the priced misses | "Then my model's misses are the cheapest on your own scoreboard." |
| To a group that offers only a number | "So what goes in the plan, and who signs for it?" |
| To a group that concedes everything | "So the vendor's model is worthless?" |

### What answer should move you, and what should not?

Concede only to an answer that makes the four moves in order:

1. **Restate** until you agree: "You want a model that captures the festival better than a copy of
   last year can."
2. **Answer from the evidence:** a fit is no forecast; on hidden weeks it missed by 16,400 and its
   misses grow the further it looks; its festive peak lands on 5 October.
3. **Concede what is true:** its fit is close, and leaning high is the right instinct at these costs:
   the plan books festival-aligned and prices both directions, about Rs 35,800 a week if it runs high
   against Rs 1.65 lakh if low, so any margin belongs above it, sized on purpose. How far above is the
   plan quantile, beyond today's row, which the fallback hour reads at 2.6 percent.
4. **Say what would change it:** the model scored on 2025's festival from data up to 22 September 2025,
   beating the copy counted from Diwali, before it books a shift.

Do not move for an answer that calls every vendor a cheat, that bins the model without a test, or
that quotes the 22 percent as if it compared festival with festival.

### How do you close the exchange?

Say "Fine. Run it on last year's festival and I will look again." to the group whose answer made all
four moves, and "I am not convinced yet." to every other group, naming the move it skipped.
