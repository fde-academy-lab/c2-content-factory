# Guesstimate of the day: how many two-wheelers are sold in India in a year?

**TRAINER ONLY.** Three minutes in the interview room slot (half two, S9), or five as the lab's warm-up.
The room estimates first; the anchor is revealed last.

## How does a strong candidate run it?

| Step | What they say | The numbers |
|---|---|---|
| Clarify | "New two-wheelers sold in India in one financial year, scooters and motorcycles together, exports left out. Sold to buyers, or by makers to dealers? I will estimate buyers and check against the makers' count, since over a year the two differ only by the change in dealer stock." | |
| Map | "Households, times the share that own a two-wheeler, gives the household fleet; divided by the years a household keeps one before buying new, that is the new vehicles needed to replace it. Then add first-time buyers and commercial riders, the delivery and bike-taxi fleets that wear vehicles out faster." | |
| Assume, with ranges | "30 crore households, from 28 to 32 crore; half own one, from 40 to 60 percent; replaced every 8 years, from 6 to 10; first-time and commercial buyers add 15 percent, from 10 to 20." | Every input is an assumption, said as one |
| Calculate | "30 crore x 0.5 / 8 is about 1.9 crore a year of replacements; times 1.15 is about 2.2 crore, and with the replacement years from 10 to 6 it runs from about 1.7 to 2.9 crore." | One input at a time: the years before replacement move the answer from 20 percent below to 33 percent above, the ownership share 20 percent either way, households 7 percent and first-time buyers 4 percent, so the replacement years and the ownership share are the inputs to argue about |
| Validate | "SIAM reported 2,17,05,974 domestic two-wheeler sales in FY 2025-26, makers' sales to dealers; about 2.2 crore against 2.17 is close, which at this precision is luck as much as method. The market grew 10.7 percent that year, so first-time buying is larger than a steady state allows, and part of the match is errors that cancel." | SIAM's release for FY 2025-26 (T3, checked 12 October 2026) |
| The catch | "October 2025's retail registrations alone were 31,49,846, so a peak month times twelve gives about 3.78 crore, 74 percent above the year. That month was more than festive: September's first 21 days were almost quiet ahead of the GST cut, so some of September's buying landed in October, and registrations lag sales. A peak month is never annualised." | FADA's release of 7 November 2025 (F5, checked 12 October 2026) |

## What does each wrong turn look like, and what do you say?

| The wrong turn | What to say |
|---|---|
| Starts from India's population and divides by family size, then stops | "How many of those households own one, and how often do they buy new?" |
| Annualises a month's news headline | "Which month was that, and does every month sell like it?" |
| Gives one number with no range | "Which input are you least sure of, and what happens to the answer at its ends?" |
| Forgets first-time buyers and delivery riders | "Is every sale a household replacing its own?" |
| Calls a match with SIAM proof | "Does SIAM count what you estimated, buyers or dealers?" |

## Where is the anchor from?

SIAM, "Auto Industry Performance of Q4 (Jan- March 2026) & FY 2025-26", which SIAM's site resolves
by its trailing number, reports 2,17,05,974 domestic two-wheeler sales in FY 2025-26, up 10.7 percent:
https://www.siam.in/news-&-updates/press-releases/x/605 (checked 12 October 2026).

FADA's October 2025 retail figure, and its note that the first 21 days of September were almost quiet
ahead of the GST 2.0 transition, are from its release of 7 November 2025:
https://fada.in/images/press-release/1690d68d7a2e6cFADA%20releases%20October%202025%20&%2042-Days%20Festive%20Vehicle%20Retail%20Data.pdf (checked 12 October 2026).

FADA counts retail registrations, collated with the road transport ministry, which lag sales; SIAM
reports manufacturers' domestic sales to dealers. The two series differ in timing as well as scope,
so the catch sets a month of registrations against a year of dispatches, and a candidate who says so
earns the point.
