# Which of the week's methods answers each brief, and what changes when it moves company?

Eight one-paragraph briefs. Four sit at companies you use, and each of those is an illustrative case:
the company is real, and the situation and every number the company did not publish are
hypothetical, written for this drill. The other four sit at Kalpa's other units, which are as
invented as Kalpa Retail. Your table takes one brief and answers it aloud.

**Who needs the answer.** The stakeholder in your brief, who has never seen this week's cases, and
any interviewer who asks you to carry a method from one company to theirs.

**The questions on the way.**

- How does the drill run?
- What does every answer name?
- The eight briefs, one question each.
- How does the week map read in your brief's words?

---

## How does the drill run?

| Part | Duration | What your table does |
|---|---|---|
| Read and prepare | 8 min | Read your brief and fill the grid below in full sentences, every line of it. |
| Answer aloud | 2 min a table | One speaker reads the grid in order in 90 seconds; then the Academic TA asks one follow-up, and a different member of the table answers it. |
| Redraw the map | 4 min | Write your brief's words into the week map at the end of this sheet; two tables read theirs to the room. |

## What does every answer name?

An answer that only renames the domain has not moved the method. Each line of the grid says what
changed, or what stayed, with a number or a named step wherever the brief gives one.

| Line of the grid | What it asks | Your table's answer |
|---|---|---|
| The method | Which day's method answers the brief, and why that one? | |
| The metric that changed | What is counted now, per what, over which window? | |
| The constraint that changed | What limits the answer here that did not limit it in the week's case? | |
| The error cost that changed | What does a wrong number cost here, and who pays for it? | |
| What stays | Which steps of the method carry over unchanged? | |
| Back on the Retail tables | If Kalpa Retail asked the same question, what would change back? | |

## Brief 1. Rapido, Bengaluru: what should a guarantee for bike-taxi drivers pay for?

Illustrative case: the company is real, and the scheme and every number below are hypothetical.
Suppose Rapido's Bengaluru team guarantees bike-taxi drivers Rs 150 for every hour they stay logged in
within 500 metres of a metro station during the two busiest evening hours, so that riders leaving a
station are picked up within three minutes. Six weeks in, logged-in hours near stations are up 35
percent and completed rides from station pickups are up 4 percent; the share of station requests
accepted within a minute has fallen from 0.81 to 0.70, and the median wait at the station gates has
gone from 3.4 to 3.3 minutes. The city lead wants to extend the guarantee to three times as many
stations. What should the guarantee pay for, what guards it, and what do you tell the city lead?

## Brief 2. Blinkit, Gurugram: what should a festival-week card suggest, and what is it worth?

Illustrative case: the company is real, and the situation and every number below except Eternal's
own figures are hypothetical. Eternal's letter to shareholders for April to June 2026 counts 2,443
Blinkit stores and defines net order value as gross order value less all discounts (checked 13
October 2026). Suppose one Gurugram store's checkout card suggests the item most often found in the
same cart as what is already there. In the week before Diwali a sweets box is in 30 percent of the
store's 1,200 carts a day, so the card shows sweets beside almost everything, and the category lead
wants a "pick two, save Rs 20" card for the festival week. What should the card rank by, counted over
which carts, and what is one card slot worth a day?

## Brief 3. Swiggy One, Bengaluru: is a steady renewal rate hiding a cohort problem?

Illustrative case: the company is real, and the situation and every number below are hypothetical.
The one published fact is Swiggy's own definition: its letter for July to September 2024 counts
the delivery charges users pay in gross order value net of discounts, including the free-delivery
discounts given to Swiggy One members (checked 13 October 2026). Suppose the Swiggy One team in Bengaluru reports that
64 percent of the members due to renew each month do so, steady for six months, while each month's
new members renew at their first renewal less often than the month before. Members pay monthly or
every three months, and a one-rupee first month brought in a third of the summer's new members. Is
it a cohort problem or an everyone problem, and what do you read before the renewal rate?

## Brief 4. Amazon India: how should a fulfilment centre book festive staff it must hire three weeks ahead?

Illustrative case: the company is real, and the situation and every number below except Amazon's own
figure are hypothetical. Amazon says its Great Indian Festival 2025 drew over 276 crore customer
visits, 70 percent of them from tier 2 and 3 cities (checked 13 October 2026). Suppose a fulfilment
centre in a tier 2 city must book seasonal associates three weeks before each festive week, in whole
weeks, and its planner copies the same calendar weeks of last year plus 10 percent. An associate-week
costs Rs 4,200 and packs about 600 shipments, and a shipment that misses its promised day costs Rs 30
in refunds and coupons. What must the planner's copy beat, how is it scored when bookings are made
three weeks ahead, and where should the plan sit against the forecast?

## Brief 5. Kalpa Financial Services: which customers should Rohan Desai's team nudge first?

Rohan Desai, head of risk at Kalpa Financial Services, Kalpa's payments and lending business, asks
which customers of the payments app to nudge first toward a second product: a credit card, a personal
loan or a savings account. A nudge that ends in a loan default costs twenty times what the business
loses by turning away a customer who would have repaid. The sales team proposes ranking customers by
how many Kalpa products they already hold, and paying its staff on products per customer. What do
you rank by, what do you guard, and what do you say about the sales team's plan?

## Brief 6. Kalpa Logistics: is on time really on time, and how many parcels will Diwali bring?

Kalpa Logistics' head of operations reports the on-time delivery rate up from 88 to 95 percent since
June, when dispatchers were allowed to move a parcel's promised date once a delay was known
(hypothetical numbers). The same head must staff the parcel hubs for Diwali: last year's festive peak
came in October, and Diwali falls on 8 November 2026, after 20 October in 2025. What would you count
as on time, what guards it, and how do you forecast the hubs' Diwali weeks?

## Brief 7. Kalpa Health: which step between a booked test and a completed visit loses the most dollars?

Dr Priya Menon, COO of Kalpa Health, which tests US patients at laboratories and patient service
centres in six metro areas and bills US payers in dollars, reports booked tests up 6 percent this
quarter while completed visits fell in Phoenix and Atlanta (hypothetical numbers). A patient books
online, the payer may have to approve the test first, and the patient can still miss the visit.
Patients whose first visit came since the spring also seem to come back less. Which step loses the
most dollars, metro area by metro area, and is the return problem a cohort problem?

## Brief 8. Kalpa Connect: is churn steady, or is the blend hiding the newer subscribers?

Ananya Bose, COO of Kalpa Connect, Kalpa's telecom and subscriptions business, sees blended monthly
churn steady at 2.1 percent for four months, while subscribers who joined since the spring seem to
cancel sooner. A spring offer brought in a third of this year's new subscribers, and most subscribers
joined years ago (hypothetical numbers). Is churn steady, and what would you show her?
