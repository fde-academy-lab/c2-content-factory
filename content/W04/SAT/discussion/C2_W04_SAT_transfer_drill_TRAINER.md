# Week 4 Saturday: the transfer drill, run sheet and answer grids

**TRAINER ONLY.** 30 minutes, run by the Academic TA straight after the solution discussion. The room
holds `C2_W04_SAT_transfer_briefs_STUDENT.md`, one copy each. The row's drill rule decides every
answer: it names the metric, the constraint and the error cost that changed, and an answer that only
renames the domain has not transferred.

The four company briefs re-pose a day's ticket at a company the room uses: Monday's at Rapido,
Wednesday's at Blinkit, Thursday's at Swiggy One and Friday's at Amazon India. Each is labelled
illustrative, its only real facts are the company's own published figures with their check dates, and
every other number is hypothetical. The four Kalpa briefs carry the client zero seeds of Kalpa's
other units, and Part 6 of the paper already matched their first questions to a method on paper.

---

## How does the 30 minutes run?

| Part | Duration | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Tables and briefs | 2 min | The room sits in eight tables of four or five, two pairs to a table where the seating allows; table 1 takes brief 1 and so on, so every brief is answered once. |
| Prepare | 8 min | Each table fills the grid on its sheet in full sentences. Walk the tables and ask one question at each: "what changed, and where is the number?" |
| Answer aloud | 16 min | Eight tables at 2 minutes each: one speaker reads the grid in 90 seconds, then you ask the follow-up below and a different member answers it. Take the tables in brief order, so the four companies come before Kalpa's units. |
| Redraw the map | 4 min | Two tables read their week map in their company's words; ask the room which day's box changed most, and why. Then open scene S1 of the week's explorer (`content/W04/D1/demos/C2_W04_D01_week_explorer_STUDENT.html`), take the room's letter, and choose one of the two tables' units: its card is a second, shorter example of the same move, read beside the table's map. |

Hold every table to the grid's order. When a speaker says "the same as Monday", ask which line of
Monday's definition carries over and which line had to be rewritten. When an answer names a
real-company fact the brief does not give, stop it: the brief's only real facts are the ones it
prints with a check date.

## Brief 1. Rapido, Bengaluru, a guarantee for bike-taxi drivers (Monday's method)

| Line of the grid | The answer to listen for |
|---|---|
| The method | Monday's metric design: write the outcome the guarantee exists for as a definition, apply the proxy test to what it pays for, and give each cheap way of hitting the number a guardrail. |
| The metric that changed | Completed rides from station pickups within three minutes of the request, per logged-in driver-hour near stations, by station and by evening; Hyderabad counted completed peak trips per enrolled driver online at peak across a city's zones. |
| The constraint that changed | The guarantee pays for being near a station, so a driver can wait at a quiet station and still be paid, and the window is the minutes after a train arrives, far shorter than Hyderabad's peak. |
| The error cost that changed | Every logged-in hour at a station that brings no rider costs Rs 150; hours rose 35 percent while rides rose 4 percent and the wait moved from 3.4 to 3.3 minutes, so most of the extra spend bought waiting. |
| What stays | The proxy test (could a driver raise logged-in hours without serving a rider?), guardrails on acceptance within a minute, the wait at the gate and the guarantee paid per completed ride, and a test by station before any extension. |
| Back on the Retail tables | At Kalpa the gamed number was reorders placed, which the app raised on its own, and the definition counted delivered orders per active customer with revenue per active customer as the guardrail. |

**The follow-up.** "A driver logs in at a busy station and declines every short ride. What catches
it?" The acceptance share within a minute, by driver and by station, read beside the rides.

## Brief 2. Blinkit, Gurugram, a festival-week card (Wednesday's method)

| Line of the grid | The answer to listen for |
|---|---|
| The method | Wednesday's: lift above a support floor, with the slot valued in margin. |
| The metric that changed | Lift counted on the store's own festival-week carts, with chance counted for that week, since sweets in 30 percent of carts make any confidence toward sweets look strong; the ranking runs store by store, because stock and neighbourhood differ. |
| The constraint that changed | One festival week and one store's shelves: a pair is suggested only where both items are in stock that week, and one week of 1,200 carts a day holds few carts for any one pair. |
| The error cost that changed | The Rs 20 discount is paid on every pick-two cart, the carts that would have held both anyway included, and a suggestion out of stock becomes a cancelled line. |
| What stays | Confidence is fooled by a popular partner, as milk fooled Pune's strip; lift above a floor answers it; each customer is counted once; a slot is worth its incremental adds times their margin, less the discount paid on every add; and a test comes before the card goes to every store. |
| Back on the Retail tables | Kalpa's popular partner was the charger, in 84.5 percent of consumer baskets, whose lift of 0.97 refused the bundle, and the lab's pick was the wireless earphones with their case at a lift of 3.1. |

**The follow-up.** "Sweets are in 30 percent of carts. Does that make sweets a strong partner?" No:
it makes every confidence toward sweets about 0.3 by chance alone, so only a lift above 1 against the
week's own base rates, above a floor fixed before the week starts, says a pair beats chance.

## Brief 3. Swiggy One, Bengaluru, a steady renewal rate (Thursday's method)

| Line of the grid | The answer to listen for |
|---|---|
| The method | Thursday's: cohorts by start month, each read at the same age, and the blend read as a rate weighted by cohort sizes. |
| The metric that changed | Renewal at each billing date, per cohort and per plan length, in place of Zomato Gold's "ordered in month one" and month-two retention. |
| The constraint that changed | Plans of different lengths: a member on a three-month plan cannot leave at the first monthly renewal, so a cohort's age is counted in billing cycles and the blend moves with the plan mix; the one-rupee cohort is a third of the summer's members. |
| The error cost that changed | A lost member costs the fee at every later renewal and the orders the membership was meant to bring, and reading the steady 64 percent as health leaves the falling cohorts unaddressed. |
| What stays | Cohorts fixed at entry; the blend as sizes times rates; the offer cohort split out before anyone blames everyone; and the denominator asked for before the rate is read. |
| Back on the Retail tables | Retail-Plus's blend read 41 percent in July, August and September while every cohort fell, and the auto-reorder window's checkout step fell to 7 of 11. |

**The follow-up.** "What is the denominator of the 64 percent?" The members due to renew that month:
three-month members fall due only every third month, so in most months the rate is read mainly on
monthly members, and the blend shifts whenever the plan mix shifts.

## Brief 4. Amazon India, festive staff booked three weeks ahead (Friday's method)

| Line of the grid | The answer to listen for |
|---|---|
| The method | Friday's: score each forecast on weeks it never saw, against the naive and a copy aligned to the festival, then plan toward the costlier side. |
| The metric that changed | The miss of forecasts made three weeks ahead, in shipments a week, since associates are booked three weeks before; Lucknow scored the miss on its hidden weeks. |
| The constraint that changed | The booking lead time and whole associate-weeks, and a planner's copy of calendar weeks that carries the festival into the wrong weeks whenever Diwali moves, as it does from 20 October 2025 to 8 November 2026. |
| The error cost that changed | A spare shipment costs Rs 4,200 over 600, Rs 7, and a late one Rs 30, so the plan covers about 30 over 37 of weeks, 0.81, close to Lucknow's 0.82 for different reasons. |
| What stays | The hold-out on the latest weeks, the festival-aligned copy, both directions of miss priced, and the plan's share set from the two costs. |
| Back on the Retail tables | Anand books months, so the board's number is monthly with the spike in November, and the two costs are an empty shelf against stock left over. |

**The follow-up.** "The sale opens on a different day this year. What do you align to?" Check how far
before the festival the sale opens; if that distance moves and the peak follows the sale, copy last
year's weeks by distance from the sale.

## Brief 5. Kalpa Financial Services, the nudge toward a second product

| Line of the grid | The answer to listen for |
|---|---|
| The method | Monday's definition and guardrail, Wednesday's lift above a base rate, and Friday's two priced errors. |
| The metric that changed | Take-up of the second product beyond what any customer takes up anyway, valued net of expected losses, in rupees per customer nudged, over a window long enough to see repayment; products per customer is Wells Fargo's cross-sell count. |
| The constraint that changed | A loan is a credit decision whose outcome arrives months after the nudge, so the outcome is late and the window long, and staff paid on products per customer can move that count. |
| The error cost that changed | A nudge that ends in default costs twenty times a wrongful rejection, so the list leans toward caution; on the Retail tables a wasted nudge cost a small discount and some annoyance. |
| What stays | The definition with its numerator, denominator and window; a guardrail the people measured cannot move; lift above the base rate; and a holdout before any lift is trusted. |
| Back on the Retail tables | The price of each error, and with it where the bar for nudging sits. |

**The follow-up.** "The sales team will be paid on your metric. What can they move?" Who is nudged and
how hard, so losses are measured by risk on the accounts themselves, where the sales team cannot
touch them, and the plan to pay staff on products per customer is the Wells Fargo case.

## Brief 6. Kalpa Logistics, the on-time rate and the Diwali hubs

| Line of the grid | The answer to listen for |
|---|---|
| The method | Monday's: a definition whose window the measured cannot move, with a guardrail; then Friday's festival-aligned forecast for the hubs. |
| The metric that changed | On time against the promise made when the order was placed, with the share of promises moved printed beside it; the moved promise is a window the dispatchers control, the way Phoenix's scheduling staff set the "desired date" its wait times ran from. |
| The constraint that changed | Dispatchers control the promised date, and the festival moved 19 days, from 20 October 2025 to 8 November 2026, so last October's peak copies into the wrong weeks. |
| The error cost that changed | A parcel counted on time and delivered late costs a customer's trust and a refund, and it hides the hubs that need staff; an idle hub shift costs the shift, and a late festival parcel costs more than an ordinary one. |
| What stays | The proxy test, a guardrail the measured cannot redirect, the festival-aligned copy scored on hidden weeks, and the plan priced in both directions. |
| Back on the Retail tables | Delivered orders per active customer as the outcome counted, and Anand's November spike as the festival placed in its month. |

**The follow-up.** "Dispatchers say the old promise was unfair when a truck broke down. Are they
partly right?" Yes, so a promise moves only with a reason code, and the rate is reported both ways,
against the first promise and against the moved one.

## Brief 7. Kalpa Health, the booking-to-visit flow in dollars

| Line of the grid | The answer to listen for |
|---|---|
| The method | Thursday's: each funnel step over the step before, in one unit, split by place and date, and cohorts by first-visit month. |
| The metric that changed | Dollars of expected payment at each step from booking to the payer's approval to the completed visit, by metro area and payer, and return visits per cohort by first-visit month, read at the same age. |
| The constraint that changed | The payer's approval sits inside the funnel, the money is in dollars, and six metro areas carry different payer mixes. |
| The error cost that changed | A lost visit loses a test billed to a US payer, and blaming the booking page sends the fix to the wrong step when the loss sits in approvals. |
| What stays | One unit per funnel, each step over the step before, comparators on the same step, cohorts at the same age, and a data fault ruled out first. |
| Back on the Retail tables | Kalpa's checkout step, 7 of 11 in the window against 10 of 12 before it, and Retail-Plus's cohorts read at the same age. |

**The follow-up.** "Phoenix's approvals fell from one date. What do you check first?" Whether the
count changed: a payer's portal or a code change can log approvals differently from that date, as
Chennai's tracking check had to rule out a logging change shipped with the Android release, so the
records are checked against the event log first.

## Brief 8. Kalpa Connect, a steady blended churn

| Line of the grid | The answer to listen for |
|---|---|
| The method | Thursday's: cohorts by start month, read at the same tenure, and the blend read as a weighted rate. |
| The metric that changed | Churn, the subscribers who leave, by start month at the same tenure, with the monthly fee each cohort loses; Zomato Gold counted the members who stayed. |
| The constraint that changed | A large base that joined years ago churns little and dominates the blend, so newer cohorts can churn sooner while the blend barely moves; the spring offer cohort is a third of the year's joiners. |
| The error cost that changed | Each lost subscriber is a monthly fee for every month after, so a cohort that churns sooner costs its remaining value, and a steady blend delays the fix. |
| What stays | The blend as sizes times rates, cohorts fixed at entry, the offer cohort split out, and the denominator asked for first. |
| Back on the Retail tables | Retail-Plus's flat 41 percent and Zomato Gold's flat 38, each hiding cohorts that fell. |

**The follow-up.** "Why would the blend stay steady if new subscribers churn more?" The old base, the
largest share of the count, churns less as it ages, and that offsets the newer cohorts until they
grow large enough to show.
