2. The Academic TA reads the key out part by part, and the marker writes a tick or a cross beside each item.
3. ## Why each answer holds

### Q1, key e (guardrails)

**Why it holds.** A north star names the outcome a plan exists to move, and a guardrail is a number that must not get worse while it rises, so it catches a harmful way of hitting the north star. On Monday's redesign the north star was completed peak trips per enrolled driver online at peak each week, and its guardrails were the share of offers accepted, the rider's wait by zone and the top-up per completed trip. Leading inputs move before the north star and proxies stand in for it, and neither catches harm. The singular words in the bank, window, baseline and target, do not fit the plural blank.

**In the interview.** The north star is the outcome the plan exists for, and each guardrail catches one cheap way of hitting it, so I name the cheap way beside each guardrail.

### Q2, key b (window)

**Why it holds.** A complete definition names its numerator, its denominator, its window and its exclusions, and the window is the period counted, which decides what a before-and-after comparison holds. Kalpa's growth-plan comparison started on 20 August because the monsoon sale ended on 19 August, so the sale's extra orders sat in neither window. A baseline is what a number is compared with, and a target is what the plan hopes it reaches; neither is part of the definition. Guardrails sit beside a definition as numbers of their own, and each word in the bank is used once, so guardrails belongs to Q1.

**In the interview.** A number is computable once its numerator, denominator, window and exclusions are written down, and it is safe to pay for once its guardrails are.
