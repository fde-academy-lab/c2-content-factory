## What did the hiring interviewer find?

Of the 23 items the key marks Hard, I accept 8 as interview grade (Q7, Q14, Q16, Q20, Q21, Q25, Q32, Q39) and would relabel 15 as medium (Q4, Q6, Q8, Q10, Q12, Q13, Q15, Q24, Q26, Q27, Q28, Q30, Q31, Q33, Q34), four of them borderline (Q4, Q15, Q26, Q33). In most of the fifteen, one of three things gives the answer away: the keyed option is the only one whose conclusion fits the part's title, the stem or exhibit hands over the method, or the two leading options share the judgment and differ only in arithmetic. One medium item reads hard (Q9), and Q3, Q11 and Q35 to Q38 read easy. With those changes the paper holds 9 hard items of 39, about 23 percent, against the 60 percent it claims. I recomputed every key, and each one is right on the printed exhibit's numbers. Ten keys are arguable (Q7, Q8, Q9, Q14, Q15, Q20, Q25, Q30, Q32, Q34). One argument wins: on Q8, a caveat-as-footnote order is as defensible as the key's, and all-or-nothing marking fails it. One partly wins: on Q30, the Diwali run-up can fall partly in late October. The other eight lose, though six of them (Q7, Q14, Q15, Q20, Q25, Q34) need a wording or exhibit fix. I checked 31 real-world facts against their sources on 13 October 2026: 29 are confirmed, 2 differ and none was out of reach.
