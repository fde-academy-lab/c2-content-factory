# Does your take-home have every part a reviewer will look for?

Read each line before you hand in. Each part comes first as you would explain it to someone who has
never seen a slide, and then as the check a reviewer runs.

**Who needs the answer.** You, before the reviewer sees it.

**The questions on the way.**

- Does the slide have its four parts, in order?
- Does the recording run the four steps?
- Is the sanity check honest?

---

## Does the slide have its four parts, in order?

| Part | In plain words | The check |
|---|---|---|
| The claim | Say what should happen, in one sentence, before anything else | The title is a sentence with a verb, and someone could act on it without reading further |
| The evidence | Show the one number that proves it, and say what it was divided by | One number or one chart, with its denominator written beside it |
| The caveat | Say what you are not sure of, where it can be seen | The caveat sits on the slide beside the evidence, never only in an appendix |
| The action | Say what to do, what it costs and who decides | The action names a cost in dollars or weeks and the person who signs |

**The appendix.** Each heading is a question one named person will ask, so the right page opens when
that person asks it.

---

## Does the recording run the four steps?

| Step | In plain words | The check |
|---|---|---|
| Clarify | Say exactly what you are counting before you count it | The unit, the period and what counts are said before the first number |
| Map | Break the number into smaller numbers that multiply together | Every factor is named before any arithmetic |
| Calculate | Say each guess and the running total out loud | Each assumption is said with a range, and the product is said at each step |
| Validate | Compare with a number someone published, counted the same way | The source, its date and its unit are named, and the gap is explained |

**The range.** You gave a low and a high answer, and you said which factor moves the answer most.

**Under five minutes.** The recording stops before five minutes.

---

## Is the sanity check honest?

- The published number counts the same thing as your estimate: shipments are not sales, and a festive month is not a year.
- You compared like with like: a per-day figure with a per-day estimate, a financial year with a financial year.
- You wrote the date you checked the source.
- Where your estimate and the source disagree, you named the assumption you would revisit first.
