# Which of Tuesday's moves can the room still make under a timer?

**Who needs the answer.** The trainer, who reads from the room's answers which move to revisit before
Wednesday, and each learner, who sees which check to redo tonight.

**The questions on the way.** In what order does a slide's content go? What stays on a crowded slide?
Does a strong defence concede anything? In what order does an estimate run? What went wrong in two
estimates? Which guardrail did Monday's redesign need? Which comparison checks a monthly total?

The quiz has eight items, ungraded and scored on correctness and speed together. Q1 to Q3 check the
morning's slide and defence, Q4 to Q6 the afternoon's estimates, Q7 returns to Monday, and Q8 checks
the sanity check's unit. Items that use invented numbers say so, so no item can be answered from the
slide's own figures.

---

## Q1. A slide for the city head has four parts. In which order do they go on the page?
*Tests: the one-slide answer's order.*

- Evidence, claim, action, caveat
- Claim, evidence, caveat, action  <- correct
- Claim, action, evidence, caveat
- Caveat, claim, evidence, action

---

## Q2. The redesign slide has room for one more element. Which one should stay?
*Tests: keep or cut, by whether the claim needs it.*

- The line "peak online hours up 24 percent" from the dashboard
- A pie of the top-up paid in each of the six weeks
- The caveat that the monsoon ended inside the window  <- correct
- A paragraph on how the bookings were computed

---

## Q3. True or false: should the best defence of a slide concede nothing to an objector?
*Tests: the third of the four moves, and the trap of conceding nothing.*

- True, because any concession hands the objector the whole meeting
- True, because the evidence on the slide already answers every doubt
- False, because conceding the true part keeps the claim believable  <- correct
- False, because a good slide should concede the claim when pushed

---

## Q4. In which order do the four steps of a guesstimate run?
*Tests: the dojo's method.*

- Map, clarify, calculate, validate
- Clarify, calculate, map, validate
- Calculate, map, clarify, validate
- Clarify, map, calculate, validate  <- correct

---

## Q5. An estimate gives a city of two million people nine million households (invented). What went wrong, and what catches it?
*Tests: clarifying the unit, and a sanity check on people per household.*

- People were counted as households; about four to a household gives about 5 lakh  <- correct
- The city is too small to estimate; use a national figure for households instead
- Nothing went wrong, since a large city can hold several households for each person in it
- The households were rounded up; recount them with two decimal places to be exact

---

## Q6. A colleague divides 1 lakh trips a day by the 2.5 trips a busy driver fits in an hour and says 40,000 drivers are needed at the peak (invented numbers). What went wrong?
*Tests: the units of each factor, the trap of the drivers-at-peak estimate.*

- The trips a driver fits in an hour should have been doubled
- A day's trips were divided by an hour's rate, and the peak window was skipped  <- correct
- Nothing went wrong; the answer is high only because a city of that size is large
- The idle share was counted twice, once in each of the two factors

---

## Q7. Monday's redesign pays per completed peak trip. Which guardrail catches drivers who accept only short trips and decline the rest?
*Tests: the return question from Monday, the guardrail for a scheme that pays per trip.*

- Peak online hours per enrolled driver per week
- The top-up paid in total over the six weeks
- Acceptance share: requests accepted over requests offered  <- correct
- The number of drivers enrolled in the scheme in each week

---

## Q8. A payments network reports 31 billion transactions in a 31-day month, and a pair estimated 1 billion a day (invented numbers). Which comparison is honest?
*Tests: validating in the unit the source published.*

- 31 billion divided by the month's 31 days, which is 1 billion a day  <- correct
- 31 billion divided by 30 days, which is about 1.03 billion a day
- 31 billion set against the pair's 1 billion, as both are printed
- The month's value in rupees set against the pair's count of payments
