# Role cards, Week 4 Tuesday: what does each objector say, which part is true, and what answers the rest?

**TRAINER ONLY.** Printed, cut and handed to the objector in each three. Each card is partly right by
design. The three Uber roles are Monday's three (`content/W04/D1/trainer/`, written by Monday's
session); on Tuesday they object to the slide rather than to the definition, and these cards are the
Tuesday versions, complete in themselves. The three Kalpa cards are for the TA lab and are proposed
for client zero v2.3 (the row's client-zero column, 21 September 2026, not yet locked).

**Who needs the answer.** The objector, who reads the card aloud, and the trainer, who checks that
each defence met the card's true part.

**The questions on the way.** What does each role say, what does it lose, which part is true, which
evidence answers the rest, and what would change the presenter's mind?

## Card 1: what does the driver-operations lead say?

| | |
|---|---|
| Says | "Drivers will log off. Pay per trip and supply at peak falls, and riders wait longer." |
| Loses | Peak online hours, up 24 percent, as the headline the team reports |
| The true part | The 180 drivers in the idle tail will log off: their idle hours are what stops being paid, and the outer zones are thin |
| What answers the rest | The other 1,020 drivers held at 1.90 to 1.89 trips per online hour, and their trips rose 6 percent; they keep the floor if they accept |
| Push once more with | "Riders in the outer zones will wait longer." |
| A strong presenter's "what would change my mind" | Median rider wait at peak rising above 4.6 minutes in the test |

## Card 2: what does the marketing lead say?

| | |
|---|---|
| Says | "The guarantee is on every hoarding. Changing it six weeks in looks like bait." |
| Loses | A clean "Rs 300 an hour, guaranteed" line |
| The true part | The hoarding promised a floor, and 140 drivers joined after it went up |
| What answers the rest | The floor stays for drivers who accept, and none of the 140 joiners is in the idle tail, so the condition costs new drivers nothing on this data |
| Push once more with | "New drivers will read the condition as a cut." |
| A strong presenter's "what would change my mind" | Weekly sign-ups falling once the condition is announced |

## Card 3: what does the finance partner say?

| | |
|---|---|
| Says | "Bookings per hour fell because the monsoon ended, not because of the scheme." |
| Loses | Its own number, top-up per trip (Rs 82.3), as the headline; it becomes a guardrail |
| The true part | The monsoon ended inside the window, and last year's weeks are not in the data |
| What answers the rest | Rider requests rose 1.8 percent; the three busiest weeks after the launch ran at 1.60 trips per online hour; drivers who joined before the launch fell from 1.90 to 1.58; the drop is a step in the launch week (the highest after week, Rs 271, sits below the lowest before week, Rs 310) |
| Push once more with | "Then show me last year." |
| A strong presenter's "what would change my mind" | Last year's same six weeks showing a fall of this size, or the test showing no change |

## Kalpa cards for the lab: which objection does each Kalpa head raise?

Proposed for client zero v2.3. The TA plays one head per presentation and pushes once; the learner
presents Monday's Kalpa north star, delivered orders per active customer per month, with its three
guardrails (revenue per active customer, the discount share of booked revenue, the return rate).

| Head | Says | The true part | What answers the rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| The marketing lead | "My campaigns win new customers, and your metric counts only how often existing ones order." | New customers are a real lever, and the metric's denominator treats a new customer like an old one | The leading input, new Retail-Plus sign-ups, keeps acquisition visible; revenue per active customer guards against buying orders with discounts |
| Operations | "More orders per customer means more small deliveries, and my cost per delivery rises." | A frequency target can push up delivery cost | The return rate and revenue per active customer are guardrails, and a delivery-cost line can be added as a fourth |
| The head of Retail-Plus | "My members are worth more than other customers, and your metric averages them away." | Retail-Plus members do order differently | The metric can be cut by tier on the appendix page; the north star stays one number for the company |

What the TA scores: the four parts of the slide and the four moves, present or absent. The number is
never scored. No Kalpa plant is named on any card.
