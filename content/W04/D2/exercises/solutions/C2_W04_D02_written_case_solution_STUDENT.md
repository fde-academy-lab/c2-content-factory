# What does a one-page case the city head can use look like?

Released after the pages are handed in. The page below is one way to pass all five checks; yours can
pass them in other words. The case: Uber's peak-hour guarantee in Hyderabad, launched on 31 August
2026.

## What does a model page say?

> **To the city head, before Thursday's city review.**
>
> The peak-hour guarantee is paying for online hours that carry no trips. An hour a driver spends
> online at peak used to bring in Rs 314 of fares; since the launch it brings in Rs 260, while the
> average fare has stayed at Rs 165, so the whole fall is fewer trips in each online hour. Riders
> gained little: the median wait went from 4.8 to 4.6 minutes.
>
> Most of the money went to a small group: Rs 1.16 crore of the Rs 1.9 crore top-up was paid for
> weeks in which the driver accepted fewer than half the rides offered, three-quarters of it in the
> three outer zones.
>
> One thing the numbers cannot yet rule out: the monsoon ended in the same six weeks, and we do not
> have last year's weeks to compare, so part of the fall may be the season. In the last three weeks,
> with rider requests back at their level before the launch, trips per hour stayed low, which points
> away from the season, but only a test can settle it.
>
> I recommend testing a change before the city pays for another six weeks: pay the guarantee per
> completed peak trip, with a floor on how many offered rides a driver accepts, first for half the
> drivers, picked at random, for six weeks. City-wide, the change saves Rs 58 lakh over six weeks if
> half of that Rs 1.16 crore is still paid to drivers who start accepting, and Rs 92.7 lakh if a
> fifth is. You sign the test; if it shows fewer completed trips per driver, or longer waits in any
> zone, we drop the change.

## Why does it pass each check?

| Check | Where the page meets it |
|---|---|
| The claim is the first sentence | "The peak-hour guarantee is paying for online hours that carry no trips." |
| One number with its denominator | Rs 314 to Rs 260 of fares per online hour at peak |
| The caveat sits in the body | The third paragraph, in full sentences |
| The action with its cost and who signs | The last paragraph: the test, the saving range with its assumption, the city head, and the result that would stop the change |
| No method words | It says "fares per online hour", never "denominator" or "ratio" |

The page asks the city head to sign a test first, which is what the caveat calls for: only a test can
separate the scheme from the season. The test splits drivers at random, one by one, because a driver
moves between zones and a split by zone would put the same driver on both sides.

## Which mistakes come back most often?

A page that opens on method ("I analysed the dashboard"); a number with no denominator ("bookings
fell 17 percent"); a caveat in a bracket at the end; a saving with no assumption behind it; and the
per-driver-week rise quoted as good news, which the morning showed came from about four more online
hours a week at 17 percent less per hour.
