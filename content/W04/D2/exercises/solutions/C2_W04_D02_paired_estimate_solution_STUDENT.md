# What does a worked UPI estimate look like, and which comparison is the honest one?

The drill asked for UPI payments in India on an average day, sized in pairs in seven minutes and
checked only after the clock stopped. This page is released after the swap; the next pair scored your
sheet on structure and the sanity check, never on how close the number came.

## What does one worked sheet look like?

| Step | What the sheet says |
|---|---|
| Clarify | Volume, the count of payments; an average day in September 2026; every kind of UPI payment |
| Map | Active users x payments per active user a day |
| Calculate | About 35 crore active users (about one in four of India's roughly 140 crore people; range 25 to 45 crore) x about 2 payments a day (a tea, a recharge or a transfer; range 1 to 3) = 70 crore, or 700 million, a day |
| Range | One factor at a time: active users move it from 50 crore to 90 crore; payments a day move it from 35 crore to 105 crore, so payments a day set the range |
| Validate | NPCI's average of 802 million a day in September 2026 (Outlook Money, 2 October 2026, checked 12 October 2026): the estimate is 13 percent below, the same order and a little low |

## Which comparison is the honest one?

The per-day average, because the estimate is per day. September's total was 24.07 billion: divided by
the month's 30 days it gives the published 802 million; divided by 31 it gives 776 million, about 3
percent low. A pair that compares its daily estimate with the monthly total, or divides by the wrong
number of days, has validated in the wrong unit.

## What would a strong pair say next?

"Our 700 million sits 13 percent under NPCI's 802 million. Payments a day moves the answer most, so
that is the assumption we would revisit first: merchant payments at small shops may run higher than
two a day for an active user."

## Where does this show up at work?

Market sizing for a payments product, capacity planning for a payments backend, and every guesstimate
round that opens a consulting or product interview.
