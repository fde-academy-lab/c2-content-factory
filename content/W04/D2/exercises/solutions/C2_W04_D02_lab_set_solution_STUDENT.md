# What do strong answers to the Kalpa lab look like?

These answers are released at the end of the lab. The Kalpa slide is scored on its parts and moves, never its number,
so many different slides pass, and this page gives the shape of a strong answer and the worked
estimate.

## What does a strong Kalpa north-star slide carry?

| Part | A strong version |
|---|---|
| The claim | "Kalpa should chase delivered orders per active customer each month, because that is the branch of revenue a customer controls" (the revenue tree of Week 1: revenue is customers times orders per customer times order value) |
| The evidence | One number with its denominator from your own Week 1 or Week 2 work, such as delivered orders per active customer in a month from the teaching extract, never a total with no denominator |
| The caveat | What the evidence cannot show yet, said on the slide |
| The action | The three guardrails named (revenue per active customer, the discount share of booked revenue, the return rate), the review date, who signs |

The two failures the TA watches for: a slide whose title is a topic ("North-star metric proposal"),
and a defence that retreats at the first objection.

## What does a worked estimate for Anand look like?

Every value is an assumption with its range; a strong sheet says each one aloud.

| Step | Worked values |
|---|---|
| Clarify | A sign-up is a new paying Retail-Plus member whose home store is the new one, in its first twelve months |
| Map | Households near the store x share who shop there in year one x share of those shoppers who sign up |
| Calculate | 50,000 households (range 30,000 to 80,000) x 0.4 who shop in year one (range 0.25 to 0.5) = 20,000 shoppers; x 0.05 who sign up (range 0.02 to 0.08) = about 1,000 sign-ups |
| Range | One factor at a time: households give 600 to 1,600 sign-ups, the share who shop 625 to 1,250, and the share who sign up 400 to 1,600, so the sign-up share sets the range |
| Validate | 20,000 shopping households x 2 visits a month x Rs 1,500 a visit x 12 months = Rs 72 crore a year, about half of DMart's about Rs 146 crore a store on the average count; a first-year store at about half a mature store passes, and one above a mature store's sales would fail. The check reaches the shoppers, never the sign-up share; Kalpa's own sign-up rate at its existing stores would be the like-for-like anchor, and it is not in the lab's data |
| The catch | A store in its first year sells less than a mature one, and DMart's average spans stores of several sizes; the year-end count (about Rs 134 crore) would move the check by about 9 percent |

## What are the ladder's answers?

**P1.** Any claim with a verb and a finding, labelled invented, passes, such as "Store sign-ups
rose in September after the festive display went up, so the display stays through Diwali (invented
finding)".

**P2.** Rs 12,000 over 240 orders is Rs 50 an order; over 40 staff-hours it is Rs 300 a staff-hour;
over 5 staff-days it is Rs 2,400 a staff-day. A scheme that pays by the hour reads Rs 300 a
staff-hour.

**P3.** Restate: "you are saying drivers will log off and supply at peak will fall." Answer: "the
drivers who log off are the 180 in the idle tail; the other 880, there before the launch, held at
1.90 trips per online hour in both windows." Concede: "some drivers will log off, and the three outer
zones get few requests at any hour." What would change my mind: "median rider wait at peak rising in
the test."

**P4.** Keep the north-star chart and the discount share of booked revenue, the guardrail that shows
whether orders were bought with discounts; the table of all orders and the return rate go to
Operations' page, the pie of orders by channel to the marketing lead's page, and the method paragraph
to the last page.

**P5.** A strong slide reads: "Fund the store, with a review six months in: we estimate about 1,000
Retail-Plus sign-ups in its first year (400 to 1,600, set by the share of shoppers who sign up), and
its shoppers would buy about Rs 72 crore a year, about half a mature DMart store, which is where a
first-year store should sit. The sign-up share stays a guess until the store's first months. Anand
signs."

**The code lines.** q s r p: the totals first, then the count of driver-weeks, then the ratio that
needs both, then the print.
