# What do strong answers to the Kalpa lab look like?

Released at the end of the lab. The Kalpa slide is scored on its parts and moves, never its number,
so this page gives the shape of a strong answer and the worked estimate, and many different slides
pass.

## What does a strong Kalpa north-star slide carry?

| Part | A strong version |
|---|---|
| The claim | "Kalpa should chase delivered orders per active customer each month, because that is the branch of revenue a customer controls" (the revenue tree of Week 1: revenue is customers times orders per customer times order value) |
| The evidence | One number with its denominator from your own Week 1 or Week 2 work, or the warehouse totals: 1,000 orders from 340 customers, Rs 10.00 crore booked in April to June and Rs 9.84 crore in July to September 2026 |
| The caveat | What the evidence cannot show yet, said on the slide |
| The action | The three guardrails named (revenue per active customer, the discount share of booked revenue, the return rate), the review date, who signs |

The two failures the TA watches for: a slide whose title is a topic ("North-star metric proposal"),
and a defence that retreats at the first objection.

## What does a worked estimate for Anand look like?

Every value is an assumption with its range; a strong sheet says each one aloud.

| Step | Worked values |
|---|---|
| Clarify | A sign-up is a new paying Retail-Plus member whose home store is the new one, in its first twelve months |
| Map | Households near the store x share who shop there in year one x share of those shoppers who sign up |
| Calculate | 50,000 households (range 30,000 to 80,000) x 0.4 who shop in year one (range 0.25 to 0.5) = 20,000 shoppers; x 0.05 who sign up (range 0.02 to 0.08) = about 1,000 sign-ups |
| Validate | 20,000 shopping households x 2 visits a month x Rs 1,500 a visit x 12 months = Rs 72 crore a year, about half of DMart's about Rs 134 crore a store on the year-end count; a first-year store at about half a mature DMart store passes the check |
| The catch | A store in its first year sells less than a mature one, which is why DMart reports its two-year-old stores separately; the average store count (about Rs 146 crore) moves the check by about 9 percent |

## What are the ladder's answers?

**P1.** Any claim with a verb and a finding, labelled invented, passes, such as "Store sign-ups
rose in September after the festive display went up, so the display stays through Diwali (invented
finding)".

**P2.** Rs 12,000 over 240 orders is Rs 50 an order; over 40 staff-hours it is Rs 300 a staff-hour;
over 5 staff-days it is Rs 2,400 a staff-day. A scheme that pays by the hour reads Rs 300 a
staff-hour.

**P3.** Restate: "you are saying drivers will log off and supply at peak will fall." Answer: "the
drivers who log off are the 180 in the idle tail; the other 880, there before the launch, held at
1.90 trips per online hour in both windows." Concede: "some drivers will log off, and the outer zones are thin." What would change my
mind: "median rider wait at peak rising in the test."

**P4.** Keep the north-star chart and one guardrail that answers the CEO's question; the table of all
orders goes to Operations' page, the pie by channel to the marketing lead's page, the discount share
and the return rate to the page of the head who owns each, and the method paragraph to the last page.

**P5.** A strong slide reads: "Fund the store if it can sign about 1,000 members in its first year:
our estimate is 1,000, set mostly by the share who sign up; a first-year store sells about half a
mature DMart store, so the case rests on year two; Anand signs, with a mid-year review."

**The code lines.** q s r p: the totals first, then the count of driver-weeks, then the ratio that
needs both, then the print.
