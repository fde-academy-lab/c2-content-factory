# Which letters answer the drill on cutting a crowded slide to the two elements that carry its claim?

Answers: 1d 2a

The case: a second slide for Thursday's city review must carry one claim, "riders got little for the
Rs 1.9 crore of top-up". A colleague's draft held five elements: the 24 percent line, the median rider
wait (4.8 to 4.6 minutes), the top-up for each extra trip (Rs 2,836 for a Rs 165 trip), the top-up
split (61 percent to low acceptance) and a table of acceptance by zone.

## What does the drill test?

An element stays on the slide only if the claim cannot stand without it. A claim about what riders got
needs what riders got (the wait barely moved) and what it cost per unit of it (each extra trip carried
Rs 2,836 of top-up). Everything else leaves the slide for the appendix page headed by the question of
the role who will ask about it, and nothing is cut silently.

## Why is each key right, and why does each other option fail?

| Item | Key | The question in one line | Why the key holds | Why each other option fails |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | d | Which two elements carry "riders got little for the Rs 1.9 crore"? | The median wait shows what riders got, 0.2 minutes, and Rs 2,836 for each Rs 165 trip shows what it cost. | a: the 24 percent line counts drivers' hours, which riders never feel. b: the split and the zone table say where the money went and who still accepts, which is the morning claim's evidence and one role's detail. c: the 24 percent line again, beside the split. |
| 2 | a | Which appendix page holds the table of acceptance share by zone? | The driver-operations lead asks what happens to the drivers who accept; the table shows where drivers still say yes, which is that page's answer. | b: marketing's question is whether the 140 new drivers lose out, and acceptance by zone does not answer it. c: the finance partner's page holds the season and where the money went, and acceptance by zone settles neither. d: an element that leaves the slide goes to the page of the role who will ask, so that its owner never finds it missing. |

## Which item is worth arguing about?

Item 1, option b. The 61 percent split is the strongest number of the morning, and it is tempting to
keep it everywhere. On this slide it answers a different claim, where the money went, so it belongs
on the finance partner's page; the slide about riders keeps the two numbers about riders.

## Where does cutting to two show up at work?

Every review deck built from a dashboard. The analyst who cuts nine numbers to two, and can say which
page each of the other seven moved to, is the one whose slide survives the meeting.

## What does the deck confirm?

The morning deck's S29 keeps the wait and the cost per extra trip, and its S27 heads one appendix page
per role with that role's question.
