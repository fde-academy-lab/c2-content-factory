# Which letters answer the drill on cutting a crowded slide to the two elements that carry its claim?

Answers: 1c 2a

The case: the city head's slide for Thursday's city review must carry one claim, "the scheme is paying
for online hours that carry no trips". A colleague's draft held five elements: the 24 percent line,
the chart of bookings per peak online hour (Rs 314 to Rs 260), a table of acceptance by zone, the
top-up split (61 percent to low acceptance) and a pie of top-up by week.

## What does the drill test?

An element stays on the slide only if the claim cannot stand without it. The chart is the evidence in
the unit the scheme pays in, and the 61 percent says where the money went; together they make the
claim. Everything else leaves the slide for the appendix page headed by the question of the role who
will ask about it, and nothing is cut silently.

## Which item is worth arguing about?

Item 1. Some will keep the 24 percent line because it is the number the city celebrated. It is the
number the claim argues against: hours rose because idle hours were paid. If it appears at all, it
appears in the driver-operations page with the trips per online hour beside it.

## Where does cutting to two show up at work?

Every review deck built from a dashboard. The analyst who cuts nine numbers to two, and can say which
page each of the other seven moved to, is the one whose slide survives the meeting.

## What does the deck confirm?

The morning deck's rebuilt slide A keeps the chart and the split, and its appendix drawer heads one
page per role with that role's question.
