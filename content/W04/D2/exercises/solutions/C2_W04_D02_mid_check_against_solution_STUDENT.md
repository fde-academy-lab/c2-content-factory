# Which letters answer the drill on the known number each estimate is checked against?

Answers: 1b 2c 3d 4a

The four estimates were UPI payments a day, Delhi Metro journeys a day, a mature grocery store's
yearly sales and Uber drivers online at Hyderabad's evening peak. The published figures were checked
on 12 October 2026: NPCI through Outlook Money (802 million UPI transactions a day in September 2026,
24.07 billion in the month), DMRC through The Tribune (64.06 lakh journeys a day), DMRC through
Republic World (a record 82,44,903 boardings on 17 September 2026), PTI in 2018 (a line-utilisation
count of 45,35,932) and DMart's FY26 release (Rs 66,968 crore, 500 stores, 85 added).

## What does the drill test?

A check counts only when it sits in the same unit, the same period and the same definition as the
estimate. Each wrong option breaks one of the three: a monthly total over the wrong number of days, a
record day's boardings for an average day's journeys, a revenue total with no store count beneath it,
a sign-up count for drivers online at one moment.

## Why is each key right, and why does each other option fail?

| Item | Key | The question in one line | Why the key holds | Why each other option fails |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | b | Which check fits UPI payments on an average day in September 2026? | NPCI's average of 802 million a day is a count, per day, for the same month. | a: September has 30 days, so dividing 24.07 billion by 31 gives 776 million, about 3 percent low. c: Rs 29.37 lakh crore is value in rupees, and the estimate counts payments. d: smartphone owners are people, not payments, and no figure is given. |
| 2 | c | Which check fits Delhi Metro journeys on an average day in 2026? | DMRC's 64.06 lakh journeys a day is the same unit, period and year. | a: 82,44,903 is a record single day of boardings, a different unit on an unusual day. b: line utilisation counts a trip across three lines three times, and it is from 2018. d: three stations' gates are a sample of the network, with no total to compare. |
| 3 | d | Which check fits what one mature large grocery store sells in a year? | Revenue over the average of 415 and 500 stores, about Rs 146 crore, divides the year's sales by the stores that were open across the year. | a: Rs 66,968 crore is the whole chain, 500 stores at once. b: 415 stores leaves out the 85 that opened and sold during the year, so it overstates, about Rs 161 crore. c: 10.8 percent is a growth rate for older stores, not a sales figure. |
| 4 | a | Which check fits Uber drivers online at one weekday evening peak in Hyderabad? | No published count exists, so the honest check is the ratio the case data can confirm, online driver-hours behind each rider request, while the city count's slot reads "to be found". | b: drivers signed up is a different definition from drivers online at one moment. c: 2,56,556 requests over six weeks in twelve zones is a different period and a slice of the city. d: Monday's 1,200 drivers are the enrolled drivers in the case data, not the city's supply. |

## Which item is worth arguing about?

Item 3. Some will argue for the year-end count, about Rs 134 crore, as the conservative check. It
divides a full year of sales by stores that were open for only part of it, so it understates a mature
store. The average count is the better check for a mature store, and either is too high for a store in
its first year, which is the catch in Anand's question in the lab.

## Where does checking in the same unit show up at work?

In every guesstimate round and in every review where someone says "that matches the published
figure". The first question back is "in which unit, for which period, counted how?"
