# Which letters answer the two interview rungs, and why do the hire-grade answers score?

Answers: 1b 2d

The first rung is "present this recommendation to a non-technical leader in two minutes" on Uber's
peak-hour guarantee in Hyderabad (bookings per peak online hour Rs 314 to Rs 260, fare flat at
Rs 165). The second moves the idea to an invented coding-assistant company whose sales deck quotes
acceptance rate, the share of suggestions developers accept.

## What do the rungs test?

Structure before the number, with judgment inside it: the decision and the claim first, then one
number with its denominator, then the caveat, then the action. Three of the four openings in item 1
lead with a claim or a method; only one leads with a claim the data can carry. And the transfer: the
same shape on a system the learner has not met, where the number customers see is easy to inflate.

## Why is each key right, and why does each other option fail?

| Item | Key | The question in one line | Why the key holds | Why each other option fails |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | b | Which opening would a strong interviewer score highest? | It names the decision the leader is making and opens on a claim the data carries, so the leader knows what to listen for. | a: claim-first, but on the flattering denominator: the per-driver-week rise came from four more online hours a week, which the scheme pays for. c: claim-first, but it overclaims: bookings per hour are what riders paid, never what drivers earned, and the monsoon may own part of the fall. d: opens on method, so the leader waits for the point. |
| 2 | d | Which plan keeps the true part of "acceptance rate is what customers see in the deck"? | Customers do see acceptance rate, so it stays as a leading input on the dashboard and in the deck, while the team is judged on code still in the file seven days later, which accepting and then deleting cannot inflate. | a: dropping acceptance everywhere ignores the true part and breaks the sales story. b: keeping acceptance as the north star keeps the number that accepting and then deleting inflates, and a survey of feelings does not fix it. c: what separates c from d is which number the team is judged on; judging it on both equally leaves it free to chase the easier one. |

## Which item is worth arguing about?

Item 2. Some will say the deck should switch to retained code at once. The head of product's objection
is partly right: customers compare vendors on the number in the deck, and a 2022 study of GitHub
Copilot's users found acceptance tracks how productive developers feel (Ziegler and colleagues,
arXiv 2205.06537, checked 12 October 2026). So acceptance stays visible, the team is judged on retained code,
and a holdout of users checks which of the two tracks seat renewals before anyone is paid on it.

## Where do these rungs show up at work?

The first is asked in nearly every analyst and product loop. The second is the measurement question
AI-product teams face whenever a visible proxy (accepted, clicked, thumbs-up) is easier to move than
the outcome it stands for.
