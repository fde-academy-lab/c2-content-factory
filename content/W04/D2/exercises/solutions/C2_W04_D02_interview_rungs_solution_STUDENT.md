# Which letters answer the two interview rungs, and why do the hire-grade answers score?

Answers: 1b 2d

The first rung is "present this recommendation to a non-technical leader in two minutes" on Uber's
peak-hour guarantee in Hyderabad (bookings per peak online hour Rs 314 to Rs 260, fare flat at
Rs 165). The second moves the idea to an invented coding-assistant company whose sales deck quotes
acceptance rate, the share of suggestions developers accept.

## What do the rungs test?

Structure before the number: the claim first, then one number with its denominator, then the caveat,
then the action. And the transfer: the same shape on a system the learner has not met, where the
number customers see is easy to inflate.

## Why is each key right, and why does each other option fail?

| Item | Key | The question in one line | Why the key holds | Why each other option fails |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | b | Which opening scores best on structure before the number? | It opens on the claim and promises one number, which is the structure a non-technical leader can follow. | a: opens on method, three reads and trends, so the leader waits for the point. c: opens on two numbers that point in opposite directions and calls the picture mixed, the claim never arriving. d: promises nine numbers, the first slide's failure spoken aloud. |
| 2 | d | Which plan keeps the true part of "acceptance rate is what customers see in the deck"? | Customers do see acceptance rate, so it stays as a leading input on the dashboard and in the deck, while the team is paid for text still present at the next commit, which an accept-then-delete cannot inflate. | a: dropping acceptance everywhere ignores the true part and breaks the sales story. b: keeping acceptance as the north star keeps the number that accept-then-delete inflates, and a survey does not fix it. c: two equal north stars let each team quote the one that flatters it, which is the per-driver-week trap again. |

## Which item is worth arguing about?

Item 2. Some will say the deck should switch to retained text at once. The head of product's objection
is partly right: customers compare vendors on the number in the deck, so it stays visible while the
team works to the outcome. The answer keeps both, with one of them doing the paying.

## Where do these rungs show up at work?

The first is asked in nearly every analyst and product loop. The second is the measurement question
AI-product teams face whenever a visible proxy (accepted, clicked, thumbs-up) is easier to move than
the outcome it stands for.
