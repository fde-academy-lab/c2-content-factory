## What does the set test?

The set tests the four parts of the one-slide answer, each on its own: a title that acts, evidence read in the unit
the decision buys, a caveat that names what the evidence cannot show yet, and an action with its cost
and its owner. Item 5 hands you a plausible wrong slide to debug.

## Why is each key right, and each other letter wrong?

| Item | Key | The question in one line | Why the key holds | Why each other option fails |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | c | Which title tells the auto driver what to do? | It is a sentence with a verb that says what is true (the same rupees in three more hours) and what to do (drive the short day). | a names a topic. b lists true numbers and leaves the reader to find the point. d is a topic again, made longer. |
| 2 | d | Which number is the evidence for whether to drive the long day again? | The decision buys three more hours, so the evidence is what those hours brought: nothing, since both days earned Rs 1,800. His average fell from Rs 200 an hour to Rs 150 for the same reason. | a rose, which flatters the long day, and rides are not what he spends. b counts rides, not what an hour earns. c is one day with nothing to compare it against. |
| 3 | b | Which line is the caveat? | It names what the evidence cannot show yet: how much of the extra Rs 700 is the season. | a is evidence beside the claim. c is the action. d is the evidence itself. |
| 4 | a | Which action can Ramesh sign? | It says what to do, for how long, what it costs (Rs 170 a day) and who signs. | b has no window, no cost and no owner. c has no date and no owner, and waits for nothing named. d has a window but no cost and no owner. |
| 5 | b | What is wrong with "sales a day rose, so the hours work"? | The decision buys two extra hours, and they sold at most Rs 350 each against Rs 1,190 in an old hour. Rs 700 is sales before the cost of the goods, and part may be the festival, so "the hours work" does not follow; the evening has to be counted on its own. | a: a true number can still answer the wrong question. c: per customer is flat and answers whether customers spent less, a different question. d: a chart adds evidence, and the title still has to say what to do. |

## Which item is worth arguing about?

Item 2, option a. Rupees a ride rose from Rs 90 to Rs 100, which makes the long day look better. The
driver sells his hours, though, and the three hours he added brought him nothing; per ride belongs on
the appendix page for whoever asks about fares.

## Where does the pattern live at work?

It lives in every review slide built from a dashboard, where the analyst picks the denominator the decision is priced in,
writes the claim as the title, puts the caveat beside the number and ends on an action with its cost.
Chapter 4 does it on Uber's twelve driver-weeks, where per hour falls 37 percent, per driver-week 23
percent and per trip not at all.
