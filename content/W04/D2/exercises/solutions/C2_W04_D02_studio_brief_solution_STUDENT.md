# Which letter answers the studio's item, and what does a defence that survives each role sound like?

Answers: 1c

The case: the redesign of Uber's peak-hour guarantee in Hyderabad, presented on one slide at
Thursday's city review to the driver-operations lead, the marketing lead and the finance partner.
Since the launch, gross bookings per peak online hour fell from Rs 314 to Rs 260 at a fare of Rs 165,
and 61 percent of the Rs 1.9 crore top-up went to driver-weeks that accepted fewer than half their
offers.

## What does the item test?

A defence runs in four moves: restate, answer from the evidence, concede the true part, say what would
change your mind. The item tests the fourth, the move that first defences skip: the fact must be one that
would show this objection right, and a true fact that belongs to another role's worry does not count.

## Why is the key right, and why does each other option fail?

| Item | Key | The question in one line | Why the key holds | Why each other option fails |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | c | In move 4 of the reply to "changing the guarantee six weeks in looks like bait", which fact would rightly change the presenter's mind? | Marketing's worry is that the change drives drivers away from signing up, so the fact that would prove it is sign-ups falling once the condition is announced, and the presenter can watch the weekly count. | a: last year's weeks would settle finance's monsoon objection, which is another role's worry. b: the median wait guards against driver operations' worry about supply. d: "nothing would change my mind" skips move 4, and it tells the objector the claim cannot be tested. |

## What does a defence of each role sound like?

| Role | Restate | Answer | Concede | What would change my mind |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Driver operations | "You are saying drivers will log off and supply at peak will fall." | "The drivers who log off are the 180 in the idle tail, whose hours rose 2.1 times while their trips fell 12 percent; the other 880 of the 1,060 there before the launch held at 1.90 trips per online hour in both windows." | "You are right that some drivers will log off, and that the three outer zones get few requests at any hour." | "The median rider wait at peak running more than half a minute above its level in the last six weeks, in the city or in any zone, for two weeks running in the test." |
| Marketing | "You are saying a changed promise looks like bait." | "The floor stays for drivers who accept, and none of the 140 joiners is in the idle tail." | "You are right that the hoarding promised a floor." | "Weekly sign-ups falling once the condition is announced." |
| Finance | "You are saying the fall may be the season ending." | "In the last three weeks after the launch, requests were back at the before window's level and trips per online hour still ran at 1.56; the 1,060 drivers who joined before the launch fell from 1.90 to 1.58 as a group, all of it in the idle tail." | "You are right that the monsoon ended inside the six weeks, and last year's weeks are not in the data." | "Last year's same six weeks showing a fall of this size, a city without the scheme showing the same fall, or the test showing no change." |

## Which part is worth arguing about?

Driver operations' row. The concession is real: an acceptance floor will cost some supply at peak.
The defence holds because the supply that leaves is idle supply, hours that carried 0.8 trips each,
and because the redesign's second guardrail, median rider wait, watches the cost of being wrong.

## Where does the four-move defence show up at work?

The four-move defence comes up in every meeting where a new metric changes the targets people are judged on, and in the interview question
"a stakeholder disagrees with your analysis; what do you do?". The interviewer listens for the
concession made in the open and the evidence named by line.
