# Which letter answers the studio's item, and what does a defence that survives each role sound like?

Answers: 1d

The case: the redesign of Uber's peak-hour guarantee in Hyderabad, presented on one slide at
Thursday's city review to the driver-operations lead, the marketing lead and the finance partner.
Since the launch, gross bookings per peak online hour fell from Rs 314 to Rs 260 at a fare of Rs 165,
and 61 percent of the Rs 1.9 crore top-up went to driver-weeks that accepted fewer than half their
offers.

## What does the item test?

A defence runs in four moves: restate, answer from the evidence, concede the true part, say what would
change your mind. The item also tests whether the evidence fits the objection: an answer that cites a
true number which does not touch the worry has not answered it.

## Why is the key right, and why does each other option fail?

| Item | Key | The question in one line | Why the key holds | Why each other option fails |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | d | Which reply to "changing the guarantee six weeks in looks like bait" runs the four moves with the right evidence? | It restates the worry, answers it with the line that touches it (none of the 140 joiners is in the idle tail, so the condition costs new drivers nothing on this data), concedes that the hoarding promised a floor, and names what would change the analyst's mind (sign-ups falling once the condition is announced). | a: concedes nothing and calls the data final, the defence that loses the room. b: skips the restatement and concedes the claim, then waits six weeks of the same top-up. c: the 1,020 drivers at 1.89 trips an hour answer driver operations, not marketing, and the monsoon is finance's objection. |

## What does a defence of each role sound like?

| Role | Restate | Answer | Concede | What would change my mind |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Driver operations | "You are saying drivers will log off and supply at peak will fall." | "The drivers who log off are the 180 in the idle tail, whose hours rose 2.1 times while their trips fell 12 percent; the other 1,020 held at 1.90 to 1.89 trips per online hour." | "You are right that some drivers will log off, and that the outer zones are thin." | "Median rider wait at peak rising above where it is now, 4.6 minutes, in the test." |
| Marketing | "You are saying a changed promise looks like bait." | "The floor stays for drivers who accept, and none of the 140 joiners is in the idle tail." | "You are right that the hoarding promised a floor." | "Weekly sign-ups falling once the condition is announced." |
| Finance | "You are saying the fall may be the season ending." | "Rider requests rose 1.8 percent; the three busiest weeks after the launch still ran at 1.60 trips per online hour; drivers who joined before the launch fell from 1.90 to 1.58." | "You are right that the monsoon ended inside the window, and last year's weeks are not in the data." | "Last year's same six weeks showing a fall of this size, or the test showing no change." |

## Which part is worth arguing about?

Driver operations' row. The concession is real: an acceptance floor will cost some supply at peak.
The defence holds because the supply that leaves is idle supply, hours that carried 0.8 trips each,
and because the redesign's second guardrail, median rider wait, watches the cost of being wrong.

## Where does the four-move defence show up at work?

In every meeting where a metric changes whose targets it judges, and in the interview question
"a stakeholder disagrees with your analysis; what do you do?". The interviewer listens for the
concession made in the open and the evidence named by line.
