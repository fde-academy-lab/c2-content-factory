# Can your redesign slide survive two rounds of objections from the people who lose under it?

Uber, Hyderabad, the peak-hour earnings guarantee. Since the launch on 31 August 2026, gross bookings
per peak online hour fell from Rs 314 to Rs 260 while the fare held at Rs 165, and 61 percent of the
Rs 1.9 crore top-up went to driver-weeks that accepted fewer than half their offers. Monday's
redesign pays per completed peak trip with an acceptance floor. The city head takes it to Thursday's
city review on one slide, in five minutes, and three roles in the room each lose something under it.

> Illustrative case: the company is real, the situation and the data are synthetic, built for this class.

**Who needs the answer.** The city head, who needs a slide that the three roles can repeat
accurately after the meeting, even while disagreeing with it.

**The questions on the way.**

- What goes on your slide?
- What will each role object to, and which part of it is true?
- How do you answer in four moves?

## What goes on your slide?

This comes up at work in every review: a slide is a claim with its evidence, and the rest waits in an
appendix until someone asks.

| Part | What it must do |
|---|---|
| 1. The claim, as the title | A sentence with a verb that a driver would understand |
| 2. One piece of evidence | One number with its denominator named, or one chart |
| 3. The caveat | In plain sight beside the evidence, never only in the appendix |
| 4. The action | What to do, its cost in rupees and weeks, and who signs |
| Appendix | One page per role, headed by the question that role will ask |

## What will each role object to, and which part is true?

This comes up at work when a metric changes whose targets it judges.

| Role | What they will say | What they lose |
|---|---|---|
| Driver-operations lead | "Drivers will log off, and supply at peak will fall" | Peak online hours as the headline |
| Marketing lead | "The guarantee is on every hoarding; changing it six weeks in looks like bait" | A clean "Rs 300 an hour" line |
| Finance partner | "The fall in bookings per hour is the monsoon ending, not the scheme" | Its own number, top-up per trip, as the headline |

Each objection is partly right. Find the true part before you answer.

---

## How do you answer in four moves?

This comes up in every stakeholder meeting and in every interview that asks how you handle pushback.

1. **Restate** the objection until its owner agrees with your version.
2. **Answer** from the evidence, naming the line of the slide it rests on.
3. **Concede** the true part, in plain words, without dropping the claim.
4. **Say what would change your mind**, naming the fact and how you would get it.

### Q1. The marketing lead objects that changing the guarantee six weeks in looks like bait. Which reply runs the four moves with the right evidence?

*Level L3 · asked in GCC and product-company screens.*

a) Restate the worry; cite rider requests up 1.8 percent; concede nothing; say the data is final
b) Skip the restatement; cite the Rs 1.16 crore paid to idle drivers; concede the claim; wait six weeks
c) Restate the worry; cite 1,020 drivers holding at 1.89 trips an hour; concede the monsoon ended; run the test
d) Restate the worry; cite that no joiner is in the idle tail; concede the floor was promised; watch sign-ups

The idle tail is Monday's name for the 180 drivers whose trips per online hour fell under 1.0 after
the launch; 140 drivers joined after the launch, and 1,020 drivers sit outside the idle tail.

**What the scorer ticks.** For each defence: restated, answered from a named line, conceded the true
