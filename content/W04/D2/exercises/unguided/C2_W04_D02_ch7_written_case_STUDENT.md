## Which sentence opens the page?

This comes up at work whenever a decision is written for someone who was not in the room: the first
sentence is the one they act on.

Ramesh's son (invented) drafted the case for keeping the shop's two extra hours. Sales ran Rs 11,900 a
day in September and Rs 12,600 in October, and Rs 1,190 an hour against Rs 1,050.

### Q1. Which sentence should open Ramesh's page?

*Level L1 · the chapter's closing check.*

a) North star (sales a day): Rs 11,900 to Rs 12,600
b) Keep the longer hours a month and count the evening's sales, because they have brought Rs 700 a day
c) This page sets out every figure for September and October in full, before it gives any view of its own
d) Sales per hour index: 100 to 88, with sales per customer held

## Where does the caveat sit?

This comes up at work in every memo that ends on a request for money: the reader has to meet the doubt
before the cost.

### Q2. Where on the page does the caveat sit?

*Level L2.*

a) First, so the reader meets the doubt before the claim
b) After the action, as a footnote
c) Before the action, so the reader meets the doubt before the cost
d) In an attachment behind the page, for any reader who asks for the detail

## Which last sentence passes the page's rules?

This comes up at work when a page ends: the last sentence is what gets done, and someone has to sign
it.

### Q3. Design. Which last sentence would pass the page's rules on your page to the Uber city head?

*Level L3, a design call · tagged [S] in this programme's calibration: a staple of analyst screens.*

a) Test paying per completed peak trip, with an acceptance floor, on half the drivers for six weeks; city-wide the change saves Rs 58 lakh to Rs 92.7 lakh, and the city head signs
b) Next steps: run further analysis of the guardrail metrics, the north star and the denominators with the data team, and only then decide anything at all about the scheme's future
c) We recommend optimising the north star going forward
d) Consider the denominator carefully before deciding

**What does the chapter add?** A page carries the slide's four parts as four sentences: the claim with
its reason, one number with what it is divided by, the caveat before the action, and the action with
its cost and who signs, in plain words. Chapter 9 is that page, written by hand to the city head.
