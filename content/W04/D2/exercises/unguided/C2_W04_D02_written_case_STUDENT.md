# Can you write the redesign as one page the city head reads in two minutes?

Uber, Hyderabad, the peak-hour earnings guarantee. Since the launch on 31 August 2026, peak online
hours rose 24 percent while completed peak trips rose 3 percent, so gross bookings per peak online hour
fell from Rs 314 to Rs 260 at a fare that held at Rs 165. Rs 1.16 crore of the Rs 1.9 crore top-up
went to driver-weeks that accepted fewer than half their offers. Monday's redesign, as a driver hears
it: "The floor now pays for trips you complete, if you say yes to most offers", which in the rule's
own terms means at least half the requests offered. The monsoon ended inside the six weeks after the
launch, and last year's weeks are not in the data.

> Illustrative case: the company is real, the situation and the data are synthetic, built for this class.

**Who needs the answer.** The Hyderabad city head, who will read your page before Thursday's city
review, with the driver-operations lead, the marketing lead and the finance partner reading over a
shoulder.

**The questions on the way.**

- What is the claim, in the first sentence?
- Which one number, with its denominator, carries it?
- Where does the caveat sit?
- What is the action, what does it cost, and who signs?

---

## What are the rules?

This is AI-free individual work: 35 minutes, on one sheet, by hand, handed in at the end of the
slot. You may not use a laptop, a phone or any notes beyond this brief, which carries every number the page needs.

| What you may use | The number, from the case |
|---|---|
| Fares per peak online hour | Rs 314 in the six weeks before the launch, Rs 260 in the six weeks after; the average fare Rs 165 in both |
| What riders got | Median rider wait at peak, 4.8 minutes before and 4.6 after |
| Where the top-up went | Rs 1.16 crore of the Rs 1.9 crore to driver-weeks that accepted fewer than half the rides offered, 74 percent of it in the three outer zones |
| The saving, from Monday | The redesign stops paying that Rs 1.16 crore (115.9 lakh, since 1 crore is 100 lakh) except to drivers who start accepting and qualify: if half of it is still paid, 115.9 / 2 = Rs 58 lakh is saved over six weeks across the enrolled drivers; if a fifth, 115.9 x 4/5 = Rs 92.7 lakh. The per-trip rate is not set yet, and it decides where in that range, or outside it, the saving lands |
| Who signs | The city head signs any test or change to the guarantee |

## What must the page do?

This comes up at work whenever a decision-maker asks for "a page on it" instead of a meeting.

These are chapter 7's five rules, in the order the city head reads them.

| Rule | What the page carries |
|---|---|
| 1. The first sentence | The claim, with what to do and why, in words a driver would follow |
| 2. The second | One number, what it is divided by and its window: rupees per peak online hour, before and after |
| 3. The caveat, before the action | In a full sentence, never in a bracket at the end |
| 4. The action | What to do, its saving in rupees over a stated number of weeks with the assumption behind it, and who signs |
| 5. Throughout | No method word: denominator, index, guardrail and north star stay off the page, and you say what each means instead |

Each part may carry a sentence or two of support after the sentence the rule names. Chapter 7 taught
these rules on Ramesh's page (invented), and once the page is handed in, notebook 09 runs the same
five rules on a typed copy and recounts the numbers this brief gives.
