# What does a hire-grade answer open on, and does the method survive a change of company?

Two rungs at the top of the day's ladder. The first is the interview question the morning's slide
answers: present a recommendation to a non-technical leader in two minutes, here the Uber peak-hour
guarantee, whose bookings per peak online hour fell from Rs 314 to Rs 260 at a flat Rs 165 fare. The
second moves the same thinking to an AI company: a coding assistant whose sales deck quotes the share
of suggestions developers accept.

> Illustrative case: the company is real, the situation and the data are synthetic, built for this class. The coding-assistant company is invented.

**Who needs the answer.** The interviewer, who listens for the structure before the number, and the
head of product, who has to keep a number customers already see.

**The questions on the way.**

- Which opening line puts the structure before the number?

## Which opening line puts the structure before the number?

This comes up in every interview loop that asks you to present: the first sentence decides whether
the rest is heard.

### Q1. The interviewer says, "Present this recommendation to a non-technical leader in two minutes." Which opening line scores best on structure before the number?

*Level L6 · asked in every screen, product companies most of all.*

a) "I ran the bookings three ways, per hour, per driver-week and per trip, and then the weekly trends."
b) "The scheme is paying for online hours that carry no trips, and one number shows it."
c) "Peak online hours rose 24 percent while trips rose 3 percent, so the picture is mixed."
d) "There are nine numbers on the dashboard, and I will take them one at a time."

## Which plan keeps the true part of the objection?

This comes up at AI companies whenever a product metric shown to customers is easy to inflate.

The coding assistant's sales deck quotes acceptance rate, the share of suggestions developers accept.
A suggestion accepted and then deleted before the next commit still counts as accepted. The analyst
proposes suggested text still present at the next commit, per active developer-week, as the north
star. The head of product objects: "Acceptance rate is what customers see in the deck."

### Q2. Design. Which measurement plan keeps the true part of the head of product's objection?

*Level L6, a design call · asked in AI-startup and product-company screens.*

a) Drop acceptance rate everywhere and report the retained text alone, in the deck too
b) Keep acceptance rate as the north star, and add a quarterly survey of the developers
c) Report acceptance rate and retained text side by side as two equal north stars
d) Make retained text the north star, and keep acceptance rate as a leading input
