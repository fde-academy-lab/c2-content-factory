# What does a hire-grade answer open on, and does the method survive a change of company?

These two items are the rungs at the top of the day's ladder. The first is the interview question the morning's slide
answers: present a recommendation to a non-technical leader in two minutes, here the Uber peak-hour
guarantee, whose bookings per peak online hour fell from Rs 314 to Rs 260 at a flat Rs 165 fare. The
second moves the same thinking to an AI company: a coding assistant whose sales deck quotes the share
of suggestions developers accept.

## Which opening line puts the structure before the number?

This comes up in every interview loop that asks you to present: the first sentence decides whether
the rest is heard.

### Q1. The interviewer says, "Present this recommendation to a non-technical leader in two minutes." Which opening line would a strong interviewer score highest?

*Level L6 · tagged [S] in this programme's calibration: a staple asked everywhere.*

a) "The guarantee works: bookings per driver-week rose 0.8 percent, so we should extend it."
b) "You are deciding whether to change the guarantee: it pays for online hours with no trips."
c) "The scheme caused a 17 percent fall in what drivers earn per hour, so we should end it now."
d) "I ran the bookings three ways, per hour, per driver-week and per trip, and then the trends."

## Which plan keeps the true part of the objection?

This comes up at AI companies whenever a product metric shown to customers is easy to inflate.

The coding assistant's sales deck quotes acceptance rate, the share of suggestions developers accept.
A suggestion accepted and then deleted soon after still counts as accepted. The analyst proposes a new
north star, the one number the team is judged on: suggested code still in the file seven days after
acceptance, per developer who saw a suggestion that week. A leading input is a number that moves
first and can be acted on this week; a guardrail is a number that must not get worse. The head of
product objects: "Acceptance rate is what customers see in the deck."

### Q2. Design. Which measurement plan keeps the true part of the head of product's objection?

*Level L6, a design call · tagged [D] in this programme's calibration: a differentiator.*

a) Drop acceptance rate everywhere and report the retained text alone, in the deck too
b) Keep acceptance rate as the north star, and add a quarterly survey of the developers
c) Judge the team on acceptance and on retained code equally, as two north stars
d) Make retained text the north star, and keep acceptance rate as a leading input
