# Defending under challenge: restate, answer, concede, what would change your mind: which reply holds the claim?

This set practises chapter 3, defending under challenge: restate, answer, concede, what would change
your mind, at Gulmohar Secondary School from the chapter. The school and every number about it are
invented. Item 1 doubles as the chapter's closing check; the rest are the evening's practice, or the
lab's opening twenty minutes.

**Who needs the answer.** A teacher at Gulmohar (invented) who claims at a staff meeting that the new
timetable, from week 3, raised attendance, and the headmaster, who decides whether it stays. The sports
teacher objects: "Attendance rose because the exams began."

The school's attendance, in percent (invented). The new timetable began in week 3 this year only, and
exams ran in weeks 6 to 8 in both years.

| Week | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Last year | 76 | 77 | 76 | 77 | 77 | 85 | 85 | 85 |
| This year | 77 | 76 | 81 | 82 | 81 | 89 | 90 | 89 |

**The questions on the way.**

- Which reply restates the objection?
- Which number is the objection's true part?
- Which reply overclaims?
- What do you do when the objector runs the meeting?

## Which reply restates the objection?

This comes up at work in the first ten seconds after an objection: the objector has to agree with your
version of it before any answer lands.

### Q1. Which reply restates the sports teacher's objection?

*Level L1 · the chapter's closing check.*

a) "Weeks 3 to 5 this year sit 4.7 points above the same weeks last year."
b) "You are saying attendance rose because the exams began, so the timetable may have done nothing."
c) "You are right that exam weeks run about 8 points higher in both years."
d) "If a school with the same exams and the old timetable showed the same rise, I would drop the claim."

---

## Which number is the objection's true part?

This comes up at work whenever an objector points at a number they can see: most objections are partly
right.

### Q2. Which number is the true part of the sports teacher's objection?

*Level L2.*

a) The term's whole rise, 12.8 points, from the first two weeks to the three exam weeks
b) Weeks 1 and 2, at 76.5 in both years
c) Weeks 3 to 5 this year, 4.7 points above last year's same weeks
d) The exam weeks' lift of about 8 points: 8.3 last year, 8.0 this year

---

## Which reply overclaims?

This comes up at work when a presenter treats every objection as an attack, and the room turns.

### Q3. Which reply overclaims, conceding nothing?

*Level L2 · tagged [F] in this programme's calibration: frequent in GCC and product-company screens.*

a) "You may be right; let us wait a term before deciding anything."
b) "Exams are irrelevant; the timetable did it."
c) "Exam weeks run higher in both years, and weeks 3 to 5 rose before any exam."
d) "Let us ask the headmaster which of us he believes."

---

## What do you do when the objector runs the meeting?

This comes up at work when the person who loses under your claim chairs the room that decides it.

### Q4. Design. The sports teacher chairs next week's staff meeting, and she loses timetable slots under the change. What do you do before the meeting?

*Level L4, a design call · tagged [D]: a differentiator that sets a strong candidate apart.*

a) Concede the exams' lift to her first, keep her number on the page, and agree the test that decides it
b) Bring the 12.8-point rise and repeat it until the room agrees with the timetable
c) Ask the headmaster to decide the timetable alone, without a staff meeting
d) Wait a term so that the evidence is stronger before raising the timetable with her or with the staff meeting at all

---

**What does the chapter add?** A defence runs in four moves: restate, answer from a named line of the
evidence, concede the true part out loud, and say what would change your mind. Chapter 5 runs the four
moves against Uber's three roles.
