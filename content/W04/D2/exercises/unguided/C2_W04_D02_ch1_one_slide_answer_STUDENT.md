# The one-slide answer: claim, evidence, caveat, action: what goes in each of the four parts?

This set practises chapter 1, the one-slide answer: claim, evidence, caveat, action, on two invented
worlds: Ramesh's kirana shop from the chapter, and the auto driver's two days from notebook 01's
your-turn cell.
Every number in the set is invented. Item 1 doubles as the chapter's closing check; the rest are the
evening's practice, or the lab's opening twenty minutes.

**Who needs the answer.** Ramesh (invented), who must decide whether his shop keeps its two extra
hours, and an auto driver (invented), who must decide whether to drive the long day again. A slide
that names the wrong denominator lets each of them read the verdict they hoped for.

**The questions on the way.**

- Which title tells the reader what to do?
- Which number is the evidence for a decision?
- Which line is the caveat?
- Which action can someone sign?
- What is wrong with a slide whose every number is right?

## Which title tells the reader what to do?

This comes up at work every time a slide goes to someone who reads only its title: the title has to
carry the decision.

An auto driver (invented) earned Rs 1,800 on Monday from 20 rides in 9 hours, and Rs 1,800 on Tuesday
from 18 rides in 12 hours.

### Q1. Which title tells the auto driver (invented) what to do on the first read?

*Level L1 · the chapter's closing check.*

a) My two days on the road, Monday and Tuesday
b) Rs 1,800 both days; 20 and 18 rides; 9 and 12 hours
c) The long day earned the same in three more hours, so drive the short day
d) Driving update for Monday and Tuesday, with the rides, the hours and the rupees earned

## Which number is the evidence for a decision?

This comes up at work whenever one total can be divided three ways: the decision picks the
denominator, never the other way round.

### Q2. Design. Which number is the evidence for the auto driver's question, whether to drive the long day again?

*Level L2, a design call · tagged [S] in this programme's calibration: a staple of analyst screens.*

a) Rupees a ride: Rs 90 on Monday, Rs 100 on Tuesday
b) Rides a day: 20 on Monday, 18 on Tuesday
c) Rupees on Tuesday alone: Rs 1,800 from 18 rides in 12 hours, the day he would repeat
d) What the three added hours brought: nothing, since both days earned Rs 1,800

## Which line is the caveat?

This comes up at work when the evidence cannot yet separate two causes, and the reader must meet that
doubt beside the number.

Ramesh's shop (invented) opened 10 hours a day in September and 12 in October. Sales ran Rs 11,900 a
day in September and Rs 12,600 in October, Rs 1,190 an hour against Rs 1,050, and Rs 100 a customer in
both months. A festival fortnight fell inside October, and the helper's two extra hours cost Rs 170 a
day.

### Q3. Which line on Ramesh's slide is the caveat?

*Level L2.*

a) Sales per customer held at Rs 100 in both months, so nobody spent less
b) A festival fortnight fell inside October, so part of the extra Rs 700 a day may be the season
c) Keep the hours through November, count the evening's sales apart and decide after thirty days
d) Sales per hour fell from Rs 1,190 in September to Rs 1,050 in October

## Which action can someone sign?

This comes up at work at the end of every review, where nobody can sign an action that names no cost and no owner.

### Q4. Design. Which action line is complete enough for Ramesh to sign?

*Level L3, a design call · tagged [F]: frequent in global capability centre (GCC) and product-company screens.*

a) Keep the hours through November and decide after thirty days; the helper's two hours cost Rs 170 a day, and Ramesh signs
b) Keep the longer hours, since sales a day are up
c) Decide later, when more data has come in from the next few months of trading and the festival season is well and truly over
d) Keep the hours through November and count the evening's sales apart

---

## What is wrong with a slide whose every number is right?

This comes up at work when a true number answers a different question from the one on the table.

### Q5. Design. A classmate's slide for Ramesh opens "Sales a day rose from Rs 11,900 to Rs 12,600, so the longer hours work." What is wrong with it?

*Level L4, a design call · tagged [S].*

a) Nothing: every number in it is right, so it can stand as the claim
b) Rs 700 is sales before the goods' cost, some may be the festival, and each added hour sold at most Rs 350
c) It should open on sales per customer, which held at Rs 100
d) It needs a chart of all 56 days and a table of both months before the title can say anything at all to Ramesh

**What does the chapter add?** A slide carries one decision in four parts, in the order a reader needs
them, and its evidence is divided by what the decision buys. Chapter 4 runs the same three divisions
on Uber's twelve driver-weeks.
