Monday, 26 October: is the number we pay for measuring what we want?

Illustrative case: the company is real, the situation and the data are synthetic, built for this class.

Uber, Hyderabad, the driver app. On Monday 31 August 2026 the city team launched a peak-hour earnings guarantee: an enrolled driver who stays online through a whole weekday peak window is guaranteed Rs 300 per peak online hour, and Uber tops up the gap between the driver's trip earnings and that floor.

Six weeks after the launch against the six weeks before, peak online hours rose from 118,000 to 146,300, completed peak trips rose from 224,200 to 230,900, and trips per peak online hour fell from 1.90 to 1.58.

The ask, due Friday: is the scheme working, which metric should it pay for, and is anyone gaming it? The known caveats: the monsoon ended inside the window, 140 drivers joined after the launch, the app counts a driver as online whenever the app is open, and the three outer zones get few requests at any hour.

Who needs the answer. The city head, with the driver-operations lead, the marketing lead and the finance partner in the room.

The questions on the way.

M1

Can online hours rise while riders wait as long?

The first answer most people give: peak online hours are up 24 percent, so supply is up and riders are better served.

Predict before you move anything. As the guarantee rises towards Rs 250 an hour, what will trips per online hour do?

No prediction yet.

Rs 0 an hour (slider value 0)

At the case's floor of Rs 300 an hour, online hours rise 24 percent, from 118,000 to 146,300, while trips rise 3 percent, from 224,200 to 230,900. Trips per online hour falls from 1.90 to 1.58, the 1.9 to 1.6 on the ticket, and about Rs 1.16 crore of the Rs 1.9 crore top-up went to idle hours. The median rider wait moved from 4.8 to 4.6 minutes.

Online hours count time with the app open, and the driver controls that count. An idle hour in an outer zone earns the same floor as a busy hour in the centre, so the number the scheme pays for rose while riders saw almost no change.

The formula, lasttrips per online hour = completed peak trips / peak online hours = 230,900 / 146,300 = 1.58

M2

Which number should the scheme pay for?

The first answer most people give: pay for whatever went up; online hours rose, so the scheme bought supply.

Predict before you switch. Which choice of paid number makes the most dots drive riders?

No prediction yet.

Paying for online hours

Paying for completed trips with an acceptance floor puts 40 of the 60 dots to driving, against 31 when the scheme paid for online hours. Paying for trips alone lets drivers decline long trips, and paying for accepted share lets them accept and then cancel; the floor on acceptance catches both.

A number a driver can raise without serving a rider will be raised that way, so every paid number needs a guardrail that names the way of gaming it.

The formula, last: the definition's linesnorth star: completed peak trips per enrolled driver per week
guardrail 1: acceptance share at or above a floor the city sets
guardrail 2: median rider wait at peak
guardrail 3: top-up per completed trip
it does not reward: hours online, requests declined, a shrinking enrolled base

M3

What does one line of a definition change?

The first answer most people give: a metric is a formula, and once the formula is right the number is the number.

Predict before you switch. Change the denominator from online hours to enrolled drivers. What happens to the fall from the week before to the week after?

No prediction yet.

With online hours as the denominator, the six drivers fall from 1.90 to 1.19 trips per online hour, down 37 percent. With enrolled drivers, they fall from 19.0 to 14.7 trips per driver, down 23 percent: the extra idle hours drop out of the denominator, so the fall looks smaller. Excluding driver-weeks with acceptance under 0.5 removes drivers E and F's after week, and the four other drivers alone barely move, 1.90 to 1.89.

Every line of a definition moves the number without moving a single rider: the denominator decides whose hours count, the window decides the unit, and an exclusion decides who disappears from view.

The formula, lasta definition names the numerator, the denominator, the window, the exclusions and what it does not reward

M4

Which number moved first?

The first answer most people give: the problem started the week the money number fell.

Predict before you move the week. Acceptance turns down in the launch week. In which week will bookings per online hour turn?

No prediction yet.

Week 6, beginning 24 August 2026 (slider value 6)

Acceptance falls below its before level in week 7, the launch week, and bookings per online hour follows in week 9: a lead of two weeks. Over the six weeks, acceptance averaged 0.86 before and 0.73 after, and bookings per online hour averaged Rs 314 before and Rs 260 after.

T2

Which denominator is the slide using?

The first answer most people give: bookings are bookings, so dividing them differently cannot change the story.

Predict before you switch. The same rupees are read three ways. Which reading rises while bookings per online hour falls?

No prediction yet.

Per online hour

Per online hour the rupees fall from Rs 314 to Rs 260, down 17 percent. Per driver-week they rise about 3 percent, because total bookings rose with trips. Per trip they hold at Rs 165, since the fare did not move. The same rupees tell three stories, so the slide names its denominator in the title.

Wednesday, 28 October: what goes with what beyond chance, and what is a slot worth?

Illustrative case: the company is real, the situation and the data are synthetic, built for this class.

Swiggy Instamart, Pune, the cart page's "frequently bought together" strip of three slots. On Monday 7 September 2026 the strip switched from a curated list to the items most often found in the same cart as the items already in it. Toned milk sits in 71 percent of Pune carts and now appears beside nearly everything.

The strip's click-through fell from 6.1 percent in the three weeks before the change to 3.8 percent in the three weeks after. The data hold 40,000 carts from 1 to 28 September 2026. The ask, due Friday: rank the pairs the strip should show, say why the click-through fell, and put a rupee value on a slot. The known caveats: curd was out of stock in two zones in week three, the top tenth of customers place 38 percent of carts, and the strip itself causes part of the co-occurrence it measures.

Who needs the answer. The category manager and the growth lead.

The questions on the way.

W1

Why is lift a ratio of two areas?

The first answer most people give: if two items turn up together in many orders, they belong together.

Predict before you move the bands. Hold the observed overlap at 0.30 and widen both bands. Where does lift end up?

No prediction yet.

0.60 (slider value 0.60)
0.40 (slider value 0.40)
0.30 (slider value 0.30)

With the overlap held at 0.30 and the bands widened to 0.80 and 0.70, the overlap chance alone would give grows to 0.80 x 0.70 = 0.56, so lift falls to 0.30 / 0.56 = 0.54: the pair now turns up together less often than chance would put it together. At the tea stall's own numbers, 0.30 seen against 0.24 expected, lift is 1.25.

Lift asks how much more often a pair turns up than independence predicts. Popularity widens the bands and raises what chance predicts, so a popular item cannot buy itself a high lift.

The formula, lastlift = P(A and B) / (P(A) x P(B)) = observed area / expected area = 0.30 / (0.60 x 0.40) = 1.25 at the tea stall

W2

What does confidence throw away?

The first answer most people give: three in four bread carts also hold milk, so bread and milk are a strong pair.

Predict before you press. The first button keeps only the carts with bread. How many of the 100 carts will be left?

No prediction yet.

After the first button 34 carts are left, the carts with bread, and 25 of them hold milk, so confidence from bread to milk is 25 / 34 = 0.74. Milk sits in 71 of all 100 carts, so 0.74 is barely above what any cart shows: lift is 0.74 / 0.71 = 1.04.

Confidence shrinks the world to the carts that hold A and forgets the carts that do not. That forgetting is what lets the item in every cart look like a strong partner of anything.

The formula, lastconfidence(A to B) = P(A and B) / P(A) = 0.25 / 0.34 = 0.74

W3

Why does the item in every cart look like the best partner?

The first answer most people give: milk has high confidence with everything, so milk is everyone's best partner.

Predict before you move the share. As milk's share of carts rises, which number moves for the pair bread and milk?

No prediction yet.

0.30 (slider value 0.30)

At the case's milk share of 0.71, confidence from bread toward milk is 0.74 and lift is 1.04. Three in four bread carts hold milk because nearly three in four of all carts do. In the case, every item in at least 2 percent of carts shows confidence between 0.68 and 0.78 toward milk and lift between 0.96 and 1.08, which is why a strip ranked by how often items appear together puts milk beside nearly everything.

Confidence carries the partner's popularity inside it. Lift divides that popularity out, so it stays near one for a partner that is only popular.

The formula, lastconfidence follows popularity; lift divides it out: lift = confidence(A to B) / P(B) = 0.74 / 0.71 = 1.04

Thursday, 29 October: who is leaving, and at which step?

Illustrative case: the company is real, the situation and the data are synthetic, built for this class.

Zomato Gold, Chennai, the membership and the app's order flow. The membership dashboard reports month-two retention for the city as flat at 38 percent for June, July, August and September 2026, while every signup cohort's own curve falls with age and each new cohort starts lower: the January 2026 cohort kept 58 percent of its members in month one, the July cohort 49 percent, and the August cohort, signed up in a sale week and 2.6 times a normal month, 31 percent.

On Monday 14 September 2026 a new checkout flow shipped on Android. Since then members' add-to-cart to order conversion on Android fell from 58 to 44 percent, while iOS held at 57 and non-members on Android at 52. The ask, due Friday: is it a cohort problem or an everyone problem, which funnel stage owns the loss, and what should be fixed first. The known caveats: the home carousel opens a menu without a search, sessions and members are different units, and the sale cohort was bought on a discount.

Who needs the answer. The membership lead, with the app team, who will say the stage is fine.

The questions on the way.

Th1

How can every batch shrink while the average stays flat?

The first answer most people give: the dashboard reads 38 percent every month, so nothing has changed.

Predict before you step. The August sale cohort, 2.6 times a normal month, joins the table in September. What does the dashboard's 38 percent do?

No prediction yet.

Reporting month: February 2026
January: 5,000 members, 1.0 times normal (slider value 1.0)

Every row falls month by month, and each cohort starts lower than the one before: 58, 56, 55, 53, 52, 50 and 49 percent in month one, then the sale cohort's 31. The dashboard still reads 38 percent from June to September. The cohorts it counts sit on slow plateaus, and the newest, weakest, biggest cohort is outside its count until it reaches its second month in October.

A blend can stay flat while every part of it shrinks, as long as new parts keep arriving and the weakest part has not been counted yet. The flat number hides a collapse that only the rows show.

The formula, lastblend = sum of (cohort size x cohort rate) / sum of sizes, over the cohorts the dashboard counts

Th2

What is the flat number made of?

The first answer most people give: if the average holds at 50 percent, each batch is holding at 50 percent.

Predict before you move a batch. Batch A of 100 stood at 50 percent last month and stands at 40 now; a new batch B of 400 joins at 52.5 percent. What does the blend do?

No prediction yet.

400 members (slider value 400)
0.40 (slider value 0.40)

Last month 50 of batch A's 100 were coming, 50 percent. This month A has 40 of 100 and B has 210 of 400, so 250 of 500 are coming: still 50 percent, while batch A fell from 50 to 40.

The blend is an average weighted by size, so a large young batch at a high early rate holds it up while every batch falls with age. Next month B ages too, and the blend has nothing left to hide behind.

The formula, lastblend = (100 x 0.40 + 400 x 0.525) / (100 + 400) = (40 + 210) / 500 = 0.50

No prediction yet.

Carousel entries are counted

Counted per session with carousel entries in, menu views are 118 percent of searches, because the home carousel opens a menu without a search: search is a step members can skip, and the rate above 100 is an artefact of the count. Menu view to add-to-cart is 34 percent per session and 61 percent per member, the same people in two units. The loss on the ticket sits further on, at add-to-cart to order for members on Android after 14 September, 58 to 44 percent, while iOS and non-members held.

A rate above 100 percent says the two counts are not the same people in the same unit. Before blaming a step, name the unit on the chart and check that every member had to pass the step before.

The formula, lasteach step's rate divides by the step before, in one unit, and the chart names the unit

Is it novelty or habit?

The first answer most people give: launch week brought the most users ever, so the AI app has found its market.
