# How is a paid number gamed, and which line of a definition stops each way?

*Balaji Kirana and the tuition app are invented for chapter 5, with every number in them. Uber's case is illustrative: the company is real, the situation and the data are synthetic, built for this class. Wells Fargo and US Veterans Affairs are public records.*

**The example, in short.** Balaji Kirana (invented), a corner shop in Hyderabad, pays its delivery
boy Rs 20 for each delivery marked done in the shop's app. In four weeks he marked all 50 orders

dispatch time the boy types himself every order reads on time; on the shop's bill clock 35 of 50

## Which single change stops gate-marking at the lowest cost?

**Gaming** is raising a number without doing what it stands for. A definition resists it when the
count needs a receipt that the person served gives.

### Q1. Design. The owner can change one thing this week to stop parcels being marked done at the gate. Which change stops it at the lowest cost?

a) A second boy who rides along and checks every single handover

b) A done press that counts only once the customer's OTP is entered

c) A photo of every parcel at the door, checked by the owner each night

d) A weekly phone call to every customer who ordered that week

## Which line stops each of four ways of gaming, three of them public?

| # | How the count was raised |
|---|---|
| 1 | The illustrative Uber case: a driver keeps the app open in a zone with no riders, and every hour counts |
| 2 | Wells Fargo: accounts that customers never used counted toward the bank's cross-sell figure, the products it sold each household |
| 3 | US Veterans Affairs: schedulers typed the next open slot as a patient's "desired date", so waits read zero |
| 4 | Wells Fargo, 2013: accounts for sending money abroad joined the count without investors being told |

| Line | What the definition says |
|---|---|
| G1 | A wait clock that starts when the system logs the request |
| G2 | A dated change log printed beside the number |
| G3 | A count that needs a trip a rider asked for |
| G4 | A larger sample of weeks before any decision |
| G5 | An account that counts only once the customer uses it |

## Which numerator and denominator resist a tuition app's gaming?

A tuition app (invented) pays its teachers per doubt resolved, and a doubt counts as resolved when
the teacher marks it so. The app routes each doubt a student raises to one teacher, and teachers can
also choose which of their routed doubts to accept.

### Q3. Design. Which numerator and denominator should the app pay on, so that neither can be raised without helping a student?
