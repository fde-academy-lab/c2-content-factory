*Illustrative case: the company is real, the situation and the data are synthetic, built for this class.*

Uber's Hyderabad city team paid Rs 1.9 crore in top-up over the six weeks after it launched a Rs 300
peak-hour floor on 31 August 2026. The redesign keeps the floor and pays it only to driver-weeks whose
share of offers accepted stays at or above a floor the city sets, with completed peak trips per
enrolled driver online at peak as its north star. Round 3 of the war game priced it and defended it
against the driver-operations lead, the marketing lead and the finance partner.

## What does the set test about pricing and defending a redesign?

A redesign earns a hearing when it says what the old rule cost, what the new rule saves with its
assumption in the same sentence, whom its threshold touches, and why the people measured cannot move
its north star on their own. The old rule sent 61 percent of the top-up, Rs 1.159 crore, to
driver-weeks that accepted fewer than half the offers they were shown. The saving is a range because
some idle drivers would start accepting enough to qualify, and the floor belongs in the gap between
the idle tail's highest week, 0.46, and every other driver's lowest week, 0.78.

## Which item is worth arguing about?

Item 2, option a. Rs 1.159 crore is the saving only if no idle driver changes behaviour, and a rule
that pays for trips is written precisely so that some of them will. The honest sheet says the range
and the assumption, and names what would settle it: the test of the redesign. The gap in item 3 is
also cleaner here than on any real city, because the data is synthetic; on a real city the two
groups overlap and the floor is a trade-off the test measures.

## Where does moving a headline number to a net figure show up at work?

Eternal defines net order value as GOV minus discounts (its Q1 FY26 shareholder letter), and in its
Q1 FY27 letter it stopped disclosing GOV because, in its words, "NOV more accurately reflects
underlying performance" (both checked 12 October 2026). The redesign makes the same move on a
smaller scale: it stops paying for the gross figure, hours online, and pays for the one that carries
the service, completed trips, with the gross figure kept off the top line.
