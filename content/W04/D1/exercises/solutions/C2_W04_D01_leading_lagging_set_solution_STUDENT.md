*Kalpa Retail is the programme's fictional client, and its numbers here are names with no values. Balaji Kirana and the chase are invented for chapter 3 and chapter 5, with every number in them.*

Kalpa's growth plan proposes delivered orders per active customer per month as its north star, and its dashboard holds
eight numbers from Week 1's revenue tree. Balaji Kirana (invented) judges each week on the share of
orders billed that customers confirmed with their OTP. The set asks which numbers move before the
north star and which confirm it afterwards.

## What does the set test about leading and lagging numbers?

A leading number moves before the north star, while the team can still act on it this week; a
lagging number adds up what has already happened. The kind of number does not decide which it is,
and neither does how often somebody reads it: the question is when it moves against the north star.
In the chase, the runs an over needed reached 10.0 after over 10 and the result arrived at over 20.

## Which item is worth arguing about?

Item 1, number 6. A group that reads "share" as a slow, averaged number calls it lagging. It is read
this week, it moves the day a van runs late, and a late delivery this week is a delivered order the
month may lose: the team can act on it before the north star moves. The two learners who defend a
call aloud should take number 6 and number 2.

## Where does a leading number show up at work?

In Uber's case (illustrative), the share of offers drivers accepted fell to its new level, 0.729, in
the scheme's launch week and stayed there, while bookings per online hour fell to Rs 271.0 that week
and kept falling to Rs 259.1 by the third. A city team watching acceptance would have seen the
change in week one.
