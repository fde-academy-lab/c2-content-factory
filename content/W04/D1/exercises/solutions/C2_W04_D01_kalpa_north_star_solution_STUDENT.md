# Which letters write Kalpa's growth-plan north star on the morning's template?

Answers: 1b 2d 3a 4c 5b

*Kalpa Retail is the programme's fictional client: Kalpa and its people are invented, and the data are synthetic.*

Meera Raghavan, Kalpa Retail's CEO, asked for one number the whole company chases and the numbers that
stop it being gamed. Three department heads proposed their own: orders per month, app sessions, and
reorders per member, which the app can raise by placing reorders on its own. The growth plan's north
star is delivered orders per active customer per month, and this set wrote it on the same seven
lines the morning used for Uber's drivers.

## What does the set test about moving the method to a new company?

The method does not change when the company does. The numerator counts the thing the business wants
(an order a customer received), the denominator is fixed before anyone knows how the month went, the
guardrail catches the cheap way of hitting the number, the inputs are the ones a team can move this
month, and the window keeps a sale or a festival out of one side of the comparison.

| Line | Kalpa's growth plan |
|---|---|
| North star | Delivered orders per active customer per month |
| Numerator | Orders delivered to the customer in the month, one per order received |
| Denominator | Customers with a delivered order in the three months before the month, the count printed beside the rate |
| Window | The calendar month; any before-and-after comparison keeps a sale inside neither window or both |
| Exclusions | Placements cancelled, merged into a later order or still pending; test accounts; staff orders |
| What it does not reward | Placements the app makes on its own, sessions, coupons sent |
| Guardrails | Revenue per active customer; discount share of booked revenue; the return rate |
| Gaming note | Revenue per customer catches frequency bought with small baskets; the discount share catches frequency bought with coupons; the return rate catches orders pushed on customers who send them back |
| Leading inputs | Add-to-cart events per active app member; new Retail-Plus sign-ups |

## Which item is worth arguing about?

Item 2, option a. "Customers with an order this month" is the most common definition of an active
customer on a dashboard, and it makes the north star blind to the customers it most needs to see:
anyone who stops ordering leaves the denominator in the same month. The morning made the same call
for Uber's drivers, whose denominator is fixed by being online at peak before any trip is counted.

**Kavya's review.** Kavya Nair, the senior analyst on Kalpa's data team, asks the same four things of every number: "Show me the baseline, then the denominator, then what was delivered, and get to
the same number a second way before it goes in front of Meera."

## Where does an "active" definition show up at work?

Spotify counts a monthly active user as an account with more than zero milliseconds of consumption
in the thirty days (Spotify's annual report on Form 20-F for 2025, checked 12 October 2026). The
window and the threshold are written down, so a reader knows an account that played anything at all
counts as active. Kalpa's definition writes its window and its threshold down the same way, so a
reader knows a placement nobody received does not count.

## What does the lab notebook confirm?

The lab notebook's checks prove the table itself: 120 members, every placement from 20 August to 30
September 2026, every status one of delivered, cancelled, merged or pending. The two your-turn cells
are the ones that show what the head of Retail-Plus's number did across the two windows, and the TA
walks them at the end of the lab.
