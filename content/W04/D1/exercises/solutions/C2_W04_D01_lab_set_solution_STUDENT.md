# Which letters rebuild the city's count and cut the dashboard to what a decision needs?

Answers: 1b 2d 3c 4a 5a

*Illustrative case: the company is real, the situation and the data are synthetic, built for this class.*

Uber's Hyderabad city team launched a Rs 300 peak-hour floor on 31 August 2026. Six weeks each side of
the launch: peak online hours 118,000 and then 146,300, completed peak trips 224,200 and then 230,900,
trips per peak online hour 1.90 and then 1.58. The lab set rebuilt that rate from the raw table and
cut the city head's dashboard of nine numbers to the five a decision needs.

## What does the set test about rebuilding a count and cutting a dashboard?

A rate is a ratio of two sums, and the order of the steps decides whether the code prints it: the
window is flagged before anything is grouped, the sums come before the division, and the print
comes last. A dashboard earns its top page with one north star, the guardrails that protect riders,
and the inputs a team can move this week; everything else is a diagnostic below it.

## Which item is worth arguing about?

Item 1, option a. Averaging each driver-week's rate looks like the natural way to get "trips per
hour", and it prints 1.71 after the launch, close enough to 1.58 to pass a glance. It gives a driver
online for one hour the same weight as a driver online for forty, so the idle tail's long weeks count
for as little as anyone's short ones. Problem 1's key adds every trip and every hour first and
divides once.

## Where does cutting a dashboard to one north star show up at work?

The SEC's 2020 order against Wells Fargo describes a bank that reported one cross-sell ratio, products
per household, with no line beside it to say whether any of those products were used (SEC order,
Release 34-88257, checked 12 October 2026). A top page with one number and nothing to guard it is the
dashboard that order describes.
