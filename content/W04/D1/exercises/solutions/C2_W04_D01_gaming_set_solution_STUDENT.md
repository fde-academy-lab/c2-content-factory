# Which letters name the lines that stop each way of gaming a paid number?

Answers: 1b 2a 3d

*Balaji Kirana and the tuition app are invented for chapter 5, with every number in them. Uber's case is illustrative: the company is real, the situation and the data are synthetic, built for this class. Wells Fargo and US Veterans Affairs are public records.*

Balaji Kirana (invented) paid its delivery boy Rs 20 for each delivery marked done: he marked 50,
customers confirmed 40 with their OTP, and all 6 complaints sat on the 10 orders with no OTP. The set
asks which change stops that at the lowest cost, which line stops each of four public ways of
gaming, and how a tuition app should write its pay rule.

## What does the set test about gaming?

A paid number is gamed in four ways: the person paid controls the count, the count needs no receipt
or use, the person timed sets the clock's start, or the definition changes quietly. Each has a line
that stops it: a count the person served confirms, a receipt in the numerator, a clock the system
starts, and dated changes printed beside the number. A denominator the person paid can choose is a
fifth door, and it stays shut only when the people served set it.

## Why is each key right, and why does each other option fail?

| Item | Key | The question in one line | Why the key holds | Why each other option fails |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | b | Which change stops gate-marking at the lowest cost? | The OTP is a receipt the customer gives at the door, so a press without it counts for nothing, and the shop's app already sends it: it costs no extra person and no extra hour. | a: a second boy doubles the delivery wage to check what a code already proves. c: photos cost the owner time every night and prove a parcel was at a door, never that a customer took it. d: a weekly call finds gate-marking a week late, after the pay is out. |
| 2 | a | Which line stops each way of gaming, one left over? | An open app counts no hour once only trips a rider asked for count (G3); an unused account stops counting once use is required (G5); a clock the system starts cannot be set by the scheduler (G1); a dated change log shows the 2013 addition beside the number (G2). G4 is left over. | b: it swaps G3 and G5, and a usage condition says nothing about a driver's hours. c: it swaps G1 and G2, and a change log cannot stop a typed date. d: a larger sample of weeks counts the same unused accounts again. |
| 3 | d | Which numerator and denominator resist the tuition app's gaming? | The student confirms the resolution, a receipt from the person served, and the denominator is every doubt raised, which no teacher chooses, so skipping hard doubts lowers the rate. | a: the teacher controls both counts. b: the numerator is right, and the teacher still picks the easy doubts that make up the denominator. c: the teacher marks the doubts and sets the hours online, so both counts are the teacher's. |

## Which item is worth arguing about?

Item 3, option b. It fixes the numerator with a receipt and looks finished, which is why a hurried
group stops there. The denominator is the second door: a teacher who accepts only easy doubts keeps
a high rate while the hard doubts go unanswered. A definition resists gaming only when the people
paid control neither line.

## Where did these four ways happen in public?

At Wells Fargo, the SEC's 2020 order found that the bank's cross-sell figure counted accounts and
products with no requirement that customers use them, and that remittance accounts joined the count
during 2013 without investors being told (SEC order, Release 34-88257, checked 13 October 2026). At
the US Veterans Affairs facility in Phoenix, 30 of 79 staff interviewed by the inspector general said
they used a wrong desired date, producing false waits of zero days (VA Office of Inspector General
report 14-02603-267, checked 13 October 2026).
