# Which letters name the number to chase and the guardrail that catches the harmful way of hitting it?

Answers: 1c 2d 3a

*The driving school is invented for chapter 2, with every number in it. Uber's case is illustrative: the company is real, the situation and the data are synthetic, built for this class.*

A driving school (invented) printed a pass rate of 0.60 in 2025 and 0.90 in 2026 while licences per
learner who enrolled fell from 0.60 to 0.36, because 40 learners took the test where 100 had. Uber's
Hyderabad city team is choosing the north star of its redesigned peak-hour scheme and the guardrail
on its budget.

## What does the set test about north stars and guardrails?

A north star names the value the people served get: a licence for a learner, a trip for a rider. A
guardrail is chosen by the harmful move it catches, so the question to ask of every candidate is what
would happen to it if the north star were hit the wrong way. A guardrail that is another count of
growth, or that divides by the number being gamed, catches nothing.

## Why is each key right, and why does each other option fail?

| Item | Key | The question in one line | Why the key holds | Why each other option fails |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | c | Which guardrail would have caught the school's 2026 move? | The share of enrolled learners who took the test fell from 1.00 to 0.40, and that fall is what lifted the rate: the guardrail sees the denominator shrink. | a: 100 enrolled in both years, so enrolment could not see the move. b: passes per instructor is the outcome counted again, and it cannot say why the rate rose. d: the city's rate is read outside the school and cannot see which of its learners took the test. |
| 2 | d | Which number should the redesigned scheme chase? | A completed peak trip is the value a rider gets, and it needs a rider's request, so a driver cannot raise it by staying online in an empty zone. | a: hours are the count the old scheme paid for and drivers raised. b: the share of offers accepted is a guardrail, which catches declining, and says nothing about trips completed. c: the top-up is the scheme's cost, read on its own guardrail. |
| 3 | a | Which guardrail catches a top-up that runs away from the trips? | Top-up per completed trip rises only when money is paid for hours with no trips; after the launch it read Rs 82.3 a trip. | b: the month's total rises with honest growth too, so it cannot tell more trips from idle hours. c: it divides by the count drivers inflate, and the floor caps the top-up at Rs 300 an hour, so it stays inside its design however many paid hours serve no rider. d: it rises when idling grows and when drivers leave, and it is tied to no trip. |

## Which item is worth arguing about?

Item 3, option c. Top-up per online hour sounds like the natural unit, since the floor is paid by the
hour. That is the trap: it divides by the count drivers inflate, and since the floor caps the top-up
at Rs 300 an hour, the guardrail reads as within design whether the hours bought trips or nothing.
Top-up per completed trip divides by what riders got, so an idle hour pushes it up at once. A
guardrail divides by something the people paid cannot inflate.

## Where does a north star that matches what a business is trying to do show up at work?

Swiggy Instamart's Maxxsaver unlocks extra discounts once a cart passes a threshold. Launched at the
start of the quarter to 30 June 2025, it had been used by "over 28% of our MTUs", the letter's
monthly transacting users; Instamart's average order value rose 16 percent on the quarter before to
Rs 612, and the letter says the offer sometimes results in "a slightly lower number of orders by the
same user" (Swiggy's Q1 FY26 shareholder letter, checked 13 October 2026). A north star of orders per
user, read with no guardrail on value, would have shown the offer's success as a fall.
