# How does a trainer who did not write the day run Monday's war game?

**TRAINER ONLY.** The war game is the day's signature activity: three city-team roles, each partly
right, and a definition that has to survive them. Round 1 opens the case after the break, once the
six concept chapters have taught the proxy test and the ways of gaming; round 2 is case chapters 7 to
9; and round 3, chapter 10, is the last round, after which chapter 11 and the 20-minute close finish
the trainer's time.

**Who needs the answer.** A trainer running the day cold, with the role cards
(`trainer/C2_W04_D01_role_cards_TRAINER.md`) in hand.

**The questions on the way.** Who plays what? What materials does each group need? What happens in
round 1, minute by minute? What does round 2 add? What happens in round 3? What do you do when it
stalls?

---

## Who plays what?

| Role | Who plays it | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| The driver-operations lead | The trainer, or a learner reading the card | Proposes peak online hours; in round 3, attacks with "drivers will log off" |
| The marketing lead | The trainer, or a learner reading the card | Proposes enrolled drivers; in round 3, attacks with "changing terms looks like bait" |
| The finance partner | The trainer, or a learner reading the card | Proposes top-up per completed trip; in round 3, attacks with "it is the monsoon's end" |
| The groups | The room, in nine groups of about four (35 learners) | Attack the roles' numbers in round 1; write, price and defend the redesign in round 3 |
| The scorer | One learner per group | Holds the proxy-test card and scores each attack in round 3 |

If learners read the roles, pick three who will commit to the role's voice, give them the card five
minutes before the day starts, and tell them their job is to be partly right.

## What happens in round 1, minute by minute?

Twelve minutes, deck S54 to S58, right after the break: the room now holds chapter 1's proxy test
and chapter 5's four ways of gaming, and attacks with them.

| Minutes | What happens | What you say |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | The chapter's opener and its map (S54): the topics it applies | "Chapter 1's test and chapter 4's lines, now on Uber's own numbers." |
| 3 | Read the full ticket (S55) in the order it is written | "This is the whole ticket. Company, what launched, what moved, the data, the ask, the caveats." |
| 1 | Where today ends (S56) | "By the close, one sentence with three blanks will be filled from the data." |
| 1 | The board's four questions (S57) | "Every number anyone proposes today gets these four questions." |
| 5 | The three roles state their numbers (S58); each group gives one way to game each | "Driver operations wants hours. Marketing wants enrolled drivers. Finance wants top-up per trip. Each group: one way each number could rise while riders gained little." |

Take one answer per role from three different groups. Write each on the board beside the role's
number. Do not correct anything yet: round 2 is where the room learns to attack with a method.

## What does round 2 add?

Round 2 is case chapters 7 to 9. Name it once at S59, after round 1's attacks are on the board:
"Round 2 is the rest of the morning. By lunch you will attack with numbers." Each chapter close adds a region to the concept
board, and each region answers one of round 1's attacks with the data.

## What happens in round 3?

Twenty-five minutes, chapter 10, deck S85 to S90, after lunch. Maxxsaver and Eternal moved to
chapter 2 (S20), so round 3 recalls them in one line on S90 and spends its time on the roles.

| Minutes | What happens | What you say |
|---|---|---|
| 1.5 | The chapter's opener and its map (S85): it applies chapter 5's lines and chapter 6's page | "The page you filled in chapter 9 now meets the people who lose something under it." |
| 2 | The brief (S86): each group writes the redesign on the template, prices it, and answers the roles | "Your definition survives when every role's attack is answered by one of its lines or a guardrail." |
| 12 | Groups write the seven lines, answer the four items of the redesign set, and write the saving with its assumption | Visit each group as one role, rotating; read the role's attack in the role's voice |
| 2.5 | The three attacks (S87), read aloud in the roles' voices | "Each attack is partly right. Name the true part before you answer the rest." |
| 4 | S88 and S89: which line answers "drivers will log off"; where the acceptance floor sits | "The median wait is the line that moves for riders; the acceptance floor sits between 0.46 and 0.78." |
| 2 | S90: the saving as a range | "A group that wrote Rs 1.16 crore assumed nobody changes. What does an idle driver do on Monday under the new rule?" |
| 1 | Scoring: each scorer reads its group's tally of answered, conceded and lost | "Lost means nothing on your sheet meets it. Tuesday's slide has to win that role back." |

## What do you do when it stalls?

| Stall | The one move |
|---|---|
| Round 1: nobody can say how hours could be gamed | Recall chapter 1's canteen (S9 and S10): "who controls the count?" |
| Round 1: the room defends hours | Ask who decides when the app is open; E and F wait for S60, where their 16 and 14 hours and 3 and 2 trips are counted by hand |
| Round 3: a group writes a growth target as a guardrail, such as bookings per driver up 20 percent | Ask: "what would that guardrail catch?" |
| Round 3: a group drops the Rs 300 floor to answer finance | Read the marketing lead's card: the floor was the promise |
| Round 3: a group writes "saves Rs 1.16 crore" | Ask what the 180 idle drivers do under the new rule; point at S90 |
| Round 3: a group cannot answer driver operations | Ask what happened to the other 880 drivers' trips per hour (1.90 in both windows) |
