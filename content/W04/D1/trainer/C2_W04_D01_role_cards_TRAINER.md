# Which number does each role propose, and what does each say against the room's definition?

**TRAINER ONLY.** Print one card per role and one proxy-test card per group. The trainer voices the
roles, or three learners read them. Every number on a card is in
`internal/C2_W04_D01_facts_INTERNAL.yaml`; the one public fact is Uber's own definition in its 10-K
for 2025 (case file M5, checked 12 October 2026).

**Who needs the answer.** The trainer running the war game: round 1 opens the day with these cards,
and round 3 closes the trainer's time with the objections on them.

**The questions on the way.** What does the driver-operations lead want, and what will they say?
What does the marketing lead want? What does the finance partner want? What does each scorer hold?

---

## What does the driver-operations lead want, and what will they say?

| | |
|---|---|
| **The number proposed** | Peak online hours |
| **The honest reason** | Supply at peak is driver operations' job, and hours are what the team can move this week, with onboarding calls and log-in nudges. |
| **How their own team could hit it while riders waited longer** | Nudge drivers to log in wherever they are, including the quiet outer zones, so hours rise with no trip behind them. |
| **Round 1, said aloud** | "Hours are up 24 percent, from 118,000 to 146,300. The scheme did what it was built to do." |
| **Round 3, the attack** | "Pay for trips and drivers will log off. Supply at peak will fall, and you will have to explain it to riders." |
| **The part that is true** | True for the idle tail: 180 drivers whose hours went to 2.1 times while their trips fell 12 percent. |
| **The answer the room should reach** | The other 880 drivers who were there before the launch made 1.90 trips an hour in both windows, so their supply is real and stays paid; if any real supply logs off, the median wait guardrail shows it. |
| **What they lose** | The headline that made the scheme look like a success. |

---

## What does the marketing lead want, and what will they say?

| | |
|---|---|
| **The number proposed** | Enrolled drivers, with the guarantee as the acquisition story |
| **The honest reason** | The guarantee is on every hoarding, and 140 drivers joined after the launch, 1,200 in all. |
| **How their own team could hit it while riders waited longer** | Sign up drivers for the floor who never drive at peak. |
| **Round 1, said aloud** | "140 new drivers in six weeks. That is the scheme working." |
| **Round 3, the attack** | "Changing the terms six weeks in looks like bait. Drivers signed up for an hourly floor." |
| **The part that is true** | The floor was the promise, and breaking it costs trust. |
| **The answer the room should reach** | The redesign keeps the floor for every driver who accepts at or above the city's floor, so the promise holds for drivers who serve riders. Uber's own 10-K counts a monthly active platform consumer only when that consumer completed a ride or received a delivery in the month, so even Uber's own count asks for activity, never a sign-up alone. |
| **What they lose** | The sign-up count as the measure of success. |

---

## What does the finance partner want, and what will they say?

| | |
|---|---|
| **The number proposed** | Top-up per completed trip |
| **The honest reason** | The top-up is finance's money, Rs 1.9 crore in six weeks, and a per-trip figure ties the spend to the output. |
| **How their own team could hit it while riders waited longer** | Set the floor so low that almost nobody qualifies: the top-up per trip falls while drivers leave and riders wait. |
| **Round 1, said aloud** | "Rs 82.3 of top-up on every completed trip. That is the number I can defend to my head." |
| **Round 3, the attack** | "The fall in bookings per hour is the monsoon's end. The scheme is a cost problem, nothing more." |
| **The part that is true** | Partly: the season moves rider requests, and the top-up budget is finance's call. |
| **The answer the room should reach** | The three busiest weeks of the twelve already read 1.60 trips per online hour, and the core zones fell from 1.90 to 1.79, so the season does not explain the fall away; and 61 percent of the top-up, Rs 1.159 crore, went to driver-weeks that accepted fewer than half their offers. |
| **What they lose** | Treating the scheme as a budget line only. |

## What does each scorer hold?

One scorer per group holds this card through rounds 1 and 3 and asks its three questions of every
number proposed and every definition attacked.

> **The proxy-test card.**
>
> 1. Could a team hit this number while riders waited longer?
> 2. Who controls the count: the rider, the driver or the app?
> 3. What does the definition deliberately not reward?
>
> **Round 3 scoring, per attack:** answered (a line of the definition or a guardrail meets it),
> conceded (the group names the true part and keeps the line), or lost (nothing on the sheet meets
> it). A definition that survives only by dropping the guarantee has lost the driver-operations
> lead.
