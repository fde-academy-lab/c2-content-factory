# How do you know your take-home is right before anybody reads it?

*Illustrative case: the company is real, the situation and the data are synthetic, built for this class.*

**Who needs the answer.** You do, tonight, before Tuesday's session walks one learner's page in
front of the room. Every checkpoint below is something you can verify alone.

**The questions on the way.** Does your sentence to the driver pass? Does every one of your seven
lines say what it should, in plain words first? Does your gaming note name a way and a line? Does your
number for Tuesday carry its denominator and window? Does your proxy test fit on one line?

If a checkpoint does not hold, the hint beside it names the likeliest reason.

---

## Does your sentence to the driver pass?

| # | Checkpoint | What you should see | If it does not hold |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | It names what earns the top-up | Completed trips at peak, with the driver accepting most of what the app offers | If it says "being online", it describes the old scheme |
| 2 | It names what no longer earns it | Hours with the app open and no trip, or offers declined | A sentence about rewards alone leaves the driver to find the gap by trying |
| 3 | It uses no metric name | No "north star", "guardrail" or "acceptance share" | Replace each term with what it counts: "trips you complete", "offers you say yes to" |

## Do your seven lines each say what they should?

The seven lines, in plain words first, then the line's name:

1. **What you count on top** is the numerator. It is the thing the product exists to deliver, counted
   the way the customer would count it.
2. **What you divide by** is the denominator. It is set by something other than the outcome you
   count, and its count is printed beside the rate.
3. **The stretch of time** is the window. It is the same length on both sides of any comparison.
4. **What you leave out, and why** are the exclusions. Each one has a reason about what the count
   means, and each would be dated if it changed.
5. **What the number refuses to pay for** is the line "what it does not reward". It names the
   behaviour out loud.
6. **The numbers that must not get worse** are the guardrails. Each one is chosen by the gaming it
   catches.
7. **Which gaming each guardrail catches** is the gaming note, one line per guardrail.

| # | Checkpoint | What you should see | If it does not hold |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | The numerator counts an outcome | Something the customer received or did: an order delivered, a song played past its first seconds, a payment that went through | A count of screens opened, sessions or notifications sent counts activity the team can create on its own |
| 5 | The denominator is set apart from the outcome | A group set by something other than what you count, such as users active in the month before, or drivers online at least one peak hour | "Users who did the thing this month" drops everyone who stopped, so the rate rises as people leave |
| 6 | The window is named | A day, a week or a month, the same length each side | "Recently" or "so far" cannot be compared next quarter |
| 7 | Each exclusion has a reason | Test accounts, refunds, cancelled orders, each with why | An exclusion with no reason is where a definition later changes without anyone being told |
| 8 | What it does not reward is named | At least two behaviours, written out | If this line is empty, the team will find the behaviour by trying |
| 9 | Each guardrail catches something | You can say what would make it move | A growth target offered as a guardrail catches nothing: ask what it would catch |

## Does your gaming note name a way and a line?

| # | Checkpoint | What you should see | If it does not hold |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | Two ways of gaming | Two specific behaviours a paid team could adopt | "People might cheat" names no behaviour, so no line can catch it |
| 11 | A line for each | Each way points at the line of your definition that catches it | A way with no line means a guardrail is missing; add one |

## Does your number for Tuesday carry its denominator and window?

| # | Checkpoint | What you should see | If it does not hold |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 | It is a rate with its denominator said | For example, trips per peak online hour, 1.90 before and 1.58 after; or the north star, 35.76 and then 35.89 trips per online driver-week | A bare total, such as 146,300 hours, says nothing about each driver or each hour |
| 13 | It has a window and both values | Six weeks before and six weeks after the launch of 31 August 2026 | One value alone cannot show a change |
| 14 | Its sentence says why this number | One reason tied to the city head's decision on Friday | "It is the most important" is a claim with no reason |

## Does your proxy test fit on one line?

| # | Checkpoint | What you should see | If it does not hold |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 | It asks the question | Could a team hit this number while the thing it stands for got no better? | A line that defines a proxy without the test cannot be applied |
| 16 | You applied it once | One sentence applying it to your product's number | If your number passes too easily, try the cheapest way to raise it |
