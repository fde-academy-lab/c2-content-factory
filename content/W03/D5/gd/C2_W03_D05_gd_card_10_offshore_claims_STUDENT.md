# GD card 10: Should Kalpa move its Texas Medicaid claim work from Dallas to the team in Bengaluru?

This card is for Friday's group discussion in Build 1, and you read it alone in six minutes.
Kalpa Health and its people are fictional, and the contract clause and the fact about a real company
were checked on 1 October 2026.

> "Revenue cycle wants our Texas Medicaid claim work moved from Dallas to the GCC in Bengaluru.
> Compliance says the Texas contract forbids it. Finance says we could drop the Texas Medicaid plan
> instead. I want one decision, and I want to be able to explain it to the state."
> Dr Priya Menon, COO, Kalpa Health

**Who needs the answer.** Dr Menon, who answers to Texas for the contract and to the board for the
cost; a wrong call breaks a state contract, wastes money every year, or takes a local lab away from
Dallas patients on Medicaid.

**The questions on the way.** Why is the work in Dallas, what does the Texas contract forbid and who
can lift the ban, what would each option save, what do the four heads want, and what do you owe
Dr Menon when time is called?

## Why is this work in Dallas?

**Who needs the answer.** The revenue cycle head, who runs the claim work in both countries.

**The questions on the way.** Who pays for Texas Medicaid patients, and is offshore claim work
unusual?

Medicaid is each state's coverage for people on low incomes. Kalpa bills Texas Medicaid through a
managed-care plan under contract with the state's Health and Human Services Commission (HHSC), and
Kalpa's lawyers read its contract with the plan as passing the state's clause down to Kalpa. The
GCC, Kalpa's Global Capability Centre in Bengaluru, works Kalpa's other claims. Offshore claim work
is common: AGS Health, headquartered in Washington DC, works claims for US hospitals with more than
15,000 professionals and centres in India that include Bengaluru (company page).

## What does the Texas contract forbid, and who can lift the ban?

**Who needs the answer.** The compliance and privacy official, who signs Kalpa's yearly statement
that it keeps its contracts.

**The questions on the way.** What may not leave the US, and what can the state approve?

Section 4.10 of the state's Managed Care Uniform Terms and Conditions, version 1.3, binds the plan,
the MCO, and "all Subcontractors, vendors, agents, and service Providers of or for the MCO". They
"must not allow any Confidential Information that the MCO receives from or on behalf of HHSC to be
moved outside the United States by any means (physical or electronic) at any time, for any period
of time, for any reason", and "must not permit any person to have remote access to HHSC
information, systems, or Deliverables from a location outside of the United States". The state's
prior approval covers work "that HHSC has approved in writing and that HHSC has confirmed will not
involve the sharing of Confidential Information outside the United States", and Confidential
Information includes protected health information (Texas HHSC).

## How big is the work, and what would each option save?

**Who needs the answer.** The finance head, who has to show the board the saving each year.

**The questions on the way.** How many patients and claims are involved, and what does each option
cost?

| What | Number | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Patients on Kalpa's Dallas register who are on Medicaid | one in seven, 14.4 percent of 1,250 | Kalpa Health patient register |
| Texas Medicaid claims Kalpa bills in a year | about 800 | Assumption (the finance head) |
| What the Texas Medicaid plan pays Kalpa in a year | about $35,000 | Assumption (the finance head) |
| Cost of the tests behind those claims, as a share of what the plan pays | about two thirds | Assumption (the finance head's rule of thumb) |
| The Dallas billing specialist who works these claims and answers Dallas patients' billing calls | $75,000 a year | Assumption (the revenue cycle head) |
| The GCC's charge to work these claims from Bengaluru | $15,000 a year | Assumption (the revenue cycle head) |
| A US answering service for Dallas patients' billing calls | $20,000 a year | Assumption (the revenue cycle head) |
| The state's time to answer a request for its approval | 8 to 12 weeks | Assumption (the compliance and privacy official) |

## What do revenue cycle, compliance, finance and operations want?

**Who needs the answer.** Your group, which tells Dr Menon what she can say to the state.

**The questions on the way.** What does each head want her to do, and what does each leave out?

- **The revenue cycle head:** "Move the claims to Bengaluru and the calls to an answering service,
  and we save $40,000 a year."
- **The compliance and privacy official:** "Texas Medicaid claims stay in the US, and so does every
  login to them."
- **The finance head:** "Or we drop the Texas Medicaid plan. It pays us $35,000 a year, and keeping
  it costs us a person."
- **The patient service centres' operations head:** "One Dallas patient in seven is on Medicaid.
  Where do they go for a blood test?"
