# GD card 10: Should Kalpa move its Texas Medicaid claim work from Dallas to the team in Bengaluru?

Build 1, Friday's group discussion. Read the card alone in four minutes; the rules and the scoring
are at its end. Kalpa Health and its people are fictional, and the contract clause and the fact
about a real company were checked on 1 October 2026.

> "Revenue cycle wants the last of our claim work in Dallas moved to the GCC in Bengaluru, which
> already works every other payer's claims for us. Compliance says the Texas Medicaid contracts
> forbid it. Finance says we could drop the Texas Medicaid plans instead. I want one decision, and I
> want to be able to explain it to the state."
> Dr Priya Menon, COO, Kalpa Health

**Who needs the answer.** Dr Menon, who answers to the state of Texas for Kalpa's Medicaid contracts
and to the board for what the work costs. A wrong call breaks a contract with a state, wastes money
every year, or takes a local lab away from Dallas patients on Medicaid.

**The questions on the way.** Why is this work still in Dallas? What does the clause forbid, and who
can lift it? What does each option save, and who bears the cost that is not money? What does each
head want? What do you owe Dr Menon when time is called?

## Why is this work still in Dallas?

**Who needs the answer.** The revenue cycle head, who runs the claim work in both countries.

**The questions on the way.** Who pays Kalpa for Texas Medicaid patients? Is offshore claim work
unusual?

Medicaid is each state's coverage for people on low incomes, run with federal money. Kalpa bills
two Texas Medicaid managed-care plans, companies that run Medicaid coverage under contracts with
the state's Health and Human Services Commission (HHSC). Kalpa's lawyers read its contracts with the
two plans as passing the state's own clause down to Kalpa. Kalpa's Global Capability Centre (GCC) in
Bengaluru already works the claims of every other payer. Offshore claim work is common: AGS Health, an
Indian revenue-cycle firm with more than 15,000 professionals worldwide and a centre in Bengaluru
among others, works claims for US hospitals and health systems (company page, checked 1 October
2026).

## What does the clause say?

**Who needs the answer.** The compliance and privacy official, who signs Kalpa's yearly statement
that it keeps its contracts.

**The questions on the way.** What may not leave the US? Does logging in from Bengaluru count? Who
can lift the ban?

Section 4.10 of the state's Managed Care Uniform Terms and Conditions, version 1.3, binds each plan,
which the clause calls the MCO, the managed care organisation, and "all Subcontractors, vendors,
agents, and service Providers of or for the MCO": they "must not
allow any Confidential Information that the MCO receives from or on behalf of HHSC to be moved
outside the United States by any means (physical or electronic) at any time, for any period of
time, for any reason", and "must not permit any person to have remote access to HHSC information,
systems, or Deliverables from a location outside of the United States". The work is done within
the United States unless "otherwise approved in advance by HHSC in writing" (Texas HHSC, checked
1 October 2026).

**The questions on the way.** How big is the Texas Medicaid business? What does each option save?
How long would the state take to answer?

## What does each head want?

**Who needs the answer.** Your group, which sits in the GCC and has to argue about its own work
without favouring it.

**The questions on the way.** Which cost does each head count, and which does each leave out?

- **The revenue cycle head:** "Move the claims to Bengaluru and the calls to the answering service.
  That saves $40,000 a year, and the team in Bengaluru already knows the work."
- **The compliance and privacy official:** "Texas Medicaid claims stay in the US, and so does every
  login to them, until the state says yes in writing."
- **The finance head:** "Or we drop the two Texas Medicaid plans. They pay us $35,000 a year, and
  keeping them costs us a person."
- **The patient service centres' operations head:** "One Dallas patient in seven is on Medicaid.
  Where do they go for a blood test?"

## What do you owe Dr Menon when time is called, and how is each of you scored?

**Who needs the answer.** Each of you: Dr Menon acts on one position, and the panel scores every
learner alone on what they said and did in the seventeen minutes.

**The questions on the way.** What does the group hand over? How does the round run? What earns
the marks?

When time is called, the group gives Dr Menon one position, or the point where it splits, named out
loud; the one number that carries the position, and where on this card that number comes from; and
the fact that would change the group's mind, with the main risk of its position.

| Part | Minutes | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Read | 4 | Each of you reads the card alone and may write. |

| The panel's questions | 4 | The chair puts two questions, each to a learner by name. |

You have pen and paper and one phone calculator in the middle of the table. A row whose source says
assumption or illustration is yours to challenge: say what you would use instead, and why.
