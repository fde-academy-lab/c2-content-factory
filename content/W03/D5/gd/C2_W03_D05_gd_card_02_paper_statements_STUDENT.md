# GD card 02: Should Kalpa Health stop mailing paper statements and bill patients by text and email only?

This card is for Friday's group discussion in Build 1, and you read it alone in four minutes.
Kalpa Health and its people are fictional, and the facts about Medicare and a real company were
checked on 1 October 2026.

> "Revenue cycle wants to stop mailing paper statements and send every patient's bill by text and
> email. It saves money. Does it cost us money we never collect, and should we do it?"
> Dr Priya Menon, COO, Kalpa Health

**Who needs the answer.** Dr Menon, who approves the change for 1 January; a switch that stalls
older patients' payments can lose more than it saves.

**The questions on the way.** What is a statement, and who receives one? What does paper cost, and
how much might older patients leave unpaid? What do the revenue cycle and operations heads want?
What do you owe Dr Menon when time is called?

## What is a statement, and who receives one?

**Who needs the answer.** The revenue cycle head, who answers for the money statements bring in.

**The questions on the way.** Why does a patient owe anything once the plan has paid, and what do
Medicare patients owe for a lab test?

After a health plan pays its share of a claim, the patient may still owe a share of their own, and
Kalpa mails the patient a statement, a bill for it. Most of Kalpa's patients aged 65
and over are on Medicare, the federal programme for that age, and Medicare.gov tells them "You
usually pay nothing for Medicare-covered diagnostic laboratory tests". Patients pay more slowly than
plans: at Quest Diagnostics they brought about 12 percent of 2025's net revenues but about 20
percent of the money owed to the company at the year's end (Form 10-K for 2025).

## What does paper cost, and how much might older patients leave unpaid?

**Who needs the answer.** The finance head, who books the saving in January's budget if Dr Menon
says yes.

**The questions on the way.** How many statements go out, and to whom? What did the survey find?

| What | Number | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Patients on Kalpa Health's register | 6,700 | Kalpa Health patient register |
| Patients aged 65 and over | 1,699, which is 25.4 percent | Kalpa Health patient register |
| Statements mailed in a year | 18,000 | Assumption (the revenue cycle head's count) |
| Statements mailed to patients aged 65 and over | 2,700 a year | Assumption (the revenue cycle head's count) |
| Cost of printing and mailing one statement | $1.10 | Assumption |
| Patients aged 65 and over, in a phone survey of 120, who said they would not pay from a text or an email | 30 percent | Assumption |
| Average balance on a statement to a patient aged 65 or over | $35 | Assumption |
| Balances left unpaid after a text or email that a later phone call recovers | half | Assumption |

## What do the revenue cycle and operations heads want?

**Who needs the answer.** Your group, which tells Dr Menon whether to stop the paper.

**The questions on the way.** What does each head want her to do, and on what evidence?

- **The revenue cycle head:** "We print and mail eighteen thousand bills a year that most patients
  pay on their phones anyway."
- **The patient service centres' operations head:** "The patients who ask my front desks for a paper
  bill are almost all over seventy."

## What do you owe Dr Menon when time is called, and how is each of you scored?

**Who needs the answer.** Each of you, since Dr Menon acts on the one position your group hands over
and the chair scores every learner alone.

**The questions on the way.** What does the group hand over? How does the round run? What earns the
marks?

The chair's opening says all of this before the clock starts, so your reading minutes are for the
case above. When time is called, the group gives Dr Menon one position, or the point where it splits,
named out loud; the one number that carries the position, and where on this card that number comes
from; and the fact that would change the group's mind, with the main risk of its position.

| Part | Minutes | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Read | 4 | Each of you reads the card alone and may write. |
| Discuss | 17 | You talk to each other while the chair listens. The chair answers no question about the numbers, so state any assumption out loud. |
| The chair's two questions | 4 | The chair puts two questions, each to a learner by name. |

You have pen and paper and one phone calculator in the middle of the table. A row whose source says
assumption or illustration is yours to challenge: say what you would use instead, and why. The
chair scores each of you alone on what you say in the discussion and in answer to the two
questions, against this rubric:
