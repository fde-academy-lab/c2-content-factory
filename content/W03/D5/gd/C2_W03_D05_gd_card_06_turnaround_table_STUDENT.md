# GD card 06: Should the lab director rank Kalpa's six labs every month on how fast they release results?

Build 1, Friday's group discussion. Read the card alone in three minutes; the rules and the scoring
are at its end. Kalpa Health and its people are fictional, and the two tables on this card are an
illustration of the lab director's report, never Kalpa's own figures.

> "The lab director wants to rank our six labs every month on the share of results released within
> 24 hours, with the bottom lab's manager explaining themselves to me. Phoenix is bottom by a mile.
> Is the table fair, and should we publish it?"
> Dr Priya Menon, COO, Kalpa Health

**Who needs the answer.** Dr Menon, whose name goes on the first table next month. A ranking that
blames a lab for work it does not control drives out a good manager, and a fair ranking left
unpublished leaves a slow lab slow.

**The questions on the way.** What does the table measure? How does each lab rank, and does each lab
do the same kind of work? What does each voice want? What do you owe Dr Menon when time is called?

## What does the league table measure?

**Who needs the answer.** The lab director, who wants every lab manager watching the same number.

**The questions on the way.** What is turnaround? Why do some tests take longer by design?

Turnaround time runs from the draw to the released result. Most of a lab's work is routine tests,
such as a blood count, run as samples arrive. Some tests are batched: the lab saves the samples up
and runs them on set days of the week, so many of their results take longer than 24 hours by
design. Kalpa Health has one laboratory in each of its six metros.

## What do the numbers on the table say?

**Who needs the answer.** The finance head, who will check every figure you quote against these
lines.

**The questions on the way.** How do the six labs rank on the director's measure? How does each lab
do on each kind of test?

## What does each voice want?

**Who needs the answer.** Your group, which has to decide whether the ranking is fair before it
decides whether to publish it.

**The questions on the way.** What does each speaker think the table shows?

- **The lab director:** "What gets ranked gets fixed. Phoenix has been bottom three months running."
- **Phoenix's lab manager, by message:** "A quarter of our work is the batched tests that a large
  practice of hormone specialists sends us. Rank me on what I control."

## What do you owe Dr Menon when time is called, and how is each of you scored?

**Who needs the answer.** Each of you: Dr Menon acts on one position, and the panel scores every
learner alone on what they said and did in the eighteen minutes.

**The questions on the way.** What does the group hand over? How does the round run? What earns
the marks?

When time is called, the group gives Dr Menon one position, or the point where it splits, named out
loud; the one number that carries the position, and where on this card that number comes from; and
the fact that would change the group's mind, with the main risk of its position.

| Part | Minutes | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Read | 3 | Each of you reads the card alone and may write. |
| Discuss | 18 | The four of you talk to each other. The chair answers no question about the numbers, so state any assumption out loud. |
| The panel's questions | 4 | The chair puts two questions, each to a learner by name. |

You have pen and paper and one phone calculator in the middle of the table. A row whose source says
assumption or illustration is yours to challenge: say what you would use instead, and why.
