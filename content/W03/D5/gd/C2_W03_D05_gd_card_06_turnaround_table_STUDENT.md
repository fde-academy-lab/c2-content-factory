# GD card 06: Should the lab director rank Kalpa's six labs every month on how fast they release results?

This card is for Friday's group discussion in Build 1, and you read it alone in five minutes.
Kalpa Health and its people are fictional, the table is invented for this discussion and comes from
none of Kalpa Health's data files, and the fact about a real company was checked on 1 October 2026.

> "The lab director wants to rank our six labs every month on the share of results released within
> 24 hours, with the bottom lab's manager explaining themselves to me. Phoenix is bottom by a mile.
> Is the table fair, and should we publish it?"
> Dr Priya Menon, COO, Kalpa Health

**Who needs the answer.** Dr Menon, whose name goes on the first table next month; a ranking that
blames a lab for work it does not control can drive out a good manager, and no ranking leaves the
managers without a shared number to improve.

**The questions on the way.** What does the director's ranking measure? How did each lab do in
September, overall and by kind of test? What do the lab director and Phoenix's manager want? What
do you owe Dr Menon when time is called?

## What does the director's ranking measure?

**Who needs the answer.** The lab director, who wants every lab manager watching the same number.

**The questions on the way.** What is turnaround, and why do some tests take longer by design?

Turnaround time runs from the draw to the released result. Most of a lab's work is routine tests,
such as a blood count, run as samples arrive. Some tests are batched: the lab saves the samples up
and runs them on set days of the week, so many of their results take longer than 24 hours by
design. Quest Diagnostics' annual report says non-routine tests "may be performed less frequently
than routine tests" (Form 10-K for 2025).

## What do the lab director and Phoenix's manager want?

**Who needs the answer.** Your group, which tells Dr Menon whether to publish the table.

**The questions on the way.** What does each of them want her to do, and why?

- **The lab director:** "What gets ranked gets fixed. Phoenix has been bottom three months running."
- **Phoenix's lab manager, by message:** "A quarter of our work is the batched tests that a large
  practice of hormone specialists sends us. Rank me on what I control."

## What do you owe Dr Menon when time is called, and how is each of you scored?

**Who needs the answer.** Each of you, since Dr Menon acts on the one position your group hands over
and the chair scores every learner alone.

**The questions on the way.** What does the group hand over? How does the round run? What earns the
marks?

minutes are for the case above; the minutes and the marks are below. When time is called, the group
gives Dr Menon one position, or the point where it splits, named out loud; the one number that
carries the position, and where on this card that number comes from; and the fact that would change
the group's mind, with the main risk of its position.
