# GD card 04: Should Kalpa Health close its phone booking line and move every patient to online booking?

This card is for Friday's group discussion in Build 1, and you read it alone in four minutes.
Kalpa Health and its people are fictional, and the fact about a real company was checked on
1 October 2026.

> "Online booking keeps growing. The centres' operations head wants to close the phone line by
> March and send everyone online. The marketing head says the phone is how our older patients book.
> Decide it with me."
> Dr Priya Menon, COO, Kalpa Health

**Who needs the answer.** Dr Menon, who has to tell the phone team this month whether they have a
job in April; closing the line saves $65,000 a year, and every patient who leaves with it takes
their bookings to another lab.

**The questions on the way.** How do patients book? How many bookings come by phone, and where would
those patients go? What do the operations and marketing heads want? What do you owe Dr Menon when
time is called?

## How do patients book, and why is the phone line in question?

**Who needs the answer.** The operations head, who runs the twelve centres where Kalpa draws blood.

**The questions on the way.** Which ways can a patient book, and does a real lab book online at
scale?

A patient books a visit online or by phone, or comes to a centre without booking. The operations
head wants to close the phone line and point callers to online booking. Quest Diagnostics' patient
portal had more than 45 million registered users at the end of 2025 and lets patients schedule
appointments (Form 10-K for 2025, checked 1 October 2026).

## How many bookings come by phone, and where would those patients go?

**Who needs the answer.** The operations head, who has promised the phone team an answer this month.

**The questions on the way.** How many bookings come by phone, what is one worth, and where would
phone bookers go?

| What | Number | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Bookings in a year | 23,000 | Assumption (the operations head's count) |
| Share of bookings made by phone | 15 percent | Assumption (the operations head's count) |
| Cost of running the phone line | $65,000 a year | Assumption |
| What one booking leaves Kalpa after its tests' cost, on average | $85 | Assumption (the finance head) |
| Where phone bookers say they would go if the line closed | half online, a third to a centre without booking, the rest, one in six, to another lab | Assumption (the marketing head, from 66 calls the team listened to) |

## What do the operations and marketing heads want?

**Who needs the answer.** Your group, which tells Dr Menon whether to close the line.

**The questions on the way.** What does each head want her to do, and on what evidence?

- **The operations head:** "Sixty-five thousand dollars a year for a line that could be a button
  on our website."
- **The marketing head:** "The people on that line are the ones who book a Healthy aging panel for
  both their parents."

## What do you owe Dr Menon when time is called, and how is each of you scored?

**Who needs the answer.** Each of you, since Dr Menon acts on the one position your group hands over
and the chair scores every learner alone.

**The questions on the way.** What does the group hand over? How does the round run? What earns the
marks?

The chair's opening covers what the group hands over and how each of you is scored, so your reading
minutes are for the case above; the minutes and the marks are below. When time is called, the group
gives Dr Menon one position, or the point where it splits, named out loud; the one number that
carries the position, and where on this card that number comes from; and the fact that would change
the group's mind, with the main risk of its position.

| Part | Minutes | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Read | 4 | Each of you reads the card alone and may write. |
| Discuss | 17 | You talk to each other while the chair listens. The chair answers no question about the numbers, so state any assumption out loud. |
| The chair's two questions | 4 | The chair puts two questions, each to a learner by name. |

You have pen and paper and one phone calculator in the middle of the table. A row whose source says
assumption or illustration is yours to challenge: say what you would use instead, and why. The
chair scores each of you alone on what you say in the discussion and in answer to the two
questions, against this rubric:

**Group discussion, 30 marks.** Each learner is scored alone.

| Criterion | Marks | What full marks look like |
|---|---|---|
| Structures the problem | 8 | The learner frames the decision and the metric before arguing. |
| Uses evidence | 8 | The learner takes a position and defends it with a number from the exhibit. |
| Engages | 8 | The learner builds on or challenges another member's point and brings a quiet member in. |
| Lands a conclusion | 6 | The discussion ends on a recommendation and its main risk. |
