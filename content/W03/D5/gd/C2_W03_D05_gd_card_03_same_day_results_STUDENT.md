# GD card 03: Should Kalpa Health promise same-day results in all six metros?

Build 1, Friday's group discussion. Read the card alone in three minutes; the rules and the scoring
are at its end. Kalpa Health and its people are fictional, and the fact about a real company was
checked on 1 October 2026.

> "Marketing wants to advertise same-day results in all six metros. The lab director says two of
> our labs cannot keep that promise without a second shift. Which of them is right, and what should
> I do?"
> Dr Priya Menon, COO, Kalpa Health

**Who needs the answer.** Dr Menon, who signs off the spring campaign this month. A promise one lab
breaks loses the doctors who believed it, and a second shift bought where it is not needed costs
$32,000 a month.

**The questions on the way.** What does a same-day promise ask of a lab? How full is each lab now,
and once the promise is advertised? What does each head want? What do you owe Dr Menon when time is
called?

## What does a same-day promise ask of a lab?

**Who needs the answer.** The lab director, who answers to doctors for every late result.

**The questions on the way.** Where are the samples tested? Does a real lab sell speed this way?

A doctor whose patient gives blood in the morning would see the result that evening. Kalpa Health
has one laboratory in each of its six metros, so each lab reports its own metro's samples. The time
from the draw to the released result is the turnaround time. Quest Diagnostics runs what it calls
rapid response laboratories, smaller sites that "quickly perform an abbreviated menu of routine
tests for customers that require rapid turnaround times", and says it operates 24 hours a day, 365
days a year (Form 10-K for 2025, checked 1 October 2026).

## What does each head want?

**Who needs the answer.** Your group, which has to decide who is right, metro by metro if it must.

**The questions on the way.** What is each head protecting?

- **The marketing head:** "A promise that holds in four metros and not in two is a promise we
  cannot print."
- **The lab director:** "Advertise it where we can keep it, or pay for the shifts first."

## What do you owe Dr Menon when time is called, and how is each of you scored?

**Who needs the answer.** Each of you: Dr Menon acts on one position, and the panel scores every
learner alone on what they said and did in the eighteen minutes.

**The questions on the way.** What does the group hand over? How does the round run? What earns
the marks?

When time is called, the group gives Dr Menon one position, or the point where it splits, named out
loud; the one number that carries the position, and where on this card that number comes from; and
the fact that would change the group's mind, with the main risk of its position.

| Part | Minutes | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Read | 3 | Each of you reads the card alone and may write. |
| Discuss | 18 | The four of you talk to each other. The chair answers no question about the numbers, so state any assumption out loud. |
| The panel's questions | 4 | The chair puts two questions, each to a learner by name. |

You have pen and paper and one phone calculator in the middle of the table. A row whose source says
assumption or illustration is yours to challenge: say what you would use instead, and why.

**Group discussion, 30 marks.** Each learner is scored alone.

| Criterion | Marks | What full marks look like |
|---|---|---|

| Uses evidence | 8 | The learner takes a position and defends it with a number from the exhibit. |
| Engages | 8 | The learner builds on or challenges another member's point and brings a quiet member in. |
| Lands a conclusion | 6 | The discussion ends on a recommendation and its main risk. |
