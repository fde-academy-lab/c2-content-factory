# GD card 03: Should Kalpa Health promise same-day results in all six metros?

This card is for Friday's group discussion in Build 1, and you read it alone in four minutes.
Kalpa Health and its people are fictional, and the fact about a real company was checked on
1 October 2026.

> "Marketing wants to advertise same-day results in all six metros. The lab director says two of
> our labs cannot keep that promise without a second shift. Which of them is right, and what should
> I do?"
> Dr Priya Menon, COO, Kalpa Health

**Who needs the answer.** Dr Menon, who signs off the spring advertising this month; a promise one
lab breaks loses the doctors who believed it, and a second shift bought where it is not needed costs
$32,000 a month.

**The questions on the way.** What does a same-day promise ask of a lab? How many samples does each
lab handle, and how many can one shift report? What do the marketing head and the lab director
want? What do you owe Dr Menon when time is called?

## What does a same-day promise ask of a lab?

**Who needs the answer.** The lab director, who answers to doctors for every late result.

**The questions on the way.** Where is each sample tested, and does a real lab sell speed?

A doctor whose patient gives blood in the morning would see the result that evening. Kalpa Health
has one laboratory in each of its six metros, and a sample is tested in its own metro's lab. Quest
Diagnostics runs rapid response laboratories, smaller sites that "quickly perform an abbreviated
menu of routine tests for customers that require rapid turnaround times" (Form 10-K for 2025,
checked 1 October 2026).

## How many samples does each lab handle, and how many can one shift report?

**Who needs the answer.** The lab director, who has to say which labs need a $32,000 second shift.

**The questions on the way.** How busy is each lab, and what can one shift report the same day?

## What do the marketing head and the lab director want?

**Who needs the answer.** Your group, which tells Dr Menon where to advertise the promise.

**The questions on the way.** What does each head want her to do, and why?

- **The marketing head:** "A promise that holds in four metros and not in two is a promise we
  cannot print."
- **The lab director:** "Advertise it where we can keep it, or pay for the shifts first."

## What do you owe Dr Menon when time is called, and how is each of you scored?

**Who needs the answer.** Each of you, since Dr Menon acts on the one position your group hands over
and the chair scores every learner alone.

**The questions on the way.** What does the group hand over? How does the round run? What earns the
marks?

The chair's opening says all of this before the clock starts, so your reading minutes are for the
case above. When time is called, the group gives Dr Menon one position, or the point where it splits,
named out loud; the one number that carries the position, and where on this card that number comes
from; and the fact that would change the group's mind, with the main risk of its position.

| Part | Minutes | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Read | 4 | Each of you reads the card alone and may write. |
| Discuss | 17 | You talk to each other while the chair listens. The chair answers no question about the numbers, so state any assumption out loud. |
| The chair's two questions | 4 | The chair puts two questions, each to a learner by name. |

You have pen and paper and one phone calculator in the middle of the table. A row whose source says
assumption or illustration is yours to challenge: say what you would use instead, and why. The
chair scores each of you alone on what you say in the discussion and in answer to the two
questions, against this rubric:
