# GD card 08: A large health plan offers to make Kalpa its preferred lab in Dallas at 12 percent below today's prices. Should Kalpa sign?

This card is for Friday's group discussion in Build 1, and you read it alone in five minutes.
Kalpa Health and its people are fictional, the plan is unnamed on purpose, and the facts about a
real company were checked on 1 October 2026.

> "One of our largest commercial plans wants Kalpa as its preferred lab in Dallas: its members pay
> nothing out of pocket at our centres, and we accept 12 percent less on every test. The plan says
> its members' tests with us would rise 40 percent; our marketing head heard 15 from a Houston lab
> that signed. I sign, counter or walk by the end of the month."
> Dr Priya Menon, COO, Kalpa Health

**Who needs the answer.** Dr Menon, whose signature binds the Dallas lab to the new prices for three
years; sign on volume that never comes, and every test these members already bring pays 12 percent
less for nothing.

**The questions on the way.** What is a preferred lab, how much of the Dallas lab's work is this
plan's and what is it paid, what do the three heads claim, and what do you owe Dr Menon when time is
called?

## What is a preferred lab, and why would a plan offer it?

**Who needs the answer.** The payer contracting head, who negotiates every price Kalpa's plans pay.

**The questions on the way.** What is an allowed amount, and do real plans and labs make such deals?

A health plan's contract sets the allowed amount for each test: the most it accepts for the test,
its own share and the patient's together. A plan that names a preferred lab steers its members
there, often by charging them nothing out of pocket, and asks the lab for lower prices in return.
Quest Diagnostics' annual report describes "zero-dollar out-of-pocket costs for members using
preferred providers" and names "The UnitedHealthcare Preferred Lab Network, which chose us to
participate" (Form 10-K for 2025).

## How much of the Dallas lab's work is this plan's, and what is it paid?

**Who needs the answer.** The finance head, who has to say what Dallas earns on these tests for the
next three years.

**The questions on the way.** What does a test bring and cost, what does the offer change, and what
do the plan and the Houston lab report?

| What | Number | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Patients on Kalpa's register in Dallas | 1,250 | Kalpa Health patient register |
| The plan's members' share of the Dallas lab's tests today | 30 percent | Assumption (the finance head) |
| Average allowed amount on one of this plan's tests today | $40 | Assumption (the payer contracting head) |
| What one of this plan's tests costs Kalpa to perform, on average | $20 | Assumption (the finance head) |
| Allowed amounts under the offer | 12 percent lower, on every test from the plan's members | The plan's offer |
| The plan's estimate | its members' tests at Kalpa rise 40 percent | The plan |
| The Houston lab that signed a similar deal | its total tests rose 15 percent; the plan's members were about 30 percent of its tests before the deal | Assumption (the marketing head, from a talk with that lab's manager) |
| When the plan would pay | 45 days after a claim, against 30 today | The plan's offer |
| What money tied up costs Kalpa | 8 percent a year | Assumption (the finance head) |
| What the plan also wants | each patient service centre's turnaround times and denial rates (the share of claims the plan refuses to pay), every month | The plan's offer |

## What do the contracting, finance and marketing heads claim?

**Who needs the answer.** Your group, which tells Dr Menon whether to sign, counter or walk.

**The questions on the way.** What does each head want her to do, and on what evidence?

- **The payer contracting head:** "Forty percent more of their members' tests. Volume like that does
  not come along twice."
- **The finance head:** "Every test their members already bring us pays twelve percent less, and
  pays it later."
- **The marketing head:** "The Houston lab grew fifteen percent on a deal like this. I heard it from
  their own manager."

## What do you owe Dr Menon when time is called, and how is each of you scored?

**Who needs the answer.** Each of you, since Dr Menon acts on the one position your group hands over
and the chair scores every learner alone.

**The questions on the way.** What does the group hand over? How does the round run? What earns the
marks?

The chair's opening says all of this before the clock starts, so your reading minutes are for the
case above. When time is called, the group gives Dr Menon one position, or the point where it splits,
named out loud; the one number that carries the position, and where on this card that number comes
from; and the fact that would change the group's mind, with the main risk of its position.

| Part | Minutes | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Read | 5 | Each of you reads the card alone and may write. |
| Discuss | 16 | You talk to each other while the chair listens. The chair answers no question about the numbers, so state any assumption out loud. |
| The chair's two questions | 4 | The chair puts two questions, each to a learner by name. |

You have pen and paper and one phone calculator in the middle of the table. A row whose source says
assumption or illustration is yours to challenge: say what you would use instead, and why. The
chair scores each of you alone on what you say in the discussion and in answer to the two
questions, against this rubric:
