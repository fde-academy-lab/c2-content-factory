# GD card 08: A large health plan offers to make Kalpa its preferred lab in Dallas at 12 percent below today's prices. Should Kalpa sign?

Build 1, Friday's group discussion. Read the card alone in four minutes; the rules and the scoring
are at its end. Kalpa Health and its people are fictional, the plan is unnamed on purpose, and the
facts about a real company were checked on 1 October 2026.

> "One of our largest commercial plans wants Kalpa as its preferred lab in Dallas. Its members would
> pay nothing out of pocket at our centres, and we would accept 12 percent less on every test. The
> plan says its members' tests with us would rise 40 percent; our marketing head heard 15 from a lab
> in Houston that signed. Finance says we would be paid later as well. I sign or walk by the end of
> the month."
> Dr Priya Menon, COO, Kalpa Health

**Who needs the answer.** Dr Menon, whose signature binds the Dallas lab to the new prices for three
years. Sign on volume that never comes, and every test these members already bring pays 12 percent
less for nothing.

**The questions on the way.** What is a preferred lab? What does Kalpa earn on this plan's tests now
and under the offer? What does each growth figure count? What does each head claim? What do you
owe Dr Menon when time is called?

## What is a preferred lab, and why would a plan offer it?

**Who needs the answer.** The payer contracting head, who negotiates every price Kalpa's plans pay.

**The questions on the way.** What is an allowed amount? Do real plans and labs make deals like
this?

A health plan's contract sets the allowed amount for each test: the most it accepts for the test,
its own share and the patient's together. A plan that names a preferred lab steers its members
there, often by charging them nothing out of pocket, and asks the lab for lower prices in return.
Quest Diagnostics' annual report describes plans moving testing to "high value, lower cost
providers" through preferred provider partners and "zero-dollar out-of-pocket costs for members
using preferred providers", and names "The UnitedHealthcare Preferred Lab Network, which chose us
to participate" (Form 10-K for 2025, checked 1 October 2026).

## What do the numbers on the table say?

**Who needs the answer.** The finance head, who will check every figure you quote against these
lines.

**The questions on the way.** How much of the Dallas lab's work is this plan's? What does Kalpa earn
on a test now and under the offer? What exactly does each growth figure count?

| What | Number | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Patients on Kalpa's register in Dallas | 1,250 | Kalpa Health patient register |
| The plan's members' share of the Dallas lab's tests today | 30 percent | Assumption (the finance head) |
| Average allowed amount on one of this plan's tests today | $40 | Assumption (the payer contracting head) |
| Allowed amounts under the offer | 12 percent lower, on every test from the plan's members | The plan's offer |
| What one test costs Kalpa to perform | $24 | Assumption (the finance head) |
| The plan's estimate | its members' tests at Kalpa rise 40 percent | The plan |

| What money tied up costs Kalpa | 8 percent a year | Assumption (the finance head) |
| What the plan also wants | each patient service centre's turnaround and denial figures, every month | The plan's offer |

## What does each head claim?

**Who needs the answer.** Your group, which has to put three figures on one footing before it weighs
them.

**The questions on the way.** What is each head counting, and against what base?

- **The payer contracting head:** "Forty percent more of their members' tests. Volume like that does
  not come along twice."
- **The finance head:** "Every test their members already bring us pays twelve percent less, and
  pays it later."
- **The marketing head:** "The Houston lab grew fifteen percent on a deal like this. I heard it from
  their own manager."

## What do you owe Dr Menon when time is called, and how is each of you scored?

**Who needs the answer.** Each of you: Dr Menon acts on one position, and the panel scores every
learner alone on what they said and did in the seventeen minutes.

**The questions on the way.** What does the group hand over? How does the round run? What earns
the marks?

When time is called, the group gives Dr Menon one position, or the point where it splits, named out
loud; the one number that carries the position, and where on this card that number comes from; and
the fact that would change the group's mind, with the main risk of its position.

| Part | Minutes | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Read | 4 | Each of you reads the card alone and may write. |
| Discuss | 17 | The four of you talk to each other. The chair answers no question about the numbers, so state any assumption out loud. |
| The panel's questions | 4 | The chair puts two questions, each to a learner by name. |

You have pen and paper and one phone calculator in the middle of the table. A row whose source says
assumption or illustration is yours to challenge: say what you would use instead, and why.
