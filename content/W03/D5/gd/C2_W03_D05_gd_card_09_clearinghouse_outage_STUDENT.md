# GD card 09: Kalpa's clearinghouse has been down for over two weeks. What does Dr Menon do this week?

This card is for Friday's group discussion in Build 1, and you read it alone in six minutes. The
outage is real and its facts were checked on 1 October 2026; Kalpa Health is fictional, and every
Kalpa number on the card is invented for this discussion.

> "It is Monday 11 March 2024, and Change Healthcare has been down since 21 February, so none of our
> claims has gone out. Revenue cycle wants a second clearinghouse today, finance wants Medicare's
> advance and patience, and compliance will not send a claim to a new company on the contract it has
> sent us. I choose this week."
> Dr Priya Menon, COO, Kalpa Health

**Who needs the answer.** Dr Menon, who chooses this week how Kalpa gets paid; wait too long and
Kalpa cannot pay its staff, and rush a new vendor and patients' claims go to a company on a contract
compliance has not cleared.

**The questions on the way.** What stopped, how much money is stuck and how long does the cash last,
what may a new vendor do with the claims, what do the three heads want, and what do you owe
Dr Menon when time is called?

## What stopped, and what is a clearinghouse?

**Who needs the answer.** The revenue cycle head, whose team cannot send a claim while it lasts.

**The questions on the way.** What does a clearinghouse do, and what did Medicare offer?

A clearinghouse checks each claim's format, passes it to the payer, the plan or programme that pays
it, and passes the payer's answer back. On 21 February 2024 an attack "encrypted and incapacitated
significant portions of Change Healthcare's functionality", a company that "annually processes 15
billion health care transactions", among them "claims transmittals and payment" (American Hospital
Association). On 9 March 2024 Medicare, the federal programme for people aged 65 and over, offered
such providers an advance of up to thirty days of their average Medicare payments, recovered from
all of their next Medicare payments until repaid, for up to 90 days (CMS fact sheet, 9 March 2024).

## How much money is stuck, and how long does Kalpa's cash last?

**Who needs the answer.** The finance head, who has to tell Dr Menon whether payroll can be met.

**The questions on the way.** What is waiting, and what does each way out cost and take?

| What | Number | Where it comes from |

| Claims waiting since 21 February | about 910, worth about $71,500 of plan money | Illustration (the revenue cycle head) |
| From an electronic claim to the plan's payment, in normal weeks | about two weeks | Illustration (the revenue cycle head) |
| Cash in the bank today | $75,000 | Illustration (the finance head) |

| Keying claims into the large plans' websites by hand | about 8 minutes of staff time a claim | Illustration (the revenue cycle head) |

## What may a new vendor do with Kalpa's claims?

**Who needs the answer.** The compliance and privacy official, who answers for every company holding
Kalpa's patients' information.

**The questions on the way.** Is a claim protected, and what must the contract say?

A claim names the patient, the test and its reason, so it is protected health information. Under
the HIPAA Privacy Rule, a lab may let a business associate, a company that handles such information
for it, hold claims only under a written contract that keeps the company to the uses the rule
allows, which are mostly the uses the lab itself could make (45 CFR 164.502(e) and 164.504(e),
eCFR).

## What do revenue cycle, finance and compliance want?

**Who needs the answer.** Your group, which tells Dr Menon what to do this week, and in what order.

**The questions on the way.** What does each head want her to do, and what does each fear?

- **The revenue cycle head:** "Sign the second clearinghouse today. Every working day we wait is
  another $5,500 we have not even asked for."
- **The finance head:** "Take Medicare's advance and wait. Change may be back before the new
  enrolments clear."
- **The compliance and privacy official:** "Not one claim goes to a new company until its agreement
  is signed, and their draft lets them sell products built on our patients' claims."

## What do you owe Dr Menon when time is called, and how is each of you scored?

**Who needs the answer.** Each of you, since Dr Menon acts on the one position your group hands over
and the chair scores every learner alone.

**The questions on the way.** What does the group hand over? How does the round run? What earns the
marks?

The chair's opening says all of this before the clock starts, so your reading minutes are for the
case above. When time is called, the group gives Dr Menon one position, or the point where it splits,
named out loud; the one number that carries the position, and where on this card that number comes
from; and the fact that would change the group's mind, with the main risk of its position.

| Part | Minutes | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Read | 6 | Each of you reads the card alone and may write. |
| Discuss | 15 | You talk to each other while the chair listens. The chair answers no question about the numbers, so state any assumption out loud. |

chair scores each of you alone on what you say in the discussion and in answer to the two
questions, against this rubric:
