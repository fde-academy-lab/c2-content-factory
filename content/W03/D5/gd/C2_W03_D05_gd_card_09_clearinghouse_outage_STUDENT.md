# GD card 09: Kalpa's clearinghouse has been down for over two weeks. What does Dr Menon do this week?

Build 1, Friday's group discussion. Read the card alone in four minutes; the rules and the scoring
are at its end. The outage on this card is real, and its facts were checked on 1 October 2026.
Kalpa Health is fictional and placed inside it as an illustration, so every Kalpa number on the
card is the card's own.

> "It is Monday 11 March 2024, and Change Healthcare has been down since 21 February, so none of our
> claims has gone out. Revenue cycle wants a second clearinghouse today, finance wants Medicare's
> advance and patience, and compliance will not send a claim to a new company on the contract it has
> sent us. I choose this week."
> Dr Priya Menon, COO, Kalpa Health

**Who needs the answer.** Dr Menon, who has about four weeks of cash. Wait too long and Kalpa cannot
pay its staff; rush a new vendor and patients' claims sit with a company on terms the law does not
allow.

**The questions on the way.** What stopped, and what is a clearinghouse? How long does the cash
last, and how soon does each way out bring money in? What may a new vendor do with the claims? What
does each head want? What do you owe Dr Menon when time is called?

## What stopped, and what is a clearinghouse?

**Who needs the answer.** The revenue cycle head, whose team cannot send a claim or post a payment
while the outage lasts.

**The questions on the way.** What does a clearinghouse do? How big was the outage? What did
Medicare offer?

A clearinghouse checks each claim's format and passes it to the payer, then passes the payer's
answer, the remittance, back to the lab. On 21 February 2024 an attack "encrypted and incapacitated
significant portions of Change Healthcare's functionality", stopping "claims transmittals and
payment" at a company that "annually processes 15 billion health care transactions" (American
Hospital Association, checked 1 October 2026). On 9 March 2024 Medicare offered providers whose
claims had stopped an advance of up to thirty days of their average Medicare payments. It takes the
advance back out of their next Medicare payments, all of each payment until it is repaid, for up to
90 days (CMS fact sheet, 9 March 2024, checked 1 October 2026).

## What may a new vendor do with Kalpa's claims?

**Who needs the answer.** The compliance and privacy official, who answers for every company that
holds Kalpa's patients' information.

**The questions on the way.** Is a claim protected? What must the contract say first?

A claim names the patient, the test and its reason, so it is protected health information. Under
the HIPAA Privacy Rule a lab may let a business associate, a company that handles such information
for it, hold it only on "satisfactory assurance" written into a contract, and the contract "may not
authorize the business associate to use or further disclose the information in a manner that would
violate the requirements of this subpart, if done by the covered entity", the lab itself (45 CFR
164.502(e) and 164.504(e), eCFR, checked 1 October 2026).

## What does each head want?

**Who needs the answer.** Your group, which has to choose between heads who are each right about
something.

**The questions on the way.** What does each head fear most?

- **The revenue cycle head:** "Sign the second clearinghouse today. Every working day we wait is
  another $5,500 we have not even asked for."
- **The finance head:** "Take Medicare's advance and stop the overtime. Change may be back before
  the new enrolments clear."
- **The compliance and privacy official:** "Not one claim goes to a new company until its agreement
  is signed, and their draft lets them sell products built on our patients' claims."
- **The lab director:** "Whatever you choose, the lab keeps testing. Doctors are waiting for
  results, and our invoices are not their problem."

## What do you owe Dr Menon when time is called, and how is each of you scored?

**Who needs the answer.** Each of you: Dr Menon acts on one position, and the panel scores every
learner alone on what they said and did in the seventeen minutes.

**The questions on the way.** What does the group hand over? How does the round run? What earns
the marks?

When time is called, the group gives Dr Menon one position, or the point where it splits, named out
loud; the one number that carries the position, and where on this card that number comes from; and
the fact that would change the group's mind, with the main risk of its position.

| Part | Minutes | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Read | 4 | Each of you reads the card alone and may write. |
| Discuss | 17 | The four of you talk to each other. The chair answers no question about the numbers, so state any assumption out loud. |
| The panel's questions | 4 | The chair puts two questions, each to a learner by name. |

You have pen and paper and one phone calculator in the middle of the table. A row whose source says
assumption or illustration is yours to challenge: say what you would use instead, and why.
