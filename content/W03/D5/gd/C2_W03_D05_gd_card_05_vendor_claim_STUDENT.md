# GD card 05: A vendor says its model cut a lab's denials by 30 percent. Is that number good enough for Kalpa to buy on?

Build 1, Friday's group discussion. Read the card alone in three minutes; the rules and the scoring
are at its end. Kalpa Health and its people are fictional, the vendor's case study is an
illustration, and the fact about real insurers was checked on 1 October 2026.

> "Revenue cycle wants to license a denial-prediction model for $240,000 a year. The vendor says it
> cut denials 30 percent at a lab like ours. Finance asked 'against what?' and got no answer. Do I
> sign?"
> Dr Priya Menon, COO, Kalpa Health

**Who needs the answer.** Dr Menon, who signs or refuses $240,000 a year. A model bought on a number
it did not earn costs that every year, and a model refused that works leaves denied claims to be
reworked by hand.

**The questions on the way.** What is a denial, and what would the model do? What did the client lab
see, and what else changed there? What did labs without the model see? What is each voice claiming?
What do you owe Dr Menon when time is called?

## What is a denial, and what would the model do?

**Who needs the answer.** The revenue cycle head, whose team reworks every denied claim.

**The questions on the way.** How is a denial rate counted? Why does it move?

A denial is a payer's decision, after it has processed a claim, not to pay all or part of it. The
initial denial rate is the claims a payer refuses on its first answer, out of the claims sent. The
model would score each claim before it leaves Kalpa, and a person would review the risky ones.
Denial rates move with the payers as well as with the lab: insurers selling plans on HealthCare.gov
denied 19 percent of in-network claims in 2024, from 3 percent at one insurer to 36 percent at
another (KFF, 24 March 2026, checked 1 October 2026).

**Who needs the answer.** The finance head, who asked "against what?" and will check every figure
you quote against these lines.

**The questions on the way.** What happened at the client lab, and what else changed there? What
happened at labs without the model? What is the model worth to Kalpa?

## What is each voice claiming?

**Who needs the answer.** Your group, which has to decide how much of the 30 percent belongs to the
model.

**The questions on the way.** What would each speaker accept as proof?

- **The revenue cycle head:** "Thirty percent. Every month we wait, my team reworks claims the model
  would have caught."
- **The finance head:** "Thirty percent against what? Show me labs that did not buy it."
- **The vendor's case study:** "Results like these are typical in our clients' first six months."

## What do you owe Dr Menon when time is called, and how is each of you scored?

**Who needs the answer.** Each of you: Dr Menon acts on one position, and the panel scores every
learner alone on what they said and did in the eighteen minutes.

**The questions on the way.** What does the group hand over? How does the round run? What earns
the marks?

When time is called, the group gives Dr Menon one position, or the point where it splits, named out
loud; the one number that carries the position, and where on this card that number comes from; and
the fact that would change the group's mind, with the main risk of its position.

| Part | Minutes | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Read | 3 | Each of you reads the card alone and may write. |
| Discuss | 18 | The four of you talk to each other. The chair answers no question about the numbers, so state any assumption out loud. |
| The panel's questions | 4 | The chair puts two questions, each to a learner by name. |

You have pen and paper and one phone calculator in the middle of the table. A row whose source says
assumption or illustration is yours to challenge: say what you would use instead, and why.
