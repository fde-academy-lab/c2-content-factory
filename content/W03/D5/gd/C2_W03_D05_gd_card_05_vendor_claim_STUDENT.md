# GD card 05: A vendor says its model cut a lab's denials by 30 percent. Is that number good enough for Kalpa to buy on?

This card is for Friday's group discussion in Build 1, and you read it alone in five minutes.
Kalpa Health and its people are fictional, the vendor's case study is an illustration, and the fact
about real insurers was checked on 1 October 2026.

> "Revenue cycle wants to license a denial-prediction model for $240,000 a year. The vendor says it
> cut denials 30 percent at a lab like ours. Finance is not convinced. Do I sign?"
> Dr Priya Menon, COO, Kalpa Health

**Who needs the answer.** Dr Menon, who signs or refuses $240,000 a year; a model bought on a number
it did not earn costs that every year, and a model refused that works leaves denied claims to be
reworked by hand.

**The questions on the way.** What is a denial, and what would the model do? What do the case study
and the benchmarking group report? What do revenue cycle, finance and the vendor claim? What do you
owe Dr Menon when time is called?

## What is a denial, and what would the model do?

**Who needs the answer.** The revenue cycle head, whose team reworks every denied claim.

**The questions on the way.** Who pays a claim, and how is a denial rate counted?

A payer is whoever pays a claim: a health plan, Medicare, Medicaid or the patient. A denial is a
payer's decision, after it has processed a claim, not to pay all or part of it, and the initial
denial rate is the claims refused on the payer's first answer, out of the claims sent. The model
would score each claim before it leaves Kalpa, and a person would review the risky ones. In 2024,
insurers selling plans on HealthCare.gov denied 19 percent of in-network claims, those from providers under contract
with them, and the rate ran from 3 percent at one insurer to 36 percent at another (KFF, 24 March 2026).

## What do the case study and the benchmarking group report?

**Who needs the answer.** The finance head, who budgets the model's saving if Dr Menon signs.

**The questions on the way.** What did the client lab and the other labs report, and what would the
model save and cost?

| What | Number | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| The client lab's initial denial rate, in the six months before the model | 12.0 percent | The vendor's case study (illustration) |
| The same, in the six months after | 8.4 percent | The vendor's case study (illustration) |
| What the vendor calls this | "30 percent fewer denials" | The vendor's case study (illustration) |
| The client lab's denials caused by its largest payer's prior authorisation rule (the payer's approval needed before certain tests), a rule the payer dropped in the same months | about a quarter of the lab's denials | The case study's footnote (illustration) |
| Fourteen labs in the same region without the model, over the same two periods | 11.5 percent before, 9.9 percent after | A benchmarking group (illustration) |
| What the model would save Kalpa in a year if Kalpa's denials fell 30 percent | $300,000, and the saving moves in step with the fall | Assumption (the revenue cycle head's sum) |
| The model's price | $240,000 a year | Assumption (the vendor's quote) |

## What do revenue cycle, finance and the vendor claim?

**Who needs the answer.** Your group, which tells Dr Menon whether to sign.

**The questions on the way.** What does each voice want her to do, and on what evidence?

- **The revenue cycle head:** "Thirty percent. Every month we wait, my team reworks claims the model
  would have caught."
- **The finance head:** "Two hundred and forty thousand a year, every year, on one case study. I am
  not convinced."
- **The vendor's case study:** "Results like these are typical in our clients' first six months."

## What do you owe Dr Menon when time is called, and how is each of you scored?

**Who needs the answer.** Each of you, since Dr Menon acts on the one position your group hands over
and the chair scores every learner alone.

**The questions on the way.** What does the group hand over? How does the round run? What earns the
marks?

The chair's opening says all of this before the clock starts, so your reading minutes are for the
case above. When time is called, the group gives Dr Menon one position, or the point where it splits,
named out loud; the one number that carries the position, and where on this card that number comes
from; and the fact that would change the group's mind, with the main risk of its position.
