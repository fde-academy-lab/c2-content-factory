# How will Mock R1 question you on Thursday 22 October, and how do you get ready without leaving the build?

Kalpa Health, Kalpa Group and everyone in them are fictional, and every record in the Build 1 files
is synthetic.

**Who needs the answer.** You do, before your slot. Mock R1 is a mock interview: one assessor
questions you alone for about 20 minutes while your group keeps building, the way a first-round
screen runs at a Global Capability Centre (GCC), the offshore centre where a global company does its
own work. Its first half tests the Weeks 1 and 2 method, and its second half is a viva, a spoken
defence of your group's work. A learner who arrives without knowing how the 20 minutes run spends the
first five of them working out what is being asked.

**The questions on the way.** What is Mock R1, and where does it sit in the Kalpa Health build week?
How are the 20 minutes spent? What does the technical half ask, and what will it never ask? What does
the viva ask about your group's work? How is the mock scored? Who questions you, and what changes
online? What should you have open, and what must be closed? How do you prepare in about 30 minutes?
What happens after your mock, and what does your group still owe today?

## What is Mock R1, and where does it sit in the Kalpa Health build week?

**Who needs the answer.** Every learner needs it before preparing. The mock asks about the question
your group has worked on since Monday and about the method you learned before it, so knowing where it
sits in the week tells you which of the week's work to have ready.

**The questions on the way.** What business is Kalpa Health, and what has its chief operating officer
asked? Which question is your group's? What has the week asked of your group so far, and what comes
after Thursday? What is the mock worth?

Kalpa Health is a US diagnostics business: a laboratory, which runs the tests, and two patient
service centres, where a phlebotomist draws patients' blood, in each of six US metro areas. It bills
each patient's payer in dollars: a commercial health plan, Medicare (the federal programme for people
aged 65 and over and some younger people with disabilities), Medicaid (each state's programme for
people on low incomes) or the patient, who pays for themselves (self-pay). Its revenue-cycle and analytics work runs from
Kalpa's GCC in Bengaluru, where you are a trainee engineer in the data and AI team. It reports in calendar
quarters: Q2 is April to June 2026 and Q3 is July to September 2026.

Its chief operating officer (COO), Dr Priya Menon, wrote to your team: her dashboard shows test
volumes up 5 percent from Q2 to Q3, against a plan of 18, and she wants to know which branch of her
business is short. Five of her heads each asked her one question, and your group is answering one of
them:

| # | Who asks | What they asked, word for word |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The finance head | "The board will ask me where the plan's growth went. Where does our lab revenue actually come from, and which branch of it is short?" |
| 2 | The patient service centres' operations head | "Bookings fell in two of our metros in Q3. Before I send a field team or cut staff there, I need to know how far they fell, and why." |
| 3 | The finance head | "The claims we billed say one thing and the posting system says another. Which claims are unpaid, how much money is that, and can I trust the figure I report?" |
| 4 | The patient service centres' operations head | "KH-ATL-03, one of our two Atlanta patient service centres, has the worst no-show rate on my Q3 report. I am being asked to add a receptionist there or close it. Is the centre really worse?" |
| 5 | The marketing head | "Our free at-home collection offer lifted bookings 9 percent. I want to offer it to every patient in all six metros. Can you confirm it worked?" |

The week runs like this. On Monday your group took its question and mapped it, on the translation
worksheet, onto the Weeks 1 and 2 method. On Wednesday it stated its headline claim: one sentence
with its number, what the number is out of, its quarter and its caveat, the one thing that could make
the claim wrong. On Thursday every learner sits Mock R1 while the group finishes the build. On Friday
the build freezes, which means the group's code stops changing before its demo runs, and the group
discussions and the first presentations begin. On Saturday 24 October the presentations close and
every Build 1 grade closes with them.

Build 1 carries three scores for each learner: the mini project, worth 40 marks with the
presentation; the group discussion, worth 30; and Mock R1, worth 30.

Mock R1 is a 20-minute conversation with one assessor, alone, on Thursday 22 October: its technical
half tests the Weeks 1 and 2 method on questions you have not seen, and its viva tests what your
group did with that method in Kalpa Health.

## How are the 20 minutes spent?

**Who needs the answer.** You need it to pace yourself. The assessor moves on at the ninth minute of
the technical half even mid-answer, so an answer that takes three minutes to warm up loses its
follow-up and the marks the follow-up carries.

**The questions on the way.** How long is each part? What happens between the two halves? How does
the mock close?

1. The assessor opens with your seat, your group and your group's question, and says how the two
   halves run.
2. The technical half runs for about nine minutes. The assessor says when it ends and moves straight
   on to your group's work, so keep your notebook and logs on screen.
3. The viva runs for about eight minutes, on your group's files, notebook and logs.
4. The assessor closes. You may ask one question about the mock itself, and the assessor gives no
   mark in the room.
5. You go straight back to your group.

The 20 minutes are about nine on Weeks 1 and 2, about eight on your group's work, and three of
opening, changeover and close.

## What does the technical half ask, and what will it never ask?

**Who needs the answer.** You need it to decide what to revise. The half carries 15 of the 30 marks
and tests the fortnight's method on questions you have not seen, so rereading your group's files
does not prepare you for it.

**The questions on the way.** How many questions, and at which levels? Which moves can it ask about?
Where are the questions set? What will it never ask?

The half asks three questions, one at each level, and each is asked the way a stakeholder would ask
it, with numbers on the table:

| Level | What it asks | What a good answer does |
|---|---|---|
| The move | A business ask that one of the fortnight's moves answers | Names the move, applies it, and says the first check |
| The number | A plausible wrong number, and where it came from | Names what produced the number, the check that catches it, and the fix |
| The judgement | A stakeholder pushing back, or a choice between ways to answer | Weighs the ways, holds a position with a number, and says what would change it |

Every question has a follow-up that takes the case one step further, so a rehearsed answer carries
you only as far as the first question.

The questions come from the moves the ten taught days of Weeks 1 and 2 trained:

| Day | The move |
|---|---|
| Week 1 Monday | Which total is the number, the revenue tree, and the typical value one large record cannot move |
| Week 1 Tuesday | Is a drop real, which branch and which segment moved, and did the price change or the mix |
| Week 1 Wednesday | Profile before you touch, what makes two records one, and the bridge between two totals |
| Week 1 Thursday | Real or the wobble, how many stand behind a rate, and did a change work |
| Week 1 Friday | The note in four parts, held when someone pushes |
| Week 2 Monday | The warehouse query: its logical order, and a number an auditor can rerun |
| Week 2 Tuesday | Which rows a join keeps, drops or repeats, and the checks before a number leaves |
| Week 2 Wednesday | Ranking inside groups, ties at the line, and change month to month |
| Week 2 Thursday | One row per member in pandas, the merge that must not repeat a row, and the as-of date |
| Week 2 Friday | Which tool owns which number, and what a director can break in a workbook |

The questions are set in Kalpa Health's US business, in parts of it your group's files do not cover,
on numbers the assessor invents for the question and reads aloud, so you need nothing open to answer
them. The half never asks you to write code from memory, though a question may ask what a query, a
join, a groupby or a lookup returns and why, which you answer in words. It never asks about your
group's files or any other group's, since that is the viva's job.

## What does the viva ask about your group's work?

**Who needs the answer.** You and your group do, because the viva tests the group's work on you
alone: a learner who cannot explain a number a group-mate built loses viva marks the group's work had
earned.

**The questions on the way.** In what order do the questions climb? What is the first question? What
will you be asked to show? What if a group-mate built the part you are asked about? What will the
assessor never tell you?

The viva climbs in three steps, the way an interviewer moves from your work to your judgement:

1. The assessor asks what your group did: how you carried the Week 1 or 2 move into Kalpa Health,
   and what you had to change to make it fit. The first question is the day's interview question,
   tagged `[S]`, the programme's mark for a staple question asked in screens everywhere: "Walk me
   through the analysis you did on unfamiliar data and one decision you would defend."
2. The assessor asks why your group chose its way: one decision it made, why that one, and what the
   alternative would have given.
3. The assessor asks what would change your group's call: your headline claim and its caveat, then
   pushes on the caveat the way Dr Menon or one of her heads would, and you hold it, give it up or
   change it, with a number either way.

Last comes the question your challenges log answers: what you would do differently if you started
again tomorrow. The challenges log is where your group records each time it was stuck, what it tried
and what it decided; the decisions log holds every cleaning or matching call, with the rows it moved
and its reason.

The assessor will ask you at least once to show where a number comes from. You answer for every
number your group will show Dr Menon, including the parts a group-mate built, and you say which
cell, query or log line made it. The assessor never confirms or denies anything about what the data
holds; if a word of the domain is unclear, ask what it means.

## How is the mock scored?

**Who needs the answer.** You need it to know where the 30 marks sit: half rest on the technical
half and half on the viva, and two of the six rows score only what you do when the assessor pushes.

**The questions on the way.** Which six rows score the mock? What does each row reward?

You are scored alone, 15 marks on each half:

<!-- sync:rubric:W03/mock -->
**Mock interview R1, 30 marks.** Each learner is scored alone, 15 marks on the technical half and 15 on the project viva.

| Half | Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| Technical | Correctness | 8 |
| Technical | Reasoning aloud with numbers | 4 |
| Technical | Handling a follow-up | 3 |
| Project viva | The translation, with one decision defended by evidence | 6 |
| Project viva | Defending a caveat under challenge | 6 |
| Project viva | What they would do differently | 3 |
<!-- /sync:rubric:W03/mock -->

| Row | What it rewards |
|---|---|
| Correctness | The right move for each question, with what produced the number named |
| Reasoning aloud with numbers | Using the numbers you are given, each with what it is out of, and working one out aloud |
| Handling a follow-up | Taking each answer one step further when the case moves |
| The translation, with one decision defended by evidence | Saying in your own words how the Week 1 or 2 move fits your group's question, and defending one decision with a count or dollars and its alternative |
| Defending a caveat under challenge | Holding your group's caveat with a number when it is pushed, and saying what would change it |
| What they would do differently | A real step you would move or change, taken from your own log |

Two rows, 9 of the 30 marks, reward what you do when the assessor pushes: the follow-up and the
caveat under challenge.

## Who questions you, and what changes if your mock is online?

**Who needs the answer.** You need it once you have your slot, because an online mock runs from your
own laptop in a separate room, and a learner who arrives without the group's files open loses viva
minutes finding them.

**The questions on the way.** Who are the assessors? Where does an online mock happen? What do you do
on the call?

One of three assessors questions you: the Programme Head or the Academic TA, in person, or the
Principal Advisor, online. All three use the same bank of questions, the same timings and the same
rubric. Your slot, your seat (your place in your group, numbered from 1) and your assessor are on the
seat list, which reaches you with this brief on Wednesday evening and again on paper at the start of
Thursday. Three mocks run at once, one with each assessor, and the three learners out at once always
come from three different groups.

For an online mock, take your laptop to the quiet room the trainer points you to, join the call from
it with your camera on and your group's files open, and share your screen when the viva starts. The
assessor will ask you to show the room once with the camera at the start.

## What should you have open, and what must be closed?

**Who needs the answer.** You need it before you walk to your slot: a file you have to search for
during the viva costs viva minutes, and an AI assistant on screen is closed at once and noted.

**The questions on the way.** Which files do you open? What must be closed?

Have these open, ready to show:

- your group's notebook or SQL, run top to bottom so every output is showing;
- your group's copy of the challenges log, `content/W03/D1/briefs/C2_W03_D01_challenges_log_STUDENT.xlsx`;
- your group's copy of the decisions log, `content/W03/D1/briefs/C2_W03_D01_decisions_log_STUDENT.xlsx`;
- your group's translation worksheet from Monday and the headline claim it stated on Wednesday.

No AI assistant is open during the mock, on any screen or device, and your phone stays out of reach.

## How do you prepare in about 30 minutes without leaving the build?

**Who needs the answer.** You and your group need it on Wednesday evening. The build has first call
on Thursday and the first mocks start ten minutes into the day, so a learner whose slot comes early
has no time on the day to prepare.

**The questions on the way.** When do you prepare, and on what? What if your slot is in the first
hour? How do you prepare for the technical half without knowing its questions?

Preparation takes about 30 minutes in all, in three pieces of about 10. If your slot is in the first
hour of the day, do all three on Wednesday evening.

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| Wednesday evening | Reread your group's challenges log and decisions log end to end. For every entry, say aloud what happened and why. Mark any entry you cannot explain, and ask its author before your slot. |
| During Thursday's build | Pick one number your group will give Dr Menon. Find the cell that produces it, say what it is out of and over which quarter, and say what would make it wrong. Swap with a group-mate and quiz each other on one number each. |
| Two slots before yours | Say your answer to "walk me through the analysis you did on unfamiliar data and one decision you would defend" in under two minutes. Then say your group's headline claim with its caveat, and let a group-mate push on the caveat as hard as they can. |

For the technical half, take each day in the move table above and say three things aloud: the
question that day's method answers, the plausible wrong number it guards against, and the check that
catches that number. That rehearses every level the half asks for without knowing a single question.

A thin log is the easiest place for a viva to find a gap, so a group that writes its challenges and
decisions down as they happen is preparing its mocks as it builds.

## What happens after your mock, and what does your group still owe today?

**Who needs the answer.** You need it on your way back, and your group needs it all day: the build
freezes on Friday, and a demo that does not run cold in front of the panel is scored as not run cold
in presentation and defence, the 6 marks each member earns alone.

**The questions on the way.** What do you say about the questions? What do you add to the log? What
must the build reach by tonight? What does the trainer check during the day? When do the marks
close?

Say nothing to anyone about the questions you were asked. The follow-ups move, so a question passed
on gives the next learner little, and it makes the mock less useful as practice for the screens it
prepares you for.

Back at your group, add one line to the challenges log if the viva showed a gap in how your group
reasoned or recorded its work, and close the gap before the build freezes.

By the end of today your group's build should be complete enough to freeze on Friday:

- the notebook or SQL runs top to bottom from the raw files, the ten files exactly as the data team
  exported them, on a machine that is not its author's;
- the decisions log and the challenges log are current;
- the one-slide answer for Dr Menon is drafted: the claim, the evidence, the caveat and the action.

The trainer visits each group three times during the day, for a few minutes each: once to ask who
is out when and what would stop your notebook running cold; once to pick one number from your
one-slide answer and ask what it is out of, which cell makes it and what would make it wrong; and
once to watch your notebook run from a fresh start on a machine that is not its author's, then ask
which sentence of your answer is the caveat. Tonight's task is the presentation draft and one
rehearsal of the demo, start to finish, on the raw files.

Your assessor enters your marks in the five minutes after your slot, and no mark is said in the
room. Every Build 1 grade, the mock's among them, closes on Saturday 24 October, the last day of the
presentations.
