# How does a group that is behind still reach a claim it can defend tomorrow, and who helps it get there?

**TRAINER ONLY.** It is for the Academic TA and the trainer. Use it for any group the checkpoint
marked stuck on two or more of its three questions, and for any group that names a blocker at the
close. Thursday opens Mock R1, and each learner's viva runs on the group's own work, so a group that
ships a small, honest claim tonight is better placed than one still chasing a full answer. Tuesday
was Dussehra, so today carries build days two and three, and the catch-up reserve is already spent
as slack: tonight's open build time is the only catch-up the week has left.

## Which groups go on the plan, and when?

**Who needs the answer.** The Academic TA, who runs the open build time after the second block and
takes stuck groups in the order the checkpoint recorded them.

**The questions on the way.** Which signals put a group on the plan? When is it reviewed?

| Signal | When it is seen | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Stuck on two or more of its three checkpoint questions | The checkpoint, first 30 minutes | The trainer sits with the group first in the morning's build time; the TA takes it first in the open build time |
| A named blocker at the close | The close, last 20 minutes | The TA starts the open build time at its table |
| No number it can reproduce from the raw files by the close | The close | The group goes on the plan, and the Programme Head hears about it today |

## What is the smallest honest claim each brief still supports?

**Who needs the answer.** A group behind on its brief, which still owes Dr Menon a sentence with its
denominators and caveat, and the TA, who helps it cut scope without cutting the method.

**The questions on the way.** What does each brief's smallest claim say? What does it cut? What does
it keep?

The rule for every brief: **cut scope, never the method.** The identity rule, the reconciliation
and the caveat stay in the smallest claim; what gets cut is the second and third branch. The words
in brackets are the group's to fill from its own run, and the TA never fills them.

| Brief | The smallest honest claim | What it cuts | What it keeps, and the Week 1 or 2 move |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Revenue | "Billed revenue moved from [Q2 total] to [Q3 total], and the branch with the largest change is [branch], where [claims or the mean claim] moved it; the mean and the median claim differ by [amount], so [caveat]." | Tests inside a claim, and every split below the first | Every amount converted, the claims reconciled to the completed bookings, the median beside the mean (Week 1 Monday's tree and typical value; Week 1 Wednesday's reconciliation) |
| 2. Bookings | "In the two metros, bookings fell from [Q2] to [Q3], counted across [the files used], against [the other count] in [the file]; the fall is [real, or partly how it was counted] because [reason]." | The channel and site rungs of the ladder | One row per booking id, every booking file profiled, bookings reconciled to claims metro by metro (Week 1 Tuesday's rung 1; Week 1 Wednesday's identity rule) |
| 3. Billing | "After one rule matching postings to claims, [share] of claims are paid; between billed and paid sit [amount], of which the largest part is [what it is]." | Classifying the gap past its two or three largest parts | The stated matching rule, the match count before and after, anti-joins both ways (Week 2 Tuesday's join counted first; Week 1 Wednesday's rows in equal rows kept plus set aside) |
| 4. No-shows | "KH-ATL-03's no-show rate is [n of d] against [n of d] at the other centres, counted on [which visits]; on a base of [d] the gap [is or is not] larger than chance would give." | Any split by weekday or patient | Numerator and denominator stated for both sides, the choice of visits in the decisions log (Week 1 Thursday's count beside the rate, and real or noise) |
| 5. The campaign | "Offered patients booked [x] per patient against [y] for the rest over [window]; inside [metro] the comparison reads [z], so the 9 percent [holds or does not] as a cause." | The weeks before the offer, and any metro past the first | One comparison inside one metro, and bookings per patient with its window (Week 1 Thursday's cause or coincidence) |

A claim that keeps its caveat and is small counts as finished. A large claim with the caveat missing
is the one the panel takes apart.

## What does the Academic TA do at the table, and in what order?

**Who needs the answer.** The TA, who has a fixed open build time and several stuck groups, and the
group, which must leave the table able to run its own number cold.

**The questions on the way.** What happens in the first minute? What comes before any analysis?
What does the TA never do?

1. **Minute zero.** Read the group's blocker back to it in its own words, from the checkpoint grid,
   and agree which of its three checkpoint questions it will answer within the hour.
2. **The smallest reproducible number.** One count from a raw file, with the code that makes it,
   run top to bottom. If the notebook does not run cold, that comes first. A syntax or runtime error
   gets two minutes and its last line, and the TA reads it with the group, never for it.
3. **The method, not the data.** The TA's questions are the follow-ups in
   `checkpoints/C2_W03_D03_checkpoint_guide_TRAINER.md`, the weak-answer column, one at a time. The
   TA never states a number the group has not computed, never names what is in the files and never
   points at a row.
4. **The claim, with the group.** Fill the bracketed claim above from the group's own outputs and
   pin it in the challenges log with the date.
5. **The work split across four.** So that Thursday's viva finds every member able to explain one
   move: one member on the identity rule and the profile, one on the reconciliation, one on the claim
   and its denominators, and one on the decisions log and the challenges log. A group of three folds
   the last two together.
6. **Before the TA leaves.** The group has run its notebook or SQL cold once, the presentation
   skeleton (claim, evidence, caveat, action) has its four headings and one line under each, and
   every member has Thursday's mock brief and knows their slot from the seat list sent at the close,
   since the first mocks start ten minutes into Thursday.

## When does the Programme Head need to hear about a group today?

**Who needs the answer.** The Programme Head, who decides what happens next for a group the plan
cannot reach; this plan never decides it for them.

**The questions on the way.** Which signals cannot wait until Thursday? Why?

Tell the Programme Head today, in person or by message, when any one of these holds.

| Signal | Why it cannot wait |
|---|---|
| A group names the same blocker at the checkpoint and again at the close, with no number moved between | Thursday's viva will find nothing to question. |
| A group cannot reproduce any number from the raw files by the end of the open build time | Nothing it presents can run cold, and the demo runs cold on Friday or Saturday. |
| One member has done all the work, or a member was absent on Monday and today | Mock R1 and the group discussion score each learner alone, and the groups were built for four. |
| Two or more groups on the same brief are stuck on the same step | The step may be a defect in the brief or the data, and one fix reaches all of them. |
| A group asks to change its brief | The allocation is the Programme Head's decision, made on Monday. |
