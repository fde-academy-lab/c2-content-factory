# What does Rohan say on the discovery call, and what does he keep back?

TRAINER. For the three role-players on Tuesday: the trainer and the two TAs, one room each. Thirteen
groups at eight minutes is 104 minutes of calls, so each room runs four or five calls in about 40
minutes of wall time. Each role-player holds this sheet, the eight constraint cards for the groups in
the room, and the tally on the last page.

**Who needs the answer.** The three role-players, who must play one Rohan the same way in three
rooms, so that a fact one group earned by asking is never handed to another group for free.

**The questions on the way.** Who is Rohan, and how does he talk? How do the eight minutes run? What
does he answer, and only when asked? What does he not know? What does he never volunteer? How does
the restate-back run, and how is the card handed over?

## Who is Rohan, and how does he talk?

| What | How to play it |
|---|---|
| Who he is | Rohan Desai, head of risk, Kalpa Financial Services. The room met him in Week 5, pricing loans at twenty to one. |
| What he wants | A list his collections team can use before October's due date, its error bill, and three people who can defend every number. |
| How he talks | Short sentences, numbers, no machine learning words. He says "list", "call", "cost" and "my team". |
| What annoys him | "Accuracy", jargon, and a question that asks him to solve the problem for the group. |
| His habit | When a question is vague he asks back: "Which part?" When a group pitches a model, he asks: "So what does my team do on Monday?" |
| What he never does | Rescue a silent group, explain the data's oddities, or say whether an answer is right. |

The research behind the format: in role-played elicitation interviews, student groups make most of
their mistakes in how they word questions, which questions they leave out and in what order they ask
(Bano, Zowghi, Ferrari and colleagues, 2019). A role-player who fills the silence takes that lesson
away, so let the silence sit.

## How do the eight minutes run?

| Minutes | What happens | Rohan's line |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:30 | Rohan opens | "Last week you priced my loans at twenty to one. This is a call, a smaller thing to get wrong in both directions. You have eight minutes. Ask." |
| 0:30 to 6:00 | The group asks; every member asks at least two | Answers from the sheet below, only to the question asked |
| 6:00 to 7:00 | The restate-back | "Stop. You, say it back to me in two sentences: whom we call, why, and what a wrong call costs." |
| 7:00 to 8:00 | The card | Rohan reads the group's card aloud and hands it over |

If a member has not asked by minute five, Rohan turns to them: "And your question?" He does not
suggest one.

## What does Rohan answer, and only when asked?

Each answer is given only to a question that asks for it, in about the words shown. A question that
asks for two things gets two answers; a question that asks for nothing specific gets "Which part?"

### The decision

| If the group asks | Rohan says |
|---|---|
| What exactly are we deciding? | "Whom my team calls before October's due date. One call each." |
| What happens on the call? | "The agent reminds them the payment is due and asks if they need help. What the agent says is my problem. Whom they call is yours." |
| Does anything else change, a limit, a letter? | "Nothing else changes. One action: a call." |
| Why not call everyone? | "Calling everyone costs something too. Work out what." |

### Who uses the list

| If the group asks | Rohan says |
|---|---|
| Who gets the list? | "My collections team lead. One sheet: who to call, in what order." |
| Will the callers understand a score? | "No. If they get a reason, it has to fit in one sentence." Card 4's group hears its card instead. |
| When do you need it? | "Saturday, in twelve minutes, with the list running live." |

### What each error costs

| If the group asks | Rohan says |
|---|---|
| What does a wasted call cost? | "One unit. An agent's time, and a good customer who feels chased." |
| What does a missed default cost? | "About five units. We write off what they owe." |
| Why units, and not rupees? | "Our call centre is not running yet, so finance cannot price a unit. The ratio I am sure of: five to one." |
| Does a big balance cost more? | "For this week, every default costs the same five units." Card 3's group hears its card instead. |

### When each column is known

| If the group asks | Rohan says |
|---|---|
| What is in the data? | "Each cardholder's limit, a few profile fields, and six monthly statements, April to September 2005: the repayment status, the bill and the amount paid." |
| What do the repayment status codes mean? | Read the UCI page's words: "-1 means pay duly; 1 means a payment delay of one month, 2 two months, up to 9, nine months and above." Nothing more. |
| What do education and marital status mean? | The UCI page's words: "Education: 1 graduate school, 2 university, 3 high school, 4 others. Marital status: 1 married, 2 single, 3 others." Nothing more. |
| Which month is which column? | "The page says the first status, the first bill and the first payment are September; the last are April." |
| When is September's statement available? | "Assume it is in hand when the list is built." Card 6's group hears its card instead. |
| When do we know who defaulted? | "After October's due date passes." Card 7's group hears its card instead. |
| What share defaulted? | "You tell me. It is in the file." |
| Is the data clean? | "It is a public book. You tell me." |

### Rules and limits

| If the group asks | Rohan says |
|---|---|
| How many calls can the team make? | Card 2's group hears its card. Everyone else: "We are staffing now. Tell me how many calls your list needs, and what each extra call buys me." |
| May we use sex, age, marital status or education? | Card 5's group hears its card. Everyone else: "Nobody has told me no. If you use them, show me on Saturday whom your list calls by group." |
| May we use names, phone numbers or contacts? | "This book has none. In our live system, only what the customer agreed to share for this purpose." |
| Can we see Kalpa's own data? | "It does not exist yet. That is why we prove the method on Taiwan's book. Your plan tells me what changes when ours arrives." |
| Why Taiwan? | "It is licensed, public, and has six months per card. Taiwan's 2005 book is never India's book, so its score is never ours." |

### What success looks like, and what could go wrong

| If the group asks | Rohan says |
|---|---|
| What does success look like? | "A list I can hand my team, its error bill against doing nothing, and three people who can each defend every number." |
| How will you judge the list? | "In cost units. I have been shown 80 percent accuracy before, and it did not tell me what the list cost." |
| What worries you most? | "A list nobody can explain, and a number that falls apart when my CFO asks how it was made." |
| What would make you reject it? | "A number on a slide that the person presenting cannot recompute." |

## What does Rohan not know, and what does he never volunteer?

**What he does not know.** Any value the UCI page does not define (a repayment status of -2 or 0,
education 0, 5 or 6, marital status 0), why some accounts that owed nothing are labelled default, and
why some rows repeat. To every such question he says:

> "I do not know, and I will not guess. Write the question in your challenges log, write what you
> assumed, and show me both on Saturday."

Card 8's group already holds the rule that a code outside the dictionary is a question; its data
team's ruling on accounts owing nothing is the group's twist at Thursday's gate. Nobody is told the
answer on Tuesday.

**What he never volunteers.** Each of these reaches a group only through a question, a card or a
twist.

| Never volunteered | How it reaches a group |
|---|---|
| The exact ratio beyond "about five" | Asked: "What does a missed default cost?" |
| The call capacity | Card 2, or asked |
| The share of the book that defaulted | Computed by the group |
| The odd codes, the accounts owing nothing, the repeated rows | Found by the group in the data |
| The CFO's twenty to one | Card 1's twist at the gate |
| Any model, algorithm or threshold | Never; "I do not care which algorithm. I care which customers, and what it costs me." |
| Whether an answer is right | Never; "Show me on Saturday." |

## How does the restate-back run?

At minute six Rohan points at one member, the quietest if he can tell, and asks for two sentences:
whom we call, why, and what a wrong call costs.

| What the member says | Rohan's reply |
|---|---|
| "Before October's due date we call the cardholders most likely to default, because a missed default costs about five times a wasted call. We hand you that list with its error bill in cost units." | "Yes." |
| Names accuracy as the goal | "Accuracy of what? I pay in units." One retry. |
| Leaves out the action | "And then what does my team do?" One retry. |
| Leaves out the costs | "What does a mistake cost me?" One retry. |
| Promises Kalpa's own numbers | "These are Taiwan's cardholders. Try again." One retry. |

After one retry Rohan says "Yes" or names the gap in one line, and the role-player writes which in
the tally. That sentence, once it earns a yes, becomes the first line of the group's slide 1.

## How is the card handed over?

Rohan reads the card's rule aloud in his own words, hands over the top half only, and says: "Copy it
onto your ticket, word for word. Bring me what it asks for on Thursday." He answers no question about
the card after that; the group asks at Wednesday's checkpoint, where a TA answers from the card's
"Where a rule like this comes from" line and nothing further.

| Card | What Rohan says as he hands it over |
|---|---|
| 1 | "A missed default costs us five times a wasted call. Set the list by that." |
| 2 | "The collections team can make 1,000 calls this month, out of these 9,000 cardholders." |
| 3 | "My collections team is paid on the balance it protects. Build them a list they would want." |
| 4 | "Every caller must be able to say the main reason a customer is on the list, in one sentence." |
| 5 | "Sex, marital status, age and education may not be used to decide whom we call." |
| 6 | "September's statement lands after the call list is built. Build only from what exists on list day." |
| 7 | "October's outcomes arrive in November. I want the list before then." |
| 8 | "A code that is not in the data dictionary is a question for my data team, never a guess." |
