## 6. What needs a rebuild rather than a raise, ranked

These are the things this sweep raised as far as a raise can go, and where the honest answer is that the artifact should be rebuilt from its curriculum row rather than improved further.

1. **The Day 1 decks.** All three now carry ten diagrams each and a question-and-answer pair, and none of them carries a single DEPTH slide, because Day 1 never had one. The row's own `STOP BEFORE` list is long and the day genuinely has no depth stops, so a trainer has no marked cut path on the opening day of the programme. Deciding what Day 1's depth stops are is a curriculum decision rather than a content one, and it should be taken before Cohort 2 opens.
2. **Day 2's two decks, at 67 and 71 slides.** They are complete and they are long. The session-length arithmetic in `09_Artifact_Specs_v2.md` puts about 2.5 minutes on an interactive slide, which is more minutes than Tuesday has even with the DEPTH slides all cut. Either the DEPTH set becomes a separate reference deck or the day loses a section.
3. **Day 4's `segment_summary` and `repair_summary` exercises.** Both kept their kind and both now sit beside a hands-on notebook that does the same work with checks. There is real overlap, and the honest fix is to decide which of the two is the unguided exercise and let the other become the take-home.
4. **The `discount` field across the week.** Day 1 teaches `.get()` on it, Day 3 decides to keep its absence, and Day 4 never mentions it. It is the only planted witness in the data that does not carry through to Thursday, and a fifth stop for it would make the week's spiral complete.

## 8. Things found during the build that the Phase 1 audit did not catch

Recorded because they were found by running the material rather than by reading it.

- The truncated vendor feed opens **three** records before it stops, not the twenty-four a reasonable reader would estimate from 47 lines. The check in the Day 2 hands-on notebook was corrected against the file.
- Day 3's coerce-everything pass replaces **six rows** carrying **five distinct raw values**, because the two empty cells share one raw value. The check now says so.
- Day 4's `summarise_by` returns `median_amount` rather than `median`. Written from the prose rather than from the code, the notebook's own summary table raised a `KeyError` on first execution.
- Executing a Day 2 or Day 3 notebook writes into `notebooks/output/`, which the layout check then failed. `verify.py` now skips paths git ignores, so the gate no longer fails on its own side effects.
- A TODO twin cannot cold-run by design. `verify.py` now skips it in the `--execute` step and `nb_check.py` measures it on its source instead.

## 9. What measuring the layout found in the builder

Reporting a block that could not be placed, rather than stopping the layout when it ran out of
room, surfaced a class of silent loss that predated this work.

| Defect | What it did | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A table was told it could use every inch left in the body and filled it | The sentence under a nine-row table never reached the slide | A block is given the room left after the blocks that follow it |
| A table was squeezed by scaling its row heights | PowerPoint treats a row height as a minimum and grows the row back to hold its text, so a squeezed table rendered taller than it was told and landed on whatever came next | A table steps its type down until the rows it really needs fit |
| Every column of a table took the same width | A column of counts got as much room as a column of sentences, so the sentences wrapped four deep and the table doubled in height | Columns are sized by their longest cell, with a floor and a ceiling |
| A block was called placed if it started above the line | A two-line claim began above the body's edge and finished under the footer | A block is placed only when it fits whole |
| Text boxes carried PowerPoint's default inset, which nothing measured | The last line of a code card printed outside the card behind it | The insets are set to zero and the padding is carried by the box |
| Monospace was measured at Consolas widths | A deck is authored where Consolas does not exist and opened where it does, so the substitute font is wider and the estimate ran short | Both estimates use the widest likely font |
| A picture in a two-column layout ignored its column | It was drawn full width across both columns, over the table | The picture is placed inside the column it belongs to |

The builder now tries seven layouts for a slide that carries a picture, at four type sizes, and
keeps the one that places every block and prints the labels largest: a band reserved at the foot
at four depths, the words at full width with the picture in what is left, the picture beside the
words, and the words in two columns. Nothing in it guesses from a ratio.
