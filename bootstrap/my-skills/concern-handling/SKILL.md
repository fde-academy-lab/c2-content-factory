# Concern handling

A framework for reading, defusing and converting learner discontent, built from FDE Academy delivery experience (the Cohort 3 expectation call of 1 Sep 2026 is the reference case) and durable across cohorts and platforms. It has three parts: a model for reading the situation, a playbook for the live room, and a playbook for the one on one, with the artefact rules both depend on.

## 1. Read the situation first: the STAKE model

Score each dimension quickly before responding to anything. The response strategy follows from the profile, not from the loudest sentence.

| Dimension | Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **S**ubstance | Is the complaint factually right? | A correct complaint is an ally wearing armour; concede the fact fast and fight only for the plan. |
| **T**heatre | Is it performed for the room or held privately? | Public complaints are bids for the room; answer the room, then the person. |
| **A**nchor | Is the anchor operational (invites, materials, comms), academic (content, pace, depth), or dignity (respect, register, being heard)? | Operational anchors need visible fixes, academic anchors need positioning, dignity anchors need acknowledgement and access. Most rooms mix them; treat the dignity anchor first because it decides how the other answers land. |
| **K**inetics | Is it one voice, or is a bloc forming (learners reinforcing each other live)? | A bloc means the concern is now a cohort position; individual remedies stop working and a structural fix must be shown. |
| **E**xpectation debt | Is a promise, a sales pitch or an earlier commitment being quoted back? | Quoted promises are debts; they are never argued down, only repaid or renegotiated explicitly. |

Sentiment rule of thumb from the reference case: experienced cohorts forgive content gaps and punish operational drift, and they read register violations ("college student" tone) as contempt. Fix the register before the roadmap.

## 2. Cohort-level playbook, in order

1. **Agenda shield.** Open every risky call with a three-part agenda and park early interruptions against it with a named return point ("I will take that categorically in part three"). Never let the first interruption set the agenda.
2. **Context before feature.** Rebuild the big picture first (the programme spine, phases, weights), because every defensive answer later points back into that drawing instead of standing alone.
3. **Reframe on contact.** Replace loaded words the moment they appear and never repeat the learner's framing: diagnostic not test, learning persona not segregation, prescribed path not restriction, trade-off not limitation. The reframe is only credible when the sentence after it carries substance.
4. **Concede the fact, own the miss, keep the plan.** For every substantively correct complaint: name what happened, why, what was done in the moment, and what changes, in four sentences or fewer. No technical over-explanation, no counter-blame, and thank the room for the patience they actually showed.
5. **Show a built fix, not an intention.** One working preview (the GitHub hub in the reference case) lowers the temperature more than any number of apologies. Keep one such fix in the pocket before any risky call, and reveal it after the complaints have been voiced, not before.
6. **Give one shared finish line.** Anxiety about placements or tracks dissolves fastest when the whole room is pointed at the same end state and the placement is framed as tuning the route, never the destination.
7. **Interaction every ten minutes.** A chat poll or a direct question keeps the silent majority engaged, which is the counterweight to a vocal bloc.
8. **Trade-off honesty for structural asks.** When the ask is a schedule or format change, explain the constraint as a trade-off already explored ("we have tried these combinations across three cohorts, this is the least-loss arrangement") and offer the legitimate channel for a majority to move it. Never pretend a constraint is a choice.
9. **Boundary with a receipt.** Hold the hard stop. A learner who pushes past it gets a named follow-up time, said twice, and that follow-up is honoured fast. The follow-up is the receipt that makes the boundary respectable.
10. **Close the loop in writing within 48 hours.** Every commitment voiced in a live room is a deadline to at least part of the room. Publish the list of commitments with owners and dates, and track it to done; an unlogged promise is a future incident.

## 3. One-on-one playbook

1. Schedule it before they demand it; a pre-emptive 1:1 converts an organiser, a reactive one rewards escalation.
2. Open by replaying their concern in its strongest form, including the parts they are right about, before saying anything else. Persistent escalators are usually partially correct, which is why they land with the room.
3. Separate the three registers explicitly: what is already fixed, what is committed with a date, and what will not change and why (the trade-off, stated plainly).
4. Convert where the profile allows: give process-anchored learners (specific, fixable complaints) early sight of fixes or a review role; give dignity-anchored learners access (a leadership connect, a named listening channel). Conversion is real only if the fix ships.
5. Never make a 1:1 commitment the cohort cannot hear about; assume every private answer becomes public.
6. Log it: date, learner, anchor, commitments, and the next check-in, in the cohort's concern log, mapped to roster IDs.

## 4. Artefact rules for concern situations

- Anything written can be quoted to a partner, a regulator or the room; keep positions that were deliberately "soft" (a spoken optional, a spoken timeline) out of slides and messages, and let them live in speaker notes and voice.
- Result and placement messages must match, word for word in logic, whatever criteria were last told to the cohort; reconcile discrepancies before sending, never after.
- Acknowledgement slides follow ambition-miss-fix-thanks in that order, one line each, with no grovel and no counter-explanation.
- After the call, the debrief file (promises, concerns, watchlist, learnings) is written the same day; the reference structure is 18_C3_Expectation_Call_Debrief_and_Intel.md.

## 5. Escalation ladder

Concern (log it) → repeated concern (1:1 with receipts) → bloc forming (structural fix shown to the whole cohort) → sales-pitch or fee language (owner plus leadership informed same day, response coordinated, nothing improvised) → threat of exit or public action (leadership handles, delivery stays warm and normal in parallel).

## 6. Anti-patterns

Arguing a correct fact, over-explaining a technical failure, repeating the learner's loaded word, promising clock times operations has not confirmed, announcing fixes before complaints have been voiced, letting the loudest voice set the agenda, private side-deals, apologising a second time for the same incident instead of showing the fix, and leaving a live-room promise unlogged.
